## About this template

**Purpose.** Investor counsel sends a request list after the term sheet; company counsel answers it through a virtual data room and later through the Disclosure Schedule. Diligence findings drive three things: the closing conditions, the Disclosure Schedule exceptions, and any pre-closing clean-up (often a § 204 ratification). A company that builds its data room before signing the term sheet closes faster and negotiates from strength.

**How to prepare a data room.**

1. *Mirror this list.* Use the category numbers below as folder names so that investor counsel can map each request to a folder; include an index with document dates and a short description.
2. *Executed copies only.* Upload signed, dated versions. Where only a form exists, say so in the index, and list every person who signed that form.
3. *Reconcile the cap table first.* The cap table must tie, share for share, to the stock ledger, board approvals, signed purchase and grant agreements, and 83(b) elections. Investors' counsel will rebuild it from the documents.
4. *"None" is an answer.* For each category with nothing responsive, post a one-line statement ("No litigation pending or threatened") rather than leaving the folder empty.
5. *Privilege and sensitive data.* Do not upload privileged legal advice (litigation assessments, internal investigations) without a common-interest agreement and counsel's sign-off. Redact employee personal data (salaries can be aggregated, Social Security numbers never) and limit customer personal data. Competitively sensitive contracts can be redacted or placed in a "clean room" folder for counsel only, especially if the investor has portfolio companies in the space.
6. *Permissions and logs.* Use view-only, watermarked access, and keep the access log; it supports the Disclosure Schedule's "made available" definition.
7. *Freeze date.* Set a date after which no new documents are added without notice, so the Disclosure Schedule can reference "documents made available in the data room as of [date]."

**Red flags investors look for (and how to fix them before diligence).**

| Red flag | Why it matters | Typical fix |
|---|---|---|
| Missing or late 83(b) elections for founder or early restricted stock | Vesting shares are taxed as they vest at then-current value; the 30-day deadline cannot be extended | Confirm proof of timely filing (certified-mail receipt or IRS acknowledgment); if missed, quantify exposure, consider restructuring (e.g., cancelling and regranting at current value with appropriate tax advice) and disclose |
| Unsigned or missing invention assignment and confidentiality agreements (CIIAAs), especially from founders and early engineers or contractors | IP chain of title gaps; acquirers and later investors will require clean title | Obtain signed CIIAAs with present-tense assignment language ("hereby assigns") and confirmatory assignments for prior work; for departed contributors, negotiate a confirmatory assignment |
| Founder IP created before incorporation or at a prior employer or university | Third-party ownership claims | Technology assignment from each founder to the Company; review prior employer agreements and university policies |
| Stale 409A valuation (more than 12 months old or pre-dating a material event) or options granted below fair market value | § 409A penalty taxes for optionholders; ISO disqualification | Obtain a current independent valuation; analyze and, where possible, remediate discounted options (repricing or correction programs within the § 409A rules) |
| Stock or option grants never approved by the board, approved after the grant date, or exceeding plan or authorized shares | Shares may be void or voidable; options may be invalid | Ratify under 8 Del. C. § 204 (board resolution, stockholder approval where required, certificate of validation if the defect relates to the charter or overissuance, and notice); or seek § 205 validation |
| Shares issued for promissory notes, future services or inadequate consideration | § 152 and § 153 consideration requirements | Board determination of adequacy; § 204 where needed |
| SAFEs or notes with inconsistent terms, missing signature pages, or unrecorded side letters (MFN, pro rata) | Conversion disputes; undisclosed investor rights | Collect all instruments and side letters; build the conversion schedule; obtain acknowledgments |
| Missing Form D or state notice filings for prior rounds; general solicitation in a 506(b) offering | Securities-law exposure; rescission rights | File late Form D (no SEC penalty for lateness, but state consequences vary); analyze integration and solicitation; disclose |
| Rule 701 issuances above $10 million in a 12-month period without the required disclosure | Loss of exemption for compensatory issuances | Count Rule 701 sales; provide required disclosure going forward; consult counsel on remediation |
| Contractors treated as employees (or vice versa); missing I-9s; unpaid overtime for misclassified staff | Wage-and-hour and tax liability | Reclassify; review state tests (California ABC test under Lab. Code § 2775 et seq.) |
| Non-competes with California or other restricted-state employees | Void and, in California, unlawful to enforce (§ 16600.5) | Remove; send required notices |
| Open-source components under copyleft licenses in distributed or network-served code | IP and disclosure obligations | Software composition analysis; remediation plan |
| Personal data practices inconsistent with published privacy policy; no incident response plan | Regulatory exposure (FTC Act § 5, state privacy laws) | Update policy; adopt a security program |
| Foreign ownership or foreign operations in sensitive sectors | CFIUS, outbound investment and DOJ data security program implications | Map ownership and operations; obtain counsel's analysis |
| Personal guarantees, related-party loans or undocumented founder arrangements | Conflicts; undisclosed liabilities | Document, approve under § 144 procedures, or unwind |

**Market practice on depth.** Seed and Series A diligence concentrates on corporate records, capitalization, IP chain of title and key contracts; Series B and later add quality-of-earnings work, deeper commercial, privacy and security review, and regulatory analysis. Investor counsel's fees are generally capped in the term sheet, which caps diligence scope too.

## Form

---

# {{company_name}} — {{series_name}} PREFERRED STOCK FINANCING

# DUE DILIGENCE REQUEST LIST

Requested by counsel to {{lead_investor_name}}. Unless a request says otherwise, it covers {{company_name}} and each of its subsidiaries (together, the "**Company**"), since inception or for the last {{lookback_years}} years, whichever is shorter. "Material" contracts are those involving payments to or by the Company of more than {{material_contract_threshold}} in any year, or that are otherwise significant to the business. Please upload executed copies to the corresponding data room folder, note "None" where nothing is responsive, and tell us if a request is not applicable. Uploads after {{data_room_freeze_date}} should be flagged by email.

## 1. Corporate Organization and Records

1.1 Certificate of incorporation and every amendment, restatement, certificate of designation, certificate of correction and certificate of validation, as filed with the Delaware Secretary of State; a current Delaware good standing certificate.

1.2 Bylaws as currently in effect, with all amendments and the dates and approvals of each.

1.3 Minutes of every board and committee meeting and every written consent of the board, committees, incorporator and stockholders; materials provided to the board for significant actions.

1.4 List of current directors and officers, committees and their members; any resignation letters.

1.5 Organizational documents and ownership of each subsidiary, joint venture, or entity in which the Company holds an interest; foreign subsidiaries' local registrations.

1.6 Foreign qualifications to do business, by state and country, with good standings.

1.7 Any predecessor entity (LLC, partnership, foreign corporation): conversion or merger documents and tax treatment.

1.8 Delaware franchise tax and annual report filings and payment history; registered agent.

1.9 Any action taken under 8 Del. C. § 204 or § 205, with the related certificates and notices.

## 2. Capitalization and Securities

2.1 Fully diluted capitalization table, by holder, class and series, showing shares, options, warrants, RSUs, SAFEs and convertible notes, with issue dates, prices and vesting.

2.2 Stock ledger (8 Del. C. § 224) and all stock certificates or uncertificated-share notices (§ 151(f)), including legends.

2.3 Every stock purchase, subscription and restricted stock agreement, including founder stock purchase agreements and technology assignments, with evidence of payment of the purchase price.

2.4 Section 83(b) elections for all restricted stock (including early-exercised options), with proof of timely filing.

2.5 Equity incentive plan(s), all amendments, board and stockholder approvals (including approval of any share increases for ISO purposes), forms of award agreements, and each award agreement; the option ledger.

2.6 All Section 409A valuation reports and the board resolutions setting the exercise price of each grant.

2.7 All SAFEs, convertible notes, warrants, side letters, pro rata letters, MFN letters, management rights letters and other rights to acquire equity, with any amendments and conversion calculations.

2.8 Prior financing documents: purchase agreements, investors' rights, voting, ROFR and co-sale agreements, and any waivers or amendments.

2.9 Securities filings for each prior issuance: Form D and amendments; state notice filings and fee receipts; Rule 701 calculations and disclosures; evidence of accredited status for each purchaser in Rule 506 offerings; any offering materials or pitch decks distributed publicly.

2.10 Repurchases, redemptions and cancellations of stock, and any outstanding repurchase or acceleration rights.

2.11 Any secondary sales of Company stock, tender offers, or liquidity programs, with the Company's ROFR waivers.

2.12 Any voting trusts, proxies or agreements among stockholders not otherwise listed.

2.13 Any agreement granting registration, preemptive, anti-dilution, information or board rights.

2.14 Analysis of qualified small business stock (I.R.C. § 1202) status: gross-asset history at each issuance, and any redemptions within the statutory windows.

## 3. Intellectual Property and Technology

3.1 Schedule of patents and patent applications (with status and jurisdictions), registered and material unregistered trademarks, copyright registrations and domain names; assignment records filed with the USPTO.

3.2 Confidential information and invention assignment agreements signed by every current and former founder, employee, consultant and contractor who contributed to Company technology; list of anyone who did not sign; state-law notices (e.g., Cal. Lab. Code § 2870).

3.3 Founder technology assignment agreements and confirmatory assignments for pre-incorporation work.

3.4 In-licenses and out-licenses of technology or IP (other than off-the-shelf software), including university, government and research agreements; any government funding (SBIR/STTR) with Bayh-Dole or data-rights implications.

3.5 Open-source software inventory and policy; any software composition analysis report; components under copyleft licenses.

3.6 Use of artificial intelligence: models developed or fine-tuned, training-data sources and licenses, third-party AI services used in the product, and any related customer commitments or policies.

3.7 IP disputes, infringement claims or demand letters, sent or received; freedom-to-operate opinions.

3.8 Source code escrow arrangements and any disclosure of source code to third parties.

3.9 Trade secret protection measures; any known misappropriation.

## 4. Employees, Contractors and Benefits

4.1 Organization chart; census of employees and contractors (title, location, hire date, full-time or part-time, exempt or non-exempt, compensation), with personal identifiers removed.

4.2 Offer letters and employment agreements for officers and key employees; severance, retention, change-in-control and bonus arrangements.

4.3 Consulting and independent contractor agreements; analysis of contractor classification by state.

4.4 Non-competition and non-solicitation agreements, by state of employee residence.

4.5 Employee handbook and key policies (anti-harassment, leave, remote work, expense).

4.6 Benefit plans: health and welfare plans, 401(k) plan documents and recent Form 5500s, PEO agreements.

4.7 Immigration: employees on visas, sponsorship obligations, and Form I-9 compliance.

4.8 Employment claims, charges, audits or investigations (EEOC, state agencies, wage-and-hour); any internal complaints of harassment or discrimination involving officers.

4.9 Layoffs or plant closings (WARN Act and state equivalents).

4.10 Background-check practices (FCRA and state law compliance).

## 5. Material Contracts

5.1 Customer agreements above the materiality threshold, top-customer list with revenue for each of the last two years, and standard terms of service or master agreement forms.

5.2 Supplier, manufacturing, reseller, distribution, marketing and partnership agreements above the threshold.

5.3 Agreements containing exclusivity, most-favored-customer, non-competition, non-solicitation, change-of-control, anti-assignment, rights of first refusal or offer, or minimum-purchase obligations.

5.4 Loan agreements, lines of credit, venture debt, guarantees, security agreements, and UCC search results; any lender consent rights over equity issuances.

5.5 Real property leases, subleases and coworking agreements.

5.6 Government contracts and subcontracts, including any facility clearance, export-controlled data access, or Federal Acquisition Regulation flow-downs.

5.7 Agreements with related parties (founders, directors, officers, investors and their affiliates), with evidence of approval under 8 Del. C. § 144 procedures.

5.8 Joint development, joint venture and strategic investment agreements.

5.9 Any contract under which a party has alleged breach, or that has been terminated or not renewed in the last year.

## 6. Finance and Accounting

6.1 Financial statements (audited if available, otherwise reviewed or management-prepared) for the last two fiscal years and the current year to date; monthly management reports.

6.2 Current operating budget, forecast and cash runway model; capitalization and use-of-proceeds plan.

6.3 Bank and investment accounts, signatories, and cash management or investment policy (including bank concentration).

6.4 Accounts receivable and payable aging; deferred revenue; revenue recognition policies (ASC 606).

6.5 Indebtedness of every kind, including credit cards, equipment financing and amounts owed to founders.

6.6 Off-balance-sheet arrangements, contingent liabilities and guarantees.

6.7 Accountant management letters and any material weaknesses identified.

6.8 Government grants, tax credits (e.g., research credits applied against payroll tax) and any repayment obligations.

## 7. Tax

7.1 Federal, state and local income tax returns (and any foreign returns) for the last {{lookback_years}} years; confirmation of C-corporation status since formation and any S-corporation or partnership history.

7.2 Sales and use tax, gross receipts tax and nexus analysis for each state where the Company has customers or employees.

7.3 Payroll tax filings and evidence of deposits; treatment of equity compensation (withholding on NSO exercises and RSU settlements).

7.4 Tax audits, notices, assessments or rulings.

7.5 Transfer pricing documentation for intercompany arrangements with foreign subsidiaries.

7.6 Treatment of research and experimental expenditures under I.R.C. § 174 and new § 174A (which, as added by Pub. L. 119-21, restores current deduction of domestic research expenditures for tax years beginning after 2024), including any election to apply the change to earlier years and any amended returns filed.

> **Verify:** Confirm the current IRS guidance on § 174A transition elections and small-business retroactive relief before relying on Request 7.6 in a tax review.

7.7 Any tax sharing, indemnity or allocation agreements.

## 8. Litigation and Disputes

8.1 Pending or threatened litigation, arbitration, mediation or government proceedings, with pleadings and counsel's status summaries (subject to a common-interest agreement for privileged material).

8.2 Settlement agreements, consent decrees, judgments and orders binding the Company.

8.3 Demand letters, cease-and-desist letters and claims received or sent.

8.4 Internal investigations and whistleblower complaints.

8.5 Audit inquiry letter responses from counsel for the most recent audit.

## 9. Regulatory, Privacy, Security and Compliance

9.1 Licenses, permits and registrations required to operate the business; correspondence with regulators.

9.2 Privacy policies (current and prior versions), data maps, records of processing, data processing agreements with vendors and customers, and cross-border transfer mechanisms.

9.3 Compliance analysis for applicable privacy laws (state comprehensive privacy laws, CCPA/CPRA, HIPAA, COPPA, GLBA, GDPR or UK GDPR where applicable).

9.4 Information security program, policies, recent penetration tests and audits (SOC 2 reports, ISO 27001 certificates), and remediation status.

9.5 Security incidents and data breaches, with notifications made.

9.6 Export control classification of products and technology (EAR/ITAR), any licenses, and any voluntary disclosures; sanctions screening procedures.

9.7 Anti-corruption policies and any interactions with foreign government officials or state-owned enterprises.

9.8 Foreign ownership, foreign investors and their rights; any analysis of CFIUS jurisdiction (TID U.S. business status: critical technologies, covered investment critical infrastructure, sensitive personal data) and any filings made.

9.9 Operations, subsidiaries, employees, revenue or expenses in China (including Hong Kong and Macau) or any other country of concern under 31 C.F.R. Part 850 and its successor regulations; analysis of "covered activity" status.

9.10 Bulk U.S. sensitive personal data or government-related data held by the Company and any access by persons associated with countries of concern (28 C.F.R. Part 202).

9.11 Industry-specific regulation (FDA, FCC, FAA, financial services licensing, healthcare, environmental), as applicable.

9.12 Accessibility (ADA) compliance for websites and applications.

## 10. Insurance

10.1 Insurance policies currently in force (D&O, general liability, property, cyber, errors and omissions, employment practices, product liability, workers' compensation, key person), with limits, retentions and carriers.

10.2 Claims history and any reservation-of-rights or denial letters.

10.3 Broker's summary of coverage and renewal dates.

## 11. Founders and Management

11.1 Résumés or biographies of founders and executive officers.

11.2 Founder vesting, acceleration and repurchase terms; any proposed re-vesting.

11.3 Founder and officer questionnaires covering Rule 506(d) bad-actor events, litigation, bankruptcy and conflicts of interest.

11.4 Outside activities, board seats and advisory roles of founders and officers; any other business they own.

11.5 Prior employer agreements (non-competition, non-solicitation, confidentiality, invention assignment) for each founder and key hire.

## 12. Miscellaneous

12.1 Pitch decks, offering memoranda and investor updates delivered in the last {{lookback_years}} years.

12.2 Press releases and public statements about financings, customers or products.

12.3 Any letters of intent, term sheets or discussions concerning acquisitions, mergers or strategic investments.

12.4 Any other document or information that a reasonable investor would consider material to an investment in the Company.

> **Drafting note:** Tailor the list to the company's stage and industry. Delete requests that cannot apply (for example, Category 9.6 for a consumer app with no controlled technology) so that "None" answers mean something. For life sciences add clinical, regulatory-submission, and product-liability requests; for hardware add manufacturing, supply-chain and product-safety requests; for fintech add licensing, BSA/AML and bank-partner agreements.
