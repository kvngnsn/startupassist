## About this template

**What it does.** A mutual NDA lets two businesses exchange non-public information for a defined Purpose while each keeps ownership of what it discloses. It does not obligate either party to do a deal, grant a license, or keep negotiating. It is the usual gate before technical diligence, pilots, integration work, channel partnerships, joint development scoping, and acquisition discussions. If the relationship proceeds, the definitive agreement (MSA, license, development agreement, merger agreement) should carry its own confidentiality article and expressly state whether it supersedes this NDA for information exchanged after its date.

**Where it sits.** Sign before any confidential disclosure. If information has already been exchanged informally, keep the "Disclosure Period" starting on the date the parties first began discussions (the form lets the drafter backdate the *coverage* without backdating the *signature*; never date the signature itself earlier than it was signed).

**Statutory overlay.**
- *Trade secrets.* The federal Defend Trade Secrets Act (18 U.S.C. § 1836) and state UTSA statutes (Delaware: 6 Del. C. ch. 20) protect trade secrets only if the owner takes "reasonable measures" to keep them secret (18 U.S.C. § 1839(3)(A)). A signed NDA with a use restriction and marking discipline is evidence of reasonable measures. Trade secrets lose protection on public disclosure, so the form keeps trade-secret confidentiality alive for as long as the information remains a trade secret, rather than letting it lapse with a fixed term.
- *DTSA immunity notice.* 18 U.S.C. § 1833(b)(3) requires an employer to give the whistleblower-immunity notice in any contract "with an employee that governs the use of a trade secret or other confidential information," and § 1833(b)(4) defines "employee" to include an individual performing work as a contractor or consultant. A business-to-business NDA does not trigger the requirement, but if either signatory is an individual who performs or will perform services for the other, include Section 5.2. Omitting a required notice forfeits exemplary damages and attorney fees against that individual (§ 1833(b)(3)(C)); it does not void the agreement.
- *Protected reporting.* SEC Rule 21F-17(a) prohibits "any person" from taking action to impede an individual from communicating with the SEC about a possible securities-law violation, "including enforcing, or threatening to enforce, a confidentiality agreement." The Speak Out Act (42 U.S.C. §§ 19401–19404) makes pre-dispute nondisclosure and nondisparagement clauses unenforceable with respect to later sexual assault or sexual harassment disputes. Neither is usually implicated by a commercial NDA, but Section 5.1 costs nothing and prevents a regulator from reading the NDA as a gag.
- *Export control.* Sharing controlled technical data with a foreign person in the United States is a "deemed export" (15 C.F.R. § 734.13(b)). Section 14 allocates compliance to the discloser, who knows the classification of its technology.

**Why most venture investors will not sign an NDA.** Institutional VCs see hundreds of companies a year, many in overlapping spaces, and often invest in competitors. An NDA would expose the fund to claims that it misused one company's information when it later backs another, and tracking NDA obligations across a deal flow of that size is impractical. Market practice is that investors decline NDAs for pitch materials and initial diligence. Founders should instead (a) share only what is needed to win the meeting (problem, traction, team, market, product at a level that does not reveal trade secrets); (b) hold back source code, unpublished patent applications, detailed technical architecture, and customer lists until term-sheet diligence; (c) at the diligence stage, rely on a data-room click-through or the confidentiality provision in the signed term sheet (many term sheets include a binding confidentiality clause); and (d) file a provisional patent application before discussing patentable inventions publicly, because investor meetings without an NDA can be a non-confidential disclosure for patent-bar purposes. Corporate venture arms and strategic investors are different: they often *will* sign an NDA, and the Company should ask for one, with an express clause that the strategic's business units do not receive the information (a "firewall" provision; add as a Section 3.3 sentence if needed).

**State-specific notes.**
- *California.* Business and Professions Code § 16600 voids contracts that restrain anyone from engaging in a lawful profession, trade, or business. Between businesses, California applies a rule of reason (Ixchel Pharma, LLC v. Biogen, Inc., 9 Cal. 5th 1130 (2020)), so the optional employee non-solicit in Section 12.4 is not automatically void in a commercial NDA, but keep it narrow (solicitation only, not hiring; no general advertisements; 12 months). Do not add customer non-solicits or non-competes to an NDA. California also does not enforce pre-dispute contractual jury waivers (Grafton Partners L.P. v. Superior Court, 36 Cal. 4th 944 (2005)); the jury-waiver clause is drafted to apply only where enforceable.
- *Delaware choice of law.* 6 Del. C. § 2708 makes a Delaware choice-of-law clause conclusively enforceable only for contracts "involving not less than $100,000." An NDA typically involves no stated dollar amount, so Delaware courts would apply ordinary Restatement (Second) Conflict of Laws § 187 analysis. Choosing Delaware law for a Delaware corporation is still defensible, but if both parties are in the same state, choosing that state's law is simpler.
- *Antitrust (competitors).* If the counterparty is a competitor, exchanges of competitively sensitive information (current or future prices, costs, wages, capacity, bids, customer-specific terms) can raise Sherman Act § 1 issues even under an NDA. Use a clean-team arrangement (Section 3.3 optional sentence) and involve antitrust counsel for M&A or joint-venture diligence between competitors.

**Common mistakes the form avoids.**
1. A definition of Confidential Information so broad that it includes information the Recipient already knows; the exclusions in Article 2 and the burden-of-proof sentence fix this.
2. A fixed term that lets trade secrets fall into the public domain; Section 10.2 keeps trade secrets protected while they qualify.
3. "Return or destroy everything" with no archival carve-out, which no company with automated backups can comply with; Section 9.2 permits backups and legal-hold copies subject to continuing confidentiality.
4. No compelled-disclosure mechanics; Article 4 requires notice and cooperation and limits disclosure to what counsel advises is legally required.
5. NDAs that silently grant a license or create a duty to negotiate; Articles 7 and 12 negate both.
6. Signing the counterparty's residuals clause without reading it. A residuals clause can effectively license any idea an engineer remembers. If the counterparty insists, use Alternative B, which excludes trade secrets, source code, and patent rights, and does not permit intentional memorization.

**Deadlines triggered by signing.** None by statute. Calendar the end of the Disclosure Period and the end of the confidentiality period in Section 10, and any return-or-destroy request.

# MUTUAL NONDISCLOSURE AGREEMENT

This Mutual Nondisclosure Agreement (this "**Agreement**") is entered into as of {{effective_date}} (the "**Effective Date**") by and between {{company_name}}, a Delaware corporation (the "**Company**"), and {{counterparty_name}}, {{counterparty_entity_description}} (the "**Counterparty**"). The Company and the Counterparty are each a "**Party**" and together the "**Parties**."

The Parties wish to exchange certain non-public information solely for the purpose of {{purpose_description}} (the "**Purpose**"), and each Party is willing to disclose such information only on the terms of this Agreement. In consideration of the mutual covenants in this Agreement and the disclosure of Confidential Information, the receipt and sufficiency of which are acknowledged, the Parties agree as follows:

## 1. Definitions

**1.1 "Affiliate"** means, with respect to a Party, any entity that directly or indirectly controls, is controlled by, or is under common control with that Party, where "control" means ownership of more than fifty percent (50%) of the voting securities or other voting interests of the entity, or the power to direct its management and policies by contract or otherwise, for only so long as that control exists.

**1.2 "Confidential Information"** means all non-public information, in any form or medium (whether oral, written, electronic, visual, or by demonstration or inspection of tangible items or premises), that is disclosed or made available by or on behalf of a Disclosing Party or its Affiliates to the Receiving Party or its Representatives during the Disclosure Period, whether before or after the Effective Date, and that (a) is marked or identified as "confidential," "proprietary," or with a similar legend at the time of disclosure, or (b) a reasonable person would understand to be confidential given the nature of the information and the circumstances of disclosure. Confidential Information includes, without limitation: technical information such as source code, object code, algorithms, models, model weights, training data, data sets, inventions, designs, schematics, specifications, know-how, processes, and research and development; business information such as product plans and roadmaps, pricing, costs, financial information and projections, customer, supplier, and partner identities and terms, personnel information, marketing plans, and business strategies; the existence and terms of any discussions between the Parties concerning the Purpose; and all notes, analyses, compilations, forecasts, studies, and other materials prepared by or for the Receiving Party to the extent they contain or reflect any of the foregoing ("**Derivatives**").

[[Optional: Notwithstanding clause (b) of this Section 1.2, information disclosed orally or visually is Confidential Information only if it is identified as confidential at the time of disclosure and summarized in a writing marked "confidential" and delivered to the Receiving Party within thirty (30) days after disclosure, except that information that a reasonable person would understand to be a trade secret is Confidential Information regardless of marking or written confirmation.]]

> **Drafting note:** Keep the bracketed oral-confirmation sentence out when the Company is mainly the discloser; it shifts administrative burden to the discloser and a missed summary can lose protection. Offer it as a concession when the counterparty is a large company that insists on marking.

**1.3 "Disclosing Party"** means the Party (or its Affiliate) disclosing or making available Confidential Information.

**1.4 "Disclosure Period"** means the period beginning on {{disclosure_start_date}} and ending on the earlier of (a) the date that is {{disclosure_period_months}} months after the Effective Date and (b) the date either Party terminates the Disclosure Period by written notice to the other Party under Section 10.1.

**1.5 "Law"** means any applicable statute, regulation, rule, ordinance, order, judgment, decree, subpoena, or rule of any securities exchange or self-regulatory organization.

**1.6 "Receiving Party"** means the Party receiving Confidential Information, directly or through its Representatives or Affiliates.

**1.7 "Representatives"** means, with respect to a Party, its and its Affiliates' directors, officers, managers, employees, and individual independent contractors, and its legal counsel, accountants, financial advisors, and, with the Disclosing Party's prior written consent (email sufficing), actual or prospective financing sources, in each case who have a need to know Confidential Information for the Purpose.

**1.8 "Trade Secret"** means Confidential Information that constitutes a trade secret under the Defend Trade Secrets Act of 2016, 18 U.S.C. § 1839(3), or the trade secret law of any applicable jurisdiction.

## 2. Exclusions

**2.1 Excluded Information.** Confidential Information does not include information that the Receiving Party can demonstrate by competent written evidence:

(a) is or becomes generally available to the public other than as a result of a disclosure by the Receiving Party or its Representatives in breach of this Agreement;

(b) was known to the Receiving Party or its Representatives, without an obligation of confidentiality, before its disclosure by or on behalf of the Disclosing Party;

(c) is or becomes available to the Receiving Party or its Representatives on a non-confidential basis from a third party that, to the Receiving Party's knowledge after reasonable inquiry, is not prohibited from disclosing it by a legal, contractual, or fiduciary obligation to the Disclosing Party; or

(d) is independently developed by or for the Receiving Party without use of or reference to the Disclosing Party's Confidential Information, by personnel who did not have access to it.

**2.2 Combinations.** Confidential Information is not within an exclusion in Section 2.1 merely because (a) individual features or components of it are within an exclusion, unless the combination itself, and its principle of operation, is within an exclusion, or (b) it is embraced by more general information that is within an exclusion.

**2.3 Burden.** The Receiving Party bears the burden of proving that an exclusion in Section 2.1 applies.

## 3. Obligations of the Receiving Party

**3.1 Use Restriction.** The Receiving Party shall use the Disclosing Party's Confidential Information solely for the Purpose and for no other purpose, including, without limitation, to compete with the Disclosing Party, to develop, train, or improve any product, service, or machine-learning model, to solicit the Disclosing Party's customers or employees, or to file or prosecute any patent application.

**3.2 Nondisclosure.** The Receiving Party shall not disclose the Disclosing Party's Confidential Information to any person other than its Representatives in accordance with Section 3.3, except as permitted by Article 4 or Article 5 or with the Disclosing Party's prior written consent.

**3.3 Representatives.** Before disclosing Confidential Information to a Representative, the Receiving Party shall inform the Representative of its confidential nature and ensure that the Representative is bound by written obligations of confidentiality and restricted use (or, for legal counsel and accountants, professional duties of confidentiality) at least as protective as those in this Agreement. The Receiving Party is responsible for any act or omission of its Representatives that would breach this Agreement if committed by the Receiving Party. [[Optional: The Receiving Party shall restrict access to the Disclosing Party's Confidential Information that is designated "Clean Team Only" to the individuals listed on a clean-team schedule agreed in writing by the Parties, none of whom may have direct responsibility for pricing, sales, or procurement decisions relating to products or services that compete with those of the Disclosing Party.]]

**3.4 Standard of Care.** The Receiving Party shall protect the Disclosing Party's Confidential Information using at least the same degree of care it uses to protect its own confidential information of similar importance, and in no event less than reasonable care, including reasonable administrative, technical, and physical safeguards against unauthorized access, use, or disclosure.

**3.5 No Reverse Engineering.** The Receiving Party shall not decompile, disassemble, reverse engineer, or analyze the composition or structure of any software, hardware, prototype, sample, or other tangible item provided by the Disclosing Party, or attempt to do so, except to the extent expressly authorized in writing by the Disclosing Party.

**3.6 Copies.** The Receiving Party shall not copy or reproduce Confidential Information except as reasonably necessary for the Purpose, and every copy shall retain all proprietary and confidentiality legends that appear on the original.

**3.7 Notice of Unauthorized Use or Disclosure.** The Receiving Party shall notify the Disclosing Party in writing promptly (and in any event within five (5) business days) after becoming aware of any actual or reasonably suspected unauthorized access to, or use or disclosure of, the Disclosing Party's Confidential Information, and shall reasonably cooperate with the Disclosing Party to regain possession of the Confidential Information and prevent further unauthorized access, use, or disclosure.

**3.8 Personal Information.** [[Optional: Neither Party shall disclose to the other any information that identifies, relates to, or could reasonably be linked with an identified or identifiable individual ("**Personal Information**") unless the disclosure is necessary for the Purpose and the Parties have first agreed in writing to any terms required by applicable privacy and data-protection Law. The Receiving Party shall process any Personal Information it receives only for the Purpose and in compliance with applicable Law.]]

> **Drafting note:** Include Section 3.8 whenever customer data, employee data, or health or financial data could be exchanged. If regulated data (HIPAA protected health information, consumer data subject to the CCPA, or EU/UK personal data) will be shared, a data processing agreement or business associate agreement is needed in addition to this NDA.

**3.9 Third-Party Tools.** The Receiving Party shall not, and shall cause its Representatives not to, upload, paste, or otherwise input any of the Disclosing Party's Confidential Information into any generative artificial-intelligence tool, translation service, or other third-party application or service, unless the tool is provided or administered by the Receiving Party under enterprise terms that prohibit the provider from retaining the input beyond the session (or beyond a retention period required for abuse monitoring), using it to train or improve any model, or disclosing it to any other person.

> **Drafting note:** Section 3.9 addresses a now-common leakage path. Large counterparties may ask to delete it; a reasonable compromise is to permit enterprise-licensed tools that meet the stated conditions, which the clause already does.

## 4. Compelled Disclosure

**4.1 Notice and Cooperation.** If the Receiving Party or any of its Representatives is required by Law to disclose any of the Disclosing Party's Confidential Information, the Receiving Party shall, to the extent legally permitted, (a) give the Disclosing Party prompt written notice of the requirement so that the Disclosing Party may seek, at its own expense, a protective order or other appropriate remedy, and (b) reasonably cooperate with the Disclosing Party's efforts to obtain that remedy.

**4.2 Limited Disclosure.** If no protective order or other remedy is obtained, or the Disclosing Party waives compliance with this Agreement, the Receiving Party (or its Representative) may disclose only that portion of the Confidential Information that its legal counsel advises is legally required to be disclosed, and shall use reasonable efforts to obtain assurance that confidential treatment will be accorded to the information disclosed. Any information so disclosed remains Confidential Information for all other purposes.

**4.3 Routine Examinations.** Notice under Section 4.1 is not required for a disclosure made to a banking, securities, or other regulatory authority in the course of a routine examination or inquiry that is not targeted at the Disclosing Party or the Purpose.

## 5. Protected Disclosures; Defend Trade Secrets Act Notice

**5.1 Protected Disclosures.** Nothing in this Agreement prohibits or restricts any Party or any individual from (a) reporting a possible violation of Law to, filing a charge or complaint with, communicating with, or participating in an investigation or proceeding conducted by any federal, state, or local governmental agency or commission, including the Securities and Exchange Commission, the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission, the National Labor Relations Board, the Occupational Safety and Health Administration, or the Department of Justice, in each case without notice to or consent from any other Party; (b) receiving an award for information provided to any governmental agency; (c) testifying truthfully in any legal proceeding; or (d) disclosing information concerning sexual assault, sexual harassment, or other conduct that the individual reasonably believes is unlawful, to the extent such a disclosure is protected by Law.

**5.2 Defend Trade Secrets Act Notice.** [[Optional: Each individual who is a Party or a Representative of a Party and who performs work for the other Party as an employee, contractor, or consultant is notified that, under 18 U.S.C. § 1833(b), an individual shall not be held criminally or civilly liable under any federal or state trade secret law for the disclosure of a trade secret that (a) is made (i) in confidence to a federal, state, or local government official, either directly or indirectly, or to an attorney, and (ii) solely for the purpose of reporting or investigating a suspected violation of law; or (b) is made in a complaint or other document filed in a lawsuit or other proceeding, if such filing is made under seal. An individual who files a lawsuit for retaliation by an employer for reporting a suspected violation of law may disclose the trade secret to the individual's attorney and use the trade secret information in the court proceeding, if the individual files any document containing the trade secret under seal and does not disclose the trade secret, except pursuant to court order.]]

> **Drafting note:** Section 5.2 tracks the statute's immunity language. It is legally required only where the agreement is between an employer and an individual "employee" (which includes a contractor or consultant, § 1833(b)(4)). Keep it when the Counterparty is an individual or a single-person consultancy that will perform services; delete it in a pure business-to-business NDA.

## 6. Residual Knowledge

**6.1 Residual Knowledge.** [[Alternative A: Nothing in this Agreement permits the Receiving Party or its Representatives to use or disclose any of the Disclosing Party's Confidential Information on the ground that it has been retained in the unaided memory of any individual. The Receiving Party's obligations under this Agreement apply to Confidential Information whether it is retained in tangible form or in memory.]]

[[Alternative B: The Receiving Party may use Residuals for any purpose, subject to the limitations in this Section 6.1. "**Residuals**" means general ideas, concepts, know-how, and techniques of a non-specific nature that are retained in the unaided memory of the Receiving Party's Representatives who had authorized access to the Disclosing Party's Confidential Information under this Agreement. An individual's memory is "unaided" only if the individual has not intentionally memorized the information for the purpose of retaining and later using or disclosing it and does not refer to any tangible or electronic record of it. Residuals do not include, and this Section 6.1 grants no right to use or disclose, (a) any Trade Secret, source code, object code, model weights, or data set; (b) the specific identity of any customer, supplier, or employee, or any pricing, financial, or personal information; or (c) any Confidential Information to the extent its use would infringe or misappropriate any patent, copyright, mask work, or trade secret of the Disclosing Party. This Section 6.1 does not grant any license under any patent, copyright, or other intellectual property right of the Disclosing Party, does not permit the Receiving Party to disclose the source of any Residuals or the fact that they were received from the Disclosing Party, and does not relieve the Receiving Party of its obligations with respect to Confidential Information that is not Residuals.]]

> **Drafting note:** Default to Alternative A. A residuals clause is a recipient-favorable term typically demanded by large technology companies and some vendors; it can operate as a de facto license to anything an engineer remembers. If the counterparty will not sign without one, Alternative B is the narrowest version commonly accepted: it excludes trade secrets, code, weights, data, and customer and pricing information; it disqualifies intentional memorization; and it expressly grants no patent or copyright license. Startups should also limit what they disclose to a counterparty with residuals rights.

## 7. Ownership; No License; Feedback

**7.1 Ownership.** As between the Parties, all Confidential Information remains the property of the Disclosing Party (or its licensors).

**7.2 No License.** Nothing in this Agreement grants the Receiving Party any license, ownership interest, or other right in or to any Confidential Information, or under any patent, patent application, copyright, trademark, trade secret, or other intellectual property right of the Disclosing Party, whether by implication, estoppel, or otherwise, except the limited right to use Confidential Information for the Purpose in accordance with this Agreement.

**7.3 Feedback.** [[Optional: If either Party voluntarily provides the other with suggestions, comments, or ideas concerning the other Party's products or services ("**Feedback**"), the recipient of the Feedback may use it without obligation or restriction, provided that Feedback does not include, and this Section 7.3 grants no right to use or disclose, the Confidential Information of the Party providing it, and the recipient shall not identify the Party providing the Feedback as its source without consent.]]

## 8. No Warranty

**8.1 As Is.** All Confidential Information is provided "AS IS." Neither Party makes any representation or warranty, express or implied, as to the accuracy, completeness, performance, non-infringement, merchantability, or fitness for a particular purpose of any Confidential Information. Neither Party has any liability to the other resulting from the use of Confidential Information, except as set forth in a definitive written agreement between the Parties or for breach of this Agreement.

**8.2 Authority.** Each Party represents that it has the authority to enter into this Agreement and to disclose the Confidential Information it discloses under this Agreement.

## 9. Return and Destruction

**9.1 Return or Destruction.** Within fifteen (15) days after the Disclosing Party's written request at any time, and in any event within thirty (30) days after the end of the Disclosure Period, the Receiving Party shall, at its election, return to the Disclosing Party or destroy (and permanently erase from all systems and devices in its or its Representatives' possession or control) all of the Disclosing Party's Confidential Information, including all copies and Derivatives, and, on request, shall certify in writing (email sufficing) by an authorized officer that it has done so.

**9.2 Permitted Retention.** Notwithstanding Section 9.1, the Receiving Party and its Representatives (a) may retain copies of Confidential Information in automatic electronic archival or backup systems in the ordinary course, so long as no attempt is made to access them other than for backup restoration, legal, or compliance purposes; (b) may retain one copy in the files of its legal department or outside counsel solely to demonstrate compliance with this Agreement or as required by Law, professional standards, or a bona fide internal document-retention policy; and (c) may retain Derivatives prepared by its board of directors or a committee of the board. All retained Confidential Information remains subject to this Agreement, notwithstanding the expiration of the period in Section 10.2, for as long as it is retained.

## 10. Term; Duration of Obligations

**10.1 Term of Agreement.** This Agreement takes effect on the Effective Date and continues until the end of the period described in Section 10.2. Either Party may end the Disclosure Period at any time, with or without cause, by written notice to the other Party. Ending the Disclosure Period does not affect any obligation with respect to Confidential Information disclosed before it ends.

**10.2 Duration of Confidentiality Obligations.** The Receiving Party's obligations under Articles 3, 4, 6, 7, and 9 with respect to Confidential Information continue until the date that is {{confidentiality_years}} years after the end of the Disclosure Period, except that those obligations continue, with respect to any Trade Secret, for as long as it remains a trade secret under applicable Law.
