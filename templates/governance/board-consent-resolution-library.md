## About this template

**What it does.** This file is two things: (1) a unanimous written consent shell that satisfies 8 Del. C. § 141(f), and (2) a library of resolution modules (A through R) that drop into that shell or into meeting minutes (see board-meeting-materials). Pick the modules the matter needs, delete the rest, and renumber the exhibits. The dedicated consents written elsewhere in this skill handle the four heaviest board actions; do not rebuild them from this library:

| Matter | Use |
|---|---|
| Adopting the equity incentive plan, reserving shares, approving forms of award | templates/equity-compensation/board-consent-adopt-plan.md |
| Option, RSU, and restricted stock grants; 409A determination | templates/equity-compensation/board-consent-equity-grants.md |
| SAFEs, convertible notes, side letters | templates/convertible-financing/board-consent-convertible-financing.md |
| Series Seed / Series A: restated charter, purchase agreement, investor agreements | templates/priced-financing/board-consent-priced-financing.md |
| Formation actions (officers, founder stock, bylaws ratification) | templates/formation/initial-board-consent.md |

**The § 141(f) rules that the shell enforces.**

1. **Unanimity of the directors in office.** Action without a meeting requires that "all members of the board" consent in writing or by electronic transmission (§ 141(f)). That means every director in office when the consent becomes effective, including interested directors, investor designees, and directors who are traveling. A vacancy does not need to be filled first, and a vacant seat does not sign. If even one director declines or cannot be reached, the consent fails; call a meeting (quorum is a majority of the total number of directors unless the charter or bylaws say otherwise, and a majority of directors present at a quorate meeting acts, § 141(b)).
2. **The charter or bylaws can restrict consents.** § 141(f) applies "unless otherwise restricted by the certificate of incorporation or bylaws." Check both before using this shell. The companion bylaws in templates/formation/bylaws.md permit consents.
3. **Definite effective time; no backdating.** The board acts when the last required consent becomes effective. A director may sign in advance and direct that the consent take effect at a future time "no later than 60 days" after signing, and the consent counts only if the person is still a director at that time and has not revoked it (§ 141(f)). The shell uses that mechanic: it names a definite Effective Time, every director's signature is a direction that the consent take effect then, and if a signature is missing at the Effective Time the consent does not take effect and must be recirculated with a new date. Do not write "effective as of" an earlier date than the last signature. The action was not taken until then, and for option grants the grant date for ISO and 409A purposes is the date the corporate action is completed (Treas. Reg. § 1.421-1(c); § 1.409A-1(b)(5)(vi)(B)). A backdated grant is a mispriced grant.
4. **Electronic signature and delivery.** Under § 116(a), the consent may be documented, signed, and delivered electronically (DocuSign, a typed name with intent to sign, a PDF signature page). § 116(b) lists documents to which § 116(a) does not apply, notably documents filed with the Secretary of State, the stock ledger, stock certificates, and documents "expressly referenced as a notice (or waiver of notice)." A board consent is not on that list.
5. **Counterparts and filing.** Directors may sign separate counterparts. After the action is taken, the Secretary files the consent "with the minutes of the proceedings of the board" in the same paper or electronic form as the minutes are kept (§ 141(f)). Those minutes and consents are "books and records" a stockholder may demand under § 220(a)(1)e. (as amended in 2025); write them knowing a plaintiff may read them.
6. **What a consent cannot do.** It cannot cure a void act (use § 204), cannot replace a required stockholder vote (§§ 242(b), 251, 271, 275), and cannot give the board power the charter reserves to investors (protective provisions in the charter, and covenants in the investors' rights agreement, often require Preferred Stock or Requisite Investor consent to debt, new equity, charter changes, or related-party deals; check them for every module).

**Market practice on consents versus meetings.** Seed-stage boards act mostly by written consent. From the first priced round, routine items still go by consent, but anything contested, conflicted, or material (a down round, an insider bridge, a sale, executive pay for a director, a material litigation decision) should be taken at a meeting with materials circulated in advance and minutes showing deliberation. A written consent records the result, not the care. Delaware courts reviewing a conflicted decision look for the process (In re Trados Inc. S'holder Litig., 73 A.3d 17 (Del. Ch. 2013)), and the § 144 safe harbors as amended in 2025 turn on disclosure of material facts to, and approval in good faith and without gross negligence by, disinterested directors. Module M documents that process in a consent when a meeting is not practical.

**How investors' agreements change these modules.** After a priced round, check before using any module: (1) the charter's protective provisions (Preferred Stock vote for new senior or pari passu equity, debt over a threshold, changes in board size, redemptions, dividends, liquidation); (2) the investors' rights agreement's matters requiring the approval of the Preferred Director(s) or Requisite Investors (budget, related-party transactions, executive comp, new subsidiaries, debt); (3) preemptive rights, which apply to stock and warrant issuances in Modules E and G unless the security is an "Exempted Security"; and (4) the voting agreement's board-composition provisions, which control Module Q.

**State-specific points.** California-based companies issuing stock outside a plan rely on Cal. Corp. Code § 25102(f) for most private placements (notice within 15 calendar days after the first sale in California) and on § 25102(o) for compensatory plans; Module G flags this. New York and other states may require notice filings for Rule 506 offerings. See references/securities-law.md for the blue-sky table.

**Common mistakes this form prevents.**
- A consent missing one director's signature, or signed by a director whose term had ended. The shell lists every director by name and the Secretary confirms the roster against the stock ledger, the last stockholder election, and the voting agreement.
- "Effective as of" backdating. The shell uses a definite future Effective Time with § 141(f) future-effective mechanics.
- Issuing stock or warrants without fixing the consideration, or without checking authorized and reserved shares. Module G and Module E require both.
- A general "ratify everything" resolution used to paper over defective stock. Module O explains why that fails and routes to § 204.
- Director-officer compensation approved by the interested director's own vote. Module A routes it through Module M.
- Delegating option grants without the three statutory limits, or allowing the CEO to grant to themself. Module P tracks § 157(c) exactly.

**Deadlines and follow-up that the modules trigger.**
- Charter amendment (Module H): file the certificate of amendment; amendment is effective on filing or at a stated future time not later than the ninetieth day after filing (§ 103(d)); the authorized-shares franchise tax is prorated for each period an authorized share count was in effect (§ 503(e)).
- Stock issuance (Module G): § 151(f) notice to holders of uncertificated shares within a reasonable time; Form D within 15 calendar days after the first sale if relying on Regulation D (17 C.F.R. § 230.503); state notices; 83(b) election within 30 days if the shares vest.
- Registered agent change (Module L): file the certificate of change; Division fee $50 (fee schedule revised August 1, 2026).
- Annual compliance (Module K): Delaware annual report and franchise tax due March 1; $200 penalty plus 1.5% monthly interest if late (§§ 502(c), 504(c)).

{{selected_resolution_modules}}

## MODULE A. OFFICERS: APPOINTMENT, REMOVAL, AND COMPENSATION

**A-1. Appointment of Officers.**

WHEREAS, Section 142 of the DGCL and the Bylaws provide that the Company shall have such officers, with such titles and duties, as are stated in the Bylaws or determined by resolution of the Board, and that any number of offices may be held by the same person unless the Certificate of Incorporation or the Bylaws provide otherwise; and

WHEREAS, the Board deems it advisable and in the best interests of the Company to appoint the officers set forth below.

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED, that each of the following persons is hereby appointed to the office or offices set forth opposite such person's name, to serve at the pleasure of the Board until such person's successor is duly appointed and qualified or until such person's earlier death, resignation, or removal:

| Name | Office |
|---|---|
| {{ceo_name}} | Chief Executive Officer |
| {{president_name}} | President |
| {{secretary_name}} | Secretary |
| {{cfo_name}} | Chief Financial Officer |
| {{treasurer_name}} | Treasurer |
| {{officer_name}} | {{officer_title}} |

RESOLVED FURTHER, that each officer so appointed shall have the powers and duties set forth in the Bylaws for such office and such additional powers and duties as the Board or the Chief Executive Officer may from time to time prescribe, and that the Secretary shall have the duty to record the proceedings of the meetings and actions of the stockholders and the Board in books kept for that purpose.

RESOLVED FURTHER, that all prior appointments of officers of the Company that are inconsistent with the foregoing resolutions are hereby superseded as of the Effective Time.

> **Drafting note:** § 142(a) requires the officers needed to sign instruments and stock certificates under §§ 103(a)(2) and 158, and requires that one officer record the proceedings of stockholders and directors. A single founder may be CEO, President, Secretary, and Treasurer. "Officer" status matters beyond titles: § 102(b)(7) exculpation (if adopted in the charter) and 10 Del. C. § 3114(b) service-of-process consent reach officers who are the president, CEO, COO, CFO, chief legal officer, controller, treasurer, or chief accounting officer, persons identified in public filings as among the most highly compensated executive officers, and persons who have consented to be identified as officers. Do not appoint "Vice President" titles freely to employees unless intended; the title can bring indemnification and advancement rights under the bylaws.

**A-2. Removal of Officer.**

RESOLVED, that, effective at the Effective Time, {{removed_officer_name}} is hereby removed from the office of {{removed_officer_title}} of the Company, and that such removal is without prejudice to any contract rights of {{removed_officer_name}}, which shall be determined in accordance with the terms of any applicable written agreement with the Company.

RESOLVED FURTHER, that the authority of {{removed_officer_name}} to act for or bind the Company in any capacity as an officer, including as an authorized signatory on any bank, brokerage, payroll, or vendor account, is hereby revoked as of the Effective Time, and the remaining officers are directed to notify each financial institution and counterparty with which {{removed_officer_name}} was an authorized signatory.

> **Drafting note:** Bylaws ordinarily permit the board to remove officers with or without cause. Removal from office is not termination of employment; coordinate with the employment agreement (good-reason triggers often include a change in title or reporting), the separation agreement, and any acceleration terms. If the removed officer is also a director, removal as a director requires a stockholder vote under § 141(k) (or the voting agreement's removal mechanics); the board cannot remove a director.

**A-3. Officer Compensation.**

WHEREAS, the Board has reviewed the proposed compensation of {{officer_name}} as {{officer_title}}, including market compensation data for comparable positions at companies of similar stage, size, and location [[Optional: and the recommendation of the Compensation Committee]]; and

WHEREAS, [[Alternative A: {{officer_name}} is not a director of the Company]] [[Alternative B: {{officer_name}} is a director of the Company and has an interest in this resolution; the material facts of that interest were disclosed to all members of the Board; {{officer_name}} did not vote on this resolution except as required to make this Consent unanimous; and this resolution is approved by the affirmative votes of a majority of the disinterested directors as contemplated by Section 144(a)(1) of the DGCL]].

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED, that, effective as of {{effective_date}}, the annual base salary of {{officer_name}} is set at {{officer_base_salary}}, payable in accordance with the Company's standard payroll practices, and {{officer_name}} shall be eligible for an annual target bonus of {{officer_bonus_target}}, based on achievement of performance objectives established by the Board [[Optional: or the Compensation Committee]], subject to the terms of any written agreement with the Company.

> **Drafting note:** A director's own pay is a self-interested transaction. Under § 144(a)(1) (2025), approval by "a majority of the disinterested directors then serving," even if fewer than a quorum, after disclosure of the material facts, gives safe-harbor protection. If a majority of the board is not disinterested, which is common at seed (two founder-directors setting each other's pay are not disinterested as to a reciprocal arrangement), § 144(a)(1) requires approval by a committee of two or more directors each of whom the board has determined to be disinterested. With no such committee available, use § 144(a)(2) (informed, uncoerced approval by a majority of the votes cast by disinterested stockholders) or be prepared to show fairness under § 144(a)(3) with market data. Because a written consent must be unanimous, the interested director signs it; the recital records that the director's signature is given to permit action by consent, not as a vote on the merits. For contested pay decisions, use a meeting and recusal instead (board-meeting-materials). Equity awards to officers go through templates/equity-compensation/board-consent-equity-grants.md.

> **Drafting note:** How § 144 works after SB 21 (85 Del. Laws c. 6, effective March 25, 2025; upheld in Rutledge v. Clearway Energy Group LLC (Del. Feb. 27, 2026)). (1) Director or officer conflicts other than controlling-stockholder transactions (§ 144(a)): no equitable relief or damages against a director or officer "because of" the conflict if (i) after disclosure of the material facts, a majority of the disinterested directors then serving approve in good faith and without gross negligence, even if fewer than a quorum; and if a majority of the board is not disinterested, approval must come from a committee of two or more directors the board has determined to be disinterested; or (ii) a majority of the votes cast by disinterested stockholders approve or ratify, informed and uncoerced; or (iii) the transaction is fair. (2) Controlling-stockholder transactions other than going-private transactions (§ 144(b)): committee of two or more disinterested directors to which the board has "expressly delegated the authority to negotiate (or oversee the negotiation of) and to reject" the transaction, approving after disclosure; or a disinterested-stockholder vote on which the transaction was conditioned when submitted; or fairness. (3) Going-private controlling-stockholder transactions (§ 144(c)) need both the committee and the stockholder condition, or fairness. (4) A "controlling stockholder" (§ 144(e)(2)) holds a majority of the voting power, has the contractual right to elect a board majority, or holds at least one-third of the voting power plus managerial authority. At a venture-backed company, a lead fund with board-majority rights under a voting agreement can be a controller, and investors who act together under a voting agreement can be a "control group" (§ 144(e)(1)). (5) Disinterestedness: a director is not disinterested merely because an interested party designated or nominated the director (§ 144(d)(3)), but a fund partner on the board is interested in a financing that fund leads. Startup transactions that commonly need this module: insider-led bridge financings, founder or investor loans, investor-affiliated vendor deals, secondary purchases by insiders, and director or officer compensation. For insider-led down rounds, market practice adds a rights offering to all qualifying holders as additional protection. § 144(d)(6) preserves claims that a transaction was not authorized in compliance with the DGCL, the charter, or the bylaws, so the safe harbor does not cure a procedural defect.

RESOLVED FURTHER, that the Board shall not approve or recommend the Strategic Matter without a favorable recommendation of the Special Committee, and the Special Committee shall keep minutes of its deliberations, including its consideration of the effect of the Strategic Matter on the holders of Common Stock.

> **Drafting note:** In re Trados Inc. S'holder Litig., 73 A.3d 17 (Del. Ch. 2013), held that, where preferred and common interests diverge, the board must generally prefer the common's interests as residual claimants; the Trados process was unfair (VC-designated directors favored a sale that triggered the preferred's preference and a management carve-out), but the court found the merger entirely fair, and imposed no liability, because the common had no realistic value as a going concern. Frederick Hsu Living Trust v. ODN Holding Corp., 2017 WL 1437308 (Del. Ch. Apr. 14, 2017), applied the same principle to redemption rights. VC-designated directors face dual fiduciary pressure (to the fund's limited partners and to the company). A special committee of disinterested directors with a real mandate, independent advisors, and minutes showing attention to the common is the process remedy; for a transaction with a controlling stockholder or control group, the same committee structure is what § 144(b) requires. For a sale, also consider a fairness opinion and how the carve-out plan is allocated.

## MODULE O. RATIFICATION OF PRIOR ACTS (COMMON LAW)

WHEREAS, the officers of the Company have taken the actions described in Exhibit C (the "**Prior Acts**"), each of which was within the power of the Company and was taken in good faith on behalf of the Company, but which were not specifically authorized by the Board in advance; and

WHEREAS, none of the Prior Acts is an issuance of capital stock, rights, or options, an election of directors, an amendment of the Certificate of Incorporation, or any other act that would be void or voidable under the DGCL, the Certificate of Incorporation, or the Bylaws by reason of a failure of authorization, each of which, if any, the Board will address separately under Section 204 of the DGCL.

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED, that each of the Prior Acts is hereby ratified, confirmed, adopted, and approved in all respects as the act and deed of the Company, effective as of the date on which it was taken.

> **Drafting note:** Common-law ratification lets a principal adopt an agent's unauthorized but voidable act. It cannot validate a void act; the Delaware Supreme Court held that stock issued in violation of the charter or the DGCL is void and cannot be cured by later board ratification (STAAR Surgical Co. v. Waggoner, 588 A.2d 1130 (Del. 1991)). § 204 (2014) and § 205 were enacted to fix that: they validate "defective corporate acts" and "putative stock" if the board adopts resolutions stating each act, its date, the shares and dates for any putative stock, the nature of the failure of authorization, and approval (§ 204(b)(1)), with stockholder approval, a certificate of validation, and notice when required. A generic "ratify all prior acts" clause in an initial board consent does none of that. If any prior act involves stock, options, warrants, SAFEs converting into stock, director elections, charter amendments, or overissued shares, use templates/governance/section-204-ratification.md. Common-law ratification remains appropriate for ordinary contracts signed by an officer without express authority.

## MODULE P. DELEGATION OF AUTHORITY TO GRANT OPTIONS AND OTHER AWARDS (SECTIONS 152(b) AND 157(c))

WHEREAS, the {{plan_name}} (the "**Plan**") permits the Board to delegate to one or more officers the authority to grant awards under the Plan to the extent permitted by applicable law, and Sections 152(b) and 157(c) of the DGCL permit the Board, by resolution, to delegate to a person or body the authority to enter into transactions to issue stock, rights, or options, provided that the resolution fixes (i) the maximum number of shares (or shares issuable upon exercise) that may be issued pursuant to the resolution, (ii) the time period during which such stock, rights, or options, and the shares issuable upon exercise, may be issued, and (iii) the minimum consideration therefor, and does not permit the delegate to issue stock, rights, or options to the delegate; and

WHEREAS, the Board has determined that the fair market value of a share of Common Stock is {{fmv_409a_value}} as of {{valuation_date}}, based on the independent valuation prepared by {{valuation_provider}}.

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED, that {{delegate_name}}, {{delegate_title}} (the "**Delegate**"), is hereby authorized, during the period beginning at the Effective Time and ending on {{delegation_end_date}} (the "**Delegation Period**"), to grant options to purchase shares of Common Stock and restricted stock unit awards under the Plan to employees and consultants of the Company who are not officers or directors of the Company, subject to the following limits:

(a) the maximum aggregate number of shares of Common Stock issuable upon exercise or settlement of all awards granted under this resolution during the Delegation Period is {{delegated_share_cap}} shares, and no single recipient may receive awards covering more than {{per_grant_cap}} shares;

(b) the shares issuable upon exercise or settlement of such awards may be issued at any time during the term of the applicable award, which shall not extend beyond {{exercise_window_end_date}};

(c) no consideration shall be required for the grant of an award, the minimum consideration for each share issuable upon exercise of an option shall be an exercise price per share not less than the greater of (A) the fair market value of a share of Common Stock on the date of grant, as most recently determined by the Board, and (B) the par value of a share of Common Stock, and the minimum consideration for each share issued in settlement of a restricted stock unit shall be services rendered to the Company having a value not less than the par value of such share;

(d) the Delegate may not grant any award to the Delegate or to any person who is an officer or director of the Company;

(e) each award shall be granted on the Company's standard form of award agreement approved by the Board under the Plan, with vesting consistent with the guidelines attached hereto as Exhibit D; and

(f) the Delegate shall not grant any award at any time when the Delegate has knowledge of a material event that may affect the fair market value of the Common Stock since the most recent valuation determined by the Board, or after the date that is twelve (12) months after {{valuation_date}}.

RESOLVED FURTHER, that the date of grant of each award shall be the date on which the Delegate approves it in a signed writing or electronic record delivered to the Secretary specifying the recipient, the number of shares, the type of award, the exercise price (if any), and the vesting schedule, and the Delegate shall report all awards granted under this resolution to the Board at its next regular meeting.

> **Drafting note:** § 157(c) and § 152(b) (as amended in 2022) require all three fixed terms (maximum shares, time period, minimum consideration) and bar self-grants; § 157(d) and § 152(c) let the resolution depend on facts ascertainable outside it (for example, a 409A valuation), but the three required terms may not depend on a determination by the delegate. Practical limits: keep grants to officers and directors with the board (Exchange Act § 16 is irrelevant for private companies, but director-officer grants are interested transactions); keep the per-grant cap within new-hire guidelines; stop the delegation when the 409A valuation goes stale (12 months, or earlier on a material event, Treas. Reg. § 1.409A-1(b)(5)(iv)(B)(2)(i)). ISO grants must be made under a plan approved by stockholders within 12 months of adoption (I.R.C. § 422(b)(1)), and the grant date is when the corporate action is complete (the delegate's signed approval). Check that the Plan's text permits delegation to an officer and that the Rule 701 aggregate limits and any California § 25102(o) conditions are respected.

## MODULE Q. BOARD SIZE AND VACANCIES

WHEREAS, the Bylaws provide that the number of directors shall be fixed from time to time by resolution of the Board, the number of directors is currently {{board_size}}, and [[Optional: the Voting Agreement among the Company and certain of its stockholders provides for the designation of directors]].

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED, that, effective at the Effective Time, the number of directors constituting the whole Board is fixed at {{new_board_size}}.

RESOLVED FURTHER, that, pursuant to Section 223 of the DGCL and the Bylaws, {{new_director_name}} is hereby elected to fill the vacancy created by the foregoing increase, to serve until the next annual meeting of stockholders (or action by written consent in lieu thereof) and until such director's successor is duly elected and qualified, or until such director's earlier death, resignation, or removal.

> **Drafting note:** § 141(b) lets the bylaws fix the number or the manner of fixing it, but if the charter fixes the number, only a charter amendment changes it. Where the charter gives a class or series the right to elect a seat, § 223(a)(2) by default lets a vacancy in that seat be filled only by a majority of the remaining directors elected by that class (or a sole remaining such director), and NVCA-style charters usually go further and reserve the vacancy to the holders of that class; the full board cannot fill it. Voting agreements require the parties to vote for designees; if a designee seat is vacant, the designating holders normally fill it by stockholder consent, not the board. A director newly elected by the board should sign the director agreement (templates/governance/director-agreement.md), an indemnification agreement, and a D&O questionnaire.
