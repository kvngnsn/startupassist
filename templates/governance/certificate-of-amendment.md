## About this template

**What it does.** This file contains (1) a certificate of amendment form with three alternative operative articles (forward stock split with proportionate increase in authorized shares; increase in authorized shares; name change), (2) the board resolution adopting the amendment and declaring it advisable, (3) the stockholder consent approving it (when required), and (4) a filing and post-filing checklist.

**The § 242 sequence.**
1. **Board.** The board adopts a resolution "setting forth the amendment proposed, declaring its advisability," and either calling a stockholder meeting or (in practice) submitting it for written consent (§ 242(b)(1); § 228). The resolution may reserve the board's power to abandon the amendment before it becomes effective, even after stockholder approval (§ 242(c)).
2. **Stockholders.** Unless an exception applies, "a majority of the outstanding stock entitled to vote thereon, and a majority of the outstanding stock of each class entitled to vote thereon as a class" must approve (§ 242(b)(1)). A class votes separately, even if otherwise non-voting, on an amendment that would increase or decrease the class's authorized shares, increase or decrease its par value, or adversely alter its powers, preferences, or special rights; a series votes separately if it alone is adversely affected (§ 242(b)(2)). The class vote on authorized-share changes can be eliminated by a charter provision adopted as § 242(b)(2) permits (venture charters routinely include it for the Common Stock). Check the charter's Preferred protective provisions, which usually require a Preferred vote for any charter amendment.
3. **No stockholder vote (§ 242(d)(1)).** Unless the charter "otherwise expressly require[s]," no stockholder meeting or vote is needed for an amendment that only (A) changes the corporate name (§ 242(a)(1)) or deletes historical provisions naming the incorporator, initial directors, and original subscribers, or provisions that effected a completed reclassification (§ 242(a)(7)); or (B) "reclassifies by subdividing the issued shares of a class of stock into a greater number of issued shares of the same class" (a forward split), with a proportionate increase in that class's authorized shares, "provided the corporation has only 1 class of stock outstanding and such class is not divided into series." Authorized but unissued Preferred Stock does not defeat clause (B); outstanding Preferred does.
4. **2026 clarification.** HB 353 (signed June 10, 2026; effective August 1, 2026) added a closing paragraph to § 242(d): a charter provision letting authorized shares be increased or decreased by a majority of the stock "irrespective of" § 242(b)(2) (the standard opt-out) "shall not constitute an express requirement" for a greater vote than § 242(d) provides, unless it expressly states that the corporation is not governed by § 242(d)(1) or (2), or requires a vote greater than or additional to the vote the last sentence of § 242(b)(2) contemplates. The amendment responded to uncertainty over whether standard opt-out language displaced § 242(d) (commentators cite Salama v. Simon on the point). Result for startups: the standard Common Stock class-vote opt-out does not prevent a board-only forward split under § 242(d)(1)(B).
5. **Listed companies only (§ 242(d)(2)).** The reduced "votes cast" standard for increasing or decreasing authorized shares and for reverse splits applies only to classes listed on a national securities exchange that meet listing holder requirements. A private company's reverse split or stand-alone authorized-share increase always needs the § 242(b) vote.
6. **Execution and filing (§ 103).** An authorized officer signs (§ 103(a)(2)); the signature is the acknowledgment, under penalties of perjury, that the instrument is the act and deed of the corporation and the facts stated are true (§ 103(b)(2)). The certificate must certify that the amendment "has been duly adopted in accordance with" § 242 (§ 242(b)(1)), and, if stockholders acted by consent, state that consent was given under § 228 in place of any statement about a meeting vote (§ 228(e)). It becomes effective on filing or at a stated later time no later than the ninetieth day after filing (§ 103(d)).

**Fees (verified against the Division's schedule revised August 1, 2026).**

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Certificate of amendment, one page, not increasing stock | $214 |
| Each additional page | $9 |
| Amendment increasing authorized stock | $214 base, adjusted under § 391(a)(2): the difference between the stock fee on the new total and the old total, minimum $30 |
| Certified copy | $50 (plus $2 per page for copies) |
| Expedited: 24-hour / same day / 2-hour / 1-hour | $100 / $200 / $500 / $1,000 |

For par-value stock, the stock-based fee counts "each $100 unit of the authorized capital stock" as one assessable share (§ 391(a)(1)). At the par values startups use ($0.00001 or $0.0001), even large increases produce a stock-based fee below the $30 minimum, so the filing typically costs the scheduled amount plus page and expedite fees. No-par stock is costly: the fee is computed per share. Run the Division's filing-fee calculator for unusual capital structures.

> **Verify:** The $214 figure is the schedule's amount for a one-page filing "however, if stock is changing this fee may vary." Confirm the exact amount with the registered agent or the Division's calculator before filing an amendment that increases authorized shares.

**Franchise tax consequences.** Increasing authorized shares raises the default authorized-shares-method tax (10,000,000 shares: $85,165; 20,000,000 shares: $170,165; the method caps at $200,000). Under the assumed-par-value-capital method, which a startup should always elect by reporting issued shares and gross assets, the tax depends on assumed par (gross assets divided by issued shares) times authorized shares; a forward split increases issued and authorized shares in the same ratio, so that tax is largely unchanged. Delaware prorates for each period of the year in which a distinct authorized share count was in effect (§ 503(e)). See delaware-franchise-tax-and-annual-report.

**Market practice.**
- Forward splits are common before a priced round to bring per-share prices into a conventional range or to enlarge the option pool's share count; they are often folded into the restated charter for the round instead of a separate amendment.
- Name changes are board-only under § 242(d)(1)(A); still update every foreign qualification, the IRS (the EIN does not change), bank accounts, contracts, insurance, domain and trademark records, and the equity plan's name if it includes the company name.
- Increases in authorized shares are needed when the plan reserve, outstanding stock, and shares reserved for SAFEs and warrants approach the authorized number (§ 161). Do the arithmetic before every issuance.

**Common mistakes this form prevents.**
- Relying on § 242(d)(1)(B) when Preferred Stock is outstanding.
- Missing the Common class vote (or a Preferred protective-provision vote) on an authorized-share increase.
- A split amendment that increases authorized shares by more than the split ratio (not "proportionate"), which takes it outside § 242(d)(1)(B).
- Forgetting to adjust the equity plan reserve, outstanding awards, SAFEs, and warrants (most instruments adjust automatically, but the cap table and the stock ledger do not).
- Filing before the stockholder consent is effective. If stock is issued in reliance on authorized shares the amendment would have created, the issuance is an overissue that requires § 204 ratification and a certificate of validation.

Each certificate or book-entry position that immediately before the Split Effective Time represented shares of Common Stock shall thereafter represent the number of shares of Common Stock into which such shares were subdivided and reclassified. Because each share is subdivided into a whole number of shares, {{fractional_share_treatment}}."]]

[[Alternative B: **(Increase in authorized shares.)** Article {{article_number}} is amended by deleting its first paragraph in its entirety and replacing it with the following:

"The total number of shares of all classes of stock which the Corporation shall have authority to issue is {{total_authorized_shares_after}} shares, consisting of {{post_split_authorized_common}} shares of Common Stock, par value {{par_value}} per share, and {{authorized_preferred_shares}} shares of Preferred Stock, par value {{par_value}} per share."]]

> **Drafting note:** Choose one operative alternative in paragraph 2 (or combine A and C; § 242(a) permits several changes in one certificate). In paragraph 3, use Alternative A only for a name change, deletion of historical provisions, or a § 242(d)(1)(B) forward split (only one class outstanding, not divided into series, authorized increase no more than proportionate, and no express charter requirement of a stockholder vote); Alternative B for stockholder approval by consent; Alternative C for a meeting. Restate the whole first paragraph of the capital article rather than editing numbers in place, so the filed text is unambiguous. If the charter authorizes Preferred Stock in series or with a different par value, conform the replacement paragraph. A future effective time may not be later than the ninetieth day after filing (§ 103(d)). Keep the certificate to one page where possible; each additional page adds $9.

RESOLVED FURTHER, that [[Alternative A: because the Amendment effects only changes described in Section 242(d)(1) of the DGCL, and the Certificate of Incorporation does not expressly require a vote of stockholders for such changes, no meeting or vote of stockholders is required to adopt the Amendment]] [[Alternative B: the Board recommends that the stockholders of the Company approve and adopt the Amendment, and directs that the Amendment be submitted to the stockholders for approval by written consent pursuant to Section 228 of the DGCL, and fixes the close of business on {{board_resolution_date}} as the record date for determining the stockholders entitled to consent]].

RESOLVED FURTHER, that, notwithstanding any approval of the Amendment by the stockholders, the Board may abandon the Amendment at any time before it becomes effective, without further action of the stockholders, pursuant to Section 242(c) of the DGCL.

RESOLVED FURTHER, that the officers of the Company are authorized to execute and file the Amendment with the Secretary of State of the State of Delaware, to pay all fees and taxes in connection therewith, [[Optional: to file amendments to the Company's qualifications to transact business in other jurisdictions reflecting the Amendment,]] and to take all other actions necessary or advisable to carry out the foregoing resolutions.
