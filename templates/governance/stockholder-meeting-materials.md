## About this template

**What it does.** Part 1 is a notice of an annual or special meeting of stockholders. Part 2 is a proxy card. Part 3 is a minutes form. Part 4 is an inspector of elections oath and certificate. Part 5 explains, for the board, the § 211(c) consequence of not holding annual elections and the written-consent alternative.

**When a private startup holds a meeting.** Most venture-backed private companies never hold a stockholder meeting: they elect directors and approve matters by written consent (§ 228; § 211(b)). A meeting is used when (1) the charter prohibits action by consent; (2) the cap table is too broad to collect consents within 60 days; (3) a contested matter needs debate or a vote of a minority that will not sign; (4) a § 204 ratification is submitted at a meeting (it then requires at least 20 days' notice to holders of valid and putative stock, § 204(d)(1)); or (5) a stockholder or director has applied, or may apply, to the Court of Chancery under § 211(c).

**§ 211(c): the cost of skipping the annual election.** If no annual meeting (or action by written consent electing directors in lieu of an annual meeting) occurs for 30 days after the date designated for the annual meeting, or, if no date is designated, for 13 months after the latest of the corporation's organization, its last annual meeting, or its last consent electing directors in lieu of an annual meeting, the Court of Chancery "may summarily order a meeting to be held upon the application of any stockholder or director." At a court-ordered meeting, "the shares of stock represented at such meeting, either in person or by proxy, and entitled to vote thereat, shall constitute a quorum ... notwithstanding any provision of the certificate of incorporation or bylaws to the contrary." The court may set the date, record date, and form of notice. For a startup, that means a disaffected founder or early holder can force an election on a quorum of whatever shares show up, outside the protections of carefully negotiated quorum provisions. Failure to hold the meeting does not invalidate corporate acts or dissolve the company (§ 211(c), first sentence), and directors hold over until their successors are elected (§ 141(b)).

**Why written consent substitutes (§ 211(b)).** Stockholders may elect directors by written consent unless the charter provides otherwise. A unanimous consent may always be in lieu of the annual meeting. A less-than-unanimous consent is in lieu of the annual meeting only if all directorships to which directors could be elected at an annual meeting held at the consent's effective time are vacant and filled by the consent; a consent re-electing the entire board satisfies that. An annual "consent in lieu of annual meeting" is the market practice and restarts the 13-month clock.

**Notice (§ 222; § 232).** Notice must be given "not less than 10 nor more than 60 days before the date of the meeting" to each stockholder entitled to vote as of the notice record date (§ 222(b)), unless the DGCL requires a longer period (for example, 20 days for a merger under § 251(c) and for a § 204 ratification under § 204(d)(1)). It must state the place (if any), date and hour, the means of remote communication (if any), the voting record date if different from the notice record date, and, for a special meeting, the purposes (§ 222(a)). For a charter amendment, the notice must set out the amendment in full or a brief summary of the changes (§ 242(b)(1)). Notice by mail is given when deposited, postage prepaid; by courier, when received or left at the address; by email, when directed to the stockholder's email address unless the stockholder has objected to email notice, and email notice must carry "a prominent legend that the communication is an important notice regarding the corporation" (§ 232(a)). Other electronic transmission requires the stockholder's consent (§ 232(b)).

**Record date (§ 213(a)).** The board may fix a record date for notice not more than 60 nor less than 10 days before the meeting (and not before the date of the resolution fixing it). That date is also the voting record date unless the board fixes a later voting record date on or before the meeting date. If the board fixes none, the record date is the close of business on the day before notice is given (or, if notice is waived, the day before the meeting).

**Stockholder list (§ 219).** The company must prepare, no later than the tenth day before the meeting, a complete alphabetical list of the stockholders entitled to vote, with addresses and share numbers (email addresses not required), and make it available for any purpose germane to the meeting for the 10 days ending on the day before the meeting, on a reasonably accessible electronic network (with access information in the notice) or at the principal place of business. Since 2022 the list need not be available at the meeting itself.

**Quorum and voting (§ 216).** Unless the charter or bylaws specify otherwise (never below one-third): a majority of shares entitled to vote, present in person or by proxy, is a quorum; matters other than director elections pass by a majority of shares present and entitled to vote; directors are elected by plurality; separate class votes need a quorum of a majority of the class. The DGCL overrides these for specific actions (for example, majority of outstanding stock for charter amendments, mergers, asset sales, and dissolution). Director elections are by written ballot unless the charter provides otherwise; a ballot submitted by electronic transmission satisfies the requirement if the board authorizes it (§ 211(e)).

**Remote and hybrid meetings (§ 211(a)).** If the board may determine the place, it may hold the meeting solely by remote communication. Remote participants are deemed present and may vote only if the company implements reasonable measures to verify that each is a stockholder or proxyholder, gives them a reasonable opportunity to participate and to read or hear the proceedings substantially concurrently, and keeps a record of votes cast remotely.

**Proxies (§ 212).** A stockholder may authorize another person to act by proxy; a proxy may not be voted after three years from its date unless it provides a longer period (§ 212(b)). It may be executed by the stockholder or an authorized officer, director, employee, or agent, transmitted electronically with information showing it was authorized by the stockholder, or documented and signed under § 116 with information identifying the stockholder (§ 212(c)). A proxy is irrevocable only if it says so and is coupled with an interest sufficient in law (§ 212(e)); the irrevocable proxies in voting agreements and drag-along provisions rely on this.

**Inspectors of elections (§ 231).** Required only if the company has a class of voting stock listed on a national securities exchange, quoted on an interdealer quotation system, or held of record by more than 2,000 stockholders, unless the charter or bylaws require them (§ 231(e)). Private companies appoint an inspector when a vote may be contested; the inspector takes an oath and certifies the count. Part 4 provides the forms.

**Common mistakes this form prevents.**
- Notice sent 9 days or 61 days before the meeting, or omitting the purpose of a special meeting.
- A record date set more than 60 or fewer than 10 days before the meeting, or before the board resolution fixing it.
- No stockholder list available for the 10 days before the meeting.
- Remote attendees counted toward quorum without the verification and participation measures § 211(a)(2) requires.
- Allowing more than 13 months to pass without an annual election, exposing the company to a § 211(c) petition with a reduced quorum.

# PART 5. BOARD MEMO: ANNUAL ELECTIONS AND SECTION 211(c)

**To:** Board of Directors of {{company_name}}
**From:** {{secretary_name}}, Secretary
**Re:** Annual election of directors

1. **Requirement.** The Bylaws designate an annual meeting for the election of directors. Section 211(b) of the DGCL permits the stockholders to elect directors by written consent in lieu of the annual meeting. A unanimous consent always qualifies; a less-than-unanimous consent qualifies only if it fills every directorship up for election.
2. **Risk of not electing.** If neither an annual meeting nor a consent electing directors occurs within 30 days after the designated date (or within 13 months after the last election or the Company's organization, if no date is designated), any stockholder or director may apply to the Court of Chancery under Section 211(c) to order a meeting. At that meeting, the shares present in person or by proxy constitute a quorum notwithstanding the Certificate of Incorporation and the Bylaws.
3. **Recommendation.** Each year, approve the slate and solicit a stockholder consent electing the entire Board (stockholder-consent-and-notice, Part 1, resolution 1.2), with separate class-vote resolutions for any seats elected by a class under the Certificate of Incorporation and the Voting Agreement, and send the § 228(e) notice to non-consenting holders.
