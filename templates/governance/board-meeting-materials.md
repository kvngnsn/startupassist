## About this template

**What it does.** This file contains five related forms: (1) a notice of a special meeting of the board, (2) a waiver of notice under § 229, (3) a meeting agenda, (4) a minutes form that records quorum, conflicts, recusal, deliberation, and executive session, and (5) an annual board calendar. A board meeting is the right vehicle whenever deliberation matters, a director will not sign a written consent, or a conflicted transaction needs a record that disinterested directors were informed and acted with care.

**What the DGCL requires and what the bylaws supply.**
- The DGCL does not prescribe notice for board meetings; the bylaws do. Typical venture-company bylaws (including templates/formation/bylaws.md) let the board fix regular meetings without further notice and require notice of special meetings a short time in advance (for example, 24 hours by email or telephone or four days by mail), stating date, time, and place or means of remote participation. Check the bylaws in force before using the notice form, and match its timing and method.
- The purpose of a special board meeting need not be stated unless the bylaws require it, and § 229 says a waiver need not state the business or purpose unless the charter or bylaws require. Even so, give fair notice of significant business. A board action taken by deliberately concealing from a director that the director's removal as CEO, or a dilutive issuance, would be considered has been held inequitable (Adlerstein v. Wertheimer, 2002 WL 205684 (Del. Ch. Jan. 25, 2002)); such action is voidable rather than void (Klaassen v. Allegro Dev. Corp., 106 A.3d 1035 (Del. 2014)).
- Quorum: a majority of the total number of directors unless the charter or bylaws require more; the bylaws may set a lower quorum, never below one-third (§ 141(b)). "Total number" means the number of seats fixed under the bylaws, not the number of directors then serving, unless the bylaws say otherwise. The act of a majority of directors present at a meeting with a quorum is the act of the board (§ 141(b)).
- Remote participation counts as presence if everyone can hear each other (§ 141(i)); meetings may be held outside Delaware (§ 141(g)).
- Interested directors may be counted toward a quorum (§ 144(d)(1)). For the § 144(a)(1) safe harbor, the transaction must be approved by "a majority of the disinterested directors then serving," even if fewer than a quorum, after disclosure of the material facts; the minutes should record the disclosure, the recusal, and the vote of the disinterested directors.
- Waiver of notice (§ 229): a written waiver signed by the director, or a waiver by electronic transmission, before or after the meeting, is equivalent to notice. Attendance waives notice unless the director attends "for the express purpose of objecting at the beginning of the meeting" to the transaction of business because the meeting was not lawfully called. A director who objects must say so at the start, and the minutes should record it.
- Electronic documents: § 116(a) (electronic documents and signatures) does not apply to "any document expressly referenced as a notice (or waiver of notice)" by the DGCL, the charter, or the bylaws (§ 116(b)(4)). § 229 itself permits a waiver "by electronic transmission," so an emailed or e-signed waiver works; rely on § 229 and the bylaws' notice provisions, not § 116, for notices and waivers.
- Minutes: § 142(a) requires one officer to record the proceedings of directors' meetings. Minutes and the materials provided to the board are "books and records" stockholders may inspect for a proper purpose (§ 220(a)(1)e.–f., as amended in 2025). If the company has no minutes for a meeting, the Court of Chancery may order production of records that are the "functional equivalent" (§ 220(f)), which in practice means directors' emails and messages. Good minutes protect the board and limit discovery.

**Minutes: how much to write.** Market practice at venture-backed companies is "summary minutes": record who was present (and when anyone left or joined), that materials were distributed in advance, the topics discussed, that directors asked questions and discussed, the advice received (by category, not substance, for privileged advice), conflicts disclosed and recusals, the resolutions adopted and the vote, and that an executive session was held. Do not transcribe debate, do not record individual director views unless a director asks that a dissent be noted, and do not characterize the strength of a decision with adjectives. For high-stakes conflicted matters (a sale, an insider financing, a CEO termination), write fuller minutes that show the information considered, alternatives discussed, time spent, and the independence of those voting, because Delaware courts evaluate process.

**Observers and privilege.** Board observers (common under investors' rights agreements or management rights letters) are not directors, cannot vote, and do not count toward quorum. Observer agreements let the company exclude observers to preserve attorney-client privilege, to protect trade secrets, or where there is a conflict of interest. The presence of a non-director third party during privileged advice can waive privilege. The minutes form records when observers and guests leave.

**Executive session.** After a priced round, boards routinely meet without management (and sometimes without the CEO-director). The minutes record only that an executive session was held, who attended, and any formal action taken; any action taken in executive session must itself satisfy quorum and voting rules.

**Annual calendar.** Part 5 lays out a quarterly cadence for a seed-to-Series-A company with a calendar fiscal year. Adjust if the fiscal year differs. The calendar dovetails with checklists/annual-compliance-calendar.md.

**Common mistakes this form prevents.**
- Acting without a quorum because a director dropped off a call; the minutes form records departures and re-verifies quorum before each vote.
- An interested director voting on the director's own transaction without a record of disclosure; the conflicts section requires it.
- An undocumented "board call" with decisions made by email afterward. Decisions require a meeting with a quorum or a unanimous written consent.
- A waiver signed by fewer than all directors, used to cure a short-notice meeting; each director who did not receive proper notice must waive or attend without objection.

# PART 1. NOTICE OF SPECIAL MEETING OF THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS

**{{company_name}}**

**To:** Each member of the Board of Directors of {{company_name}}

NOTICE IS HEREBY GIVEN that a special meeting of the Board of Directors (the "**Board**") of {{company_name}}, a Delaware corporation (the "**Company**"), has been called by {{called_by}} in accordance with the Bylaws of the Company and will be held on {{meeting_date}} at {{meeting_time}} [[Alternative A: at {{meeting_location}}, with participation also available by {{remote_access}}]] [[Alternative B: solely by {{remote_access}}, by means of which all persons participating in the meeting can hear each other, as permitted by Section 141(i) of the General Corporation Law of the State of Delaware]].

[[Optional: The purpose of the meeting is to consider and act upon {{meeting_purpose}}, and to transact such other business as may properly come before the meeting.]]

Materials for the meeting will be distributed to directors by electronic mail [[Optional: and through the Company's board portal]] before the meeting.

Dated: {{notice_date}}

______________________________
{{recording_secretary}}, Secretary

> **Drafting note:** Give notice by the method and within the time the bylaws require ({{notice_method}} on {{notice_date}}). If the bylaws require the notice to state the purpose, keep the optional paragraph. Even where not required, stating a significant purpose (a sale, a financing, an officer's removal) avoids later claims that a director was misled. If a director cannot be given timely notice, obtain that director's written waiver (Part 2) or attendance without objection.

> **Drafting note:** Use one waiver per director, or a joint waiver signed by all. A waiver delivered by email ("I waive notice of the April 14 board meeting") also works under § 229. File the waivers with the minutes.

# PART 5. ANNUAL BOARD CALENDAR

**{{company_name}} — Annual Board Calendar (fiscal year ending {{fiscal_year_end}})**

| Quarter | Meeting focus | Approvals and reviews | Compliance tie-ins |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 (January–March) | Prior-year results; annual plan kickoff | Approve the operating budget (if not approved in Q4); approve year-end bonuses; review and approve the annual stockholder consent electing directors (or call the annual meeting); review the cap table and plan reserve | Delaware annual report and franchise tax due March 1 (Module K); Forms W-2, 1099-NEC, and 3921 to recipients by January 31; refresh 409A valuation if more than 12 months old or a financing has occurred |
| Q2 (April–June) | Strategy and product roadmap | Approve the 409A valuation and any refresh or new-hire grants; review D&O insurance before renewal if on a mid-year cycle | Federal and state income tax returns or extensions (April 15 for a calendar-year C corporation); Form D annual amendment if an offering is continuing; state annual reports due in Q2 |
| Q3 (July–September) | Fundraising readiness or mid-year review | Review cash runway and financing plan; consider Module N special committee if insiders will lead a financing | Extended returns due October 15; review qualification in new states where employees were hired |
| Q4 (October–December) | Next-year budget and compensation | Approve the next year's operating budget and headcount plan; annual executive compensation review (Module A-3, through disinterested directors or the compensation committee); review insurance and key policies; approve annual director compensation | Confirm Delaware estimated franchise tax installments if the tax is $5,000 or more (June 1, September 1, December 1); plan year-end Form 3921 and 1099 data collection |

> **Drafting note:** Seed-stage boards often meet monthly or every other month; post-Series A boards usually meet quarterly, with written consents between meetings for routine items. If the fiscal year is not the calendar year, shift the quarters, but keep the Delaware March 1 date, which is fixed by statute (§ 502(a)) regardless of fiscal year.
