## About this template

**What it does.** Part 1 is a written consent of stockholders under 8 Del. C. § 228, in majority and unanimous alternatives. Part 2 is the board resolution fixing a record date for the consent under § 213(b). Part 3 is a short consent-solicitation cover memo. Part 4 is the prompt notice to non-consenting stockholders required by § 228(e). Part 5 is a voting-requirement worksheet for class and series votes.

**When written consent is available.** Under § 228(a), any action that may be taken at an annual or special meeting may be taken without a meeting, without prior notice, and without a vote, if consents setting forth the action are signed by holders of outstanding stock having "not less than the minimum number of votes that would be necessary to authorize or take such action at a meeting at which all shares entitled to vote thereon were present and voted," and are delivered to the corporation as § 228(d) requires. This applies "unless otherwise provided in the certificate of incorporation." Venture-backed private company charters almost always permit written consent; public company charters usually prohibit it. Check the charter first.

**The threshold is measured against all outstanding voting shares.** Because the test assumes every share is present and voted, a matter that needs "a majority of the votes cast" at a meeting still needs a majority of all outstanding voting shares by consent. Matters that need "a majority of the outstanding stock entitled to vote" at a meeting (charter amendments under § 242(b)(1), mergers under § 251(c), sales of substantially all assets under § 271, dissolution under § 275(b)) need the same by consent. Add every class or series vote the DGCL, charter, or an agreement requires (Part 5).

**Majority versus unanimous consent.**

| Feature | Unanimous consent | Majority (non-unanimous) consent |
|---|---|---|
| Who signs | Every holder entitled to vote | Holders of at least the required votes |
| § 228(e) notice | Not required | Required, promptly, to non-consenting holders who would have been entitled to notice of a meeting |
| Election of directors in lieu of annual meeting (§ 211(b)) | Always permitted | Permitted only if all directorships that would be up for election at an annual meeting at the effective time "are vacant and are filled by such action" (so elect the entire board, even if re-electing the same people) |
| Disclosure duty | Applies | Applies with more force; minority holders did not participate |
| Typical use | Seed-stage companies with a handful of holders | After a priced round with many holders |

**Record date (§ 213(b)).** The board may fix a record date for a consent; it cannot precede the date of the board resolution fixing it and cannot be more than ten (10) days after that date. If the board does not fix one: (1) if no prior board action is required, the record date is the first date on which a signed consent is delivered to the corporation under § 228(d); (2) if prior board action is required (for example, the board must adopt and declare advisable a charter amendment under § 242(b)(1) before stockholders act), the record date is the close of business on the day the board adopts the resolution taking that prior action. A person who signs a consent before becoming a holder of record counts only if the person is a holder of record on the record date (§ 228(c)).

**The 60-day window and future-effective consents (§ 228(c)).** No consent is effective unless consents signed by enough holders are delivered within sixty (60) days of the first date on which a consent is delivered. A stockholder may direct that the consent become effective at a future time (including on the happening of an event) not later than sixty (60) days after the direction, and may revoke the consent before it becomes effective unless the consent provides otherwise. Practical rule: collect all signatures within a few days, date-stamp each delivery, and do not rely on a consent delivered more than 60 days before the last needed one. § 228(c) no longer requires each consent to bear the date of signature, but the delivery record (e-signature audit trail or email time stamp) does the same job; keep it.

**Delivery (§ 228(d)).** A consent must be delivered (i) to the corporation's principal place of business; (ii) to an officer or agent having custody of the minute book; (iii) to the registered office in Delaware by hand or by certified or registered mail, return receipt requested; or (iv) under § 116 to an information processing system the corporation has designated for receiving consents, with information that lets the corporation determine the date of delivery and the identity of the person giving the consent. An electronic consent delivered by methods (i) through (iii) must be reproduced and delivered in paper form. This form designates the company's e-signature platform and a legal email address as information processing systems (method (iv)), which is the simplest path. A consent given by a proxy must satisfy § 212(c)(2)–(3).

**Prompt notice to non-consenting holders (§ 228(e)).** If the action is taken by less than unanimous consent, "prompt notice" must be given to holders as of the consent record date who did not consent and who would have been entitled to notice of a meeting if the record date for the meeting were the consent record date. That usually means non-consenting holders of voting stock; non-voting holders get notice only where the DGCL requires notice to them (for example, appraisal notices under § 262 for a merger, which carry their own content and timing rules). "Prompt" is not defined; market practice is to send the notice within days, and before any certificate is filed or the action is implemented where feasible. The notice may be sent by email under § 232(a) with a prominent legend unless the holder has objected to email notice. If the action required a filed certificate (a § 242 amendment, for example), the certificate states that consent was given under § 228 in place of any statement about a meeting vote (§ 228(e)).

**Disclosure.** When directors seek stockholder action, they must disclose fully and fairly all material information within the board's control (Malone v. Brincat, 722 A.2d 5 (Del. 1998)). A consent is not cleansing under § 144(a)(2) or Corwin v. KKR Fin. Holdings LLC, 125 A.3d 304 (Del. 2015), unless it is "informed" and "uncoerced." Part 3 is the minimum package; for a sale, a recapitalization, or an insider transaction, prepare a full information statement.

**Securities law.** Asking holders to approve an action is not itself an offer of securities. Where the action involves a new investment decision by the holders (an exchange of securities, a merger in which they receive new securities), the consent solicitation may be an offer and needs an exemption (often § 4(a)(2) or Regulation D for accredited holders, or § 3(a)(9) for exchanges with existing holders). Flag it.

**Common mistakes this form prevents.**
- Counting votes against shares present rather than all outstanding shares.
- Missing a class or series vote (the § 242(b)(2) class vote on authorized-share changes; Preferred protective-provision votes; series votes).
- Electing only some directors by majority consent "in lieu of the annual meeting" (§ 211(b) requires filling all seats up for election).
- Consents collected over more than 60 days, or signed by people who were not holders of record on the record date.
- Forgetting the § 228(e) notice, or sending it only to holders of the class that voted when other voting holders did not consent.
- Delivering an e-signed consent to the registered office by email (not a permitted method); designate an information processing system instead.

# PART 5. VOTING-REQUIREMENT WORKSHEET

| Action | DGCL default vote | Separate class or series vote | Contract or charter overlays to check |
|---|---|---|---|
| Charter amendment (§ 242) | Majority of outstanding stock entitled to vote (§ 242(b)(1)) | Class vote if the amendment increases or decreases authorized shares of the class (unless the charter opts out under the last sentence of § 242(b)(2)), increases or decreases par value, or adversely alters the class's powers, preferences, or special rights; series vote if only a series is adversely affected | Preferred protective provisions; series-specific protective provisions; investors' rights agreement covenants |
| Board-only amendments (§ 242(d)(1)) | None for a name change, deletion of historical provisions, or a forward split with a proportionate authorized-share increase when only one class is outstanding and not divided into series | None | Charter may expressly require a vote; a § 242(b)(2) opt-out alone does not remove § 242(d) (2026 amendment) |
| Election of directors | Plurality at a meeting (§ 216(3)); by consent, holders of enough shares to elect at a fully attended meeting | Class-elected seats voted by the class alone | Voting agreement designation rights |
| Removal of directors (§ 141(k)) | Majority of shares entitled to vote in an election of directors | Class-elected directors removed without cause only by that class | Voting agreement removal mechanics |
| Merger (§ 251(c)) | Majority of outstanding stock entitled to vote | As required by the charter | Drag-along; appraisal notice under § 262 |
| Sale of all or substantially all assets (§ 271) | Majority of outstanding stock entitled to vote | As required by the charter | Drag-along; liquidation preference allocation |
| Dissolution (§ 275(b)) | Majority of outstanding stock entitled to vote (or all holders, without board action, under § 275(c)) | As required by the charter | Preferred protective provisions |
| Equity plan approval for ISOs | Majority of votes cast at a meeting with a quorum, or a method state law treats as equivalent (Treas. Reg. § 1.422-3) | None under the Code | Within 12 months before or after board adoption (I.R.C. § 422(b)(1)) |
| § 204 ratification (§ 204(c)–(d)) | The vote that would have applied to the defective act (counting only valid stock; putative stock does not vote) | As would have applied at the time of the act, if larger | See section-204-ratification |

> **Drafting note:** Count votes, not heads: if the charter grants more or less than one vote per share for any matter, every reference to a majority of stock means a majority of votes (§ 212(a)). For Preferred Stock that votes on an as-converted basis with the Common, use the as-converted vote count.
