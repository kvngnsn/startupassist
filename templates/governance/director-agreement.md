## About this template

**What it does.** Part 1 is an agreement between the company and an independent director who is joining the board. It sets expectations (time, meetings, committees), states compensation and equity, confirms expense reimbursement, indemnification and D&O coverage, and imposes confidentiality, conflict-disclosure, and corporate-opportunity obligations that supplement (and never limit) the director's fiduciary duties. Part 2 is a short resignation letter usable by a director, an officer, or both.

**What a director agreement cannot do.**
- **It cannot guarantee a board seat.** Stockholders elect directors (§ 211; § 216) and may remove them with or without cause by a majority vote, subject to the charter and class-election rights (§ 141(k)). The company can promise to nominate or recommend, and a voting agreement can bind stockholders to vote for a designee, but the company itself cannot contract for re-election.
- **It cannot dictate how the director votes or narrow fiduciary duties.** Directors owe duties of care and loyalty (including good faith and oversight) to the corporation and its stockholders. A contract that purports to restrict a director's judgment is unenforceable to that extent. The form says so expressly.
- **It does not make the director an employee.** Director fees are nonemployee compensation, reported on Form 1099-NEC; for payments made after December 31, 2025, the reporting threshold is $2,000 (I.R.C. § 6041(a) as amended by P.L. 119-21 § 70433), indexed after 2026.

**Duties to explain to a new outside director.**
- **Care:** informed decisions, reading materials, asking questions. The charter's § 102(b)(7) exculpation eliminates money damages for duty-of-care breaches by directors (and, if adopted, by officers for direct claims), but not for loyalty breaches, bad faith, or knowing violations.
- **Loyalty:** no self-dealing without disclosure and disinterested approval under § 144; no usurping corporate opportunities.
- **Oversight (Caremark):** directors must make a good-faith effort to implement a reporting system for mission-critical risks and respond to red flags (In re Caremark Int'l Inc. Derivative Litig., 698 A.2d 959 (Del. Ch. 1996); Stone v. Ritter, 911 A.2d 362 (Del. 2006); Marchand v. Barnhill, 212 A.3d 805 (Del. 2019)).
- **Preferred versus common:** an independent director on a venture-backed board is often the swing vote when investor and founder interests diverge; Delaware expects attention to the common stockholders as residual claimants where contractual preferred rights are not at stake (In re Trados Inc. S'holder Litig., 73 A.3d 17 (Del. Ch. 2013)).

> **Verify:** Before recommending a grant size, check current board-compensation survey data (for example, Carta or law-firm venture surveys) for the company's stage and sector. Commonly cited ranges for an independent director at seed through Series A run from roughly 0.25% to 1% of fully diluted capitalization, but data vary and change; do not present a number to the client without a current source.

The board may fix director compensation unless the charter or bylaws restrict it (§ 141(h)). Because directors who set their own pay are interested, Delaware applies entire fairness to discretionary self-awarded director compensation unless stockholders approved the specific awards or the plan's terms are self-executing (In re Investors Bancorp, Inc. Stockholder Litigation, 177 A.3d 1208 (Del. 2017)). For a new director whose grant is set before joining, the approving directors are typically disinterested as to that grant. For grants to sitting directors, use disinterested approval under § 144(a)(1) where possible, or stockholder approval of the specific awards.

**Corporate opportunities.** § 122(17) lets a corporation renounce, in its charter or by board action, an interest or expectancy in specified business opportunities or classes of opportunities presented to directors, officers, or stockholders. Venture charters typically renounce opportunities that come to investor-designated directors other than in their capacity as directors. An independent director who serves on several boards needs a clear rule; the form requires the director to offer the company opportunities that come to the director in that capacity or that relate to the company's line of business, unless the charter or the board has renounced them.

**Confidentiality and whistleblowing.** The form protects company information but carves out reports to government agencies and includes the Defend Trade Secrets Act immunity notice (18 U.S.C. § 1833(b)); without the notice, the company cannot recover exemplary damages or attorneys' fees under the DTSA against an individual "employee" (defined to include contractors and consultants) who misappropriates. Directors also have broad inspection rights for purposes reasonably related to their position (§ 220(d)); the agreement does not limit them.

**Independence questionnaires.** Since the 2025 amendments, director and officer independence questionnaires are inspectable "books and records" (§ 220(a)(1)i.). Have the director complete a D&O questionnaire on joining and annually, drafted on the assumption that a stockholder may read it.

**Securities and tax.** Director equity is eligible for Rule 701 (directors are covered persons) and must be priced at no less than fair market value on the grant date (409A). ISOs are available only to employees; a non-employee director receives NSOs or restricted stock. Restricted stock subject to vesting requires an 83(b) election within 30 days if the director wants the tax at grant. Deferred cash fees can create § 409A issues; pay fees currently.

**Resignations.** A director may resign "at any time upon notice given in writing or by electronic transmission"; the resignation is effective when delivered unless it specifies a later effective date or an effective date determined by events (§ 141(b)). An officer may resign at any time upon written notice (§ 142(b)). Acceptance is not required. A vacancy is then filled under § 223, the bylaws, the charter, and the voting agreement.

# PART 1. INDEPENDENT DIRECTOR AGREEMENT

This Independent Director Agreement (this "**Agreement**") is entered into as of {{effective_date}} (the "**Effective Date**") by and between {{company_name}}, a Delaware corporation (the "**Company**"), and {{director_name}} ("**Director**").

## 1. Board Service

**1.1 Election.** Director has been elected as a member of the Board of Directors of the Company (the "**Board**") [[Alternative A: by the stockholders of the Company]] [[Alternative B: by the Board to fill a vacancy]], effective as of the Effective Date, to serve until Director's successor is duly elected and qualified or until Director's earlier death, resignation, or removal in accordance with the Company's Certificate of Incorporation (the "**Charter**"), Bylaws (the "**Bylaws**"), and the General Corporation Law of the State of Delaware (the "**DGCL**"). Nothing in this Agreement obligates the Company or its stockholders to nominate or re-elect Director, and Director's service is subject to removal by the stockholders as provided in the DGCL and the Charter.

**1.2 Duties.** Director shall perform the duties of a director of a Delaware corporation in accordance with Director's fiduciary duties, the Charter, the Bylaws, and the Company's written policies applicable to directors, including by (a) attending, in person or by remote means, the {{meetings_per_year}} regular meetings of the Board expected each year and special meetings as reasonably practicable; (b) reviewing materials provided in advance of meetings; (c) serving on {{committee_assignments}} and such other committees as the Board may request and Director may accept; and (d) making Director reasonably available for consultation with management between meetings. Director anticipates devoting the time reasonably necessary to perform these duties.

**1.3 Independence; Questionnaire.** Director represents that Director is not an employee of the Company or of any holder of more than five percent (5%) of the Company's capital stock and has no relationship that would reasonably be expected to impair Director's independent judgment as a director, except as disclosed in writing to the Company before the Effective Date. Director shall complete the Company's director and officer questionnaire on or before the Effective Date and annually thereafter, and shall promptly notify the Chair of the Board of any change in the information provided.

**1.4 Fiduciary Duties Not Limited.** Nothing in this Agreement shall be construed to limit, expand, or otherwise modify Director's fiduciary duties under applicable law, to require Director to vote or act in any particular manner as a director, or to limit Director's rights as a director under the DGCL, including the right to inspect books and records under Section 220(d) of the DGCL. If any provision of this Agreement conflicts with Director's fiduciary duties, Director's fiduciary duties shall control.

**1.5 No Employment.** Director is not an employee of the Company and shall not be eligible for employee benefits by reason of Director's service. Director is responsible for all taxes on compensation received under this Agreement, and the Company will report such compensation to the extent required by law.

**2.3 Expenses.** The Company shall reimburse Director for reasonable, documented out-of-pocket expenses incurred in performing Director's duties, including travel to Board meetings, in accordance with the Company's expense policy; any single expense exceeding {{expense_cap}} requires advance approval of the Chair of the Board or the Chief Executive Officer. Reimbursement shall be made within thirty (30) days after submission of documentation and in no event later than the end of the calendar year following the year in which the expense was incurred; the amount eligible for reimbursement in one year shall not affect the amount eligible in any other year, and the right to reimbursement is not subject to liquidation or exchange for another benefit.

## 3. Indemnification and Insurance

**3.1 Indemnification Agreement.** On the Effective Date, the Company and Director shall enter into the Company's standard form of indemnification agreement for directors (the "**Indemnification Agreement**"). Director shall also be entitled to indemnification and advancement of expenses to the fullest extent provided in the Charter, the Bylaws, and Section 145 of the DGCL.

**3.2 D&O Insurance.** The Company shall maintain directors' and officers' liability insurance with coverage, limits, and terms that the Board determines in good faith to be reasonable for a company of its stage, and Director shall be covered under such insurance to the same extent as the Company's other non-employee directors, during Director's service and for so long thereafter as Director may be subject to claims arising from that service and the Company maintains such insurance for former directors.

## 4. Confidentiality

**4.1 Confidential Information.** "**Confidential Information**" means all non-public information concerning the Company or its subsidiaries, customers, suppliers, investors, or business that Director receives in connection with Director's service, including Board materials, deliberations, financial information, technology, and trade secrets. Confidential Information does not include information that (a) is or becomes generally available to the public other than through Director's breach; (b) Director lawfully knew before receiving it from the Company, as shown by written records; or (c) Director lawfully receives from a third party without a duty of confidentiality.

**4.2 Obligations.** During Director's service and at all times thereafter, Director shall hold Confidential Information in confidence, use it only to perform Director's duties as a director, and not disclose it to any person other than the Company's directors, officers, and advisors who need to know it, except as required by law or legal process (in which case Director shall, where legally permitted, give the Company prompt notice so that it may seek a protective order). Within thirty (30) days after Director's service ends, Director shall return or destroy all Confidential Information in Director's possession, except that Director may retain one copy of Board materials as reasonably necessary to defend any claim relating to Director's service, subject to this Section 4.

**4.3 Protected Activity.** Nothing in this Agreement prohibits Director from reporting possible violations of law to, filing a charge with, or participating in an investigation by any governmental agency, including the Securities and Exchange Commission, or from receiving an award for information provided to any governmental agency, and Director need not notify the Company before doing so. **Notice under the Defend Trade Secrets Act (18 U.S.C. § 1833(b)):** An individual shall not be held criminally or civilly liable under any federal or state trade secret law for the disclosure of a trade secret that is made (i) in confidence to a federal, state, or local government official, either directly or indirectly, or to an attorney, solely for the purpose of reporting or investigating a suspected violation of law; or (ii) in a complaint or other document filed in a lawsuit or other proceeding, if such filing is made under seal. An individual who files a lawsuit for retaliation by an employer for reporting a suspected violation of law may disclose the trade secret to the individual's attorney and use the trade secret information in the court proceeding, if the individual files any document containing the trade secret under seal and does not disclose the trade secret, except pursuant to court order.

## 5. Conflicts of Interest and Corporate Opportunities

**5.1 Disclosure.** Director shall promptly disclose to the Board any direct or indirect interest of Director, or of any person with whom Director has a material relationship, in any transaction, proposed transaction, or matter before the Board, and any relationship that would reasonably be expected to impair Director's independence with respect to any such matter, so that the Board may proceed in accordance with Section 144 of the DGCL. Director shall recuse from deliberation and vote on any matter in which Director has a material interest when requested by the Chair of the Board or counsel to the Company.

**5.2 Other Activities.** Director may serve as a director, officer, employee, advisor, or investor of other companies, provided that Director shall not, during Director's service, serve as a director, officer, employee, or consultant of any business that competes directly with the Company's then-current principal lines of business without the prior approval of the disinterested members of the Board. Director represents that Director's service will not breach any agreement or obligation to which Director is bound.

**5.3 Corporate Opportunities.** Except to the extent the Company has renounced its interest or expectancy in a business opportunity under its Charter or by action of the Board pursuant to Section 122(17) of the DGCL, Director shall present to the Board any business opportunity that (a) is presented to or discovered by Director in Director's capacity as a director of the Company, or (b) is within the Company's line of business and that Director knows or reasonably should know the Company would be interested in, before pursuing it personally or on behalf of another person.

## 6. Term and Termination

**6.1 Term.** This Agreement begins on the Effective Date and ends automatically when Director ceases to be a director for any reason. Director may resign at any time by notice in writing or by electronic transmission to the Company, which shall be effective when delivered unless it specifies a later effective date or an effective date determined upon the happening of an event or events, as provided in Section 141(b) of the DGCL.

**6.2 Effect of Termination.** On termination, the Company shall pay any earned but unpaid cash compensation and unreimbursed expenses through the termination date, and the Award shall be governed by the Plan and the applicable award agreement. Sections 1.4, 3, 4, 6.2, and 7 survive termination.

**7.2 Forum.** Each party irrevocably submits to the exclusive jurisdiction of the {{forum}} (or, if that court lacks subject-matter jurisdiction, the federal or other state courts located in the State of Delaware) for any action arising out of or relating to this Agreement.

**7.5 Entire Agreement.** This Agreement, together with the Indemnification Agreement, the Plan, and the applicable award agreement, constitutes the entire agreement between the parties regarding its subject matter and supersedes all prior agreements and understandings, written or oral, regarding that subject matter.

> **Drafting note:** A director's resignation is effective on delivery unless it specifies a later effective date or an event-based effective date (§ 141(b)); Alternative C (closing-conditioned) is common in acquisitions and board reconstitutions at a financing.
