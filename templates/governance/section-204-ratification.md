## About this template

**Why § 204 exists.** Before 2014, Delaware treated stock issued without compliance with the DGCL or the charter as void, and void acts could not be ratified by later board action (STAAR Surgical Co. v. Waggoner, 588 A.2d 1130 (Del. 1991)). A missing board approval or an overissue could cloud the entire cap table. Sections 204 and 205 changed that: under § 204(a), "no defective corporate act or putative stock shall be void or voidable solely as a result of a failure of authorization if ratified as provided in this section or validated by the Court of Chancery" under § 205. Ratification relates back: from the validation effective time, each ratified act "shall no longer be deemed void or voidable ... and such effect shall be retroactive to the time of the defective corporate act," and each share of putative stock is "an identical share ... of outstanding stock as of the time it was purportedly issued" (§ 204(f)).

**Key definitions (§ 204(h)).**
- **Defective corporate act:** an overissue; an election or appointment of directors that is void or voidable due to a failure of authorization; or any act within the corporation's power under subchapter II that is void or voidable due to a failure of authorization.
- **Failure of authorization:** failure to authorize or effect an act in compliance with the DGCL, the charter or bylaws, or any plan or agreement to which the corporation is a party (or disclosure in a proxy or consent solicitation), if the failure would render the act void or voidable; or failure of the board or an officer to approve an act that required its approval.
- **Overissue:** purported issuance of shares of a class or series beyond the number the corporation could issue under § 161, or of a class or series not then authorized by the charter.
- **Putative stock:** shares (including shares issued on exercise of options, warrants, or convertible securities created by a defective act) that would be valid but for a failure of authorization, or that the board cannot determine to be valid. The board's judgment on whether shares are valid or putative is conclusive absent actual fraud, unless the Court of Chancery decides otherwise (§ 204(h), last paragraph).
- **Validation effective time:** the latest of (a) stockholder approval (or, if none is required, immediately after the board resolutions), (b) a later time specified in the board resolutions where no certificate of validation is required, and (c) the effective time of any certificate of validation (§ 204(h)(6)).

**Step 1: Board resolutions (§ 204(b)(1)).** The board must adopt resolutions stating, for each defective corporate act:
- (A) the act or acts to be ratified;
- (B) the date of each;
- (C) for putative stock, the number and type of shares and the date or dates on which they were purportedly issued;
- (D) the nature of the failure of authorization; and
- (E) that the board approves the ratification.

The resolutions may reserve the board's right to abandon the ratification before the validation effective time, even after stockholder approval. Quorum and vote: those that apply to the type of act at the time of ratification, except that if the charter, bylaws, a plan or agreement, or the DGCL as in effect at the time of the defective act required a larger number or specified directors, that larger requirement applies (disregarding directors elected by a class with no shares then outstanding, or by persons no longer stockholders). The resolutions are normally adopted by unanimous written consent under § 141(f).

**Step 1A: Ratifying the initial board (§ 204(b)(2)).** If the defect is that the initial board was never properly elected (the incorporator never acted, or acted only after the "directors" did), a majority of the persons who, when the resolutions are adopted, "are exercising the powers of directors under claim and color of an election or appointment" may adopt resolutions stating (A) the names of the persons who first took action as the initial board, (B) the earlier of the date they first acted or were purportedly elected, and (C) approval of the ratification. That ratification does not need stockholder approval under § 204(c), which applies only to acts ratified under § 204(b)(1), but it does need § 204(g) notice. Then the ratified board ratifies the acts it took (Step 1).

**Step 2: Stockholder approval (§ 204(c)–(d)).** Each act ratified under § 204(b)(1) must be submitted to stockholders unless (1)(A) no provision of the DGCL, the charter or bylaws, or any plan or agreement would have required stockholder approval of the act, either at the time of the act or at the time of ratification, and (B) the act did not result from a failure to comply with § 203; or (2) when the board adopts the resolutions there are no shares of valid stock outstanding and entitled to vote. Examples: a stock issuance by the board alone does not need stockholder approval (unless the charter or an agreement required it); a charter amendment increasing authorized shares (the usual cure for an overissue) does.
- **Meeting:** at least 20 days' notice to each holder of valid and putative stock, voting or nonvoting, and also to holders as of the time of the defective act (other than those whose identities or addresses cannot be determined) (§ 204(d)(1)–(2)). The notice contains the resolutions (or the § 204(b)(1)(A)–(E) information) and a statement that claims that the act or putative stock is void or voidable, or that the Court should decline to give the ratification effect, "must be brought within 120 days from the applicable validation effective time" (§ 204(d)(3)).
- **Written consent:** § 204(g) expressly contemplates stockholder approval by § 228 consent; the § 204(g) notice can be combined with the § 228(e) notice.
- **Vote:** the vote that applies to the type of act at the time of approval, increased to any larger vote that applied at the time of the act; ratification of a director's election requires a majority of shares present and entitled to vote on that director's election (or more if the charter or bylaws required more); a § 203 failure requires the § 203(a)(3) vote (§ 204(d)(4)). **Putative stock does not vote and does not count toward a quorum** (§ 204(d)(5)).

**Step 3: Certificate of validation (§ 204(e)).** Required when the defective act would have required a filing under § 103 (for example, a certificate of amendment) and either the filed certificate needs a change to give effect to the act (including a change to its effective date and time) or no certificate was filed. File a certificate of validation instead of the certificate otherwise required. One certificate per act, except that acts that would have been effected by a single certificate may share one, and multiple overissues of a class or series may share one with the increase in authorized shares effective as of the date of the first overissue (§ 204(e)(2)). The attached certificate need not be separately executed or recite its own approval (§ 204(e)(4)). Fees (schedule revised August 1, 2026): $2,599 for a one-page filing, more if the certificate increases authorized stock (§ 391(a)(28) adds the § 391(a)(2) stock fee); optional preclearance $1,500; expedited service not offered for validations. Franchise tax: a certificate of validation "shall not reduce the annual franchise tax and interest due for any period prior to" its filing, does not entitle the corporation to a refund, and additional tax and interest for prior periods are computed at current rates (§ 503(e)); no refund is granted in connection with a certificate of validation (§ 505(a)).

**Step 4: Notice (§ 204(g)).** Unless notice was given under § 204(d) for a meeting, give "prompt notice" of each ratification to all holders of valid and putative stock, voting or nonvoting, as of the date the board adopts the resolutions (or a date within 60 days after, as the board establishes), and to holders as of the time of the defective act (other than those who cannot be identified or located from the records). The notice includes the resolutions (or the required information) and the statement that claims "must be brought within 120 days from the later of the validation effective time or the time at which the notice required by this subsection is given." Holders who approved by § 228 consent, and holders of putative stock who otherwise consented in writing, need not receive the combined notice.

**The 120-day bar (§ 205(f)).** After 120 days from the later of the validation effective time and the § 204(g) notice, no action may assert that a ratified act or putative stock is void or voidable due to the failure of authorization identified in the resolutions, or ask the Court to deny or condition the ratification. The bar does not apply to a claim that the ratification itself was not accomplished in accordance with § 204, or to a person who was entitled to notice under § 204(d) or (g) and did not receive it. So the notice list matters: reconstruct the stock ledger at the time of each defective act.

**When to petition under § 205 instead.** The Court of Chancery may determine the validity of any defective act or ratification, validate acts and putative stock, modify or waive § 204 procedures, approve a stock ledger, order a meeting, and set effective times for filings (§ 205(a)–(b)). Applicants include the corporation, any director, any record or beneficial holder of valid or putative stock (now or at the time of the act), and anyone substantially and adversely affected by a ratification. Service on the registered agent is service on the corporation, and no other party need be joined (§ 205(c)). Use § 205 when: (1) the board cannot determine who the valid stockholders are; (2) stockholder approval cannot be obtained or the defect involves a contested election; (3) the defect is not a "failure of authorization" (for example, a plain mistake in a filed instrument that § 103(f) correction cannot fix); or (4) an acquirer or underwriter wants judicial certainty before closing. The Court weighs good-faith belief in validity, treatment of the act as valid and reliance on the public record, harm from validation, harm from non-validation, and any other equitable factor (§ 205(d)).

**Non-exclusivity (§ 204(i)).** § 204 and § 205 are not the only ways to ratify, and failure to use them does not create a presumption that an act was defective. Common-law ratification remains available for voidable acts. But void acts, including stock issued in violation of the charter or the DGCL, need § 204 or § 205.

**Tax caution.** § 204(f) makes ratification retroactive for Delaware purposes. It does not decide federal tax questions. For ratified option grants, the IRS could treat the grant date for ISO and § 409A purposes as the date the corporate action became complete, which may be the validation effective time; if the fair market value rose in between, an ISO may fail § 422(b)(4) and an option may be discounted under § 409A. For ratified restricted stock, consider whether a protective 83(b) election is advisable. Involve tax counsel before ratifying equity awards.

**Market practice.** Investor and acquirer counsel review the minute book for defects in diligence. The usual cure is a "§ 204 package" signed before the financing closes, with the notice sent at or before closing, and a closing condition or representation that no defects remain. Clean-up packages commonly bundle: § 204(b)(2) ratification of the initial board; ratification of founder issuances; ratification of option grants approved only by the CEO; and a certificate of validation for any overissue.

# PART 5. SECTION 205 PETITION — OUTLINE

> **Drafting note:** A § 205 petition is a Court of Chancery pleading and must be prepared by Delaware counsel. Outline: (1) parties and jurisdiction (§ 205(e) exclusive jurisdiction; service on the registered agent under § 205(c)); (2) background and the defective acts, with dates and share numbers; (3) why § 204 ratification is unavailable, impractical, or insufficient; (4) the § 205(d) factors (good-faith belief in validity; consistent treatment as valid and reliance; absence of harm from validation; harm from non-validation); (5) relief requested under § 205(b) (validation of specific acts and putative stock, effective as of the original times; approval of a stock ledger; an order directing the Secretary of State to accept a filing with a specified effective time; notice to affected persons as the Court directs). Uncontested petitions are often resolved on a short schedule; budget for Delaware counsel fees and allow several weeks.

# PART 6. WORKED EXAMPLES

**Example 1 — Stock issued before the initial board was elected.** Northwind Robotics, Inc. filed its charter on January 6, 2027, without naming directors. The incorporator never signed an action electing directors. On January 10, 2027, Alex Rivera and Jordan Lee signed a "board consent" issuing 4,000,000 shares of Common Stock to each of themselves. Defects: the election of the initial board (§§ 107, 108) and, because the "board" was not duly elected, the issuance of 8,000,000 shares.
1. Rivera and Lee, as a majority of the persons exercising director powers under claim and color of election, adopt Resolution 1.1 under § 204(b)(2), naming themselves and the date January 10, 2027.
2. As the ratified board, they adopt Resolution 1.2 with two Schedule 1 rows (one per founder): act, January 10, 2027, 4,000,000 shares of Common Stock each, failure: approved by persons not then duly elected as directors.
3. Stockholder approval: not required. A stock issuance did not require stockholder approval (§ 204(c)(1)), and in any case all outstanding stock is putative, so there is no valid stock entitled to vote (§ 204(c)(2)).
4. Certificate of validation: not required (no § 103 filing would have been needed).
5. Notice under § 204(g) to Rivera and Lee (the only holders); each signs an acknowledgment. The 120-day period runs from the later of the validation effective time and the notice.
6. Tax: the founders' 83(b) elections were filed within 30 days of January 10, 2027; keep them in the file. If they were not, consult tax counsel; ratification does not reopen the 30-day window.

**Example 2 — Overissue of Common Stock.** The charter authorizes 10,000,000 shares of Common Stock. Before March 15, 2028, 9,100,000 shares were outstanding (8,500,000 founder shares and 600,000 shares from option exercises). On March 15, 2028, the board approved a restricted stock issuance of 1,200,000 shares to a new executive, bringing the total to 10,300,000, which is 300,000 shares more than authorized.
1. The board adopts Resolution 1.2 ratifying (i) a charter amendment increasing authorized Common Stock to 15,000,000 shares, deemed effective at 12:01 a.m. Eastern Time on March 15, 2028, immediately before the overissue, and (ii) the March 15, 2028 issuance of 1,200,000 shares of Common Stock (putative stock, because the board cannot determine which of the 1,200,000 shares are within the authorized number). Failure of authorization: the issuance exceeded the number of shares authorized by the Certificate of Incorporation (§ 161; § 204(h)(3)).
2. Stockholder approval is required (§ 204(c)): the charter amendment would have required stockholder approval under § 242(b), including the Common class vote under § 242(b)(2) unless the charter opts out, and any Preferred protective-provision vote. The 1,200,000 putative shares do not vote (§ 204(d)(5)).
3. A certificate of validation is required (§ 204(e)): Alternative A in Part 3, with a certificate of amendment attached as Exhibit A deemed effective at 12:01 a.m. Eastern Time on March 15, 2028. Fee about $2,599 plus any stock-based fee; the Division will compute additional franchise tax for the period from March 15, 2028 at current rates (§ 503(e)).
4. Notice under § 204(g), combined with the § 228(e) notice, to all holders of valid and putative stock as of the board adoption date and as of March 15, 2028.
5. Prevent recurrence: maintain a live authorized-versus-issued-reserved-convertible reconciliation in the cap table.

**Example 3 — Option grants never approved by the board.** Between June 1 and October 1, 2027, the CEO of Northwind signed option agreements under the 2027 Equity Incentive Plan for five employees covering 200,000 shares, using the plan's form, at the then-current 409A price, but no board or committee approval was obtained. Two employees early-exercised 30,000 shares.
1. The board adopts Resolution 1.2 with a Schedule 1 row for each grant (holder, date, shares, exercise price, ISO or NSO) and a row for each exercise (30,000 shares of putative Common Stock and the exercise dates). Failure of authorization: grants not approved by the board or a committee as required by § 157 and the Plan; shares issued on exercise of options created by a defective act are putative stock (§ 204(h)(4)).
2. Stockholder approval: not required, unless the grants exceeded the plan reserve and the Plan requires stockholder approval to increase it, or the charter or an agreement required stockholder approval (§ 204(c)(1)(A)). If the reserve was exceeded, also ratify (with stockholder approval) a plan amendment increasing the reserve, and note the separate ISO stockholder-approval rule (I.R.C. § 422(b)(1)).
3. No certificate of validation.
4. Notice under § 204(g) to all holders as of the adoption date and as of each grant date.
5. Tax: obtain tax advice on whether the grant date for ISO and § 409A purposes is the original date or the validation effective time; if the 409A value has increased, consider amending the exercise price to fair market value at the validation effective time or treating the options as NSOs with a compliant price. Going forward, use a § 157(c) delegation (board-consent-resolution-library, Module P) if the CEO needs to make grants.
