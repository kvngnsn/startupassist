## About this template

**What it does.** This is a Restricted Stock Unit Grant Notice and RSU Award Agreement under Plan § 9 for a private company. Each RSU is an unfunded promise to deliver one share of Common Stock. The RSUs vest only when **both** (1) a time-based **Service-Based Requirement** and (2) a **Liquidity Event Requirement** (a qualifying Change in Control or IPO) are met before the **Expiration Date**. Shares are delivered after both triggers within the § 409A short-term deferral period, net of tax withholding.

**Why double-trigger.** A single-trigger (time-only) RSU at a private company would make the holder owe income tax on illiquid shares as they vest. The liquidity condition defers taxation until shares can be sold or the Company can facilitate withholding. Because the liquidity condition is generally treated as a substantial risk of forfeiture under Treas. Reg. § 1.409A-1(d), settling promptly after it is satisfied keeps the award exempt from § 409A as a short-term deferral (Treas. Reg. § 1.409A-1(b)(4)).

### Key legal points

- **Short-term deferral deadline.** Payment must be made by the later of the 15th day of the third month after the end of the holder's or the Company's first taxable year in which the right is no longer subject to a substantial risk of forfeiture (Treas. Reg. § 1.409A-1(b)(4)(i)(A)). For calendar-year holders and companies, that is March 15 of the year after vesting. The agreement uses a hard outside date to stay inside the rule.
- **Is a liquidity condition a substantial risk of forfeiture?** Under Treas. Reg. § 1.409A-1(d)(1), a condition related to a purpose of the compensation can be one if the possibility of forfeiture is substantial. Practitioners treat an IPO or change-in-control condition as qualifying, but the IRS has not issued bright-line guidance, and secondary sources report informal IRS skepticism of the view that every liquidity condition automatically qualifies. That uncertainty is why the agreement (a) limits the condition to a fixed outside date, (b) includes a fallback 409A-compliant payment schedule (Alternative B in § 3.1), and (c) defines the Change in Control trigger to also be a § 409A "change in control event."
- **Expiration period.** The common market outside date for the liquidity condition is **seven years** from grant; some companies use ten. It is a facts-and-circumstances judgment, not a statutory rule. A longer period reduces the risk of forfeiture for holders but weakens the argument that the condition is substantial. The Board may extend the period only with care: extending a deferral is a § 409A issue if the award is not exempt, and extending a short-term deferral's SRF can be challenged as illusory.
- **FICA timing.** FICA (Social Security and Medicare) generally applies when RSUs are no longer subject to a substantial risk of forfeiture (I.R.C. § 3121(v)(2)(A)). For double-trigger RSUs, that is usually when the Liquidity Event occurs (or, for holders who leave first and keep time-vested units, when the event occurs after departure).
- **Rule 701.** An RSU is "sold" on its **grant date**, and if 12-month Rule 701 sales exceed $10 million, the Rule 701(e) disclosure must be delivered a reasonable time **before grant** (SEC C&DI 271.24, updated March 6, 2026). Large RSU programs often trigger this; plan for it before the grant cycle.
- **Withholding mechanics.** Before an IPO, the Company typically uses net settlement (withholding shares) and pays the taxes in cash, which consumes Company cash. After an IPO, sell-to-cover through a broker is common; the holder's authorization is drafted as a Rule 10b5-1(c)(1) instruction. Withholding at up to the maximum statutory rate is permitted without causing liability accounting (ASC 718, as amended by ASU 2016-09).
- **QSBS.** RSUs are not stock; the § 1202 holding period starts when shares are delivered. Stock received as compensation for services can qualify as acquired at original issuance (I.R.C. § 1202(c)(1)(B)(ii)), subject to the other § 1202 tests at issuance.
- **California.** RSUs under the Plan are "purchase or bonus" securities covered by 10 CCR § 260.140.42 (Plan Appendix A).

### Common mistakes

1. Settling "as soon as practicable after the IPO lock-up" with no outside date. If the lock-up is extended past March 15 of the following year, the award fails the short-term deferral rule.
2. Using a Change in Control definition broader than the § 409A "change in control event" for the payment trigger.
3. Forgetting that a terminated holder whose time-vested RSUs survive is still a Rule 701 holder and still needs tax withholding at the Liquidity Event; keep contact information current.
4. Granting RSUs without checking the $10 million Rule 701 threshold.
5. Letting the Board accelerate settlement "in its discretion," which is prohibited for deferred compensation (Treas. Reg. § 1.409A-3(j)(1)).

## 1. GRANT OF THE RSUs

**1.1 Grant.** The Company grants you the RSUs on the Grant Date. Each RSU represents the right to receive one share of Common Stock (a "**Share**") on the terms of this Agreement. The RSUs are an Award under Section 9 of the Plan, and the Plan is incorporated into this Agreement by reference. **1.3 Bookkeeping Account.** The Company will credit the RSUs to a bookkeeping account maintained for you. The RSUs are unfunded and unsecured obligations of the Company, and you have only the rights of a general unsecured creditor with respect to them.

## 2. VESTING

**2.1 Two Requirements.** An RSU vests only on the Vesting Date, which is the first date on which both (a) the Service-Based Requirement and (b) the Liquidity Event Requirement are satisfied for that RSU, if that date occurs on or before the Expiration Date. An RSU for which only one requirement has been satisfied is not vested.

**2.2 Service-Based Requirement.** The Service-Based Requirement is satisfied according to the schedule in the Grant Notice, subject to your Continuous Service through each applicable date. Any acceleration of the Service-Based Requirement under the Plan or a written agreement with you will apply as stated in that document.

**2.3 Liquidity Event Requirement.** The Liquidity Event Requirement is satisfied for all RSUs on the first to occur of the following, if it occurs on or before the Expiration Date:

(a) **Change in Control.** The effective time of a Change in Control that also constitutes a "change in control event" within the meaning of Treasury Regulations Section 1.409A-3(i)(5); or

(b) **IPO Settlement Trigger.** The earlier of (i) the date that is {{ipo_settlement_days}} after the effective date of the registration statement for the Company's IPO (or, for a direct listing or business combination described in the definition of IPO, after the first day the Common Stock trades on a national securities exchange), and (ii) March 1 of the calendar year following the year in which the IPO occurs (the "**IPO Settlement Trigger**").

> **Drafting note:** Clause (b)(ii) is the safety valve: because the IPO Settlement Trigger is what satisfies the Liquidity Event Requirement, it is also the vesting date for short-term deferral purposes, and settlement must follow by March 15 of the next year. Tying the trigger to "181 days after the IPO" places vesting just after a standard 180-day lock-up so holders can sell to cover taxes; the March 1 backstop prevents a late-year IPO from pushing vesting into a year that misaligns with payroll. Some companies instead treat the IPO effective date itself as the trigger and settle on the next quarterly settlement date, using net settlement and Company cash to pay taxes. Either is acceptable if the payment date falls within the short-term deferral period measured from the vesting date.

**2.4 Termination of Continuous Service.**

(a) **Service-Based Requirement Not Satisfied.** Any RSU for which the Service-Based Requirement has not been satisfied on the date your Continuous Service ends is forfeited on that date without consideration.

(b) **Service-Based Requirement Satisfied.** [[Alternative A: Any RSU for which the Service-Based Requirement has been satisfied on the date your Continuous Service ends (other than a termination for Cause) will remain outstanding and will vest if the Liquidity Event Requirement is satisfied on or before the Expiration Date. You must keep the Company informed of your current mailing address and email address, and you agree to cooperate with the Company to satisfy any Withholding Obligation at that time.]] [[Alternative B: Any RSU for which the Service-Based Requirement has been satisfied on the date your Continuous Service ends will be forfeited on that date without consideration unless the Liquidity Event Requirement has been satisfied on or before that date.]]

(c) **Cause.** If your Continuous Service is terminated for Cause, all RSUs that have not been settled, whether or not the Service-Based Requirement has been satisfied, are forfeited immediately without consideration.

(d) **Death or Disability.** [[Alternative A: If your Continuous Service ends because of your death or Disability, the Service-Based Requirement will be deemed satisfied for an additional number of RSUs equal to the number for which it would have been satisfied during the twelve (12) months after that termination, and those RSUs will remain outstanding under clause (b).]] [[Alternative B: Death or Disability is treated as any other termination under clauses (a) and (b).]]

> **Drafting note:** Alternative A in § 2.4(b) (retaining time-vested units after departure) has become market at venture-backed companies, because forfeiting earned units at departure is viewed as unfair and harms recruiting. It also strengthens the argument that the liquidity condition, and not continued service, is the substantial risk of forfeiture for the time-vested units. The trade-off is a growing population of former-employee holders whose withholding must be collected at the Liquidity Event.

**2.5 Expiration.** Any RSU that has not vested on or before the Expiration Date is forfeited on the Expiration Date without consideration. The Expiration Date will not be extended except as the Administrator determines is permissible without causing the RSUs to become subject to additional tax under Section 409A.

**2.6 Leaves; Change in Time Commitment.** Sections 16.4 and 16.5 of the Plan apply to the Service-Based Requirement.

## 4. TAX WITHHOLDING

**4.1 Obligation.** You are responsible for any Withholding Obligation arising from the RSUs, including income tax withholding at settlement and Social Security and Medicare (FICA) taxes when the RSUs are no longer subject to a substantial risk of forfeiture (which may occur at the Vesting Date even if Shares are delivered later, or at a time when you are no longer providing services). The Company may not deliver Shares until the Withholding Obligation is satisfied.

**4.2 Methods.** You authorize the Company to satisfy any Withholding Obligation by one or more of the following methods, as the Company determines in its sole discretion:

(a) **Net Settlement.** Withholding from the Shares otherwise issuable in settlement of the RSUs a number of whole Shares with a Fair Market Value on the date of withholding equal to the Withholding Obligation (which may be computed at up to the maximum individual statutory rate in the applicable jurisdiction to the extent that does not result in liability accounting), with the Company remitting the corresponding cash to the taxing authorities;

(b) **Sell-to-Cover.** If the Common Stock is then publicly traded, arranging for the sale on your behalf, through a broker the Company designates, of a number of Shares issued in settlement sufficient to generate proceeds (after commissions and fees) at least equal to the Withholding Obligation, with any excess proceeds paid to you. You irrevocably authorize the broker to make those sales and the Company to instruct the broker. This authorization is intended to satisfy the requirements of Rule 10b5-1(c)(1) under the Exchange Act as a written instruction that you may not alter or revoke, and you will not attempt to influence how, when, or whether those sales occur. You acknowledge that the broker has no obligation to obtain any particular price and that the proceeds may not cover the full Withholding Obligation, in which case clause (c) or (d) applies to the shortfall;

(c) **Cash.** Requiring you to pay the Withholding Obligation to the Company in cash (by check or wire transfer) by the date the Company specifies, which will be no later than the Settlement Date; or

(d) **Payroll Deduction.** Withholding from your wages or other cash compensation payable by the Company or an Affiliate, to the extent permitted by Applicable Law.

**4.3 Default Method.** Unless the Company determines otherwise, the Withholding Obligation will be satisfied (a) before an IPO, by net settlement under Section 4.2(a), and (b) after an IPO, by sell-to-cover under Section 4.2(b).

**4.4 Failure to Satisfy.** If you fail to satisfy the Withholding Obligation by the Settlement Date, the Company may (a) satisfy it by any method in Section 4.2, or (b) if the Withholding Obligation cannot be satisfied by the latest date permitted under Section 3.1, and to the extent permitted by Section 409A, forfeit the RSUs for which the Withholding Obligation was not satisfied.

**4.5 Responsibility for Taxes.** Regardless of any action the Company takes with respect to any Withholding Obligation, you are ultimately responsible for all taxes owed by you in connection with the RSUs, and the amount withheld may be more or less than your actual tax liability (for example, because supplemental wage withholding at the flat federal rate is often lower than your marginal rate). The Company makes no representation about the tax treatment of the RSUs.

> **Verify:** Confirm the current federal supplemental wage withholding rates under Treas. Reg. § 31.3402(g)-1 and IRS Publication 15 for the year of settlement (historically a flat optional rate for supplemental wages up to $1 million and a mandatory rate at the highest individual rate above $1 million) before describing them to holders.
