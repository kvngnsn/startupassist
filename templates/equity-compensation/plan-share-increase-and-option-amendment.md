## About this template

### Part A — share reserve increase

- **ISO approval.** Any increase in the maximum aggregate number of shares issuable under the Plan (other than a capitalization adjustment) is treated as the adoption of a **new plan** for ISO purposes, requiring stockholder approval within 12 months before or after the Board's action (Treas. Reg. § 1.422-2(b)(2)(iii)). Without it, options on the new shares are NSOs. The new ISO plan's 10-year grant window for the added shares runs from the earlier of the increase's Board adoption and stockholder approval.
- **California.** California's 10 CCR § 260.140.41(g) approval requirement attaches to the plan; prudent practice is to have stockholders approve each increase and to confirm with counsel whether DFPI expects an amended § 25102(o) notice.
- **Authorized shares.** Check the charter. If authorized Common Stock is insufficient, amend the charter (8 Del. C. § 242) before or with the increase. 8 Del. C. § 242(d) (added in 2023 and clarified by the 2026 amendments effective August 1, 2026) relaxes the stockholder vote for certain forward splits and, for classes listed on a national securities exchange, certain authorized-share changes; it rarely helps a private venture-backed company, whose charter protective provisions usually require a Preferred vote anyway.

> **Verify:** Confirm the scope of 8 Del. C. § 242(d) as amended effective August 1, 2026 before relying on it to avoid a stockholder or class vote on an authorized-share increase.
- **Investor consents.** Venture charters and voting agreements commonly require Preferred approval for reserve increases; pool increases are also negotiated in financing term sheets ("post-closing pool percent").

### Part B — extending post-termination exercise periods (PTEPs)

- **Why.** Departing employees often cannot afford to exercise within 90 days, and longer windows (one to ten years) are a recruiting tool. Companies extend for individual departing employees (often in separation agreements) or for all optionholders prospectively.
- **ISOs.** Any extension of the exercise period, including after termination, is a "modification" of an ISO and a new grant (Treas. Reg. § 1.424-1(e)(2), (e)(4)(i)). If the option is in the money at the time, the "new" option has an exercise price below FMV and cannot be an ISO; it becomes an NSO. Separately, any exercise more than 3 months after employment ends is an NSO exercise in any case (I.R.C. § 422(a)(2)). Holders must be told in writing; many companies obtain consent.
- **§ 409A.** An extension of an in-the-money stock right is generally an "extension" that makes the option deferred compensation from its original grant date, **unless** the new expiration date is no later than the earlier of the original maximum term and the 10th anniversary of grant (Treas. Reg. § 1.409A-1(b)(5)(v)(C)(1)). Extending an underwater option is a modification (new grant) rather than an extension. Draft every extension to stay within the original term.
- **Accounting.** An extension is a modification under ASC 718 that generally triggers incremental compensation cost; involve the Company's accountants.
- **California.** The minimum periods (30 days; 6 months for death or disability) are floors; extensions are permitted.

### Part C — repricing underwater options

- **Methods.** (1) Unilateral amendment to lower the exercise price of outstanding options (with holder consent where the change could adversely affect ISO status); or (2) an exchange offer in which holders elect to exchange old options for new options (often with fewer shares or a new vesting schedule).
- **Tax.** For ISOs, repricing is a modification and new grant (I.R.C. § 424(h)): the new option must meet all ISO requirements on the repricing date (price at FMV then; counts against the $100,000 limit in the year it first becomes exercisable; holding periods restart). An offer to change ISO terms that remains open **30 days or more** is a modification of every ISO to which it is made, even if declined (Treas. Reg. § 1.424-1(e)(4)(iii)); keep a voluntary election window under 30 days or accept the consequence. For § 409A, a reduction of the exercise price is a modification and a new grant (Treas. Reg. § 1.409A-1(b)(5)(v)(A)–(B)); the new price must be at least FMV on the repricing date.
- **Securities.** An offer to many optionholders to exchange options may be a "tender offer." Section 14(e) of the Exchange Act and Regulation 14E apply to tender offers by private companies (Rule 13e-4 applies only to Exchange Act reporting issuers). Regulation 14E requires the offer to stay open at least 20 business days (Rule 14e-1(a)) and prompt payment or return. The exchange itself needs a Securities Act exemption: Rule 701 (repriced options count as a new sale on the repricing date under C&DI 271.10) or Section 3(a)(9). California § 25102(o) generally covers new options issued in the exchange to California residents. Note the tension: 20 business days is about 28 calendar days, inside the 30-day ISO window only if the offer is not extended.
- **Board considerations.** Repricing is a fiduciary decision; document the business reasons (retention after a down round), consider excluding executives and directors or treating them separately, and consider a vesting reset or "value-for-value" exchange ratio. Investors frequently negotiate a consent right.
- **Rule 701.** The repriced options are a new sale and must fit within the 12-month Rule 701 limits; the original grant may be excluded if within the same 12 months (C&DI 271.10).

### Part D — correcting a grant priced below FMV (and other defective grants)

- **Discovery.** Typical causes: a stale 409A; a grant dated before the last director signed; a grant made after a material event (term sheet) at the old value; a new hire granted at the price in effect on the offer date.
- **§ 409A correction (IRS Notice 2008-113).** For an option that would otherwise be an exempt stock right except that its exercise price was erroneously set below FMV on the grant date: (1) **§ IV.D** — if, before exercise and no later than the last day of the holder's taxable year in which the option was granted, the price is reset to at least FMV on the grant date, the option is treated as never providing deferred compensation; available to all holders, including insiders, and requires a statement attached to the Company's tax return (§ IX); (2) **§ V.E** — for holders who are **not insiders** (directors, officers, or 10% beneficial owners under Section 16 principles, Notice § III.G), the price may be reset before exercise and by the last day of the holder's taxable year **following** the year of grant, with return attachments and information to the holder. After those windows, the option is a discounted option subject to § 409A, and the alternatives are worse (for example, a fixed exercise date to fit § 409A, or cancellation and replacement).
- **ISOs.** An ISO priced below FMV fails § 422(b)(4) unless the price was set in a good-faith attempt to meet FMV (I.R.C. § 422(c)(1)). Raising the price to FMV is not a modification adverse to ISO status (a change that reduces benefits is not a "modification" under Treas. Reg. § 1.424-1(e)(4)(i)), but confirm with tax counsel whether the original grant ever qualified.
- **Make-whole.** Holders lose value when the price goes up. Companies often pay a cash make-whole equal to the increase times the vested (or all) shares, payable as wages on a fixed schedule; structure it to comply with or be exempt from § 409A, and do not pay it in a way that looks like a price reduction.
- **Corporate defects.** If a grant exceeded the Share Reserve, was made under a defective delegation, or was otherwise not authorized, the stock or option may be void or voidable under Delaware law. 8 Del. C. § 204 allows ratification of defective corporate acts (with stockholder approval where the original act required it and notice to stockholders); § 205 allows Court of Chancery validation. Use counsel.
