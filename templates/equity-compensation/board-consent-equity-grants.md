## About this template

**What it does.** Approves a schedule of awards under the Plan, determines Fair Market Value for the grant date based on a 409A valuation, designates each option as an ISO or NSO, confirms vesting and any early-exercise rights, and directs compliance with Rule 701, California § 25102(o), and other requirements.

### The grant date and price rules (the most important part)

1. **The Grant Date is the date the corporate action is complete.** For a statutory option, the grant date is the date the corporation completes the corporate action constituting an offer, provided the maximum number of shares and the minimum price are fixed or determinable then (Treas. Reg. § 1.421-1(c)(1)); § 409A uses the same concept (Treas. Reg. § 1.409A-1(b)(5)(vi)(B)). Communicating the grant to the holder later does not move the date. For a unanimous written consent under 8 Del. C. § 141(f), corporate action is complete when the **last director signs and delivers** the consent, unless the consent specifies a later effective time (up to 60 days). **Never date a grant earlier than the last signature.**
2. **No backdating.** The exercise price must be at least FMV on the actual Grant Date. If the consent is signed over several days, or a director signs late, the FMV determination must be valid on the date the last signature is delivered. If the stock value may have changed (for example, a term sheet signed between the first and last signatures), the grant must be re-priced to FMV on the later date before it is made. Dating a consent "as of" an earlier date to capture a lower price is backdating: it creates a discount option (§ 409A penalties; loss of ISO status unless the good-faith exception applies), misstated financial statements, and potential fraud exposure.
3. **Future grant dates.** The Board may approve a grant today with a Grant Date of a specified future date (for example, a new hire's start date). The exercise price must then be FMV on that future date, and the Board must confirm (or the consent must provide) that no material event has occurred between approval and the Grant Date.
4. **409A safe harbor freshness.** An independent appraisal supports a presumption of reasonableness only if it is **as of a date no more than 12 months before** the grant (Treas. Reg. § 1.409A-1(b)(5)(iv)(B)(2)(i)). And a valuation is not reasonable if it fails to reflect information available after its date that may materially affect value (for example, a new financing, an acquisition offer, the resolution of material litigation, the issuance of a key patent, a large customer contract, IPO preparations) (Treas. Reg. § 1.409A-1(b)(5)(iv)(B)(1)). The consent therefore requires an express "no material change" determination.
5. **ISO checks.** ISOs only for employees (of the Company or a parent or subsidiary corporation); 110% price and 5-year term for Ten Percent Stockholders (with § 424(d) attribution); the $100,000 rule (I.R.C. § 422(d); Treas. Reg. § 1.422-4), which on early-exercisable grants applies to the whole grant in the grant year.
6. **Rule 701 tally.** Options count at their aggregate exercise price on the grant date; RSUs count when granted; restricted stock counts when sold. Use a consistent 12-month method (C&DI 271.06). If 12-month sales are expected to exceed $10 million, Rule 701(e) disclosure must be delivered before option exercise and before RSU grant (C&DIs 271.12, 271.24, 271.26).
7. **California.** The § 25102(o) notice is due within 30 days after the first issuance under the Plan to a California resident; the Company generally treats the option grant as the issuance of a security for this purpose. Confirm whether the notice has been filed.
8. **Directors.** Grants to directors are interested transactions. Have them approved by the disinterested directors with full disclosure (8 Del. C. § 144, as amended in 2025, provides safe harbors), and consider whether the director grant program is within a stockholder-approved limit (Plan § 4.8). The interested director should still sign a unanimous written consent; § 144 addresses the effect of the interest, not the mechanics of § 141(f).

### Market practice

- **Grant cadence.** Many venture-backed companies approve grants monthly or quarterly in batches, often on a fixed date after each 409A refresh, and grant new hires on or after their start date at the then-current FMV. Offer letters should say "subject to Board approval" and quote the share number but not a price.
- **Vesting.** Four years with a one-year cliff and monthly vesting thereafter is standard for new hires; refresh grants often have no cliff. Vesting may start on the start date (before the Grant Date); the price is still set on the Grant Date.
- **Early exercise.** Common for executives and early employees; requires the escrow and repurchase mechanics in the grant package.

## 1. DETERMINATION OF FAIR MARKET VALUE

**WHEREAS**, the Board adopted the Plan on {{board_adoption_date}}, and the Company's stockholders approved it on {{stockholder_approval_date}};

**WHEREAS**, {{valuation_provider}}, an independent appraiser that the Board has determined is qualified, delivered to the Company on {{valuation_report_date}} a written report (the "**Valuation Report**") concluding that the fair market value of a share of the Company's Common Stock as of {{valuation_date}} (the "**Valuation Date**") was {{fmv_409a_value}} per share, on a non-marketable minority basis;

**WHEREAS**, the Valuation Date is not more than twelve (12) months before the Consent Date or any Grant Date approved in this consent;

**WHEREAS**, the Board has reviewed the Valuation Report, including its methodology, assumptions, and the financial information on which it is based, and has inquired of management whether, since the Valuation Date, any event has occurred or any information has become available that may materially affect the value of the Common Stock, including (a) the receipt, negotiation, or execution of any term sheet or letter of intent for an equity financing, a secondary sale, an acquisition of the Company, or an IPO; (b) any material change in the Company's revenue, bookings, financial condition, or prospects compared with the projections used in the Valuation Report; (c) the gain or loss of a significant customer, partner, or contract; (d) a material product, regulatory, intellectual property, or litigation development; (e) any sale of Common Stock or Preferred Stock (including secondary sales) at a price materially different from the value in the Valuation Report; and (f) any change in the Company's capital structure; and

**WHEREAS**, management has informed the Board that no such event has occurred and no such information has become available, [[Optional: other than ___________, which the Board has considered and determined does not materially affect the value of the Common Stock because ___________]].

**NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED**, that, having considered the Valuation Report and all other information available to it, the Board determines in good faith, by the reasonable application of a reasonable valuation method in accordance with Section 409A of the Internal Revenue Code of 1986, as amended (the "**Code**"), and Treasury Regulations Section 1.409A-1(b)(5)(iv)(B), that the Fair Market Value of a share of Common Stock on the Consent Date is {{fmv_409a_value}};

**RESOLVED FURTHER**, that this determination applies to each award with a Grant Date on the Consent Date, and to each award with a later Grant Date specified in Schedule A only if, on that later Grant Date, (a) the Valuation Date is not more than twelve (12) months earlier and (b) the Chief Executive Officer or Chief Financial Officer certifies in writing to the Board that no event described in the recitals above has occurred since the Consent Date; and if either condition is not met, the award will not be granted on that date and will be resubmitted to the Board for approval at a Fair Market Value determined as of the new Grant Date; and

**RESOLVED FURTHER**, that the Board relies on the Valuation Report for the presumption of reasonableness under Treasury Regulations Section 1.409A-1(b)(5)(iv)(B)(2)(i), and the officers are directed to retain the Valuation Report and the materials the Board considered with the minutes of the Board.

## 2. APPROVAL OF GRANTS

**WHEREAS**, the Board has reviewed the proposed awards described in **Schedule A** (the "**Awards**") and has determined that granting them is in the best interests of the Company and its stockholders as an incentive for the recipients' continued service.

**RESOLVED**, that each Award described in Schedule A is approved and granted under the Plan to the recipient named in Schedule A (each, a "**Recipient**"), on the terms stated in Schedule A and in the applicable form of Award Agreement previously approved by the Board, effective on the Grant Date stated in Schedule A;

**RESOLVED FURTHER**, that the Grant Date of each Award is the Consent Date, unless Schedule A specifies a later Grant Date (such as the Recipient's first day of employment or service), in which case the Grant Date is that later date, subject to the conditions in Section 1; and in no event is the Grant Date of any Award earlier than the Consent Date;

**RESOLVED FURTHER**, that the exercise price per share of each option is the Fair Market Value of a share of Common Stock on its Grant Date as determined in Section 1, which is {{fmv_409a_value}} (or, for any incentive stock option granted to a Ten Percent Stockholder, one hundred ten percent (110%) of that Fair Market Value), and no option is granted with an exercise price below the Fair Market Value on its actual Grant Date;

**RESOLVED FURTHER**, that the purchase price per share of each Restricted Stock Award described in Schedule A is the price stated there, which is not less than the Fair Market Value on the Grant Date [[Optional: (except for any Restricted Stock Award identified in Schedule A as granted for services, which the Board approves with knowledge that the Recipient will recognize compensation income)]], and not less than the par value of the Common Stock;

**RESOLVED FURTHER**, that each Restricted Stock Unit described in Schedule A will vest on satisfaction of the Service-Based Requirement in Schedule A and the Liquidity Event Requirement on or before the Expiration Date stated in Schedule A, and the Board determines that the Recipient's services have a value not less than the aggregate par value of the shares issuable on settlement;

**RESOLVED FURTHER**, that each Award will vest according to the vesting schedule in Schedule A, subject to the Recipient's Continuous Service, and to any acceleration provided in a written agreement identified in Schedule A that the Board has previously approved or approves in this consent; and

**RESOLVED FURTHER**, that each option identified in Schedule A as early-exercisable may be exercised before vesting, with any Unvested Shares subject to the Company's Repurchase Option at the lower of the exercise price and Fair Market Value on the date of repurchase, and to escrow, as provided in the approved form of Exercise Notice and Stock Purchase Agreement.

## 3. INCENTIVE STOCK OPTION DESIGNATIONS

**RESOLVED**, that each option identified in Schedule A as an "ISO" is designated an Incentive Stock Option to the maximum extent permitted by Section 422 of the Code, and the Board confirms, based on information provided by management, that each such Recipient (a) is an employee of the Company or of a parent or subsidiary corporation of the Company on the Grant Date (or will be on the specified later Grant Date), and (b) is not a Ten Percent Stockholder, or, if a Ten Percent Stockholder, that the option has an exercise price of at least one hundred ten percent (110%) of Fair Market Value and a term of no more than five (5) years;

**RESOLVED FURTHER**, that the officers are directed to determine, for each Recipient of an Incentive Stock Option, the portion of the option (together with all other incentive stock options held by the Recipient) that will first become exercisable in each calendar year, based on Fair Market Value on the applicable grant dates, and any portion that exceeds one hundred thousand dollars ($100,000) in any calendar year (applying options in the order granted and, for early-exercisable options, treating the entire early-exercisable portion as first exercisable in the year of grant) will be a Nonstatutory Stock Option, and that determination will be reflected in the Award Agreement and the Company's records; and

**RESOLVED FURTHER**, that each option granted to a Recipient who is a Consultant, a non-employee Director, or an employee of an entity that is not a parent or subsidiary corporation of the Company is a Nonstatutory Stock Option.
