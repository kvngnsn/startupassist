## About this template

**What it does.** This is an omnibus equity incentive plan for a private Delaware corporation. It authorizes incentive stock options (ISOs), nonstatutory stock options (NSOs), restricted stock awards (including restricted stock purchases), restricted stock units (RSUs, including double-trigger RSUs), and, optionally, stock appreciation rights (SARs). It is the master document: every award agreement in this folder incorporates it, and its defined terms ("Change in Control," "Corporate Transaction," "Continuous Service," "Fair Market Value," "Cause," "Disability," "Liquidity Event") control unless an award agreement or a written employment agreement expressly overrides them.

**Where it sits.** (1) The Board adopts the plan and reserves shares ([board-consent-adopt-plan](board-consent-adopt-plan.md)); (2) the Company obtains a 409A valuation; (3) stockholders approve the plan within 12 months before or after Board adoption ([stockholder-consent-approve-plan](stockholder-consent-approve-plan.md)); (4) the Board (or a properly authorized delegate) approves grants ([board-consent-equity-grants](board-consent-equity-grants.md)); (5) the Company files the California § 25102(o) notice within 30 days after the first issuance to a California resident, if any. Grants may be made after step (1); ISOs granted before step (3) are valid ISOs only if approval occurs within the 12-month window, and California options may not be exercised before approval (any exercise before approval must be rescinded if approval fails).

### State and non-U.S. variations

- **California.** Rule 701 does not preempt state law. For California participants the Company typically relies on Cal. Corp. Code § 25102(o), which incorporates Rule 701 and requires the plan to include the terms in 10 CCR § 260.140.41 (options) or § 260.140.42 (stock purchases and bonuses) and to comply with § 260.140.45 and § 260.140.46. Appendix A does this. The notice fee under Cal. Corp. Code § 25608(y) is the § 25608(e) permit fee: $200 plus 0.2% of the aggregate value of the securities to be sold in California, capped at $2,500. The current rules impose **no** minimum vesting schedule and **no** limit on repurchase of unvested shares at cost. Rules with those limits were removed by the July 9, 2007 amendments, and older forms that still include them are out of date.
- **New York.** New York's Martin Act has no self-executing Rule 701 analog; practitioners analyze N.Y. Gen. Bus. Law § 359-f(2) (including the employee-benefit-plan exemption, which is understood to require an application to the Attorney General) or conclude that compensatory grants are not a public offering. See the reference file.
- **Other states.** Most states exempt compensatory plans that comply with Rule 701, often without filing (the Uniform Securities Act employee-plan exemption). A few require a notice or fee. Check each state where a participant resides **before** the first grant there.
- **Non-U.S.** participants need a country sub-plan or addendum (securities exemptions, exchange controls, tax-qualified sub-plans, data transfer). § 5.6 authorizes them.

### Common mistakes this form guards against

1. **Blank or ambiguous share reserve.** A plan that does not state a number of shares cannot support ISOs (I.R.C. § 422(b)(1)) or a California exemption (10 CCR § 260.140.41(a)), and it leaves the Board without authority to issue. § 4.1 requires a stated number, and § 4.3 states a separate fixed ISO limit.
2. **Mislabeling why stockholder approval is sought.** Rule 701 does **not** require stockholder approval. Approval is needed (a) for ISO status (I.R.C. § 422(b)(1)), (b) for California's exemption (10 CCR §§ 260.140.41(g), 260.140.42(e)), and (c) where the charter, the bylaws, or an investor agreement requires it. § 15.1 and the stockholder consent state this correctly.
3. **Missing California terms.** Plans that rely on § 25102(o) without the 10 CCR § 260.140.41 terms (120-month maximum term, 30-day and 6-month post-termination minimums, capitalization adjustments, nontransferability, 10-year grant window, stockholder approval with rescission, information delivery) lose the exemption. See Appendix A.
4. **Thin corporate-transaction provisions.** A plan that says only "the Board may accelerate vesting" gives an acquirer nothing to work with. § 11.3 authorizes assumption, continuation, substitution, cash-out (including cancellation of underwater awards for no payment), termination after notice and exercise opportunity, and escrow, earnout, and holdback terms. It states expressly that the Board need not treat all awards alike and need not obtain participant consent. The SEC staff treats assumption in a merger as needing no new exemption only when the plan permitted it **without holder consent** at the time of grant (C&DI 271.17).
5. **ISO traps.** Granting ISOs to consultants or directors who are not employees, or to employees of an entity that is not a "subsidiary corporation" (50% vote test, I.R.C. § 424(f)); omitting the 110%/5-year rule for 10% holders (with § 424(d) attribution); allowing early exercise of ISOs without warning that the full grant becomes "exercisable" in the grant year for the $100,000 test; extending a PTEP on an ISO (a modification under Treas. Reg. § 1.424-1(e)(4)(i)).
6. **Delegation without § 157(c) limits.** An officer-delegation resolution must fix the maximum shares, time period, and minimum consideration, and it cannot let the officer grant to themself. § 3.4 tracks the statute.
7. **Stale or ignored 409A valuation.** The safe harbor expires 12 months after the valuation date and earlier on any material event (financing term sheet, acquisition offer, major customer win or loss, litigation resolution). See § 2 ("Fair Market Value") and the grants consent.

## 2. DEFINITIONS

As used in the Plan, the following terms have the following meanings:

"**Administrator**" means the Board or, to the extent the Board has delegated authority under Section 3.3 or Section 3.4, the Committee or the Officer to whom authority has been delegated, acting within the scope of that delegation.

"**Applicable Law**" means any applicable securities, federal, state, foreign, material local or municipal, or other law, statute, constitution, principle of common law, resolution, ordinance, code, edict, decree, rule, listing rule, regulation, judicial decision, ruling, or requirement issued, enacted, adopted, promulgated, implemented, or otherwise put into effect by or under the authority of any governmental body (including under the authority of any applicable self-regulatory organization or stock exchange).

"**Award**" means any right to receive Common Stock, cash, or other property granted under the Plan, including an Option, a Restricted Stock Award, a Restricted Stock Unit Award, [[Optional: or a Stock Appreciation Right]].

"**Award Agreement**" means a written or electronic agreement between the Company and a Participant setting out the terms and conditions of an Award, including any grant notice and any exhibits. Each Award Agreement is subject to the terms of the Plan.

"**Board**" means the Board of Directors of the Company (or its designee as permitted by the Plan). Any decision or determination by the Board is a decision or determination that the Board (or its designee) makes in its sole discretion, and is final and binding on all Participants.

"**Capitalization Adjustment**" means any change made in, or other event that occurs with respect to, the Common Stock subject to the Plan or any Award after the Effective Date without the receipt of consideration by the Company through merger, consolidation, reorganization, recapitalization, reincorporation, stock dividend, dividend in property other than cash, large nonrecurring cash dividend, stock split, reverse stock split, liquidating dividend, combination of shares, exchange of shares, change in corporate structure, or any similar equity restructuring transaction, as that term is used in Financial Accounting Standards Board Accounting Standards Codification Topic 718 (or any successor). Conversion of any convertible securities of the Company (including any Preferred Stock or any simple agreement for future equity) is not a Capitalization Adjustment.

"**Cause**" has the meaning given in any written employment, offer letter, consulting, severance, or similar agreement between the Participant and the Company or an Affiliate that is in effect when the determination is made and that defines "cause" or a similar term. If there is no such agreement or definition, "Cause" means the occurrence of any of the following: (a) the Participant's commission of, or plea of guilty or no contest to, any felony or any crime involving fraud, dishonesty, or moral turpitude; (b) the Participant's attempted commission of, or participation in, a fraud or act of dishonesty against the Company or an Affiliate; (c) the Participant's intentional, material violation of any contract or agreement between the Participant and the Company or an Affiliate, of any statutory duty owed to the Company or an Affiliate, or of any material written policy of the Company or an Affiliate; (d) the Participant's unauthorized use or disclosure of the confidential information or trade secrets of the Company or an Affiliate; (e) the Participant's gross negligence or willful misconduct in the performance of duties; or (f) the Participant's willful failure to perform reasonably assigned duties (other than because of illness or Disability) after written notice and a period of not less than ten (10) days to cure, if curable. The Administrator determines whether a termination is for Cause, and that determination has no effect on any determination of the rights or obligations of the Company, an Affiliate, or the Participant for any other purpose.

> **Drafting note:** For California participants, 10 CCR § 260.140.41(e) permits forfeiture of vested options on a termination "for cause as defined by applicable law, the terms of the plan or option grant or a contract of employment." This definition supplies the plan-level definition. Keep it tight: a broad "Cause" forfeiture of **vested** options draws employee pushback and, for executives, conflicts with negotiated offer-letter definitions, which this definition defers to.

"**Change in Control**" means the occurrence, in a single transaction or a series of related transactions, of any one or more of the following events:

(a) any Exchange Act Person becomes the Owner, directly or indirectly, of securities of the Company representing more than fifty percent (50%) of the combined voting power of the Company's then outstanding securities, other than by virtue of a merger, consolidation, or similar transaction. A Change in Control will not be deemed to occur under this clause (a) (i) because of the acquisition of securities of the Company directly from the Company, (ii) on account of the acquisition of securities of the Company by an investor, any affiliate of an investor, or any other Exchange Act Person that acquires the Company's securities in a transaction or series of related transactions the primary purpose of which is to obtain financing for the Company through the issuance of equity securities, or (iii) solely because the level of Ownership held by any Exchange Act Person (the "**Subject Person**") exceeds the designated percentage threshold as a result of a repurchase or other acquisition of voting securities by the Company reducing the number of shares outstanding, provided that if a Change in Control would occur (but for this clause (iii)) as a result of the acquisition of voting securities by the Company, and after that share acquisition the Subject Person becomes the Owner of any additional voting securities that, assuming the repurchase or other acquisition had not occurred, increases the percentage of the then outstanding voting securities Owned by the Subject Person over the designated percentage threshold, then a Change in Control will be deemed to occur;

(b) there is consummated a merger, consolidation, or similar transaction involving (directly or indirectly) the Company, and, immediately after the consummation of that transaction, the stockholders of the Company immediately before the transaction do not Own, directly or indirectly, either (i) outstanding voting securities representing more than fifty percent (50%) of the combined outstanding voting power of the surviving Entity in the transaction or (ii) more than fifty percent (50%) of the combined outstanding voting power of the parent of the surviving Entity, in each case in substantially the same proportions as their Ownership of the outstanding voting securities of the Company immediately before the transaction;

(c) there is consummated a sale, lease, exclusive license, or other disposition of all or substantially all of the consolidated assets of the Company and its Subsidiaries, other than a sale, lease, license, or other disposition of all or substantially all of the consolidated assets of the Company and its Subsidiaries to an Entity more than fifty percent (50%) of the combined voting power of the voting securities of which is Owned by stockholders of the Company in substantially the same proportions as their Ownership of the outstanding voting securities of the Company immediately before that sale, lease, license, or other disposition; [[Optional: or

All employees of the Company and of each such parent and subsidiary corporation, including subsidiary corporations that become subsidiaries after the Effective Date, are eligible to receive Incentive Stock Options.

**5.3 Ten Percent Stockholders.** A Ten Percent Stockholder may be granted an Incentive Stock Option only if the Exercise Price is at least one hundred ten percent (110%) of the Fair Market Value on the Grant Date and the Option is not exercisable after the expiration of five (5) years from the Grant Date.

**5.4 Consultants and Rule 701.** A Consultant is eligible for an Award only if, when the Award is granted, either (a) the offer or sale of the Company's securities to the Consultant is exempt from registration under Rule 701 because the Consultant is a natural person providing bona fide services to the Company or an Affiliate that are not in connection with the offer or sale of securities in a capital-raising transaction and do not directly or indirectly promote or maintain a market for the Company's securities, or (b) the Company determines that the grant is exempt under another available exemption, or (c) the Company is subject to Exchange Act reporting and the offer and sale are registered on Form S-8. Awards may not be granted to an Entity that provides services; the Administrator may instead grant an Award to the natural person who renders services through that Entity.

> **Drafting note:** Rule 701(c)(1) requires consultants and advisors to be natural persons. Grants to a consultant's LLC or loan-out company, to fundraising finders, to investor-relations firms, and to securities analysts (C&DI 271.05) are outside Rule 701 and need a different exemption (usually Section 4(a)(2) or Regulation D with an accredited-investor check). Advisors who do nothing but make investor introductions are a frequent problem.

**5.5 Section 409A Eligibility.** Options and SARs may be granted only to Employees, Directors, and Consultants who are providing continuing services to the Company or to an Entity with respect to which the Company is an "eligible issuer of service recipient stock" within the meaning of Treasury Regulations Section 1.409A-1(b)(5)(iii), unless the Administrator determines that the Award is otherwise exempt from or complies with Section 409A.

**5.6 Participants Outside the United States.** The Administrator may grant Awards to eligible persons who reside or work outside the United States on terms that differ from those in the Plan, may adopt sub-plans or country addenda for that purpose, and may take any other action it deems necessary or advisable to comply with or take advantage of local law (including local securities, exchange-control, tax, labor, and data-protection law). No sub-plan or addendum may increase the Share Reserve or the limit in Section 4.3.

## 6. OPTIONS

Each Option will be in the form the Administrator deems appropriate. Each Option will be designated in writing as an Incentive Stock Option or a Nonstatutory Stock Option. If an Option is not specifically designated as an Incentive Stock Option, or if an Option is designated as an Incentive Stock Option but some portion or all of it fails to qualify as an Incentive Stock Option under Applicable Law, the Option (or portion) will be a Nonstatutory Stock Option. The provisions of separate Options need not be identical, but each Option Agreement will conform to (through incorporation of provisions by reference or otherwise) the substance of each of the following provisions:

**6.1 Option Agreement.** Each Option will be evidenced by an Award Agreement (an "**Option Agreement**") stating the number of shares subject to the Option, the Exercise Price, the vesting schedule, the expiration date, and whether the Option is an Incentive Stock Option or a Nonstatutory Stock Option.

**6.2 Term.** No Option will be exercisable after the expiration of ten (10) years from its Grant Date, or a shorter period specified in the Option Agreement. An Incentive Stock Option granted to a Ten Percent Stockholder will not be exercisable after the expiration of five (5) years from its Grant Date.

**6.3 Exercise Price.** The Exercise Price of each Option will be not less than one hundred percent (100%) of the Fair Market Value of the Common Stock subject to the Option on the Grant Date (one hundred ten percent (110%) for an Incentive Stock Option granted to a Ten Percent Stockholder). An Option may be granted with an Exercise Price lower than that set out in the preceding sentence if the Option is granted under an assumption of, or substitution for, another option in a manner that satisfies Section 424(a) of the Code (whether or not the Option is an Incentive Stock Option) and Treasury Regulations Section 1.409A-1(b)(5)(v)(D). In no event may the Exercise Price be less than the par value of the Common Stock.

> **Drafting note:** The Exercise Price must equal or exceed Fair Market Value on the **actual Grant Date** — the date the corporate action is complete — not on the date of the offer letter, the start date the employee expected, or the date of the last valuation. A discounted NSO is "nonqualified deferred compensation" under § 409A: the spread is taxable as it vests and each year after, plus a 20% additional federal tax and interest (and a 5% additional California tax under Cal. Rev. & Tax. Code § 17508.2). A discounted ISO loses ISO status unless the price was set in a good-faith attempt to meet FMV (I.R.C. § 422(c)(1)). For correction options, see [plan-share-increase-and-option-amendment](plan-share-increase-and-option-amendment.md).

**6.4 Payment of the Exercise Price.** To exercise an Option, the Optionholder must deliver a notice of exercise in the form the Administrator approves and pay the Exercise Price for the shares being purchased, to the extent permitted by Applicable Law and as determined by the Administrator, by one or more of the following methods specified in the Option Agreement:

(a) by cash (including electronic funds transfer), check, bank draft, or money order payable to the Company;

(b) if the Common Stock is then publicly traded, under a program developed under Regulation T as promulgated by the Federal Reserve Board that, before the issuance of the shares, results in either the receipt of cash (or check) by the Company or the receipt of irrevocable instructions to pay the aggregate Exercise Price to the Company from the sales proceeds;

(c) by delivery to the Company (either by actual delivery or attestation) of shares of Common Stock that are already owned by the Optionholder free and clear of any liens, claims, encumbrances, or security interests, with a Fair Market Value on the date of exercise that does not exceed the aggregate Exercise Price, provided that (i) the Common Stock is publicly traded at the time of exercise or the Administrator consents, (ii) any remaining balance of the Exercise Price is paid in cash or other permitted form, (iii) the delivery would not violate any Applicable Law or agreement restricting the redemption of Common Stock, and (iv) any certificated shares are endorsed or accompanied by an executed assignment separate from certificate;

(d) for a Nonstatutory Stock Option, by a "net exercise" arrangement under which the Company reduces the number of shares of Common Stock issuable on exercise by the largest whole number of shares with a Fair Market Value on the date of exercise that does not exceed the aggregate Exercise Price, provided that the Company accepts a cash or other permitted payment from the Optionholder to the extent of any remaining balance of the aggregate Exercise Price not satisfied by that reduction;

[[Optional: (e) to the extent permitted by Applicable Law and approved by the Administrator at the time of exercise, by delivery of a full-recourse promissory note of the Optionholder, bearing interest at a rate not less than the rate required to avoid imputed interest under Sections 483, 1274, and 7872 of the Code, secured by the shares purchased, and on the other terms the Administrator determines; or]]

(f) in any other form of legal consideration acceptable to the Administrator and specified in the Option Agreement.

> **Drafting note:** Net exercise of an ISO is permitted by some forms, but the tax result for the shares withheld is unsettled; this form limits net exercise to NSOs unless tax counsel approves. Promissory notes for option exercises raise § 409A, § 7872, accounting (the note may be treated as non-recourse and the purchase as an option), and Sarbanes-Oxley § 402 issues for any future public company; most venture counsel no longer recommend them. Under 8 Del. C. § 152, the Board determines the form of consideration, and its judgment of value is conclusive absent actual fraud; consideration must be at least par value (§ 153).

**6.5 Vesting and Exercisability.** An Option may vest, and become exercisable, in periodic installments that may or may not be equal, or on the satisfaction of performance or other conditions, as the Administrator determines and states in the Option Agreement. The vesting provisions of individual Options may vary. No Option may be exercised for a fraction of a share. The Administrator may set a minimum number of shares (not more than one hundred (100) shares, or the remaining vested shares if fewer) that may be purchased on any exercise.

**6.6 Early Exercise.** The Option Agreement may permit the Optionholder to exercise the Option, in whole or in part, before it is vested ("**Early Exercise**"). Any Unvested Shares received on Early Exercise will be subject to a Repurchase Right in favor of the Company, exercisable on the Optionholder's termination of Continuous Service at the lower of (a) the Exercise Price paid and (b) the Fair Market Value of those shares on the date of repurchase, on the terms set out in the Option Agreement and Section 8.4, and to any other restriction the Administrator determines appropriate. The Company will not be required to exercise its Repurchase Right until at least six (6) months (or a longer or shorter period required to avoid classification of the Option as a liability for financial accounting purposes) have elapsed after the Early Exercise unless the Administrator otherwise specifically provides. The Optionholder is solely responsible for deciding whether to make an election under Section 83(b) of the Code within thirty (30) days after the Early Exercise.

> **Drafting note:** Early exercise lets the holder start the capital-gain, ISO, and QSBS (I.R.C. § 1202) holding periods early, with little or no tax if made at a price equal to FMV with an 83(b) election. Two ISO cautions: (1) an option that is immediately exercisable in full is "exercisable for the first time" in the grant year, so only the first $100,000 of grant-date value can be an ISO (§ 6.14); and (2) the ISO holding periods start at exercise, but the spread at vesting is an AMT preference unless an 83(b) election is made for AMT purposes. Market default repurchase price is the lower of cost or FMV (protects against holders paying cash, then quitting when the stock is down).

**6.7 Transferability.** An Option may not be transferred except by will or by the laws of descent and distribution, and is exercisable during the lifetime of the Optionholder only by the Optionholder; provided that the Administrator may permit transfer of a Nonstatutory Stock Option (a) to a revocable trust of which the Optionholder is the sole beneficiary during the Optionholder's lifetime, or (b) to a "family member" (as defined in Rule 701(c)(3)) by gift or under a domestic relations order, in each case only to the extent permitted by Rule 701, Rule 12h-1(f) under the Exchange Act, and Applicable Law. An Incentive Stock Option transferred other than by will or the laws of descent and distribution will be treated as a Nonstatutory Stock Option. An Option may not be pledged, hypothecated, or used as collateral, and the Optionholder may not enter into any short position, put equivalent position, or call equivalent position (as defined in Rule 16a-1(h) or 16a-1(b) under the Exchange Act) with respect to the Option or the underlying shares before the Option is exercised. Subject to the Administrator's approval, an Optionholder may designate a beneficiary who, on the Optionholder's death, will be entitled to exercise the Option.

**6.8 Termination of Continuous Service.**

(a) **General Rule.** Except as otherwise provided in the Option Agreement or another written agreement between the Optionholder and the Company, if the Optionholder's Continuous Service terminates (other than for Cause or on the Optionholder's death or Disability), the Optionholder may exercise the Option (to the extent the Optionholder was entitled to exercise it on the date of termination) within the period ending on the earlier of (i) the date {{post_termination_exercise_period}} after the termination of the Optionholder's Continuous Service and (ii) the expiration of the term of the Option set out in the Option Agreement.

(b) **Unvested Portion.** Unless the Administrator determines otherwise, the unvested portion of the Option is forfeited on the date the Optionholder's Continuous Service terminates.

(c) **Lapse.** If, after termination of Continuous Service, the Optionholder does not exercise the Option within the applicable period, the Option will terminate.

(d) **Incentive Stock Option Status.** An Incentive Stock Option that is exercised more than three (3) months after the Optionholder ceases to be an employee of the Company or a parent or subsidiary corporation (or more than one (1) year after that date if the cessation is because of Disability within the meaning of Section 22(e)(3) of the Code), including because the Optionholder changes from Employee to Consultant or non-employee Director status, will be treated as a Nonstatutory Stock Option.

**6.9 Disability of the Optionholder.** Except as otherwise provided in the Option Agreement or another written agreement between the Optionholder and the Company, if the Optionholder's Continuous Service terminates as a result of the Optionholder's Disability, the Optionholder may exercise the Option (to the extent the Optionholder was entitled to exercise it on the date of termination) within the period ending on the earlier of (a) the date {{disability_exercise_period}} after that termination and (b) the expiration of the term of the Option set out in the Option Agreement.

**6.10 Death of the Optionholder.** Except as otherwise provided in the Option Agreement or another written agreement between the Optionholder and the Company, if (a) the Optionholder's Continuous Service terminates as a result of the Optionholder's death, or (b) the Optionholder dies within the period (if any) after termination of Continuous Service for a reason other than death during which the Option remains exercisable, then the Option may be exercised (to the extent the Optionholder was entitled to exercise it on the date of death) by the Optionholder's estate, by a person who acquired the right to exercise the Option by bequest or inheritance, or by a beneficiary designated under Section 6.7, within the period ending on the earlier of (i) the date {{death_exercise_period}} after the date of death and (ii) the expiration of the term of the Option set out in the Option Agreement.

**6.11 Termination for Cause.** Except as explicitly provided otherwise in the Option Agreement or another written agreement between the Optionholder and the Company, if the Optionholder's Continuous Service is terminated for Cause, the Option (whether vested or unvested) will terminate immediately on that termination, and the Optionholder will be prohibited from exercising the Option from and after the time of that termination. If the Optionholder's Continuous Service is suspended pending an investigation of whether it will be terminated for Cause, all of the Optionholder's rights under the Option will also be suspended during the investigation period.

**6.12 Extension of the Termination Date.** If the exercise of an Option following the termination of the Optionholder's Continuous Service (other than for Cause) would be prohibited at any time solely because the issuance of shares would violate the registration requirements under the Securities Act or any other Applicable Law, or would violate the Company's insider trading policy, then the Option will terminate on the earlier of (a) the expiration of a total period of time (which need not be consecutive) equal to the applicable post-termination exercise period after the termination of Continuous Service during which the exercise of the Option would not be in violation of those requirements or that policy, and (b) the expiration of the term of the Option set out in the Option Agreement. No extension under this Section 6.12 may extend an Option beyond the earlier of its original maximum term or the tenth (10th) anniversary of its Grant Date, or for more than thirty (30) days after the restriction lapses, in each case to the extent required to comply with Treasury Regulations Section 1.409A-1(b)(5)(v)(C)(1).

> **Drafting note:** Under Treas. Reg. § 1.409A-1(b)(5)(v)(C)(1), extending an in-the-money option's exercise period is not a § 409A "extension" if the new date is no later than the earlier of the original maximum term or the 10th anniversary of grant, and tolling for a legal prohibition is permitted if the extension lasts no more than 30 days after the restriction lifts. For ISOs, any extension of the exercise period (including after termination) is a "modification" (Treas. Reg. § 1.424-1(e)(4)(i)), so a discretionary PTEP extension usually converts an in-the-money ISO into an NSO. Explain this to the holder in writing.

**6.13 Non-Exempt Employees.** If an Option is granted to an Employee who is a non-exempt employee for purposes of the Fair Labor Standards Act of 1938, as amended, the Option will not be first exercisable for any shares until at least six (6) months after its Grant Date (although it may vest before that date). Consistent with the Worker Economic Opportunity Act, the Option may be exercised earlier for vested shares (a) on the Optionholder's death or Disability, (b) on a Corporate Transaction in which the Option is not assumed, continued, or substituted, (c) on a Change in Control, or (d) on the Optionholder's retirement (as defined in the Option Agreement or another applicable agreement, or, if none, in the Company's then-current employment policies). This Section 6.13 is intended to exclude any income the Employee derives from the exercise or vesting of an Option from the Employee's regular rate of pay under 29 U.S.C. § 207(e)(8).

**6.14 Incentive Stock Option $100,000 Limit.** To the extent that the aggregate Fair Market Value (determined at the time of grant) of Common Stock with respect to which Incentive Stock Options are exercisable for the first time by any Optionholder during any calendar year (under all plans of the Company and any Affiliates) exceeds one hundred thousand dollars ($100,000) (or another limit set out in Section 422(d) of the Code), the Options or portions of them that exceed that limit (according to the order in which they were granted) will be treated as Nonstatutory Stock Options, notwithstanding any contrary provision of the applicable Option Agreement(s). For this purpose, an Option that permits Early Exercise is treated as exercisable for the first time on the date it may first be exercised, whether or not vested, and an Option that accelerates is treated as exercisable for the first time in the year of acceleration.

**6.15 Notice of Disqualifying Disposition.** An Optionholder who disposes of shares of Common Stock acquired on the exercise of an Incentive Stock Option within two (2) years after the Grant Date or within one (1) year after the date the shares were transferred to the Optionholder (a "**Disqualifying Disposition**") shall notify the Company in writing within fifteen (15) days after the disposition, stating the date of disposition, the number of shares disposed of, and the amount realized. The Company may require that shares acquired on exercise of an Incentive Stock Option be held in book-entry form with the Company's transfer agent or equity administration platform, or bear a legend, so that the Company can identify Disqualifying Dispositions.

**6.16 Corporate Records Control.** If the records of the Company constituting the corporate action granting an Option contain terms (including Exercise Price, vesting schedule, or number of shares) that are inconsistent with those in the Option Agreement or related documents as a result of a clerical error in preparing those documents, the corporate records control and the Optionholder has no legally binding right to the incorrect term.

## 8. RESTRICTED STOCK AWARDS

**8.1 Restricted Stock Award Agreement.** Each Restricted Stock Award will be evidenced by an Award Agreement (a "**Restricted Stock Award Agreement**") in the form the Administrator deems appropriate. The terms of separate Restricted Stock Award Agreements need not be identical, but each will conform to (through incorporation of provisions by reference or otherwise) the substance of this Section 8.

**8.2 Consideration.** A Restricted Stock Award may be granted (a) in consideration of a cash payment, check, bank draft, or money order payable to the Company at a price per share determined by the Administrator, which, unless the Administrator determines otherwise and Applicable Law permits, will be not less than one hundred percent (100%) of the Fair Market Value on the Grant Date; (b) in consideration of past or future services rendered or to be rendered to the Company or an Affiliate; or (c) for any other form of legal consideration (including future services) that is acceptable to the Board and permissible under Sections 152 and 153 of the Delaware General Corporation Law, provided that the consideration for each share is at least its par value.

> **Drafting note:** Restricted stock sold at FMV produces no income at purchase; with a timely 83(b) election, no income at vesting either. Stock granted for services (no purchase price) is compensation equal to its FMV — at issuance with an 83(b) election, or at each vesting date without one — and requires withholding for employees. Grants for services still need consideration at least equal to par value under 8 Del. C. § 153; past services satisfy this, and the 2022 amendments to § 152 confirm the Board may accept "any benefit to the corporation."

**8.3 Vesting.** Shares of Common Stock issued under a Restricted Stock Award may be subject to a vesting schedule determined by the Administrator. Shares that have not vested are Unvested Shares and are subject to the Company's Repurchase Right (for shares purchased for cash) or forfeiture (for shares issued for services without cash payment).

**8.4 Termination of Continuous Service; Repurchase Right.**

(a) **Repurchase or Forfeiture.** If a Participant's Continuous Service terminates for any reason, then, unless the Award Agreement provides otherwise, (i) all Unvested Shares issued without a cash payment are forfeited to the Company without consideration, effective on the date of termination, and (ii) the Company has the right (but not the obligation) to repurchase all or any portion of the Unvested Shares purchased for cash at a price per share equal to the lower of (A) the Participant's original purchase price per share (as adjusted under Section 11.1) and (B) the Fair Market Value of a share on the date of repurchase.

The Company shall pay the repurchase price in cash (including by check or wire transfer) or by cancellation of any indebtedness of the Participant to the Company, or both, within the period stated in the notice and in any event within the period described in this Section 8.4(b).

(c) **Automatic Exercise.** [[Alternative A: Unless the Company notifies the Participant within the period described in Section 8.4(b) that it does not intend to exercise the Repurchase Right for some or all of the Unvested Shares, the Repurchase Right will be deemed automatically exercised for all Unvested Shares as of the end of that period, and the Company shall pay the repurchase price promptly after that date.]] [[Alternative B: If the Company does not exercise the Repurchase Right within the period described in Section 8.4(b), the Repurchase Right will terminate for the Unvested Shares not repurchased, and those shares will become Vested Shares.]]

(d) **Effect of Repurchase.** From and after the date the Repurchase Right is exercised (or deemed exercised) and the repurchase price is paid or tendered, the Participant will have no further rights as a holder of the repurchased shares (other than the right to receive the repurchase price), and the shares will be deemed transferred to the Company, whether or not the certificates (if any) have been delivered.

The 6-month deferral addresses an accounting concern (repurchase within 6 months of acquisition can cause liability treatment).

## 9. RESTRICTED STOCK UNITS

**9.1 RSU Award Agreement.** Each RSU Award will be evidenced by an Award Agreement (an "**RSU Award Agreement**") in the form the Administrator deems appropriate. The terms of separate RSU Award Agreements need not be identical, but each will conform to (through incorporation of provisions by reference or otherwise) the substance of this Section 9.

**9.2 Consideration.** No cash consideration is required for the grant or settlement of an RSU. The consideration for any shares issued on settlement is the Participant's services to the Company or an Affiliate, which the Board has determined have a value at least equal to the aggregate par value of those shares.

**9.3 Vesting Conditions.** An RSU vests on the satisfaction of the vesting conditions set out in the RSU Award Agreement, which may include (a) a time-based or performance-based service requirement (the "**Service-Based Requirement**") and (b) a requirement that a Liquidity Event occur on or before the expiration date stated in the RSU Award Agreement (the "**Liquidity Event Requirement**"). If an RSU Award Agreement includes a Liquidity Event Requirement, an RSU vests only when both the Service-Based Requirement and the Liquidity Event Requirement are satisfied, and any RSU for which the Liquidity Event Requirement is not satisfied on or before that expiration date is forfeited without consideration.

**9.4 Settlement.** Each vested RSU will be settled by the Company's delivery of one share of Common Stock (or, if the RSU Award Agreement so provides, cash equal to the Fair Market Value of one share on the vesting date, or a combination of shares and cash). Unless the RSU Award Agreement provides a different time of settlement that complies with Section 409A, the Company shall settle each vested RSU on a date it selects that is no later than the fifteenth (15th) day of the third (3rd) month following the end of the calendar year (or, if later, the Company's fiscal year) in which the RSU vests, so that settlement qualifies as a "short-term deferral" under Treasury Regulations Section 1.409A-1(b)(4). The Participant has no right to designate the year of settlement.

**9.5 Dividend Equivalents.** The Administrator may provide in the RSU Award Agreement that, when the Company pays a cash dividend on Common Stock, the Participant will be credited with dividend equivalents for each outstanding RSU, which will be subject to the same vesting conditions and settled at the same time as the related RSUs. Any dividend equivalent is a separate payment for Section 409A purposes.

**9.6 Termination of Continuous Service.** Except as otherwise provided in the RSU Award Agreement, RSUs for which the Service-Based Requirement has not been satisfied are forfeited on the termination of the Participant's Continuous Service. The RSU Award Agreement will state whether RSUs that have satisfied the Service-Based Requirement on the date of termination remain outstanding and eligible to vest on satisfaction of the Liquidity Event Requirement after termination.

**9.7 No Stockholder Rights.** A Participant has no voting or other rights as a stockholder with respect to RSUs until shares of Common Stock are issued in settlement of the RSUs. RSUs are unfunded and unsecured obligations of the Company.

**9.8 Section 409A.** Each RSU is intended either to be exempt from Section 409A as a short-term deferral or to comply with Section 409A, and the RSU Award Agreement will state which. If an RSU Award constitutes nonqualified deferred compensation, (a) its settlement may be triggered only by a permissible payment event under Treasury Regulations Section 1.409A-3(a), (b) any "Change in Control" that triggers settlement must also be a "change in control event" under Treasury Regulations Section 1.409A-3(i)(5), and (c) Section 13.2(c) (six-month delay for specified employees) applies.

> **Drafting note:** Private-company RSUs are almost always "double-trigger": a service requirement plus a Liquidity Event Requirement. The liquidity condition defers taxation (no income until shares are delivered and saleable) and is usually treated as a substantial risk of forfeiture for § 409A, so settlement within the short-term deferral period keeps the award exempt. The expiration date for the liquidity condition is a facts-and-circumstances judgment; practitioners commonly use seven years from grant, and some use ten. See [rsu-award-agreement](rsu-award-agreement.md) and the [reference](../../references/equity-compensation-and-tax.md).

## 12. TRANSFER RESTRICTIONS; RIGHT OF FIRST REFUSAL; MARKET STAND-OFF

**12.1 Transfer Restrictions.** Before an IPO, no Participant may sell, assign, transfer, pledge, encumber, or otherwise dispose of any shares of Common Stock acquired under the Plan, or any interest in them, except (a) to the Company, (b) under Section 12.2, or (c) in a transfer described in Section 12.2(f), and any purported transfer in violation of this Section 12 is void and will not be recorded on the books of the Company. **12.2 Right of First Refusal.**

(a) **Notice of Proposed Transfer.** Before any Vested Shares held by a Participant or any permitted transferee (each, a "**Holder**") may be sold or otherwise transferred (including by gift or operation of law), the Holder shall deliver to the Company a written notice (the "**Transfer Notice**") stating (i) the Holder's bona fide intention to sell or otherwise transfer the shares, (ii) the name of each proposed purchaser or other transferee, (iii) the number of shares proposed to be transferred, (iv) the bona fide cash price or other consideration per share for which the Holder proposes to transfer the shares (the "**Offered Price**"), and (v) all other material terms of the proposed transfer, together with a copy of any written offer or term sheet. The Holder shall offer the shares at the Offered Price to the Company or its assignee(s).

(b) **Exercise of Right.** At any time within {{rofr_days}} after receipt of a complete Transfer Notice, the Company or its assignee(s) may, by giving written notice to the Holder, elect to purchase all (or, with the consent of the Holder, fewer than all) of the shares proposed to be transferred, at the purchase price determined under Section 12.2(c).

(c) **Purchase Price.** The purchase price for the shares purchased by the Company or its assignee(s) is the Offered Price. If the Offered Price includes consideration other than cash, the cash equivalent value of the non-cash consideration will be determined by the Board in good faith.

(d) **Payment.** Payment of the purchase price will be made, at the option of the Company or its assignee(s), in cash (by check or wire transfer), by cancellation of all or a portion of any outstanding indebtedness of the Holder to the Company (or, in the case of repurchase by an assignee, to the assignee), or by any combination of them, within thirty (30) days after the Company's notice of exercise or, if later, in the manner and at the times set out in the Transfer Notice. If the Company or its assignee(s) fail to pay the purchase price when due, the election to purchase lapses as to the unpaid shares and the Holder may transfer them under Section 12.2(e).

(e) **Holder's Right to Transfer.** If all of the shares proposed in the Transfer Notice to be transferred are not purchased by the Company or its assignee(s) as provided in this Section 12.2, the Holder may sell or otherwise transfer those shares to the transferee identified in the Transfer Notice at the Offered Price or at a higher price, and on terms no more favorable to the transferee than those in the Transfer Notice, provided that the sale or transfer is consummated within one hundred twenty (120) days after the date of the Transfer Notice, is made in compliance with the Securities Act and other Applicable Law, and the transferee agrees in writing to be bound by this Section 12 as a Holder. If the shares are not transferred within that period, a new Transfer Notice must be given, and the Company will again be offered the right of first refusal, before any shares held by the Holder may be sold or otherwise transferred.

(f) **Exempt Transfers.** The right of first refusal does not apply to (i) a transfer by gift during the Holder's lifetime, or by will or intestacy on the Holder's death, to the Holder's "family member" (as defined in Rule 701(c)(3)) or to a trust for the benefit of the Holder or the Holder's family members, (ii) a transfer to the Company, or (iii) a transfer approved in writing by the Board (excluding any interested Director), in each case provided that the transferee agrees in writing to be bound by this Section 12 and that the transfer complies with Applicable Law.

(g) **Assignment.** The Company may assign its right of first refusal, in whole or in part, to one or more stockholders or other persons, including under any right of first refusal and co-sale agreement among the Company and its investors.

(h) **Termination.** The right of first refusal terminates on the earlier of (i) an IPO and (ii) a Change in Control in which the successor's securities are publicly traded.

**13.4 Section 83(i).** No Participant may make an election under Section 83(i) of the Code with respect to any Award unless the Company expressly permits it in writing. The Company does not intend to maintain a written plan described in Section 83(i)(2)(C)(i)(II) of the Code.

> **Drafting note:** Section 83(i) lets eligible employees of "eligible corporations" defer income on options and RSUs for up to five years, but it requires a written plan under which at least 80% of U.S. employees receive grants with the same rights and privileges, notice and escrow obligations, and withholding at the top rate. An § 83(i) election also disqualifies an option as an ISO (I.R.C. § 422(b), flush language). Almost no venture-backed companies use it; § 13.4 confirms the Company does not.

## 14. CLAWBACK; FORFEITURE EVENTS

**14.1 Clawback Policy.** Each Award is subject to recoupment (a) under any clawback or recoupment policy the Company adopts, including any policy adopted to comply with Section 10D of the Exchange Act and Rule 10D-1 under it and the listing standards of any national securities exchange on which the Common Stock is listed, and (b) as otherwise required by Applicable Law. The Board may also impose any other clawback, recovery, or recoupment provisions in an Award Agreement as it determines necessary or appropriate, including a reacquisition right regarding previously acquired shares of Common Stock or other cash or property on the occurrence of Cause. No recovery of compensation under such a policy or provision will be an event giving rise to a right to resign for "good reason" or "constructive termination" (or any similar term) under any agreement with the Company.

**14.2 Forfeiture Events.** The Administrator may specify in an Award Agreement that the Participant's rights, payments, and benefits with respect to an Award will be subject to reduction, cancellation, forfeiture, or recoupment on the occurrence of specified events, in addition to any otherwise applicable vesting or performance conditions, including termination for Cause or a material breach of any confidentiality, invention assignment, or other restrictive covenant agreement with the Company, to the extent enforceable under Applicable Law.

> **Drafting note:** Rule 10D-1 applies only to listed companies; a private company adopts a compliant policy shortly before its IPO. Before that, § 14 operates through the Company's own policy and Award Agreements. Be careful with forfeiture of vested equity for post-employment competition: such provisions are generally void in California (Bus. & Prof. Code §§ 16600–16600.5, with §§ 16600.1 and 16600.5 effective January 1, 2024) and restricted in several other states. The Delaware Supreme Court has held that forfeiture-for-competition provisions are generally enforced as written rather than reviewed for reasonableness (Cantor Fitzgerald, L.P. v. Ainslie (Del. 2024), extended beyond limited partnerships in LKQ Corp. v. Rutledge (Del. 2024)), but a Delaware choice-of-law clause will not necessarily displace California law for a California employee (Cal. Lab. Code § 925). Limit forfeiture events to Cause and breaches of enforceable covenants.

> **Verify:** Confirm the current status of Delaware forfeiture-for-competition case law and any 2025–2026 California or federal noncompete developments before relying on a forfeiture-for-competition clause.

**A.1 Number of Securities and Eligible Persons.** The total number of securities that may be issued under the Plan is stated in Section 4.1 (as adjusted under Sections 4.2 and 11.1, if applicable), and the persons eligible to receive Awards are stated in Section 5 (10 CCR §§ 260.140.41(a), 260.140.42(a)).
