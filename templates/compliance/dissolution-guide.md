## About this template

**What it does.** This file guides a voluntary dissolution of a solvent (or marginally solvent) Delaware corporation: the approvals, the certificate of dissolution (updated for the 2026 amendments), the franchise tax prerequisite, the two statutory claims procedures, distributions, tax and employment filings, and records retention. It includes board resolutions, a stockholder consent, a certificate of dissolution, a § 280 notice to claimants, and a wind-down checklist.

**Fiduciary context.** Directors of a solvent corporation owe duties to the corporation and its stockholders. When a corporation is insolvent, creditors gain standing to bring derivative claims for breach of fiduciary duty, but not direct claims (North American Catholic Educational Programming Foundation, Inc. v. Gheewalla, 930 A.2d 92 (Del. 2007)); directors' duties remain owed to the corporation, now for the benefit of all residual claimants. In practice: (1) establish solvency with a liquidation budget before approving dissolution; (2) do not prefer insiders (insider loans, accrued founder salaries) over other creditors; (3) consider the alternatives (sale of assets under § 271 with a stockholder vote, acqui-hire, an assignment for the benefit of creditors, or a bankruptcy case); and (4) document the process, including attention to the liquidation preference waterfall in the charter, which governs distributions to stockholders. Venture charters also usually require a Preferred Stock vote to liquidate or dissolve.

**Approval (§ 275).**
- **Board, then stockholders.** The board adopts a resolution deeming dissolution advisable "by a majority of the whole board at any meeting called for that purpose" (§ 275(a)); a unanimous written consent under § 141(f) serves the same purpose. Notice of the resolution and of a stockholders' meeting goes to stockholders entitled to vote; a majority of the outstanding stock entitled to vote must approve (§ 275(b)). Stockholders may instead approve by written consent under § 228, with prompt § 228(e) notice to non-consenting holders. Add any class or series vote the charter requires.
- **All stockholders, no board.** Dissolution may also be authorized without board action if all stockholders entitled to vote consent in writing (§ 275(c)).
- **Abandonment.** The resolution may let the board abandon the dissolution after stockholder approval (§ 275(e)).

**Franchise tax and annual report first (§ 277).** No corporation may be dissolved until it has paid all franchise taxes due or assessable, "including all franchise taxes due or which would be due or assessable for the entire calendar month during which such dissolution ... becomes effective," and has filed all annual franchise tax reports, "including a final annual franchise tax report for the year in which such dissolution ... becomes effective." Compute the final tax using the assumed par value capital method (report issued shares and gross assets) to avoid an authorized-shares-method bill. Pay any California, New York, or other state taxes and file withdrawals there as well (see foreign-qualification-guide).

**Certificate of dissolution (§ 275(d), as amended effective August 1, 2026).** It must state: (1) the corporation's name; (2) the date dissolution was authorized; (3) that dissolution was authorized by the board and stockholders under § 275(a)–(b), or by all voting stockholders under § 275(c); (4) the names and addresses of the directors and officers; (5) the date of filing of the original certificate of incorporation; and (6) the agreement required by § 275(i). Under § 275(i), the dissolving corporation agrees that it may be served with process in Delaware in any proceeding to enforce any obligation of the dissolved corporation, irrevocably appoints the Secretary of State as its agent to accept service effective on and after dissolution, and specifies the address to which the Secretary of State will forward process. The certificate is executed, acknowledged, and filed under § 103 and is effective on filing or at a stated future time not later than 90 days after filing.

**Service of process after dissolution (2026 changes).** The registered agent's authority and responsibilities end when the dissolution becomes effective, except as to process received before then (§ 275(h)). Afterward, plaintiffs serve the Secretary of State (electronically only as the Secretary prescribes), serve duplicate copies, and pay the § 391(a)(29) fee, now $100; the Secretary forwards the process by mail or courier with a record of mailing and signed delivery to the address in the certificate (or a later address designated in writing), and keeps a record of the service for at least 5 years (§ 275(i)). Practical points: give a forwarding address that someone will monitor for at least the three-year § 278 period (counsel's office or a responsible officer), update it in writing if it changes, and tell the registered agent of the dissolution so it can close the account.

**Fees (schedule revised August 1, 2026).** Certificate of dissolution under § 275: $224 for one page, plus taxes and $9 per additional page; expedited service available ($100 for 24-hour, $200 same day). A short-form certificate under § 391(a)(5)b. ($50) is available only if the certificate certifies that the corporation has no assets and has ceased transacting business, has owed only the minimum franchise tax for every year since incorporation, and has paid all franchise taxes and fees through the end of the year in which the certificate is filed. A venture-backed startup that has ever had substantial assets usually does not qualify, because it will have owed more than the minimum tax.

**Winding up (§ 278).** A dissolved corporation continues for three years (or longer if the Court of Chancery directs) to prosecute and defend suits, settle its business, dispose of property, discharge liabilities, and distribute remaining assets, but not to continue its business. Suits begun before or within the three years continue until judgment.

**Two ways to handle claims (§§ 280–281).**

| Feature | § 280 notice procedure, then § 281(a) | § 281(b) plan of distribution |
|---|---|---|
| Notice | Written notice to all claimants, published once a week for two consecutive weeks in a newspaper of general circulation in the county of the last Delaware registered agent's office and in the principal place of business (plus once in all editions of a national daily if total assets are $10,000,000 or more), and mailed by certified or registered mail to known claimants on or before the first publication | None required |
| Claims bar date | At least 60 days after the notice; claims of claimants given actual notice who miss it are barred | No bar date |
| Rejections | Reject within 90 days after receipt, and at least 150 days before the § 278 period ends; rejected claimant has 120 days to sue | Not applicable |
| Contingent contractual claims | Offer security; claimant who does not reject within 120 days is limited to the security | Make reasonable provision |
| Court involvement | Petition the Court of Chancery to set security for pending suits, rejected security offers, and unknown claims likely to arise within 5 years (up to 10 years if the Court so determines) | None required |
| Distributions to stockholders | Only after paying or providing for claims, and not before 150 days after the last rejection notice | After paying or making reasonable provision for known, pending, and unknown claims likely to arise within 10 years |
| Directors' protection | Not personally liable to claimants if the procedure is followed (§ 281(c)) | Same (§ 281(c)) |
| Stockholders' exposure | Lesser of pro rata share of a claim or amount received, capped at the amount received; no liability for claims not begun before the § 278 period expires (§ 282) | Lesser of pro rata share of a claim or amount received, capped at the amount received (§ 282(a), (c)) |

Most startups with modest, known liabilities use the § 281(b) plan of distribution, holding back a reserve for contingent and unknown claims; companies with meaningful litigation, product, or lease exposure use § 280 for the certainty of the bar date and Court-approved security.

**Distributions.** Pay creditors first (including taxes, wages, and leases). Then distribute remaining assets according to the charter's liquidation provisions: Preferred Stock liquidation preferences first (and any participation), then Common Stock. Outstanding SAFEs are typically paid in a dissolution event before Common Stock and alongside Preferred Stock, per their terms; convertible notes are debt. Options usually terminate under the plan; check the plan's dissolution clause and give notice. Report liquidating distributions on Form 1099-DIV.

**Tax filings.** File Form 966 within 30 days after adopting the resolution or plan to dissolve (I.R.C. § 6043(a); Treas. Reg. § 1.6043-1); file final federal Form 1120 (marked "final"), final Forms 941 and 940, W-2s, and 1099s; file final state returns, pay the final California minimum franchise tax if applicable, and file each state's withdrawal or surrender.

**Employees.** Final pay must comply with state law (California requires final wages, including accrued vacation, at termination; Cal. Lab. Code §§ 201–203, 227.3). The federal WARN Act requires 60 days' notice of a plant closing or mass layoff at employers with 100 or more employees (29 U.S.C. § 2102); California's WARN Act applies at covered establishments with 75 or more employees. COBRA notices, benefit plan terminations (including a final Form 5500 for a 401(k) plan), and separation agreements (with Older Workers Benefit Protection Act timing for group terminations) follow.

> **Verify:** Confirm the current procedure and form for withdrawing a foreign corporation in each state where the company is qualified (for California, the Certificate of Surrender of Right to Transact Intrastate Business under Cal. Corp. Code § 2112, and any FTB requirements), and whether the state requires a tax clearance before the withdrawal is accepted.

**Records retention.** Designate a custodian (an officer or counsel) for the minute book, stock ledger, and records for at least the three-year § 278 period, and longer where other law requires:

| Records | Minimum retention | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Minute book, charter, bylaws, stock ledger, cap table, equity grant records | Permanently (or at least until all claims periods and stockholder questions have closed) | DGCL §§ 220, 224, 278 (practice) |
| Federal income tax returns and supporting records | Generally 3 years after filing; 6 years if income was substantially understated; indefinitely if no return was filed | I.R.C. § 6501 |
| Employment tax records | At least 4 years after the tax is due or paid | Treas. Reg. § 31.6001-1(e)(2) |
| Payroll records under the FLSA | 3 years (2 years for supplementary records) | 29 C.F.R. §§ 516.5, 516.6 |
| Form I-9 | 3 years after hire or 1 year after employment ends, whichever is later | 8 C.F.R. § 274a.2(b)(2) |
| Employee benefit plan records | 6 years after filing the related report | ERISA § 107 (29 U.S.C. § 1027) |
| Contracts, IP assignments, licenses | At least the limitations period plus the § 278 period; IP assignments permanently if assets were sold | Practice |

**Common mistakes.**
- Filing the certificate of dissolution before paying franchise tax and filing the final annual report (the filing will be rejected under § 277).
- Using a pre-August 1, 2026 certificate form without the § 275(i) agreement and forwarding address.
- Distributing cash to stockholders before resolving claims, exposing stockholders to clawback (§ 282) and directors to liability.
- Ignoring the charter's liquidation preferences and SAFE terms in the distribution waterfall.
- Missing Form 966, final payroll filings, or state withdrawals, so state minimum taxes keep accruing.

# PART 1. BOARD RESOLUTIONS APPROVING DISSOLUTION AND PLAN OF DISTRIBUTION

**{{company_name}} — Resolutions of the Board of Directors (effective {{effective_date}})**

WHEREAS, the Board of Directors (the "**Board**") of {{company_name}}, a Delaware corporation (the "**Company**"), has reviewed the Company's financial condition, its prospects, the alternatives available to it (including continued operation, a sale of the Company or its assets, and other strategic transactions), a liquidation budget prepared by management, and the rights of the holders of each class and series of the Company's capital stock and of its creditors; and

WHEREAS, the Board has determined that the dissolution of the Company on the terms described below is advisable and in the best interests of the Company and its stockholders.

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED, that, pursuant to Section 275(a) of the General Corporation Law of the State of Delaware (the "**DGCL**"), the Board deems it advisable that the Company be dissolved, adopts this resolution by a majority of the whole Board, and recommends that the stockholders of the Company approve the dissolution, and directs that the dissolution be submitted to the stockholders for approval by written consent pursuant to Section 228 of the DGCL [[Optional: and to the holders of Preferred Stock for any separate approval required by the Certificate of Incorporation]].

RESOLVED FURTHER, that, notwithstanding approval of the dissolution by the stockholders, the Board may abandon the dissolution at any time before the certificate of dissolution becomes effective, without further action of the stockholders, as permitted by Section 275(e) of the DGCL.

RESOLVED FURTHER, that, following stockholder approval and the payment of all franchise taxes and the filing of all annual franchise tax reports required by Section 277 of the DGCL (including a final annual franchise tax report computed under the assumed par value capital method), the officers are authorized to execute, acknowledge, and file with the Secretary of State of the State of Delaware a certificate of dissolution in the form of Exhibit A, including the agreement and appointment required by Section 275(i) of the DGCL designating {{service_forwarding_address}} as the address to which process shall be forwarded.

RESOLVED FURTHER, that the Company shall wind up its affairs in accordance with [[Alternative A: Section 281(b) of the DGCL, and the plan of distribution attached as Exhibit B (the "**Plan of Distribution**") is hereby adopted, under which the Company shall pay or make reasonable provision to pay all claims and obligations, including all contingent, conditional, or unmatured contractual claims known to the Company, shall make such provision as will be reasonably likely to be sufficient to provide compensation for any claim against the Company that is the subject of a pending action, suit, or proceeding to which the Company is a party, and shall make such provision as will be reasonably likely to be sufficient to provide compensation for claims that have not been made known to the Company or that have not arisen but that, based on facts known to the Company, are likely to arise or to become known to the Company within ten (10) years after the date of dissolution, including by maintaining a reserve of not less than {{reserve_amount}}]] [[Alternative B: Sections 280 and 281(a) of the DGCL, and the officers are authorized to give the notice to claimants in the form of Exhibit C, to accept, reject, and pay claims, to offer security for contingent contractual claims, and to petition the Court of Chancery to determine the security required by Section 280(c) of the DGCL]].

RESOLVED FURTHER, that, after payment of or provision for all claims and obligations as provided above, the remaining assets of the Company shall be distributed to its stockholders in accordance with the liquidation provisions of the Certificate of Incorporation and the terms of any outstanding simple agreements for future equity, and the officers shall cause the Company to report such distributions as required by law.

RESOLVED FURTHER, that the officers are authorized and directed to (a) file Internal Revenue Service Form 966 within thirty (30) days after the adoption of these resolutions; (b) file all final federal, state, and local tax returns and reports; (c) withdraw or surrender the Company's qualification to transact business in each jurisdiction in which it is qualified; (d) pay all final wages and benefits in compliance with applicable law and give all notices required under the Worker Adjustment and Retraining Notification Act and similar state laws; (e) terminate or assign the Company's contracts, leases, benefit plans, and insurance (other than run-off directors' and officers' liability insurance, which the officers are directed to obtain for a term of not less than {{runoff_years}} years); (f) sell or otherwise dispose of the Company's assets for fair value, subject to any required stockholder approval under Section 271 of the DGCL; and (g) designate {{records_custodian}} as custodian of the Company's books and records after dissolution.

RESOLVED FURTHER, that each outstanding option and other award under the Company's equity incentive plan shall be treated in accordance with the plan's provisions applicable to a dissolution or liquidation, and the officers shall give holders any notice the plan requires.

> **Drafting note:** Adopt by unanimous written consent (board-consent-resolution-library) or at a meeting called for the purpose (§ 275(a)). Run-off ("tail") D&O coverage for former directors and officers is customary and inexpensive relative to exposure; six years is common. If the board is approving a sale of substantially all assets first, add the § 271 resolution and stockholder approval.
