## About this template

**What it does.** The certificate of incorporation (the "charter") creates the corporation when the Delaware Secretary of State files it (§ 103(d)). It fixes the name, registered agent, purpose, authorized capital, and any provisions that must or should be in the charter rather than the bylaws (exculpation, written-ballot waiver, board power to amend bylaws, blank-check preferred, class-vote opt-out, forum selection, corporate-opportunity renunciation). It is a public record.

**Where it sits.** First document in the formation sequence: name check → registered agent → **file this charter** → action of sole incorporator (adopts bylaws, elects directors, resigns) → initial board consent → founder stock purchase agreements → 83(b) elections within 30 days. See checklists/formation-checklist.md.

**Mandatory contents (§ 102(a)).** (1) Name containing "Corporation," "Incorporated," "Company," "Limited" (or an abbreviation such as "Inc." or "Corp.") or another listed word, and distinguishable on the Division's records; (2) registered office address (street, number, city, county, postal code, per § 131(c)) and registered agent name; (3) purpose (may be "any lawful act or activity"); (4) total authorized shares, classes, par value, and, if more than one class, the rights of each class or an express grant of authority to the board to fix them; (5) the name and mailing address of each incorporator; (6) if the incorporator's powers end on filing, the names and mailing addresses of the initial directors.

**Charter-only provisions in this form and why they belong here.**
- *Exculpation* (§ 102(b)(7)) must be in the charter. Since August 1, 2022 it may cover officers, but officer exculpation never reaches claims by or in the right of the corporation (derivative claims), and for both directors and officers it cannot reach breaches of the duty of loyalty, bad-faith acts, intentional misconduct, knowing violations of law, or improper personal benefit; for directors it also cannot reach unlawful dividends or repurchases under § 174. "Officer" for this purpose means only a person deemed to have consented to service under 10 Del. C. § 3114(b) — the president, CEO, COO, CFO, CLO, controller, treasurer, chief accounting officer, SEC-reported most highly compensated officers, and anyone who agrees in writing to be treated as an officer for that purpose. Exculpation cannot be retroactive (§ 102(b)(7)), so put it in the original charter.
- *Written ballot waiver* (§ 211(e)): elections of directors must be by written ballot unless the charter says otherwise.
- *Board power to amend bylaws* (§ 109(a)): after stock is paid for, only stockholders may amend bylaws unless the charter confers the power on the board.
- *Blank-check preferred* (§§ 102(a)(4), 151(a), 151(g)): the board can designate series only if the charter expressly grants that authority.
- *§ 242(b)(2) opt-out*: a vote of the common stock as a separate class on increases in authorized common can be eliminated only by a provision in the original charter (or an amendment adopted before issuance, or approved by the class). See below for the 2026 § 242(d) interaction.
- *Restriction of stockholder written consent* would have to be in the charter (§ 228(a)). This form does **not** restrict it; venture-backed private companies rely on it.

**2026 clarification of § 242(d) (85 Del. Laws c. 279, effective August 1, 2026).** Section 242(d) (added in 2023) lets a corporation (1) do a forward stock split, with a proportionate increase in authorized shares, without any stockholder vote if it has only one class outstanding and that class is not divided into series, and (2) if listed on a national securities exchange, increase or decrease authorized shares or effect a reverse split by a majority of votes cast rather than a majority of outstanding shares. Both apply "unless otherwise expressly required by the certificate of incorporation." Practitioners worried that the common § 242(b)(2) opt-out language ("may be increased or decreased by the affirmative vote of the holders of a majority of the stock ... irrespective of Section 242(b)(2)") might be read as such an express requirement and so switch off § 242(d). The 2026 amendment added a final sentence to § 242(d): a § 242(b)(2) opt-out does **not** constitute an express requirement for a greater vote, and does not take the corporation out of § 242(d), **unless** the provision expressly says the corporation is not governed by § 242(d)(1) or (2), or requires a greater or additional vote than § 242(b)(2)'s last sentence contemplates. Practical result: use the optional § 242(b)(2) opt-out in Article IV without fear of losing § 242(d); add the express § 242(d) opt-out only if you want to require a stockholder vote for forward splits, which is rarely desirable.

**Authorized shares and par value — the franchise tax drives the choice.**
- *Market default:* 10,000,000 shares of Common Stock, par value $0.00001. Founders buy at par (or slightly above) for nominal cash, which supports a clean "purchase at fair market value" with no compensation income. Shares cannot be issued for less than par (§ 153(a)), so a high par value makes founder purchases and option exercises costlier.
- *Franchise tax — Authorized Shares Method:* $175 for 5,000 shares or fewer; $250 for 5,001–10,000 shares; $85 for each additional 10,000 shares or portion; maximum $200,000 ($250,000 for a Large Corporate Filer). For 10,000,000 authorized shares: $250 + (999 × $85) = **$85,165**. The Division's annual notice is often computed this way, which alarms founders. Do not pay it.
- *Franchise tax — Assumed Par Value Capital Method:* (i) divide total gross assets (defined in § 503(i); the Division's instructions use Form 1120, Schedule L, total assets for the fiscal year ending in the prior calendar year) by total issued shares (including treasury shares), carried to six decimal places, to get the "assumed par"; (ii) if the assumed par is less than the stated par, use the stated par; (iii) multiply by total authorized shares to get assumed par value capital; (iv) tax is $400 per $1,000,000 of assumed par value capital or portion of $1,000,000; minimum **$400**. A corporation must report its issued shares and gross assets to use this method (§ 503(b)); the Division's calculator says to use whichever method yields the lesser tax.
- *Worked example (10,000,000 authorized at $0.00001; 8,000,000 issued; gross assets $250,000):* assumed par = $250,000 ÷ 8,000,000 = $0.031250 (greater than $0.00001, so use $0.031250); APVC = $0.03125 × 10,000,000 = $312,500; tax = $400 (minimum, because $312,500 is a portion of one $1,000,000 unit). Plus the $50 annual report fee: **$450** due March 1.
- *Pitfall:* authorizing far more shares than needed (for example, 100,000,000) raises the APVC tax as the company's assets grow, because APVC multiplies by authorized shares. Authorize what the cap table plus a reasonable plan reserve requires; increase later by amendment.
- *Blank-check preferred* adds authorized shares and therefore franchise tax under both methods. Most startups do not authorize preferred at formation; the first priced round restates the charter.
- *Annual obligations:* annual franchise tax report and payment due **March 1** each year (§ 502(a)); $50 annual report fee for non-exempt domestic corporations (§ 391(a)(18)); $200 penalty for a late report (§ 502(c)) plus interest at 1.5% per month on unpaid tax (§ 504(c)); the charter becomes void if tax is unpaid or the report unfiled for one year (§ 510); no certificate of good standing issues while tax or report is outstanding (§ 502(f)). First-year tax is prorated (§ 503(d)).

**Filing mechanics and current fees (Division fee schedule revised August 1, 2026).**
- Minimum fee to file a stock corporation's certificate of incorporation: **$109** for a one-page filing; add $9 for each additional page. The Division's form notes the authorized stock affects the fee: the § 391(a)(1) filing tax is computed on authorized capital (for par stock, each $100 of aggregate par counts as one assessable share; minimum $15). For 10,000,000 shares at $0.00001 par, aggregate par is $100, one assessable share, so the $15 minimum applies and the total remains the $109 minimum for one page. No-par stock is taxed per share and costs more.
- Certified copy: $50 plus $2 per page (§ 391(a)(10)). Get one; banks and foreign-qualification filings often require it.
- Expedited service for an incorporation (fee schedule): 24-hour $50; same day $100; 2-hour $500; 1-hour $1,000. HB 400 (85 Del. Laws c. 273, effective August 1, 2026) raised the statutory **caps** on expedited fees (§ 391(h)); the Division's schedule, not the caps, sets the actual charge.
- Name reservation: $75 for 120 days, renewable (§ 102(e); fee schedule).
- Submission: through a registered agent (most common) or directly via the Division's Document Filing and Certificate Request Service (eCorp) or by mail (corp.delaware.gov/howtoform). The filing date is the date and time of delivery unless a later time (not more than 90 days after filing) is specified (§ 103(c)(3), (d)). Errors are fixed by a certificate of correction (§ 103(f)).
- The incorporator's signature alone is the acknowledgment; it affirms under penalty of perjury that the facts stated are true (§ 103(b)(2)). Facsimile and electronic signatures are accepted (§ 103(h)).

> **Verify:** Check the Division's current fee schedule and the registered agent's invoice on the filing date. The fee schedule is revised each August; HB 400 changed several Title 8 fees effective August 1, 2026, though not the incorporation minimum, franchise tax rates, minimums, maximums, or the $50 annual report fee.

**Name availability.** Search the Division's entity database (or ask the registered agent to run a name check). The name must be distinguishable from every existing Delaware entity name (§ 102(a)(1)(ii)), may not contain "bank" or "trust" except as permitted, and a name the Division considers that of a natural person needs care. Delaware availability says nothing about trademark rights: run a USPTO and common-law trademark search and check availability in the headquarters state, where the company must qualify under that name or an assumed name.

**Registered agent (§§ 131–132).** Every corporation must maintain a registered agent in Delaware: the corporation itself, a Delaware-resident individual, or a domestic or authorized foreign entity (§ 132(a)). The agent must maintain a Delaware business office generally open (or, for an individual, be generally present at a designated Delaware location) at sufficiently frequent times to accept service, forward process and annual report notices, and comply with the Secretary's identity-verification regulations (§ 132(b)(1)). Since 2025, an agent may not operate solely through a virtual office or mail-forwarding service (§ 132(b)(2)). Commercial agents (more than 50 entities) must hold a Delaware business license and be present during normal business hours (§ 132(c)). The corporation must give its agent a natural-person contact (§ 132(d)). If the agent resigns without a successor and the corporation does not designate a new agent within 30 days, the charter is forfeited (§ 136). Use a reputable commercial agent; the registered office address goes in Article II.

**Public benefit corporation option (§§ 362–368).** To form a PBC, (1) identify one or more specific public benefits within the purpose clause, and (2) state in the heading that it is a public benefit corporation (§ 362(a)). The name may (but need not) include "public benefit corporation," "P.B.C." or "PBC"; if it does not, the corporation must give notice of PBC status to each person to whom it issues stock, unless the offering is registered or the company has a registered class (§ 362(c)). Stock certificates and § 151(f) notices must note PBC status conspicuously (§ 364); every notice of a stockholder meeting must say so (§ 366(a)); and the board must provide stockholders a statement on the public benefit at least biennially (§ 366(b)). Directors must balance stockholders' pecuniary interests, the interests of those materially affected, and the specified public benefit (§ 365). Since 2020, conversion to or from PBC status requires only the ordinary charter-amendment vote and no longer triggers appraisal rights for conversion into a PBC. Investors increasingly accept PBCs, but confirm with the lead investor before choosing.

**Key decisions and market positions.**

| Issue | Choices | Market / this form's default |
|---|---|---|
| Initial directors | Named in charter (incorporator's powers end on filing) or elected by incorporator | Elected by incorporator (keeps directors' addresses off the public record and allows the action-of-sole-incorporator flow) |
| Authorized shares | Common only, or common plus blank-check preferred | Common only (Alternative A) |
| § 242(b)(2) opt-out | Include or omit | Include for Common (Optional); harmless to § 242(d) after the 2026 amendment |
| Officer exculpation | Include or omit | Include; investors and officers expect it; note derivative-claim limit |
| Forum selection | Charter, bylaws, or none | Charter: Chancery for internal claims, federal courts for Securities Act claims |
| Corporate opportunity renunciation | Include for non-employee directors and their funds, or omit | Optional; investors usually request it at the priced round |

**Common mistakes this form avoids.**
- Filing a charter with authorized shares far above need, producing large franchise tax notices.
- Omitting the written-ballot waiver, so that stockholder elections technically require ballots.
- Omitting the board's power to amend bylaws, so every bylaw change needs a stockholder vote once stock is paid for.
- Naming initial directors in the charter and also having the incorporator "elect" directors — two inconsistent sources of authority. Choose one (this form's Alternative in Article XII).
- Exculpation for officers drafted without the derivative-claim limit, which the statute does not permit (§ 102(b)(7)(v)).
- Fee-shifting provisions, which § 102(f) prohibits (and, since 2025, for any claim a stockholder brings as a stockholder, not only internal corporate claims).

## ARTICLE II — REGISTERED OFFICE AND AGENT

The address of the Corporation's registered office in the State of Delaware is {{registered_agent_address}}. The name of its registered agent at that address is {{registered_agent_name}}.

## ARTICLE III — PURPOSE

The purpose of the Corporation is to engage in any lawful act or activity for which corporations may be organized under the General Corporation Law of the State of Delaware, as it now exists or may later be amended (the "**DGCL**").

[[Optional: The Corporation is a public benefit corporation under Subchapter XV of the DGCL. The specific public benefit to be promoted by the Corporation is {{public_benefit}}.]]

## ARTICLE IV — CAPITAL STOCK

[[Alternative A: **4.1 Authorized Stock.** The total number of shares of all classes of stock that the Corporation is authorized to issue is {{authorized_common_shares}} shares of Common Stock, par value {{par_value}} per share ("**Common Stock**").

**4.2 Voting.** Each share of Common Stock carries one (1) vote on every matter submitted to the stockholders generally.

**4.3 Dividends and Liquidation.** Subject to applicable law, the Board of Directors of the Corporation (the "**Board**") may declare dividends on the Common Stock from time to time, and the holders of Common Stock share in each such dividend in proportion to their holdings. If the Corporation is liquidated, dissolved or wound up, the holders of Common Stock share ratably in whatever assets remain available for distribution to stockholders.]]

[[Alternative B: **4.1 Authorized Stock.** The total number of shares of all classes of stock that the Corporation is authorized to issue is {{total_authorized_shares}} shares, consisting of (a) {{authorized_common_shares}} shares of Common Stock, par value {{par_value}} per share ("**Common Stock**"), and (b) {{authorized_preferred_shares}} shares of Preferred Stock, par value {{par_value}} per share ("**Preferred Stock**").

**4.2 Common Stock.**

(a) *Voting.* Each share of Common Stock carries one (1) vote on every matter submitted to the stockholders generally. Unless the DGCL requires otherwise, however, the holders of Common Stock have no vote, in that capacity, on an amendment to this Certificate of Incorporation (including a certificate of designations) whose only effect is on the terms of one or more outstanding series of Preferred Stock, so long as the holders of each series so affected have a vote on that amendment, alone or with holders of other series, under this Certificate of Incorporation or the DGCL.

(b) *Dividends and liquidation.* Subject to the rights of any outstanding Preferred Stock, the Board of Directors of the Corporation (the "**Board**") may declare dividends on the Common Stock from time to time, which the holders of Common Stock share in proportion to their holdings; and if the Corporation is liquidated, dissolved or wound up, the holders of Common Stock share ratably in the assets remaining for distribution to them.

**4.3 Preferred Stock.** The Board may, without further stockholder action and to the extent the law allows, create one or more series of Preferred Stock out of the unissued Preferred Stock and issue shares of any such series. For each series, the Board shall determine by resolution, and set out in a certificate of designations filed under the DGCL, the number of shares, the designation, the voting powers (which may be full, limited or none), the preferences, the relative, participating, optional or other special rights, and any qualifications, limitations or restrictions. After a series is designated, the Board may change its number of shares as Section 151(g) of the DGCL permits, but not above the total authorized Preferred Stock or below the number of shares of that series then outstanding.

> **Drafting note:** Blank-check preferred at formation is uncommon for venture-backed startups: the lead investor in a priced round will require an amended and restated charter with negotiated preferred terms, and authorized-but-unissued preferred adds franchise tax. It can help a company that expects a quick structured seed round led by a strategic investor without a full restatement, or a company that wants to issue preferred to a parent. If chosen, the investor-protective provisions of a later round will typically prohibit the Board from designating senior or pari passu series without investor consent.]]

**4.4 Changes in Authorized Shares.** [[Optional: An increase or decrease in the number of authorized shares of Common Stock (but not below the number then outstanding) requires only the approval of the holders of a majority of the voting power of all stock of the Corporation entitled to vote. Under the last sentence of Section 242(b)(2) of the DGCL, the holders of Common Stock have no separate class vote on such a change.]]

[[Optional: The same rule governs the authorized Preferred Stock: its number may be increased or decreased (but not below the number then outstanding) with the approval of the holders of a majority of the voting power of all stock of the Corporation entitled to vote, and, under the last sentence of Section 242(b)(2) of the DGCL, neither the Preferred Stock nor any series of it has a separate vote on that change unless a certificate of designations expressly provides one.]]

[[Optional: The Corporation shall not be governed by Section 242(d)(1) or Section 242(d)(2) of the DGCL.]]

> **Drafting note:** The first optional paragraph is the standard § 242(b)(2) opt-out for Common Stock; the second applies the same rule to Preferred Stock and belongs only with Alternative B of Section 4.1. The opt-out must be in the original charter (or an amendment adopted before any shares of the class are issued, or approved by a majority of the class) to be effective. Without it, once Preferred Stock is outstanding, the holders of Common Stock voting as a separate class could block an increase in authorized Common Stock needed for a financing, the conversion reserve, or an option-pool increase; investors expect the opt-out, and the NVCA-style restated charter carries one. After the 2026 amendment to § 242(d), this opt-out does not by itself switch off § 242(d). The third optional sentence is an express § 242(d) opt-out; include it only if the founders want every forward split and every authorized-share change to require a full stockholder vote. Almost no startup needs it. When the investors' restated charter is prepared, carry forward whichever choice is made here.

## ARTICLE V — BOARD OF DIRECTORS; BYLAWS; ELECTIONS

**5.1 Management.** The business and affairs of the Corporation shall be managed by or under the direction of the Board.

**5.2 Number of Directors.** The bylaws of the Corporation (the "**Bylaws**") set the number of directors that makes up the entire Board, or the method for setting it, subject to any right of a series of Preferred Stock to elect directors.

**5.3 Written Ballot.** Elections of directors need not be by written ballot unless the Bylaws so provide.

**5.4 Bylaws.** As Section 109(a) of the DGCL permits, the Board may adopt, amend or repeal the Bylaws. This grant does not limit the stockholders' own power to adopt, amend or repeal the Bylaws as provided in the Bylaws and the DGCL.

## ARTICLE VI — STOCKHOLDER ACTION BY WRITTEN CONSENT

Any action required or permitted to be taken by the stockholders of the Corporation may be taken without a meeting, without prior notice and without a vote, by written consent in accordance with Section 228 of the DGCL.

> **Drafting note:** Written consent is the statutory default (§ 228(a)); this Article restates it so that no reader of the charter wonders whether it has been restricted. Public-company charters commonly prohibit stockholder action by written consent. Never do that in a private venture-backed company: financings, option-pool increases and annual director elections are all done by consent.

## ARTICLE VII — LIMITATION OF LIABILITY OF DIRECTORS AND OFFICERS

**7.1 Exculpation.** A director or officer of the Corporation has no personal liability to the Corporation or its stockholders for monetary damages for breach of fiduciary duty in that capacity, to the fullest extent the DGCL permits. This Section 7.1 does not reach any liability that Section 102(b)(7) of the DGCL does not allow a certificate of incorporation to eliminate, which, as of the filing of this Certificate of Incorporation, is: (a) the liability of a director or officer for breaching the duty of loyalty to the Corporation or its stockholders; (b) the liability of a director or officer for acts or omissions not in good faith or involving intentional misconduct or a knowing violation of law; (c) the liability of a director under Section 174 of the DGCL; (d) the liability of a director or officer for a transaction from which that person derived an improper personal benefit; and (e) the liability of an officer in an action by or in the right of the Corporation. Any later amendment to the DGCL that widens the protection a certificate of incorporation may give directors or officers applies under this Section 7.1 automatically, without further amendment of this Certificate of Incorporation.

**7.2 Officer.** For purposes of this Article VII, "**officer**" has the meaning given in Section 102(b)(7) of the DGCL, as it may be amended (which, as of the filing of this Certificate of Incorporation, refers to a person who at the time of an act or omission as to which liability is asserted is deemed to have consented to service of process by delivery to the Corporation's registered agent under 10 Del. C. § 3114(b)).

**7.3 No Retroactive Effect.** A director or officer keeps the full protection of this Article VII for any act or omission that occurred before this Article VII is amended, repealed or eliminated, or before an inconsistent provision is added to this Certificate of Incorporation; no such change applies to that earlier conduct.

## ARTICLE VIII — INDEMNIFICATION AND ADVANCEMENT

**8.1 Authority.** The Corporation may indemnify, and advance expenses to, its directors, officers, employees and agents, and any other person the DGCL allows it to indemnify, by bylaw, agreement, vote of stockholders or disinterested directors, or otherwise. That indemnification and advancement may go beyond what Section 145 of the DGCL expressly provides, limited only by Delaware statutes and case law governing actions for breach of duty to the Corporation, its stockholders and others.

**8.2 Directors and Officers.** Present and former directors and officers of the Corporation are entitled to indemnification and to advancement of expenses to the fullest extent the DGCL permits, on the terms and subject to the procedures set out in the Bylaws and in any indemnification agreement that person has with the Corporation.

**8.3 No Retroactive Effect.** No amendment, repeal or modification of this Article VIII reduces any person's rights under it for an act or omission that occurred before the change, no matter when a proceeding about that act or omission begins.

> **Drafting note:** Section 145 itself does not require a charter provision; the mandatory indemnification and advancement mechanics live in Article VI of the Bylaws and in individual indemnification agreements. Article VIII anchors those rights in the charter (harder to amend) and authorizes indemnification beyond § 145's permissive grants, to the extent Delaware public policy allows. Section 145(f) separately protects bylaw and charter rights from elimination after the relevant act or omission.

## ARTICLE IX — FORUM SELECTION

**9.1 Delaware Courts.** To the fullest extent the law permits, the Court of Chancery of the State of Delaware is the sole and exclusive forum for each of the following, unless the Corporation agrees in writing to another forum: (a) a derivative suit on the Corporation's behalf; (b) a claim that a current or former director, officer, stockholder or employee of the Corporation breached a fiduciary duty owed to the Corporation or its stockholders; (c) a claim against the Corporation or any of its current or former directors, officers or stockholders under the DGCL, this Certificate of Incorporation or the Bylaws, as each may be amended; (d) a suit to interpret, apply or enforce, or to determine the validity of, this Certificate of Incorporation or the Bylaws; and (e) any other suit asserting an internal corporate claim, as Section 115 of the DGCL defines that term. If the Court of Chancery lacks subject matter jurisdiction over such a suit, the exclusive forum is another state court located in Delaware or, if no such court has jurisdiction, the United States District Court for the District of Delaware.

[[Optional: **9.2 Federal Forum for Securities Act Claims.** To the fullest extent the law permits, any complaint that asserts a cause of action under the Securities Act of 1933, as amended, must be brought exclusively in the federal district courts of the United States, unless the Corporation agrees in writing to another forum.]]

**9.3 Notice and Consent; Severability.** Every person or entity that buys, otherwise acquires, or holds an interest in shares of the Corporation's capital stock is deemed to have notice of, and to have consented to, this Article IX. A holding that any part of this Article IX is invalid, illegal or unenforceable in a particular application does not affect that part in its other applications or any other part of this Article IX.

> **Drafting note:** § 115(a) authorizes requiring internal corporate claims to be brought only in Delaware courts and forbids any provision that prohibits bringing them in Delaware. The federal-forum provision in Section 9.2 is facially valid under Delaware law (*Salzberg v. Sciabacucchi*, 227 A.3d 102 (Del. 2020)); it matters chiefly for a future IPO, because Securities Act claims may otherwise be brought in state court. It is optional at formation but costs nothing. Section 115(c), added in 2025, also permits a charter or bylaw to require stockholders to bring non-internal claims relating to the corporation's business or affairs in prescribed forums, provided a Delaware court with jurisdiction remains available; this form does not use it, and a broader clause should be drafted with litigation counsel. Do not add fee-shifting (§ 102(f)). Note that § 115 does not apply to claims under the Securities Exchange Act of 1934, which are within exclusive federal jurisdiction.

**10.1 Definitions.** A "**Covered Person**" is (a) each director of the Corporation who is not an employee of the Corporation or of a subsidiary, and (b) each stockholder of the Corporation, and each partner, member, director, stockholder, employee, affiliate or agent of a stockholder, in each case other than an individual employed by the Corporation or a subsidiary. > **Drafting note:** § 122(17) allows a corporation to renounce particular opportunities, or particular classes or categories of them. Venture investors (and their designated directors) sit on many boards in overlapping markets and usually request this renunciation in the restated charter at their first priced round. At formation it is optional. It should never cover founders or employees: the Corporation needs their opportunities.
