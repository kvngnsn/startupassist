## About this template

**What it does.** Bylaws are the corporation's internal operating rules: how stockholders and directors meet, vote, and act by consent; which officers exist and what they may do; who is indemnified; how stock is issued, recorded, and transferred. Under 8 Del. C. § 109(b) bylaws may contain any provision "not inconsistent with law or with the certificate of incorporation." The certificate of incorporation (the "charter") controls over the bylaws, and stockholder agreements (voting agreement, investors' rights agreement) govern their parties by contract on top of both.

**Who adopts them and when — sequencing matters.**
- Under § 109(a), before the corporation receives payment for any stock, the original bylaws may be adopted by the incorporator, by initial directors named in the charter, or by the board. Once any payment for stock is received, the power to amend lies with the stockholders unless the charter also confers it on the board (the companion charter form does, in its Article on bylaws).
- **Adopt the bylaws before any stock is issued.** Two reasons. First, § 109(a) as just described. Second, § 202(b): a transfer restriction imposed by the bylaws (such as the right of first refusal in Section 7.6) does not bind shares issued before the restriction was adopted unless the holder is a party to an agreement or voted for it. If founder shares go out first and the bylaws come later, the right of first refusal is unenforceable against those founder shares absent a separate signed agreement. The action-of-sole-incorporator form adopts these bylaws; the initial-board-consent form only ratifies them (or adopts them in the alternative, before any stock issues).
- The Secretary signs the Certificate of Adoption at the end. Keep a signed copy in the minute book.

**Statutory checkpoints this form satisfies.**
- Stockholder meetings: place and remote communication (§ 211(a)); annual meeting or election by consent in lieu (§ 211(b)); special meetings called by the board or persons authorized in the charter or bylaws (§ 211(d)); notice 10–60 days with required content (§ 222(a)–(b)); adjournment notice rules (§ 222(c)); record dates (§ 213(a)–(c)); quorum floor of one-third (§ 216); proxies valid three years unless longer (§ 212(b)); stockholder list prepared by the tenth day before the meeting, open for ten days ending the day before the meeting (§ 219(a)); inspectors optional unless the company is listed or has more than 2,000 holders of record (§ 231(e)).
- Written consent: action by holders of the minimum votes needed at a meeting (§ 228(a)); 60-day aggregation window and future-effective consents (§ 228(c)); delivery methods, including to a designated information processing system (§ 228(d)); prompt notice to non-consenting holders (§ 228(e)).
- Board: number fixed by or in the manner provided in the bylaws unless fixed in the charter (§ 141(b)); quorum not less than one-third (§ 141(b)); committees and subcommittees (§ 141(c)(2)–(4)); consent in lieu of meeting with future effectiveness up to 60 days (§ 141(f)); remote participation (§ 141(i)); removal with or without cause by a majority (§ 141(k)); vacancies (§ 223).
- Officers: titles and duties set by bylaws or board resolution; one officer records minutes; one person may hold several offices (§ 142(a)).
- Indemnification: mandatory indemnification and advancement for directors and officers to the fullest extent permitted, permissive for employees and agents (§ 145(a)–(e)); non-exclusivity and protection against retroactive elimination (§ 145(f)); insurance (§ 145(g)); Court of Chancery exclusive jurisdiction for advancement and indemnification suits (§ 145(k)).
- Stock: uncertificated shares by board resolution (§ 158); written notice to holders of uncertificated shares within a reasonable time after issuance or transfer containing the information that would appear on a certificate (§ 151(f)); transfer restrictions enforceable only if noted conspicuously on the certificate or included in the § 151(f) notice, or against persons with actual knowledge (§ 202(a)); lost certificates (§ 167).
- Notice by electronic mail without prior consent unless the stockholder objects, with a prominent legend (§ 232(a)); other electronic transmission requires consent (§ 232(b)).
- Interested-party transactions: the 2025 amendments to § 144 (85 Del. Laws c. 6) replaced the old voidability rule with safe harbors that protect directors, officers and controlling stockholders from equitable relief and damages when the transaction is approved by disinterested directors (or a committee of at least two disinterested directors if a majority of the board is not disinterested), by a majority of votes cast by disinterested stockholders, or is fair. Section 3.15 tracks this.
- Fee-shifting: § 109(b) (as amended in 2025) bars bylaws that impose attorneys' fees on stockholders for internal corporate claims or any other claim a stockholder brings as a stockholder. This form contains no fee-shifting provision. Do not add one.

**Coordination with other documents.**
- The charter controls on: written consent (do not restrict it in the charter if this form relies on it), number of directors (if the charter fixes the number, Section 3.2 must defer), director class voting, protective provisions, and § 242(b)(2) opt-out.
- After a priced round, the NVCA-style Voting Agreement usually fixes board size and designation rights; Sections 3.2, 3.4 and 3.5 are drafted "subject to the Certificate of Incorporation and any Stockholder Agreement" so they defer.
- The NVCA Investors' Rights Agreement and most founder stock purchase agreements contain a market stand-off ("lock-up"). This form's right of first refusal (Section 7.6) terminates on an IPO and does not duplicate the lock-up.
- **Cross-reference name.** Other templates in this skill refer to the bylaw right of first refusal as "the right of first refusal set forth in Section 7.6 of the Bylaws." Keep that section number if you renumber anything.
- The investor right-of-first-refusal and co-sale agreement (priced-financing family) usually gives the Company a first right and the investors a secondary right over **founder** transfers; when both apply, the bylaws ROFR and the contractual ROFR should run concurrently with one notice. Section 7.6(i) so provides.

**Common mistakes this form avoids.**
- Issuing shares before bylaws exist (ROFR unenforceable; see § 202(b)).
- A quorum or vote rule inconsistent with § 141(b) or § 216 floors.
- Omitting the § 151(f) notice for uncertificated shares, which leaves the ROFR and repurchase legends unenforceable against holders without actual knowledge (§ 202(a)).
- Indemnification "to the extent permitted" with no mandatory language, no advancement, no suit right, and no fund-indemnitor coordination — investor directors will refuse to join the board on those terms.
- Inconsistent officer titles across documents. The officers named in the initial board consent must exist in Article V.
- Fee-shifting provisions barred by § 109(b).

**State-specific notes.** Delaware law governs internal affairs wherever the company operates. But California Corporations Code § 2115 can apply certain California corporate provisions (for example, cumulative voting and some stockholder inspection rights) to a non-listed foreign corporation if more than half of its business (by a property, payroll and sales factor test) is in California and more than half of its voting securities are held of record by California residents. Flag this for California-heavy companies.

> **Verify:** For any company likely to meet the Cal. Corp. Code § 2115 tests, confirm current application of § 2115 and the related case law on its constitutionality (Delaware courts have refused to apply it to Delaware corporations; *VantagePoint Venture Partners 1996 v. Examen, Inc.*, 871 A.2d 1108 (Del. 2005)).

---

# BYLAWS

# OF

# {{company_name}}

(a Delaware corporation)

Adopted {{bylaws_adoption_date}}

## ARTICLE I — CORPORATE OFFICES

**1.1 Registered Office.** The address of the registered office of {{company_name}} (the "**Corporation**") in the State of Delaware, and the name of its registered agent at that address, shall be as set forth in the Corporation's certificate of incorporation, as amended and restated from time to time and including any certificate of designations (the "**Certificate of Incorporation**"), or as most recently changed by the Board of Directors of the Corporation (the "**Board**") by a certificate filed with the Secretary of State of the State of Delaware.

**1.2 Other Offices.** The Corporation may have additional offices at any place or places, within or outside the State of Delaware, as the Board may from time to time establish or the business of the Corporation may require. The Board shall designate the Corporation's principal place of business.

## ARTICLE II — MEETINGS OF STOCKHOLDERS

**2.1 Place of Meetings.** Meetings of stockholders shall be held at any place, within or outside the State of Delaware, designated by the Board. The Board may, in its sole discretion, determine that a meeting of stockholders shall not be held at any place but shall instead be held solely by means of remote communication as authorized by Section 211(a)(2) of the General Corporation Law of the State of Delaware (the "**DGCL**") and Section 2.15. If the Board does not designate a place and does not determine that the meeting will be held solely by remote communication, the meeting shall be held at the Corporation's principal place of business.

**2.2 Annual Meeting.**

(a) Unless directors are elected by written consent in lieu of an annual meeting as permitted by Section 211(b) of the DGCL and Section 2.16, an annual meeting of stockholders shall be held each year for the election of directors on a date and at a time designated by the Board. Any other proper business may be transacted at the annual meeting.

(b) Stockholders may act by written consent to elect directors; however, if the consent is less than unanimous, the action may be taken in lieu of an annual meeting only if all of the directorships to which directors could be elected at an annual meeting held at the effective time of the action are vacant and are filled by the action.

(c) A failure to hold the annual meeting at the designated time, or to elect directors by consent in lieu of an annual meeting, does not affect otherwise valid corporate acts or work a forfeiture or dissolution of the Corporation. Directors then in office continue to serve until their successors are elected and qualified.

> **Drafting note:** Section 211(c) lets any stockholder or director ask the Court of Chancery to order a meeting if no annual meeting (or election by consent in lieu) occurs for 30 days after the designated date or, if no date is designated, for 13 months after the later of organization, the last annual meeting, or the last election by consent. Most private companies satisfy § 211(b) by an annual unanimous stockholder consent re-electing the board. Diarize it. A late election by consent does not retroactively validate acts of persons who were never validly elected; directors elected by the incorporator at formation hold office until their successors are elected and qualified (§ 141(b)), so there is no gap to "fix" with a backdated stockholder consent. If a gap or defect actually exists, use the § 204 ratification template in the governance family.

**2.3 Special Meetings.**

(a) A special meeting of the stockholders may be called at any time only by (i) the Board, (ii) the chairperson of the Board, (iii) the Chief Executive Officer, (iv) the President (if no Chief Executive Officer is then serving)[[Optional: , or (v) one or more stockholders holding shares in the aggregate entitled to cast not less than {{special_meeting_percent}} of the votes entitled to be cast on the matters proposed for the meeting]]. No other person may call a special meeting.

(b) The notice of a special meeting shall state the purpose or purposes for which the meeting is called. No business may be transacted at a special meeting other than the business described in the notice.

[[Optional: (c) If a special meeting is called by stockholders under clause (v) of Section 2.3(a), the request shall be in writing, signed by each requesting stockholder, delivered to the Secretary at the Corporation's principal place of business, and shall specify the time (not fewer than thirty-five (35) nor more than one hundred twenty (120) days after delivery of the request) and the general nature of the business proposed. Within ten (10) days after receipt of a valid request, the Board shall fix a record date and the Secretary shall cause notice to be given in accordance with Sections 2.4 and 8.1. If notice is not given within that period, the requesting stockholders may give the notice themselves. Nothing in this Section 2.3(c) limits the Board's power to fix the time of a meeting called by the Board.]]

> **Drafting note:** Holder-called special meetings are not required by Delaware law (§ 211(d)) and are uncommon in venture-backed private companies because the stockholders can act by written consent. Keep the optional right only if a founder or investor negotiated it. Never restrict written consent in the charter of a private company that relies on it.

**2.4 Notice of Stockholder Meetings.**

(a) Whenever stockholders are required or permitted to take action at a meeting, notice of the meeting shall be given in accordance with Section 8.1 not less than ten (10) nor more than sixty (60) days before the date of the meeting to each stockholder entitled to vote at the meeting as of the record date for determining stockholders entitled to notice of the meeting, except as otherwise required by the DGCL or the Certificate of Incorporation.

(b) The notice shall state (i) the place, if any, date and hour of the meeting; (ii) the means of remote communication, if any, by which stockholders and proxyholders may be deemed to be present in person and vote at the meeting; (iii) the record date for determining the stockholders entitled to vote at the meeting, if that date is different from the record date for determining stockholders entitled to notice; (iv) in the case of a special meeting, the purpose or purposes for which the meeting is called; and (v) any other information required by the DGCL (including, where an amendment of the Certificate of Incorporation is to be considered, the amendment in full or a brief summary of its effect as required by Section 242(b)(1) of the DGCL).

**2.5 Waiver of Notice.** Notice of a meeting need not be given to any stockholder who signs a waiver of notice or waives notice by electronic transmission, before or after the meeting. Attendance of a stockholder at a meeting, in person or by proxy, constitutes a waiver of notice of the meeting, except when the stockholder attends for the express purpose of objecting, at the beginning of the meeting, to the transaction of any business because the meeting is not lawfully called or convened. Neither the business to be transacted at, nor the purpose of, any meeting need be specified in a waiver of notice unless the Certificate of Incorporation requires it.

**2.6 Quorum.**

(a) Except as otherwise provided by the DGCL or the Certificate of Incorporation, the holders of a majority in voting power of the shares of capital stock issued and outstanding and entitled to vote at the meeting, present in person, present by means of remote communication in a manner authorized by the Board, or represented by proxy, shall constitute a quorum for the transaction of business. Where a separate vote by a class or series, or by classes or series voting together, is required, the holders of a majority in voting power of the outstanding shares of that class or series, or those classes or series, present in person, by remote communication, or by proxy, shall constitute a quorum entitled to take action with respect to that vote.

(b) Once a quorum is present to organize a meeting, it is not broken by the subsequent withdrawal of any stockholder.

(c) If a quorum is not present or represented at any meeting, then either the person presiding over the meeting or the holders of a majority in voting power of the shares present or represented may adjourn the meeting from time to time in the manner provided in Section 2.7 until a quorum is present or represented.

**2.7 Adjourned Meetings; Notice.**

(a) Any meeting of stockholders, annual or special, may be adjourned from time to time to reconvene at the same or another place, if any. Notice need not be given of the adjourned meeting (including an adjournment taken to address a technical failure to convene or continue a meeting using remote communication) if the time, place, if any, and means of remote communication, if any, of the adjourned meeting are (i) announced at the meeting at which the adjournment is taken, (ii) displayed, during the time scheduled for the meeting, on the same electronic network used to enable stockholders and proxyholders to participate by remote communication, or (iii) set forth in the notice of meeting given in accordance with Section 2.4.

(b) At the adjourned meeting the Corporation may transact any business that might have been transacted at the original meeting.

(c) If the adjournment is for more than thirty (30) days, notice of the adjourned meeting shall be given to each stockholder of record entitled to vote at the meeting. If after the adjournment a new record date for stockholders entitled to vote is fixed for the adjourned meeting, the Board shall fix a new record date for notice of the adjourned meeting in accordance with Section 2.11(a) and shall give notice of the adjourned meeting to each stockholder of record entitled to vote at the adjourned meeting as of the record date fixed for notice.

**2.8 Conduct of Business.**

(a) Meetings of stockholders shall be presided over by the chairperson of the Board or, in that person's absence, by the Chief Executive Officer, or in that person's absence, by the President, or in the absence of all of them, by a person designated by the Board or, failing such designation, by a chairperson chosen at the meeting by the holders of a majority in voting power of the shares present or represented. The Secretary, or in the Secretary's absence an Assistant Secretary or any person the presiding person appoints, shall act as secretary of the meeting and keep the minutes.

(b) The Board may adopt rules and procedures for the conduct of stockholder meetings. Except to the extent inconsistent with those rules, the person presiding over the meeting may prescribe rules and procedures and take all actions that, in that person's judgment, are appropriate for the proper conduct of the meeting, including (i) establishing an agenda or order of business; (ii) maintaining order and safety; (iii) limiting attendance to stockholders of record, their duly authorized proxies, and other persons the presiding person permits; (iv) restricting entry after the time fixed for commencement; (v) limiting the time allotted to questions or comments; (vi) fixing the opening and closing of the polls for each matter to be voted on; and (vii) adjourning or recessing the meeting, whether or not a quorum is present.

(c) Unless and only to the extent determined by the Board or the presiding person, meetings of stockholders need not be held in accordance with any rules of parliamentary procedure.

**2.9 Voting.**

(a) *Voting power.* Except as otherwise provided in the Certificate of Incorporation and subject to Section 2.11, each stockholder is entitled to one (1) vote for each share of capital stock held of record by that stockholder that has voting power on the matter in question.

(b) *Required vote.* Except as otherwise required by the DGCL, the Certificate of Incorporation, these Bylaws, or the rules of any stock exchange applicable to the Corporation, (i) directors shall be elected by a plurality of the votes of the shares present in person, by remote communication, or by proxy at the meeting and entitled to vote on the election of directors; and (ii) every other matter shall be decided by the affirmative vote of the holders of a majority in voting power of the shares present in person, by remote communication, or by proxy at the meeting and entitled to vote on the subject matter. Where a separate vote by a class or series is required, the affirmative vote of the holders of a majority in voting power of the shares of that class or series present in person, by remote communication, or by proxy at the meeting shall be the act of that class or series, except in the election of directors, which shall be by plurality.

(c) *Ballots.* Elections of directors need not be by written ballot to the extent the Certificate of Incorporation so provides (Section 211(e) of the DGCL makes written ballots the default unless the Certificate of Incorporation provides otherwise). If authorized by the Board, any requirement of a written ballot is satisfied by a ballot submitted by electronic transmission that sets forth or is submitted with information from which it can be determined that the electronic transmission was authorized by the stockholder or proxyholder.

(d) *No cumulative voting.* Stockholders do not have the right to cumulate their votes in the election of directors unless the Certificate of Incorporation so provides.

**2.10 Stockholder Action by Written Consent Without a Meeting.** Unless otherwise provided in the Certificate of Incorporation, any action required or permitted to be taken at an annual or special meeting of stockholders may be taken without a meeting, without prior notice and without a vote, in accordance with Section 2.16.

**2.11 Record Dates.**

(a) *Meetings.* So that the Corporation may determine the stockholders entitled to notice of any meeting of stockholders or any adjournment, the Board may fix a record date, which shall not precede the date on which the resolution fixing the record date is adopted and shall be not more than sixty (60) nor less than ten (10) days before the date of the meeting. If the Board so fixes a date, that date is also the record date for determining the stockholders entitled to vote at the meeting unless the Board determines, at the time it fixes the record date, that a later date on or before the date of the meeting will be the date for making that determination. If the Board does not fix a record date, the record date for determining stockholders entitled to notice of and to vote at a meeting is the close of business on the day next preceding the day on which notice is given or, if notice is waived, at the close of business on the day next preceding the day on which the meeting is held. A determination of stockholders of record entitled to notice of or to vote at a meeting applies to any adjournment, but the Board may fix a new record date for determining stockholders entitled to vote at the adjourned meeting, in which case it shall also fix as the record date for stockholders entitled to notice of the adjourned meeting the same or an earlier date.

(b) *Consents.* So that the Corporation may determine the stockholders entitled to consent to corporate action without a meeting, the Board may fix a record date, which shall not precede the date on which the resolution fixing the record date is adopted and shall be not more than ten (10) days after that date. If the Board does not fix a record date, (i) when no prior action of the Board is required by the DGCL, the record date is the first date on which a signed consent setting forth the action taken or proposed to be taken is delivered to the Corporation in accordance with Section 2.16(c); and (ii) when prior action of the Board is required by the DGCL, the record date is the close of business on the day on which the Board adopts the resolution taking that prior action.

(c) *Other actions.* So that the Corporation may determine the stockholders entitled to receive payment of any dividend or other distribution or allotment of rights, to exercise any rights in respect of any change, conversion or exchange of stock, or for any other lawful action, the Board may fix a record date, which shall not precede the date on which the resolution fixing the record date is adopted and shall be not more than sixty (60) days before that action. If the Board does not fix a record date, the record date for that purpose is the close of business on the day on which the Board adopts the resolution relating to it.

**2.12 Proxies.**

(a) Each stockholder entitled to vote at a meeting of stockholders or to express consent to corporate action without a meeting may authorize another person or persons to act for that stockholder by proxy, but no proxy may be voted or acted upon after three (3) years from its date unless the proxy provides for a longer period.

(b) A stockholder may grant a proxy by (i) a document signed by the stockholder or the stockholder's authorized officer, director, employee or agent; (ii) transmitting or authorizing the transmission of an electronic transmission to the person who will hold the proxy, or to a proxy solicitation firm, proxy support service organization, or like agent authorized by that person, if the transmission sets forth or is submitted with information from which it can be determined that it was authorized by the stockholder; or (iii) any other means permitted by Section 212(c) of the DGCL, including documentation, signature and delivery in accordance with Section 116 of the DGCL if the authorization sets forth, or is delivered with information enabling the Corporation to determine, the identity of the stockholder granting it.

(c) A proxy that states it is irrevocable is irrevocable if, and only as long as, it is coupled with an interest sufficient in law to support an irrevocable power, as provided in Section 212(e) of the DGCL. A stockholder may revoke any proxy that is not irrevocable by attending the meeting and voting in person or by delivering to the Secretary a written revocation or a later-dated proxy.

**2.13 List of Stockholders Entitled to Vote.** The Corporation shall prepare, no later than the tenth (10th) day before each meeting of stockholders, a complete list of the stockholders entitled to vote at the meeting (but if the record date for determining the stockholders entitled to vote is less than ten (10) days before the meeting date, the list shall reflect the stockholders entitled to vote as of the tenth (10th) day before the meeting date), arranged in alphabetical order and showing the address of each stockholder and the number of shares registered in the name of each stockholder. The Corporation is not required to include electronic mail addresses or other electronic contact information on the list. The list shall be open to the examination of any stockholder, for any purpose germane to the meeting, for a period of ten (10) days ending on the day before the meeting date (i) on a reasonably accessible electronic network, if the information required to gain access to the list is provided with the notice of the meeting, or (ii) during ordinary business hours at the Corporation's principal place of business. If the Corporation makes the list available on an electronic network, it may take reasonable steps to ensure that the information is available only to stockholders of the Corporation. The stock ledger is the only evidence as to who are the stockholders entitled to examine the list or to vote in person or by proxy at any meeting of stockholders.

**2.14 Inspectors of Election.** The Board may, and if required by Section 231 of the DGCL shall, appoint one or more inspectors of election, and may designate one or more alternate inspectors, to act at any meeting of stockholders and make a written report of it. If no inspector or alternate is able to act, the person presiding over the meeting may appoint one or more inspectors. Each inspector, before discharging the inspector's duties, shall take and sign an oath faithfully to execute those duties with strict impartiality and according to the best of the inspector's ability. Inspectors shall have the duties specified in Section 231(b) of the DGCL to the extent that section applies, and otherwise shall determine the number of shares outstanding and the voting power of each, the shares represented at the meeting, the existence of a quorum, and the validity of proxies and ballots; count all votes and ballots; determine and retain a record of the disposition of any challenges; and certify their determination of the number of shares represented and their count of all votes and ballots.

**2.15 Participation by Remote Communication.** If authorized by the Board in its sole discretion, and subject to any guidelines and procedures the Board adopts, stockholders and proxyholders not physically present at a meeting of stockholders may, by means of remote communication, (a) participate in the meeting and (b) be deemed present in person and vote at the meeting, whether the meeting is held at a designated place or solely by means of remote communication, provided that the Corporation shall (i) implement reasonable measures to verify that each person deemed present and permitted to vote by means of remote communication is a stockholder or proxyholder; (ii) implement reasonable measures to provide those stockholders and proxyholders a reasonable opportunity to participate in the meeting and to vote on matters submitted to the stockholders, including an opportunity to read or hear the proceedings substantially concurrently with the proceedings; and (iii) maintain a record of any vote or other action taken at the meeting by remote communication.

**2.16 Action by Written Consent — Procedure.**

(a) *Required consents.* Any action that may be taken at a meeting of stockholders may be taken without a meeting, without prior notice and without a vote, if a consent or consents, setting forth the action so taken, are signed by the holders of outstanding stock having not less than the minimum number of votes that would be necessary to authorize or take the action at a meeting at which all shares entitled to vote on the action were present and voted, and are delivered to the Corporation in the manner required by Section 2.16(c).

(b) *Form; timing; future effectiveness.* A consent shall be set forth in writing or in an electronic transmission, and may be documented and signed in any manner permitted by Section 116 of the DGCL. No consent is effective to take the corporate action referred to in it unless consents signed by a sufficient number of holders to take the action are delivered to the Corporation within sixty (60) days of the first date on which a consent is delivered to the Corporation. Any person executing a consent may provide, whether through instruction to an agent or otherwise, that the consent will be effective at a future time, including a time determined upon the happening of an event, occurring not later than sixty (60) days after the instruction is given or the provision is made, if evidence of the instruction or provision is provided to the Corporation. If the person is not a stockholder of record when the consent is executed, the consent is not valid unless that person is a stockholder of record as of the record date for determining stockholders entitled to consent to the action. Unless otherwise provided in the consent, a consent is revocable before it becomes effective.

(c) *Delivery.* A consent shall be delivered (i) to the Corporation's principal place of business; (ii) to an officer or agent of the Corporation having custody of the book in which proceedings of meetings of stockholders are recorded; (iii) to the Corporation's registered office in the State of Delaware by hand or by certified or registered mail, return receipt requested; or (iv) in accordance with Section 116 of the DGCL to an information processing system, if any, designated by the Corporation for receiving consents. A consent delivered under clause (iv) must set forth or be delivered with information that enables the Corporation to determine the date of delivery and the identity of the person giving the consent, and, if given by a proxy, must comply with Section 212(c)(2) and (3) of the DGCL. A consent delivered under clause (i), (ii) or (iii) must be reproduced and delivered in paper form. The Corporation designates the electronic mail address of the Secretary set forth in the Corporation's records, and any electronic signature or equity management platform the Board or the Secretary designates in writing, as information processing systems for receiving consents.

(d) *Effective date to be stated.* Each consent solicited by the Corporation shall state the date as of which the action is to be effective, which shall be a date on or after the date on which consents sufficient to take the action have been delivered (or, for a future-effective consent, the future time specified in accordance with Section 2.16(b)).

(e) *Prompt notice to non-consenting holders.* If action is taken by less than unanimous consent, prompt notice of the taking of the action shall be given to those stockholders as of the record date for the action who have not consented and who would have been entitled to notice of the meeting if the action had been taken at a meeting and the record date for notice of that meeting were the record date for the action by consent.

(f) *Certificates.* If the action consented to would have required the filing of a certificate under any section of the DGCL if it had been voted on at a meeting, the certificate filed shall state, in lieu of any statement required by that section concerning a vote of stockholders, that consent has been given in accordance with Section 228 of the DGCL.

(g) *Filing.* The Secretary shall file each consent, and evidence of each notice given under Section 2.16(e), with the minutes of proceedings of the stockholders.

> **Drafting note:** Section 2.16(d) addresses a recurring defect: consents that say they are "effective when signed by all parties" with no date. Section 228(c) measures the 60-day window from the first delivered consent, and § 213(b) fixes the record date by reference to the first delivery or the Board resolution, so a consent with no stated date makes the record date, the effective time, and (for a stockholder election) the directors' term all uncertain. Always state a definite effective date and collect the signatures before or on that date.

> **Drafting note:** Under § 228(e), prompt notice goes to non-consenting holders who would have been entitled to notice of a meeting. Common practice is to send it within ten days. For financings approved by majority consent, the notice usually goes out with an information statement; consider securities-law disclosure duties before relying on a bare notice.

**3.15 Interested Directors and Officers; Controlling Stockholder Transactions.**

(a) *Approval procedures.* Any act or transaction described in Section 144(a) of the DGCL in which one or more directors or officers has a relationship or interest (an "**Interested Transaction**") shall, unless the Board determines that it is fair to the Corporation and its stockholders and elects to rely on that standard, be submitted for approval as follows: (i) the material facts as to the director's or officer's relationship or interest, and as to the Interested Transaction (including any involvement of the director or officer in its initiation, negotiation or approval), shall be disclosed to or known by all members of the Board or the approving committee; and (ii) the Interested Transaction shall be authorized in good faith and without gross negligence by the affirmative votes of a majority of the disinterested directors then serving on the Board or the committee, even though the disinterested directors are less than a quorum; provided that if a majority of the directors are not disinterested with respect to the Interested Transaction, it shall be approved (or recommended for approval) by a committee of the Board consisting of two (2) or more directors, each of whom the Board has determined to be a disinterested director with respect to it. Alternatively, the Interested Transaction may be approved or ratified by an informed, uncoerced, affirmative vote of a majority of the votes cast by the disinterested stockholders.

(b) *Controlling stockholder transactions.* Any controlling stockholder transaction (as defined in Section 144(e)(3) of the DGCL) shall be considered by a committee of two (2) or more directors, each determined by the Board to be a disinterested director, to which the Board has expressly delegated authority to negotiate (or oversee the negotiation of) and to reject the transaction, or shall be conditioned by its terms on approval or ratification by a majority of the votes cast by disinterested stockholders, or both where Section 144(c) of the DGCL so requires for a going private transaction, unless the Board determines to rely on the transaction being fair to the Corporation and its stockholders.

(c) *Quorum; definitions; effect.* Interested directors may be counted in determining the presence of a quorum at a meeting that authorizes an Interested Transaction. Terms used in this Section 3.15 and defined in Section 144(e) of the DGCL have the meanings given there. This Section 3.15 is procedural only. It does not create any right in any person, does not invalidate any act or transaction that is otherwise valid, and does not limit any safe harbor, presumption or protection available under Section 144 of the DGCL or otherwise.

> **Drafting note:** The 2025 amendments (85 Del. Laws c. 6, effective on enactment in March 2025 and applying to acts and transactions whenever occurring, other than those subject to proceedings pending or completed on or before February 17, 2025) rewrote § 144. The old § 144 dealt with whether interested transactions were void or voidable; the new § 144 provides safe harbors against equitable relief and damages claims against directors, officers and controllers. For startups the practical points are: (1) founder-related transactions (founder loans, a founder-owned entity licensing IP, founder secondary sales) should be approved by disinterested directors after full disclosure; (2) if most of the board is interested — common on a two-founder board — the approval must come from a committee of at least two disinterested directors, which a two-person founder board does not have, so use disinterested stockholder approval or document fairness; (3) a person can be a "controlling stockholder" under § 144(e)(2)(c) with as little as one-third of the voting power plus managerial authority, which can describe a founder-CEO.

> **Verify:** Confirm the application provisions of 85 Del. Laws c. 6, § 3 (which acts and transactions, and which pending proceedings, the amended § 144 reaches) before advising on a pre-2025 transaction.

## ARTICLE V — OFFICERS

**5.1 Officers.** The officers of the Corporation shall be a Chief Executive Officer, a President, a Secretary, and a Chief Financial Officer or a Treasurer (or both). The Board may also appoint a Chief Operating Officer, a Chief Technology Officer, one or more Vice Presidents, one or more Assistant Secretaries and Assistant Treasurers, a Controller, and any other officers with titles and duties the Board determines. Any number of offices may be held by the same person. No officer need be a director or stockholder.

> **Drafting note:** § 142(a) requires the bylaws or a board resolution to provide officers sufficient to sign instruments under § 103(a)(2) and stock certificates under § 158 (two authorized officers), and requires one officer to record proceedings of stockholder and director meetings (the Secretary here). Each subordinate officer holds office for the period, has the authority, and performs the duties provided in these Bylaws or determined by the Board or the appointing officer. **5.4 Removal and Resignation of Officers.**

A resignation is effective on the date of receipt or at any later time specified in the notice. Any resignation is without prejudice to the rights, if any, of the Corporation under any contract to which the officer is a party.

**5.5 Vacancies in Offices.** Any vacancy occurring in any office of the Corporation shall be filled by the Board or as provided in Section 5.3.

**5.6 Chief Executive Officer.** Subject to the control of the Board and any supervisory powers the Board gives to the chairperson of the Board, the Chief Executive Officer has general supervision, direction and control of the business and affairs of the Corporation and its officers, and has the general powers and duties of management usually vested in the office of chief executive officer of a corporation, together with any other powers and duties the Board or these Bylaws prescribe. In the absence or disability of the chairperson of the Board (or if there is none), the Chief Executive Officer presides at meetings of the stockholders and, if a director, of the Board.

**5.7 President.** Subject to the powers of the Board and the Chief Executive Officer, the President has general supervision of the operations of the Corporation and the other powers and duties the Board or the Chief Executive Officer prescribes. If no Chief Executive Officer is serving, the President is the chief executive officer of the Corporation and has the powers and duties described in Section 5.6. The same person may serve as Chief Executive Officer and President.

**5.8 Vice Presidents.** In the absence or disability of the President, the Vice Presidents, in the order of rank the Board fixes or, if not ranked, a Vice President the Board designates, shall perform the duties of the President and, when so acting, have the powers of and be subject to the restrictions on the President. Each Vice President has the other powers and duties the Board, the Chief Executive Officer, the President, or these Bylaws prescribe.

**5.9 Secretary.**

(b) Each Assistant Secretary has the powers and duties of the Secretary when the Secretary is absent or unable to act, and the other powers and duties the Board or the Secretary prescribes.

**5.10 Chief Financial Officer.** The Chief Financial Officer is the principal financial officer of the Corporation and shall keep and maintain, or cause to be kept and maintained, adequate and correct books and records of accounts of the properties and business transactions of the Corporation, including accounts of its assets, liabilities, receipts, disbursements, gains, losses, capital, and retained earnings. ## ARTICLE VI — INDEMNIFICATION AND ADVANCEMENT OF EXPENSES

**6.1 Definitions.** For purposes of this Article VI:

(a) "**Covered Person**" means any person who is or was a director or officer of the Corporation, or who, while a director or officer of the Corporation, is or was serving at the request of the Corporation as a director, officer, manager, partner, trustee, employee, agent or fiduciary of another corporation, partnership, limited liability company, joint venture, trust, employee benefit plan, nonprofit entity, or other enterprise (each, an "**Other Enterprise**").

(b) "**officer**" means (i) any person appointed by the Board as an officer under Section 5.2, and (ii) any other person the Board expressly designates as an officer for purposes of this Article VI; it does not include a subordinate officer appointed under Section 5.3 by an officer unless the Board so designates.

(c) "**Proceeding**" means any threatened, pending or completed action, suit, arbitration, alternative dispute resolution mechanism, investigation, inquiry, administrative hearing, or other proceeding, whether civil, criminal, administrative, regulatory, legislative or investigative, and whether formal or informal, including any appeal.

(d) "**Expenses**" means all attorneys' fees, retainers, court costs, transcript costs, fees of experts and witnesses, travel expenses, duplicating and printing costs, costs of electronic discovery and data hosting, bond premiums, and all other disbursements or expenses of the types customarily incurred in connection with prosecuting, defending, preparing to prosecute or defend, investigating, being or preparing to be a witness in, or otherwise participating in, a Proceeding.

(e) "**Losses**" means Expenses, judgments, fines (including excise taxes assessed with respect to an employee benefit plan), penalties, and amounts paid in settlement.

**6.2 Mandatory Indemnification of Directors and Officers.**

(a) *Third-party Proceedings.* The Corporation shall indemnify, to the fullest extent permitted by the DGCL as it now exists or may later be amended (but, in the case of an amendment, only to the extent the amendment permits broader indemnification than before), any Covered Person who was or is a party or is threatened to be made a party to, or is otherwise involved in, any Proceeding (other than a Proceeding by or in the right of the Corporation) by reason of the fact that the person is or was a director or officer of the Corporation or is or was serving at the request of the Corporation in any capacity with an Other Enterprise, against all Losses actually and reasonably incurred by the person in connection with the Proceeding, if the person acted in good faith and in a manner the person reasonably believed to be in or not opposed to the best interests of the Corporation and, with respect to any criminal Proceeding, had no reasonable cause to believe the person's conduct was unlawful. The termination of any Proceeding by judgment, order, settlement, conviction, or upon a plea of *nolo contendere* or its equivalent does not, of itself, create a presumption that the person did not meet that standard.

(b) *Proceedings by or in the right of the Corporation.* The Corporation shall indemnify, to the fullest extent permitted by the DGCL, any Covered Person who was or is a party or is threatened to be made a party to any Proceeding by or in the right of the Corporation to procure a judgment in its favor by reason of the fact that the person is or was a director or officer of the Corporation or is or was serving at the request of the Corporation in any capacity with an Other Enterprise, against Expenses actually and reasonably incurred by the person in connection with the defense or settlement of the Proceeding, if the person acted in good faith and in a manner the person reasonably believed to be in or not opposed to the best interests of the Corporation; except that no indemnification shall be made with respect to any claim, issue or matter as to which the person has been adjudged liable to the Corporation unless and only to the extent that the Court of Chancery or the court in which the Proceeding was brought determines on application that, despite the adjudication of liability but in view of all the circumstances, the person is fairly and reasonably entitled to indemnity for the Expenses that court deems proper.

(c) *Successful defense.* To the extent that a present or former director or officer of the Corporation has been successful on the merits or otherwise in defense of any Proceeding described in Section 6.2(a) or 6.2(b), or in defense of any claim, issue or matter in it, the person shall be indemnified against Expenses actually and reasonably incurred in connection with it, whether or not the person would otherwise be entitled to indemnification under this Article VI.

(d) *Witness and other participation.* To the extent a Covered Person is, by reason of that person's status with the Corporation, a witness or otherwise asked to participate in any Proceeding to which the person is not a party, the Corporation shall indemnify the person against all Expenses actually and reasonably incurred in connection with it.

**6.3 Indemnification of Employees and Agents.** The Corporation may, to the extent authorized from time to time by the Board, indemnify and advance Expenses to any person who is or was an employee or agent of the Corporation (other than a Covered Person), or who is or was serving at the request of the Corporation as an employee or agent of an Other Enterprise, on the terms and subject to the conditions the Board determines, up to the fullest extent permitted by the DGCL.

**6.4 Advancement of Expenses.**

(a) The Corporation shall pay the Expenses actually and reasonably incurred by a Covered Person in defending, or participating as a witness in, any Proceeding described in Section 6.2 in advance of its final disposition, within thirty (30) days after the Corporation receives a written request for advancement accompanied by reasonable documentation of the Expenses (which may be redacted to preserve privilege) and, if required by the DGCL at the time, an undertaking by or on behalf of the Covered Person to repay the amounts advanced if it is ultimately determined by final judicial decision from which there is no further right to appeal that the person is not entitled to be indemnified for those Expenses under this Article VI or otherwise.

(b) The undertaking shall be an unlimited general obligation of the Covered Person. It need not be secured, shall bear no interest, and shall be accepted without reference to the person's ability to repay or financial condition.

(c) Advancement under this Section 6.4 is not subject to any determination that the Covered Person has met any standard of conduct, and the Corporation may not condition advancement on any such determination.

(d) Expenses incurred by former directors and officers in any capacity other than the defense of a Proceeding described in Section 6.2, and by employees and agents, may be advanced on the terms the Board deems appropriate.

> **Drafting note:** Under § 145(e), advancement for current directors and officers requires an undertaking to repay; it is otherwise permissive. Making it mandatory, prompt, unsecured and untested against ability to pay is the market position for venture-backed companies and what investor directors expect. Section 145(f) protects the right from later elimination after the act occurs, so Section 6.12 must not purport to let the Board take it back retroactively. Delaware courts treat advancement as distinct from indemnification and enforce it summarily under § 145(k) (*Homestore, Inc. v. Tafeen*, 888 A.2d 204 (Del. 2005)).

**6.5 Exceptions.** Notwithstanding anything to the contrary in this Article VI, the Corporation is not obligated to indemnify or advance Expenses to a Covered Person:

(a) in connection with any Proceeding (or part of any Proceeding) initiated by that person, including a counterclaim or cross-claim, unless (i) the Board authorized the Proceeding (or that part) before its initiation, (ii) the Proceeding is brought to enforce rights to indemnification or advancement under Section 6.7, or (iii) indemnification or advancement is otherwise required by applicable law;

(b) for an accounting or disgorgement of profits from the purchase and sale (or sale and purchase) by the person of securities of the Corporation under Section 16(b) of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934, as amended (the "**Exchange Act**"), or similar provisions of state law;

(c) for any reimbursement of the Corporation of any bonus or other incentive-based or equity-based compensation, or of profits realized from the sale of securities, required under Section 304 of the Sarbanes-Oxley Act of 2002, Section 10D of the Exchange Act and the rules thereunder, or any clawback policy of the Corporation adopted to comply with them;

(d) to the extent payment has actually been made to or on behalf of the person under any insurance policy or other indemnity provision, except for any amount in excess of the amount paid; or

(e) if prohibited by applicable law, as determined by a final judicial decision from which there is no further right to appeal.

**6.6 Determination of Entitlement.**

(a) Any indemnification under Section 6.2(a) or 6.2(b) (unless ordered by a court, and other than under Section 6.2(c)) shall be made by the Corporation only as authorized in the specific case upon a determination that indemnification is proper in the circumstances because the person has met the applicable standard of conduct. With respect to a person who is a director or officer at the time of the determination, the determination shall be made (i) by a majority vote of the directors who are not parties to the Proceeding, even though less than a quorum; (ii) by a committee of those directors designated by a majority vote of those directors, even though less than a quorum; (iii) if there are no such directors, or if those directors so direct, by independent legal counsel in a written opinion; or (iv) by the stockholders. With respect to former directors and officers, the determination shall be made by any person or persons having the authority to act on the matter on behalf of the Corporation.

(b) The determination shall be made within sixty (60) days after the Corporation receives a written request for indemnification with reasonable supporting documentation. If no determination is made within that period, the person is deemed to have met the applicable standard of conduct, to the fullest extent permitted by law.

(c) The person seeking indemnification is presumed to be entitled to it, and the Corporation has the burden of proving, by clear and convincing evidence, that the person has not met the applicable standard of conduct. Neither the failure of the Corporation to have made a determination before the commencement of an action under Section 6.7, nor an actual determination that the person has not met the applicable standard of conduct, creates a presumption that the person has not met it.

**6.7 Right of Claimant to Bring Suit.**

(a) If a claim for indemnification under this Article VI is not paid in full within sixty (60) days after the later of the Corporation's receipt of a written claim and the final disposition of the Proceeding, or a claim for advancement is not paid in full within thirty (30) days after the Corporation's receipt of a written request in accordance with Section 6.4, the claimant may at any time thereafter bring suit against the Corporation in the Court of Chancery of the State of Delaware to recover the unpaid amount.

(b) If the claimant is successful in whole or in part in any such suit, or in a suit brought by the Corporation to recover an advancement under the terms of an undertaking, the Corporation shall also pay the claimant the Expenses of prosecuting or defending that suit to the fullest extent permitted by law, which may be prorated in the case of partial success.

(c) In any suit to enforce a right to indemnification or advancement, or by the Corporation to recover an advancement, the burden of proving that the claimant is not entitled to indemnification or advancement is on the Corporation. In a suit by the claimant to enforce a right to advancement, it is not a defense that the claimant has not met any applicable standard of conduct.

> **Drafting note:** § 145(k) vests the Court of Chancery with exclusive jurisdiction over advancement and indemnification actions brought under § 145 or any bylaw or agreement, so the venue in Section 6.7(a) is mandatory, not a forum choice.

**6.8 Non-Exclusivity of Rights.** The rights to indemnification and advancement conferred by this Article VI are not exclusive of any other right that any person may have or acquire under any statute, the Certificate of Incorporation, these Bylaws, any agreement (including any indemnification agreement), any vote of stockholders or disinterested directors, or otherwise, both as to action in that person's official capacity and as to action in another capacity while holding office. The Corporation is specifically authorized to enter into individual indemnification agreements with any or all of its directors, officers, employees and agents that provide for indemnification and advancement to the fullest extent not prohibited by the DGCL.

**6.9 Insurance.** The Corporation may purchase and maintain insurance, at its expense, on behalf of any person who is or was a director, officer, employee or agent of the Corporation, or is or was serving at the request of the Corporation in any capacity with an Other Enterprise, against any liability asserted against the person and incurred in any such capacity, or arising out of the person's status as such, whether or not the Corporation would have the power to indemnify the person against that liability under the DGCL.

**6.10 Coordination with Other Indemnitors; Indemnitor of First Resort.**

(a) The Corporation acknowledges that certain Covered Persons who are directors designated by, or affiliated with, investors in the Corporation may have rights to indemnification, advancement of expenses, or insurance provided by those investors or their affiliates (collectively, the "**Fund Indemnitors**").

(b) The Corporation is the indemnitor of first resort with respect to each such Covered Person: its obligations to advance Expenses and to indemnify under this Article VI are primary, and any obligation of the Fund Indemnitors to advance or indemnify for the same Losses is secondary.

(c) The Corporation shall advance the full amount of Expenses and shall be liable for the full amount of all Losses to the extent legally permitted and required by this Article VI, without regard to any rights the Covered Person may have against the Fund Indemnitors.

(d) The Corporation irrevocably waives, relinquishes and releases the Fund Indemnitors from all claims for contribution, subrogation or any other recovery of any kind in respect of any amount advanced or paid by the Corporation under this Article VI.

(e) No advancement or payment by the Fund Indemnitors on behalf of a Covered Person relieves the Corporation of its obligations under this Article VI. To the extent of any such advancement or payment, the Fund Indemnitors are subrogated to all of the rights of recovery of the Covered Person against the Corporation, and the Corporation shall reimburse them.

(f) The Fund Indemnitors are express third-party beneficiaries of this Section 6.10.

> **Drafting note:** Indemnitor-of-first-resort language responds to *Levy v. HLI Operating Co.*, 924 A.2d 210 (Del. Investors require it, both here and in the individual indemnification agreement (see the indemnification-agreement template, Section 13). At formation, with no investor directors, it is harmless; including it now avoids a bylaw amendment at the first priced round.

**6.11 Nature of Rights; Vesting.** The rights conferred by this Article VI are contract rights. They vest when a person becomes a Covered Person, continue as to a person who has ceased to be a director or officer, and inure to the benefit of that person's heirs, executors, administrators and legal representatives.

**6.12 Amendment or Repeal.** Any amendment, repeal or modification of this Article VI, and the adoption of any provision of the Certificate of Incorporation inconsistent with it, is prospective only and does not eliminate, impair or otherwise adversely affect any right or protection of any person under this Article VI with respect to any act or omission occurring before that amendment, repeal, modification or adoption, regardless of when any Proceeding relating to that act or omission arises or is commenced.

**6.13 Successors.** For purposes of this Article VI, references to the Corporation include, in addition to the resulting corporation, any constituent corporation (including any constituent of a constituent) absorbed in a consolidation or merger that, if its separate existence had continued, would have had power and authority to indemnify its directors, officers, employees or agents, as provided in Section 145(h) of the DGCL. (a) *Methods.* Without limiting the manner by which notice may otherwise be given effectively to stockholders, any notice to stockholders given by the Corporation under the DGCL, the Certificate of Incorporation, or these Bylaws may be given in writing directed to the stockholder's mailing address, or by electronic mail directed to the stockholder's electronic mail address, in each case as it appears on the Corporation's records, and is given (i) if mailed, when deposited in the U.S. mail, postage prepaid; (ii) if delivered by courier service, the earlier of when the notice is received or left at the stockholder's address; or (iii) if given by electronic mail, when directed to the stockholder's electronic mail address, unless the stockholder has notified the Corporation in writing or by electronic transmission of an objection to receiving notice by electronic mail or the notice is prohibited by Section 8.1(d). A notice by electronic mail must include a prominent legend that the communication is an important notice regarding the Corporation.

(b) *Other electronic transmission.* Subject to Section 8.1(d), any notice to stockholders is also effective if given by a form of electronic transmission (other than electronic mail) consented to by the stockholder, and is given (i) if by facsimile telecommunication, when directed to a number at which the stockholder has consented to receive notice; (ii) if by posting on an electronic network together with separate notice to the stockholder of the specific posting, upon the later of the posting and the giving of the separate notice; and (iii) if by any other form of electronic transmission, when directed to the stockholder. A stockholder may revoke that consent by written notice or electronic transmission to the Corporation.

(c) *Attachments.* If a notice is given by mail or courier, each document enclosed with, annexed to, or appended to the notice is deemed part of the notice for purposes of determining whether notice was duly given. An electronic mail includes any files attached to it and any information hyperlinked to a website if the electronic mail includes the contact information of an officer or agent of the Corporation who is available to assist with accessing those files and that information.

(d) *Failed delivery.* Notice may not be given by an electronic transmission from and after the time that (i) the Corporation is unable to deliver by that electronic transmission two (2) consecutive notices, and (ii) that inability becomes known to the Secretary, an Assistant Secretary, the transfer agent, or another person responsible for giving notice; but the inadvertent failure to discover the inability does not invalidate any meeting or other action.

(e) *Affidavit.* An affidavit of the Secretary, an Assistant Secretary, the transfer agent, or another agent of the Corporation that notice has been given is, in the absence of fraud, prima facie evidence of the facts stated in it.

**8.2 Notice to Stockholders Sharing an Address.** Without limiting the manner by which notice may otherwise be given effectively, any notice to stockholders given by the Corporation is effective if given by a single written notice to stockholders who share an address, if consented to by the stockholders at that address to whom the notice is given, as provided in Section 233 of the DGCL. A stockholder that fails to object in writing to the Corporation within sixty (60) days after having been given written notice of the Corporation's intention to send a single notice is deemed to have consented. Any consent is revocable by written notice to the Corporation.

**8.3 Notice to Persons with Whom Communication Is Unlawful.** Whenever notice is required to be given to any person with whom communication is unlawful, the giving of notice to that person is not required, and there is no duty to apply to any governmental authority for a license or permit to give it. Any action or meeting taken or held without notice to that person has the same force and effect as if notice had been duly given.

**8.4 Notice to Directors.** Any notice to a director may be given by any method described in Section 3.8(b), and notice by electronic mail or other electronic transmission to a director is effective when directed to the electronic mail address or other electronic contact information the director has provided to the Secretary.

**8.5 Definitions.** For purposes of these Bylaws, "**electronic transmission**," "**electronic mail**," and "**electronic mail address**" have the meanings given in Section 232(d) of the DGCL.

> **Drafting note:** Since the 2020 amendments, § 232(a) lets a corporation give notice by email without prior consent unless the stockholder objects; other forms of electronic transmission still require consent (§ 232(b)). Keep stockholders' email addresses current in the stock ledger system. Notices required by §§ 164, 296, 311, 312 and 324 (calls on partly paid shares, receiverships, and similar) are carved out of most of § 232 by § 232(h).

## ARTICLE IX — EMERGENCY BYLAWS

**9.1 Emergency Bylaws.** This Article IX is operative during any emergency described in Section 110(a) of the DGCL, including an attack on the United States or on a locality in which the Corporation conducts its business or customarily holds meetings of the Board or the stockholders, a nuclear or atomic disaster, a catastrophe (including an epidemic or pandemic, and a declaration of a national emergency by the United States government), or other similar emergency condition, irrespective of whether a quorum of the Board or a standing committee can readily be convened (an "**Emergency**"), notwithstanding any different or conflicting provision in the preceding Articles of these Bylaws or in the Certificate of Incorporation.

**9.2 Meetings; Notice.** During any Emergency, a meeting of the Board or any committee may be called by any director or officer. Notice of the meeting may be given only to those directors whom it is feasible to reach at the time, and by any means then feasible, including publication, radio, or electronic transmission.

**9.3 Quorum.** At any meeting of the Board called in accordance with Section 9.2, the director or directors in attendance constitute a quorum. To the extent required to constitute a quorum, the officers present shall be deemed directors for the meeting, in order of rank and, within the same rank, in order of seniority, unless the Board has approved a different list or order of priority before the Emergency.

**9.4 Lines of Succession; Offices.** The Board, before or during an Emergency, may provide, and from time to time modify, lines of succession in the event that during the Emergency any or all officers or agents of the Corporation are for any reason rendered incapable of discharging their duties, and may, effective in the Emergency, change the principal office or designate alternative principal or regional offices, or authorize the officers to do so.

**9.5 Stockholder Meetings.** During any Emergency, the Board (or, if a quorum cannot readily be convened, a majority of the directors present) may take any action it determines to be practical and necessary to address the circumstances of the Emergency with respect to a meeting of stockholders, including postponing the meeting to a later time or date (with the record date for the original meeting applying to the postponed meeting) or changing the place of the meeting to a meeting held solely by remote communication, as permitted by Section 110(i) of the DGCL.

**9.6 Liability.** No officer, director or employee acting in accordance with this Article IX is liable except for willful misconduct.

**9.7 Termination.** To the extent not inconsistent with this Article IX, these Bylaws remain in effect during any Emergency, and upon its termination this Article IX ceases to be operative, except as to acts taken during the Emergency. This Article IX may be repealed or changed by the stockholders, and the Board may adopt, amend or repeal emergency bylaws as permitted by Section 110 of the DGCL.

## ARTICLE XI — AMENDMENTS

**11.1 Amendment by Stockholders.** These Bylaws may be adopted, amended or repealed by the stockholders entitled to vote, by the affirmative vote of the holders of a majority in voting power of the outstanding shares entitled to vote generally in the election of directors (or by written consent under Section 2.16), subject to any greater vote required by the Certificate of Incorporation or any Stockholder Agreement.

**11.2 Amendment by the Board.** If the Certificate of Incorporation confers the power on the directors, the Board may also adopt, amend or repeal these Bylaws by the affirmative vote of a majority of the Whole Board (or by unanimous written consent), but (a) a bylaw adopted by the stockholders that specifies the votes necessary for the election of directors may not be further amended or repealed by the Board (Section 216 of the DGCL); (b) no amendment by the Board may eliminate or impair rights under Article VI with respect to any act or omission occurring before the amendment (Section 6.12); and (c) any amendment to Section 7.6 that imposes a new or more restrictive transfer restriction binds shares issued before its adoption only as provided in Section 202(b) of the DGCL. The fact that the power has been conferred on the Board does not divest or limit the stockholders' power to adopt, amend or repeal bylaws.

**11.3 Record of Amendments.** Whenever an amendment or new bylaw is adopted, it shall be copied in the minute book with the original Bylaws, together with the date of its adoption. If any bylaw is repealed, the fact of repeal and the date of the meeting or consent at which it was enacted shall be stated in the minute book.
