# Investor Questionnaire

## About this template

### 1. What this document does

The questionnaire is the company's evidence for its Regulation D exemption and for several non-securities compliance regimes. Every investor completes and signs it before the company countersigns a SAFE, note, or stock purchase agreement. It:

- establishes the company's **reasonable belief** of accredited status under each Rule 501(a) category, including the 2020 additions (professional certifications, knowledgeable employees, family offices and family clients, entities owning more than $5,000,000 of investments, LLCs and exempt reporting advisers, and the **spousal equivalent** concept);
- collects **Rule 506(c) verification** materials when the offering uses general solicitation (third-party letter, income documents, net-worth documents, prior verification, or the March 2025 staff-endorsed minimum-investment method);
- obtains a **Rule 506(d) bad-actor certification** from investors who will be covered persons (20% beneficial owners by voting power, or compensated solicitors) and, at the company's option, from all investors;
- identifies **foreign persons** for CFIUS purposes and screens for **OFAC** sanctions and outbound-investment concerns;
- identifies **ERISA benefit plan investors** and IRAs;
- identifies **FINRA member and associated-person** affiliations (which can trigger notice obligations for the investor under FINRA Rules 3210, 3270, and 3280);
- collects residence (for blue-sky notices), tax status, and investment experience.

### 2. How Claude should use it

1. Send it with the deal documents; require it back before countersignature. Keep it in the data room with the signed instrument.
2. **Rule 506(b)**: review answers for red flags (e.g., a net-worth claim inconsistent with other information; an entity formed shortly before the investment). Verification is not required, but a reasonable belief is.
3. **Rule 506(c)**: Part D is mandatory. Do not accept self-certification alone, except under the minimum-investment method (with all conditions) or for a person the company verified within the prior five years.
4. **Non-accredited investors**: the form assumes none. If Part C is used, the company must deliver Rule 502(b) disclosure and confirm sophistication; consult counsel first.
5. **Covered persons**: if the investor will hold 20% or more of the voting equity (as converted), Part E is mandatory.
6. **Foreign persons**: if Part F indicates a foreign person and the company may be a TID U.S. business, run the CFIUS analysis before signing and do not grant board, observer, or technical-information rights without it.
7. Update the questionnaire (or obtain a bring-down certificate) for each later closing and for conversion into preferred stock if facts may have changed; accredited status is measured at the time of each sale.

### 3. Key legal points embedded in the form

- **Net worth (Rule 501(a)(5))** excludes the primary residence as an asset; mortgage debt on it is excluded up to its fair market value, except that any increase in that debt in the 60 days before the sale (other than to acquire the residence) counts as a liability, and debt above fair market value counts as a liability.
- **Income (Rule 501(a)(6))**: more than $200,000 individually, or more than $300,000 jointly with a spouse or spousal equivalent, in each of the two most recent years, with a reasonable expectation of the same in the current year. The individual and joint tests cannot be mixed across years.
- **Professional certifications (Rule 501(a)(10))**: currently only the Series 7, Series 65, and Series 82 licenses, held in good standing. The SEC's September 30, 2026 notices proposing additional credentials (CPA, CFA, CFP, Series 79, Series 86/87, and a FINRA-developed exam) are not effective until an SEC order.
- **Knowledgeable employees (Rule 501(a)(11))** qualify only for investments in private funds (issuers relying on Investment Company Act § 3(c)(1) or 3(c)(7)); an operating startup is not such an issuer, so the category almost never applies to a SAFE or note. It is included for completeness.
- **Entities.** Several categories require that the entity not be "formed for the specific purpose of acquiring the securities offered" (Rule 501(a)(3), (7), (9), (12)). A special-purpose vehicle qualifies only if all equity owners are accredited (Rule 501(a)(8)); otherwise each owner may count as a separate purchaser (Rule 501(e)(2)).
- **Minimum-investment verification** (staff letter of March 12, 2025): $200,000 for natural persons; $1,000,000 for entities accredited by assets or investments; for entities accredited only because all owners are accredited, $1,000,000, or $200,000 per owner if the owners are fewer than five natural persons, with each owner's commitment meeting the natural-person or entity minimum; plus written representations of accredited status and that the minimum is not financed by a third party for the specific purpose of the investment, and no actual knowledge to the contrary. Staff guidance only.

> **Verify:** Before use, confirm on sec.gov whether the SEC has issued any order designating additional Rule 501(a)(10) credentials, and whether the staff has modified the Rule 506(c) minimum-investment position, after November 2, 2026.

### 4. Privacy and data handling

The questionnaire collects sensitive personal and financial data. Limit circulation to those who need it, store it securely, and delete verification documents (tax returns, credit reports) after the retention period counsel recommends. Do not send these documents by unencrypted email when avoidable. State privacy laws (e.g., the California Consumer Privacy Act, where it applies to the company) may impose notice and handling obligations.
