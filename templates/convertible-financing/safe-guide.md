# SAFE Guide and SAFE Term Summary

## About this template

### 1. What this file does

The Simple Agreement for Future Equity ("SAFE") was created at Y Combinator in 2013 and became the post-money SAFE in 2018. It is the default instrument for U.S. pre-seed and seed financings. This file does three things:

1. Identifies the **current official YC forms**, their license, and the skill's policy on them (link, never reproduce or modify).
2. Explains, blank by blank, **how to complete each form**, how the key definitions operate, and the mistakes that most often cause disputes or cap table errors.
3. Supplies an **original SAFE Term Summary** (the Form below): a one-page-per-SAFE record for the cap table, the data room, the auditors, and the priced-round lawyers, plus a round register.

The SAFE itself is always the official YC form, downloaded fresh from https://www.ycombinator.com/documents (or generated with YC's free "Send a SAFE" tool, which currently supports U.S.-incorporated companies only). Extra rights go in a side letter (see [safe-side-letter](safe-side-letter.md)). The board approves the issuance first (see [board-consent-convertible-financing](board-consent-convertible-financing.md)).

### 2. The current YC forms (verified November 2, 2026)

| Form (U.S. companies) | Version on the form | Use | Key blank(s) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Post-Money SAFE — Valuation Cap, no Discount | 1.2 | The standard SAFE | Post-Money Valuation Cap |
| Post-Money SAFE — Discount, no Valuation Cap | 1.2 | Discount-only; also YC's recommended route to an **uncapped** SAFE (set the Discount Rate to 100%) | Discount Rate (= 100 minus the discount) |
| Post-Money SAFE — "Uncapped MFN" (no Valuation Cap, no Discount) | 1.3 | First check before terms are set; picks up better terms of later convertible securities on the holder's election | None beyond the common blanks |
| Pro Rata Side Letter | 1.0 | Optional right to buy a pro rata share of the Equity Financing; only with SAFEs that have a post-money valuation cap | Date, parties |
| SAFE User Guide (PDF) | — | YC's explanation, sample conversion math, best practices | — |

YC also posts Valuation Cap SAFEs and Pro Rata Side Letters for **Canadian, Cayman Islands, and Singapore** companies, with a note to consult local counsel.

**Cap-and-discount.** YC's user guide still describes a post-money "Valuation Cap and Discount" version in its appendix, but the documents page lists only the three U.S. versions above. Do not build a cap-and-discount SAFE by editing a YC form. If the parties insist on both, either obtain YC's form if YC offers one at the time, or use a convertible note (or a bespoke instrument drafted by counsel and clearly not presented as a YC form).

> **Verify:** Re-check https://www.ycombinator.com/documents on the drafting date for new versions, re-dated forms, or a restored cap-and-discount form, and use the version numbers printed on the downloaded forms in the SAFE Term Summary.

### 3. License and the skill's policy

- **What YC's page says.** The documents page calls the SAFE "open-source" and states that all SAFE forms are "free to download and use." The page itself names no license.
- **What the forms say.** Each U.S. form's footnote states it is copyright Y Combinator Management, LLC (© 2023 for the three SAFEs; © 2018 for the side letter) and "made available under a Creative Commons Attribution-NoDerivatives 4.0 License (International)," and adds that users may modify the form for use in transactions but should not publicly disseminate a modified version without asking YC first.
- **What that means.** CC BY-ND 4.0 permits copying and redistributing the unmodified form with attribution, but not sharing adapted versions. YC's footnote separately permits private modification for a transaction. Each form also contains a statement that the company and the investor agree neither has modified the form except to fill in blanks and bracketed terms; YC's user guide explains that removing the statement signals that the form was changed.
- **Policy for this skill (regardless of the license):** do not reproduce, excerpt, or modify YC's forms in this repository or in Claude's output. Link to the official source, explain how to complete the blanks, and put any additional or different rights in an original side letter. If a client truly needs a changed SAFE, counsel should prepare a separate instrument that does not claim to be the YC form, delete the "unmodified" statement, and disclose every change to the investor.

### 4. SAFE, convertible note, or priced Series Seed?

| Factor | Post-money SAFE | Convertible note | Priced Series Seed / Series A |
|---|---|---|---|
| Legal nature | Contract right to future preferred; not debt | Debt (principal, interest, maturity) | Preferred stock now |
| Cost and speed | Lowest; one standardized form; YC tool | Low to moderate; negotiated purchase agreement and note | Highest; charter amendment, purchase agreement, investor agreements, stockholder approval |
| Valuation | Deferred (cap is a ceiling, not a price) | Deferred (cap and discount) | Set now |
| Founder certainty about dilution | High with post-money caps (ownership = amount ÷ cap) | Moderate (interest accrues; cap basis negotiated) | Highest |
| Investor protections | Liquidation preference-like payout in a Liquidity or Dissolution Event; no governance | Creditor priority; maturity leverage; events of default | Full: liquidation preference, protective provisions, board seat, information, pro rata, anti-dilution |
| Accounting and tax | Usually a liability under ASC 480; tax character uncertain (YC states intent to treat as stock) | Debt; interest/OID; QSBS clock starts at conversion | Stock; QSBS clock starts at issuance |
| Best fit | Pre-seed and seed, many small checks, speed | Bridges; investors requiring debt; companies with notes already outstanding | Institutional lead, larger round, governance wanted |

Default recommendation: valuation-cap post-money SAFE, side letters only for meaningful checks, and a priced round once the company raises several million dollars from an institutional lead. See [references/convertible-instruments.md](../../references/convertible-instruments.md) §§ 1 and 12.

### 5. Field-by-field completion guide

**Common blanks (all three SAFEs):**

| Blank in the YC form | How to complete | Pitfalls |
|---|---|---|
| Company name (header and first paragraph) | Exact legal name from the certificate of incorporation, including "Inc." | Using a trade name; mismatch with the Form D and the board consent |
| Investor Name | Exact legal name of the investor. For an entity, the full name and (in the signature block) entity type and state; for a trust, the trustee "as trustee of" the trust; for joint investors, both names | Investing through an entity other than the one that wires; an individual signing for a fund without authority |
| Purchase Amount ($) | Dollar amount actually being funded, in figures with commas (e.g., $250,000) | Mismatch with the wire. YC's FAQ: for a small shortfall ask the investor to wire the difference (or cover it), and refund any overpayment rather than changing the SAFE |
| Date of Safe ("on or about") | The date the parties sign and the company receives the funds. Countersign only after funds arrive | YC's FAQ says the date that matters for MFN purposes is the later of signing and receipt of the wire; an unfunded SAFE does not take effect. If funds arrive late, re-date by signing a fresh SAFE |
| State of Incorporation | "Delaware" for a Delaware corporation | Writing the headquarters state |
| Governing Law Jurisdiction | Usually "Delaware" (matches the corporation's internal-affairs law and the skill's other documents); California is sometimes used by California-based companies | Leaving it blank; choosing a state with no connection |
| Company signature block | Company name; "By:" signature; printed name and title of an officer authorized by the board; company notice address and email | Signature by someone the board did not authorize; personal email for notices |
| Investor signature block | "By:" signature; name and title (entities); address and email for notices | Individuals adding a title; missing email (the SAFE allows notice by email) |
| Two signers (e.g., a fund requiring two signatories, or joint investors) | YC's FAQ says to download the form and add a second signature block manually, or split the investment into two SAFEs | Adding signature blocks is the only change YC itself describes; record it in the Term Summary |

**Form-specific blanks:**

| Form | Blank | How to complete | Pitfalls |
|---|---|---|---|
| Valuation Cap | Post-Money Valuation Cap ($) | The negotiated **post-money** cap in full dollars (e.g., $10,000,000). Ownership at conversion (before the round's dilution) = Purchase Amount ÷ cap | Entering a pre-money number; abbreviations ("$10M"); different caps in the term sheet, the email thread, and the form |
| Discount | Discount Rate (%) | Enter **100 minus the discount**: a 20% discount is a Discount Rate of **80%**. For an uncapped, no-discount SAFE, YC's FAQ says to use this form with a Discount Rate of **100%** | Entering "20%" (which gives an 80% discount); forgetting that the discount form has no cap |
| MFN | None (no cap, no discount) | Complete common blanks only. Track every later convertible security and send an MFN Notice | Issuing later SAFEs without notice; issuing the MFN SAFE before closing lower-cap SAFEs whose terms the company does not want picked up |
| Pro Rata Side Letter | Company name; date (same as the SAFE); Investor Name; signature blocks | Use only with a valuation-cap SAFE. Give it only to investors meeting the company's threshold | Granting to every small check (Section 7 below); using it with a discount-only or MFN SAFE |

### 6. Key definitions and how they operate

Paraphrased for the drafter; the form controls.

- **Equity Financing.** Any bona fide capital-raising sale of **preferred** stock at a fixed pre- or post-money valuation. No minimum size. A tiny priced round converts every SAFE; a common-stock financing does not.
- **Standard Preferred Stock / Safe Preferred Stock.** Standard Preferred is the series sold to new money at the initial closing. Safe Preferred is identical except that price-based terms (per-share liquidation amount, initial conversion price, per-share dividend) are based on the SAFE's conversion price. The priced-round charter must create it (the "shadow series"; see [convertible-instruments.md](../../references/convertible-instruments.md) § 8).
- **Conversion (cap form).** At the initial closing the SAFE converts into the greater number of shares of (1) Standard Preferred at the new-money price, or (2) Safe Preferred at the **Safe Price** = Post-Money Valuation Cap ÷ **Company Capitalization**. The current cap form has no separately defined "Conversion Price"; the "greater of" test does its work. (The original pre-money SAFE and older post-money cap-and-discount forms defined a "Conversion Price" as whichever of the Safe Price or Discount Price produced more shares.)
- **Discount form.** Converts into Safe Preferred at the **Discount Price** = lowest new-money price × Discount Rate.
- **MFN form.** Converts into Standard Preferred at the new-money price unless, before then, the holder elected to amend the SAFE to match a later convertible security.
- **Company Capitalization** (measured immediately before the Equity Financing, as converted, without double counting): includes all issued capital stock, **all Converting Securities** (every SAFE, including this one, convertible notes, and other convertibles), all outstanding Options and **Promised Options**, and the **Unissued Option Pool**, but **not** any pool increase made in connection with the Equity Financing except to the extent Promised Options exceed the pre-increase pool. Because every SAFE is in the denominator, each capped post-money SAFE owns exactly Purchase Amount ÷ cap of Company Capitalization.
- **Converting Securities.** This SAFE and other convertible securities: SAFEs, convertible notes and other convertible debt, and other securities convertible into capital stock.
- **Options / Promised Options / Unissued Option Pool.** Options are broadly defined (options, restricted stock awards or purchases, RSUs, SARs, warrants and similar, vested or not). Promised Options are promised but ungranted awards, measured by reference to promises made before or in connection with the term sheet (or as treated in pricing the round). The Unissued Option Pool is reserved, available shares not subject to outstanding or promised awards.
- **Liquidity Event** = **Change of Control** (a person or group acquiring more than 50% of the voting securities; a merger in which the pre-transaction holders do not keep a majority of voting power; or a sale of all or substantially all assets), a **Direct Listing**, or an **IPO**. The holder receives the greater of the **Cash-Out Amount** (Purchase Amount) and the **Conversion Amount** (proceeds on Purchase Amount ÷ **Liquidity Price** common shares; Liquidity Price = cap ÷ **Liquidity Capitalization**, which excludes the Unissued Option Pool). The discount form computes the Liquidity Price as the common stock's fair market value (by reference to the deal price) × Discount Rate. The MFN form pays only the Cash-Out Amount. The board may reduce the cash portion (without reducing total proceeds, pro rata with equal-priority holders) to keep a tax-free reorganization.
- **Dissolution Event.** Voluntary termination of operations, general assignment for the benefit of creditors, or other liquidation, dissolution, or winding up (excluding a Liquidity Event): the holder is entitled to the Cash-Out Amount.
- **Priority.** Like non-participating preferred: Cash-Out Amounts are junior to debt and creditor claims (including unconverted notes), on par with other SAFEs and preferred (pro rata if short), senior to common.
- **Dividends.** If the company pays a non-stock dividend on common while the SAFE is outstanding, it must pay the holder a **Dividend Amount** computed on an as-converted basis.
- **Amendment.** Company plus either the holder or a majority-in-interest (more than 50% of Purchase Amount) of all outstanding SAFEs with the same cap and discount; the Purchase Amount (and, in the MFN form, the MFN section) cannot be changed by majority; every holder's consent must be solicited; all must be treated alike.
- **Transfer.** Company consent required, except to the investor's affiliates and controlled or affiliated funds, and on death or disability.
- **Investor representations.** Power and authority; **accredited investor** status, with the company's right to void the SAFE and return the Purchase Amount if the holder is not accredited at the Equity Financing; investment intent; no public market; understanding of risk.
- **Tax.** The parties state their intent to treat the SAFE as stock (common stock for I.R.C. §§ 304, 305, 306, 354, 368, 1036, and 1202). The IRS is not bound; see [convertible-instruments.md](../../references/convertible-instruments.md) § 10.

### 7. Side letters and pro rata: how to grant them without conflict

- Keep the SAFE unmodified; put pro rata, information, MFN, or major-investor rights in a side letter. YC's FAQ recommends the same.
- **Pro rata budget.** Every pro rata right is a claim on the Series A allocation. Before granting, run the YC user guide's budget (share of SAFEs that can carry pro rata = 100% − new investors' target % ÷ total round %) or use a dollar threshold (e.g., checks of $250,000 or more).
- **Conflicts to avoid:** a side letter that purports to change conversion economics (that is a SAFE modification, breaks the "unmodified" statement, and may trigger other holders' MFN rights); two side letters giving the same investor overlapping pro rata rights (YC's letter plus the skill's letter); information rights that conflict with confidentiality or CFIUS limits for foreign investors; MFN side letters that reach other side letters without the company tracking them.

### 8. Board approval and securities compliance (summary)

1. **Board approval before issuance** (YC's FAQ and user guide: the board must formally approve SAFEs). Use [board-consent-convertible-financing](board-consent-convertible-financing.md): maximum aggregate Purchase Amount, permitted forms and terms (cap, discount, MFN), side-letter parameters, authorized officers, closing window, Form D and state notices, and the reservation note (preferred cannot be reserved until the charter authorizes it). If existing preferred stock is outstanding, check the charter's protective provisions and any investors' rights agreement for consent or pro rata requirements.
2. **Exemption.** Rule 506(b) is the default (no general solicitation; all purchasers accredited). Use Rule 506(c) if the founders publicize the raise; then verify every investor. See [securities-law.md](../../references/securities-law.md).
3. **Investor questionnaire** from each investor before signing ([investor-questionnaire](investor-questionnaire.md)).
4. **Form D** within 15 calendar days after the first sale; **state notices** (e.g., California § 25102.1(d): 15 days after first California sale, $300). Calendar an annual Form D amendment if the round stays open more than a year.
5. **Records.** Fully executed SAFE, wire confirmation, questionnaire, side letters, and the SAFE Term Summary into the data room; update the cap table (as a convertible, not as stock).

### 9. Common mistakes (and how the Term Summary catches them)

1. **Pre- versus post-money confusion.** Quoting "$8M cap" without saying post-money; using a pre-money number in the post-money form. The Term Summary requires the cap type and the implied ownership.
2. **Cap misstatement.** The cap in the form differs from the term sheet or the investor's email; caps written as "$10M." Record the cap in full figures and in words.
3. **Discount inversion.** Entering the discount instead of the Discount Rate.
4. **Mixing forms.** Pre-money and post-money SAFEs, or SAFEs and notes, outstanding together without modeling; MFN SAFEs issued before lower-cap SAFEs close. Model with `scripts/conversion_calculator.py` before every new issuance.
5. **Side-letter conflicts.** See Section 7.
6. **Forgetting board approval** or approving a smaller maximum than is raised. The Term Summary records the resolution and remaining capacity.
7. **Treating the SAFE as stock on the cap table** (showing shares before conversion) or omitting it from "fully diluted" calculations used in later negotiations.
8. **Section 83(b) confusion.** An investor buying a SAFE for cash does not file an 83(b) election; 83(b) applies to property received for services subject to vesting. Never issue SAFEs as compensation.
9. **Signing before funding** (or accepting funds without a signed SAFE). Countersign on receipt of funds and record both dates.
10. **Non-accredited investors.** The YC SAFE assumes accredited investors; a non-accredited purchaser triggers Rule 502(b) disclosure and sophistication requirements.
11. **Missed filings.** Form D and state notices are each keyed to the date of first sale (in each state).
12. **Unrecorded MFN notices and amendments,** leaving the conversion terms of an MFN SAFE uncertain at the Series A.
13. **QSBS expectations.** Investors should not assume the § 1202 holding period starts at the SAFE date; see convertible-instruments.md § 10.
14. **Foreign investors** given information or board-observer rights without a CFIUS analysis.

### 10. Deadlines triggered

| Trigger | Deadline | Authority |
|---|---|---|

### 11. State-specific notes

- **California:** notice under § 25102.1(d) for Rule 506 sales; § 25102(f) if relying on § 4(a)(2) without Regulation D; California anti-fraud liability (Cal. Corp. Code §§ 25401, 25501) applies to private sales; California does not conform to § 1202.
- **New York, Texas, Washington:** Rule 506 notices via EFD, commonly within 15 days after the first in-state sale (see securities-law.md § 9.2 for fees).
