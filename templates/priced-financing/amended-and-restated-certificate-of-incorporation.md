# Amended and Restated Certificate of Incorporation (Preferred Stock Financing)

## About this template

**What it does.** The Restated Certificate creates the new series of Preferred Stock (and any SAFE or note "shadow" series), states every economic and control right that must live in the charter (dividends, liquidation preference, conversion, anti-dilution, board-election rights, protective provisions, mandatory conversion, redemption), and modernizes the rest of the charter (officer exculpation, indemnification, corporate opportunity renunciation, forum selection). Rights that are merely contractual — registration, information, pro rata, board designation, drag-along, rights of first refusal and co-sale — belong in the Investors' Rights Agreement, Voting Agreement and Right of First Refusal and Co-Sale Agreement prepared by the ancillary drafter. Under Section 151(a), the powers, preferences and rights of a class or series must be stated in the certificate of incorporation (or in a board resolution under blank-check authority in the certificate); a right that exists only in a side agreement is not a term of the stock.

**Where it sits in the closing.** Sequence: (1) term sheet signed; (2) Board approves the Restated Certificate, Stock Purchase Agreement and other Transaction Agreements, the sale of the shares, the reservation of conversion shares, and any pool increase; (3) stockholders approve the Restated Certificate (and any plan increase) by written consent; (4) the Restated Certificate is filed with the Delaware Secretary of State and becomes effective; (5) the shares are issued and funds released. The shares do not exist until the Restated Certificate is effective, so filing must occur before (or at a stated effective time no later than) the Initial Closing. Most deals file on the closing date using Delaware's expedited service, confirm acceptance with the Division of Corporations (or obtain a filed-stamped copy), then release signature pages and wires. The Stock Purchase Agreement makes the filing a closing condition (Section 5.1(d) of that form).

**Approvals required (8 Del. C. §§ 242 and 245).**

1. *Board.* The Board must adopt a resolution setting forth the amendment (here, the full Restated Certificate), declaring it advisable, and either calling a stockholder meeting or directing that it be submitted to the stockholders by written consent (§ 242(b)(1)). Because the Restated Certificate amends as well as restates, it must be proposed by the Board and adopted under § 242 (§ 245(b)). Board action by unanimous written consent under § 141(f) is customary; a director's consent may be made effective up to 60 days in the future. If any director is an investor in the round, consider the 2025 version of § 144 (see "Insider rounds" below).
2. *Stockholders — general vote.* Holders of a majority of the outstanding stock entitled to vote must approve (§ 242(b)(1)), counting each existing Preferred share on an as-converted basis if the existing charter so provides.
3. *Stockholders — class and series votes under § 242(b)(2).* A class votes separately on any amendment that would increase or decrease its authorized shares, change its par value, or alter or change its powers, preferences or special rights so as to affect it adversely; a series votes separately if the amendment adversely affects it but not the entire class. In a first priced round where only Common Stock is outstanding, the Common Stock vote is both the general vote and the class vote. In a later round, check whether the Restated Certificate changes the authorized shares or adversely alters the rights of any existing series (for example, by changing its Original Issue Price, preference ranking, protective provisions or Conversion Price mechanics) — each affected series needs its own vote. The § 242(b)(2) opt-out for the Common Stock in Part A, Section A.4 can be adopted only in the original certificate, in an amendment adopted before the class was issued, or with the approval of a majority of the Common Stock itself; obtain that class approval if the opt-out is new.
4. *Stockholders — charter-based votes.* The existing charter's protective provisions (for example, "Requisite Preferred Holders" consent to any charter amendment or the creation of senior or parity stock) apply in addition to the statutory votes.
5. *Contractual consents.* Check existing Investors' Rights Agreements and side letters for preemptive rights (waivers needed for the new shares and any SAFE conversion shares), consent rights, and most-favored-nation clauses.
6. *Written consent mechanics.* Stockholders may act by written consent unless the charter prohibits it, if holders of not less than the minimum votes needed at a meeting sign (§ 228(a)). Consents must be delivered to the corporation within 60 days of the first consent delivered (§ 228(c)); the record date is the first date a signed consent is delivered unless the Board fixes one (§ 213(b)). Prompt notice of the action must be given to stockholders who did not consent and who would have been entitled to notice of a meeting (§ 228(e)). Send the § 228(e) notice promptly after the action — usually with the closing deliverables or within a few days — together with any information-statement disclosure counsel considers prudent (especially if non-consenting stockholders include employees or angels whose economic position is affected).

**Filing and effectiveness (8 Del. C. §§ 103 and 245).**

- An authorized officer signs the Restated Certificate (§ 103(a)(2)); the signature is an affirmation under penalties of perjury that the facts stated are true (§ 103(b)).
- File with the Delaware Division of Corporations (§ 103(c)). The certificate is effective on filing unless it specifies a later effective time, which may be no later than the 90th day after filing (§ 103(d)). If the closing is delayed past a specified future effective time, the certificate must be terminated or amended before that time by a filed certificate.
- On filing, the Restated Certificate supersedes the original certificate and all prior amendments, but the original incorporation date does not change (§ 245(d)).
- Delaware filing fees and expedited-service fees change periodically.

> **Verify:** The current Division of Corporations fee schedule and expedited-service cutoffs before scheduling the closing.

- File any required foreign-qualification amendments (some states require updated charter documents) and update the Company's franchise tax reporting.

**Franchise tax.** Delaware franchise tax under the default "authorized shares" method rises steeply with the number of authorized shares. Venture charters authorize tens of millions of shares with a very low par value; the Company should compute and pay franchise tax using the "assumed par value capital" method, which typically produces a much smaller tax for startups with low gross assets. Warn the client when the authorized share count in Section A.1 increases materially.

**SAFE and note conversions.** Each distinct SAFE or note conversion price normally becomes its own shadow series (Series A-1, A-2, and so on) whose Original Issue Price equals that conversion price, so that the holders' liquidation preference equals the cash they paid. See the drafting note to "Original Issue Price" in Part C, Section 1 (Definitions), and the worked example in references/venture-financing-terms.md. Read every SAFE: post-money SAFEs define the converting holders' stock this way; some notes and older SAFEs do not.

**Insider rounds and Section 144.** If existing investors who are directors (or who, with others, control the Board or a majority of the vote) are buying in the round, the financing is an interested transaction. Section 144 as amended in 2025 provides safe harbors: approval by a majority of disinterested directors (or a committee of at least two disinterested directors if a majority of the Board is not disinterested), or by an informed, uncoerced vote of a majority of the votes cast by disinterested stockholders, or proof of fairness (§ 144(a)); for a controlling stockholder or control group, approval by a committee of at least two disinterested directors with authority to negotiate and reject the transaction, or a disinterested stockholder vote, or fairness (§ 144(b)). Section 144(e) defines a controlling stockholder to include a person (or a group acting under an agreement) that has the contractual right to elect a majority of the Board — which can capture investors who together control Board seats under a Voting Agreement. Offering all existing stockholders a pro rata opportunity (a rights offering) further reduces exposure. See references/venture-financing-terms.md.

**State-specific variations.**

- *California.* A Delaware corporation can be a "quasi-California corporation" under Cal. Corp. Code § 2115 if more than half its business (averaging property, payroll and sales) is in California and more than half its outstanding voting securities are held of record by California residents; California then asserts that certain provisions (including cumulative voting under § 708 and the limits on classified boards) apply. Most venture-backed private companies treat § 2115 as a risk to manage rather than a reason to change the charter, but flag it for California-headquartered companies with mostly California holders.
- *Other states.* No other state routinely overrides the internal affairs of a Delaware corporation in a venture financing, but state blue-sky notice filings for the stock sale are discussed in the Stock Purchase Agreement template.

**Common mistakes this form avoids.**

- Forgetting the series vote of an existing series whose rights or authorized shares the Restated Certificate changes (§ 242(b)(2)).
- Too few authorized shares of Common Stock to cover conversion of all Preferred Stock, outstanding Options and the pool (Section A.3 imposes a reservation covenant; size Section A.1 accordingly).
- Protective provisions that omit "by merger, consolidation or otherwise" (see *Avatex* and *Benchmark* in the Section 6 drafting note).
- Liquidation alternatives that do not match (a participating Section 4.2 with a "greater of" Section 4.1(b)).
- No allocation rule for escrows and earnouts (Section 4.3.4).
- Treating the § 242(b)(2) opt-out as also opting out of § 242(d), or the reverse (Section A.4 and its drafting note, reflecting the 2026 amendments).
- Leaving officers out of the exculpation clause after the 2022 amendment to § 102(b)(7).
- A forum clause that does not let stockholders sue in at least one Delaware court for non-internal claims (§ 115(c), 2025).
- Inconsistent defined terms across the deal: this form uses exactly the shared terms "{{series_name}} Preferred Stock," "Preferred Stock," "Common Stock," "Original Issue Price," "Conversion Price," "Requisite Preferred Holders," "Deemed Liquidation Event," "Qualified Public Offering," "Mandatory Conversion," "Exempted Securities," "Series Directors," "Common Directors," "Independent Directors" and "Restated Certificate."

**Cross-reference map for the other Transaction Agreements.** Part C, Section 1 (Definitions) defines Exempted Securities, Deemed Liquidation Event, Original Issue Price, Conversion Price, Requisite Preferred Holders and Qualified Public Offering; Section 4 (Liquidation) contains the waterfall the Voting Agreement's drag-along must respect; Section 5 (Voting) fixes the Series Directors, Common Directors and Independent Directors; Section 6 (Protective Provisions) contains the charter vetoes; Section 9 (Mandatory Conversion) contains the Mandatory Conversion; Section 8 (Anti-Dilution Adjustments) contains the price-based adjustment.

**Deadlines triggered.** Prompt § 228(e) notice after stockholder action by written consent; Form D within 15 calendar days after the first sale and state notice filings (see the Stock Purchase Agreement); updated stock ledger and cap table at closing; franchise tax recalculated for the next annual report (due March 1).

# AMENDED AND RESTATED CERTIFICATE OF INCORPORATION OF {{company_name}}

{{company_name}}, a corporation organized and existing under the General Corporation Law of the State of Delaware (as amended from time to time, the "**DGCL**"), hereby certifies as follows:

1. The name of the corporation is {{company_name}} (the "**Corporation**"). [[Optional: The Corporation was originally incorporated under the name {{original_company_name}}.]] The original Certificate of Incorporation of the Corporation was filed with the Delaware Secretary of State on {{incorporation_date}}.

2. This Amended and Restated Certificate of Incorporation (this "**Restated Certificate**") restates, integrates and further amends the Certificate of Incorporation of the Corporation as heretofore in effect. It was duly adopted by the Board of Directors of the Corporation and by the stockholders of the Corporation in accordance with Sections 242 and 245 of the DGCL [[Optional (required if the stockholders acted by written consent): , and the approval of the stockholders was given by written consent in accordance with Section 228 of the DGCL]].

> **Drafting note:** Section 245(c) requires the restated certificate to identify itself as a restated certificate, give the corporation's current name, any different name it was first incorporated under, and the filing date of its original certificate, and recite that it was duly adopted under Section 245. Because this Restated Certificate also amends the charter, it must be adopted under Section 242 as well (Section 245(b)). Keep paragraph 2 consistent with the approvals actually obtained.

> **Drafting note:** Keep the Section 228 recital whenever the stockholders approved by written consent rather than at a meeting. The last sentence of Section 228(e) requires a certificate filed for an action taken by consent to state, in place of any statement about a stockholder vote, that written consent was given in accordance with Section 228. Delete it only if the approval was given at a meeting.

3. The Certificate of Incorporation of the Corporation is hereby amended and restated in full, and its text shall read in its entirety as follows:

## ARTICLE I — NAME

The name of the Corporation is {{company_name}}.

## ARTICLE II — REGISTERED OFFICE AND AGENT

The address of the registered office of the Corporation in the State of Delaware is {{registered_agent_address}}. The name of the registered agent of the Corporation at that address is {{registered_agent_name}}.

> **Drafting note:** Section 102(a)(2) requires the street address of the registered office, including the street, number, city, county and postal code, and the name of the registered agent at that address. Confirm against the Delaware Division of Corporations' online entity record before filing; a mismatch is a common reason for rejection.

## ARTICLE III — PURPOSE

The nature of the business or purposes to be conducted or promoted by the Corporation is to engage in any lawful act or activity for which corporations may be organized under the DGCL.

## ARTICLE IV — CAPITAL STOCK

### Part A. Authorized Capital Stock

**A.1 Authorized Shares.** The total number of shares of all classes of capital stock that the Corporation is authorized to issue is {{authorized_total_shares}} shares, consisting of (a) {{authorized_common_shares}} shares of Common Stock, par value {{par_value}} per share (the "**Common Stock**"), and (b) {{authorized_preferred_shares}} shares of Preferred Stock, par value {{par_value}} per share (the "**Preferred Stock**").

**A.2 Designation of Series of Preferred Stock.** The Preferred Stock is divided into the following series:

(a) {{authorized_series_shares}} shares are designated "{{series_name}} Preferred Stock";

[[Optional: (b) {{authorized_shadow_series_shares}} shares are designated "{{shadow_series_name}} Preferred Stock";]]

[[Optional: (c) {{authorized_prior_series_shares}} shares are designated "{{prior_series_name}} Preferred Stock".]]

> **Drafting note:** The sum of the series in Section A.2 must equal the number of shares of Preferred Stock in Section A.1, and the sum of Common Stock and Preferred Stock must equal the total in Section A.1. Authorize enough {{series_name}} Preferred Stock to cover the Initial Closing, every permitted Additional Closing under the Purchase Agreement, and any tranche closings, but not materially more: unissued authorized shares of a series are easy to issue later without a further series vote unless Part C, Section 6 (Protective Provisions) requires one. Authorize a separate shadow series (for example, "Series A-1 Preferred Stock") for each distinct SAFE or convertible-note conversion price; see Part C, Section 1 (Definitions), "Original Issue Price," and the related drafting note.

**A.3 Authorized Common Stock Reserved for Conversion.** At all times, the Corporation shall keep reserved and available, out of its authorized but unissued Common Stock and solely to permit conversion of the Preferred Stock, a number of duly authorized shares of Common Stock sufficient at each such time to convert every outstanding share of Preferred Stock. If at any time the number of authorized but unissued shares of Common Stock is not sufficient to effect the conversion of all then-outstanding shares of Preferred Stock, then, in addition to any other remedies available to the holders of Preferred Stock, the Corporation shall promptly take all corporate action necessary to increase its authorized but unissued shares of Common Stock to a number sufficient for that purpose, including using its best efforts to obtain the requisite stockholder approval of any necessary amendment to this Restated Certificate.

**A.4 Changes in Authorized Common Stock.** Subject to any separate vote of the holders of Preferred Stock, or of any series of Preferred Stock, that this Restated Certificate requires, the number of authorized shares of Common Stock may be increased or decreased (but not decreased below the sum of (a) the number of shares of Common Stock then outstanding and (b) the number of shares of Common Stock then reserved for issuance under Section A.3 and upon the exercise or conversion of outstanding Options and Convertible Securities) by the affirmative vote of the holders of shares of capital stock of the Corporation representing a majority of the votes represented by all outstanding shares of capital stock of the Corporation entitled to vote thereon, voting together as a single class on an as-converted basis, irrespective of the provisions of Section 242(b)(2) of the DGCL, and no vote of the holders of the Common Stock voting separately as a class shall be required therefor.

[[Alternative A: Nothing in this Section A.4 is intended to be, or shall be construed as, an election by the Corporation not to be governed by Section 242(d)(1) or Section 242(d)(2) of the DGCL, or as a requirement of a vote greater than the vote contemplated by the last sentence of Section 242(b)(2) of the DGCL.]]

[[Alternative B: The Corporation elects not to be governed by paragraph (1) or paragraph (2) of Section 242(d) of the DGCL, and any amendment to this Restated Certificate of the kind described in either of those paragraphs shall require the vote of stockholders that would be required by Section 242(b) of the DGCL and this Restated Certificate if Section 242(d) of the DGCL did not apply.]]

> **Drafting note:** Section 242(b)(2) gives each class a separate class vote on an increase or decrease in its authorized shares unless the certificate of incorporation opts out. The opt-out for Common Stock is standard in venture charters; without it, the Common Stock (often controlled by founders) would hold a separate veto over every increase in authorized Common Stock needed for future rounds and pool increases. The statute permits the opt-out to appear in the original certificate, in an amendment adopted before any shares of the class were issued, or in an amendment approved by a majority of the class — confirm the Common Stock class approves this Restated Certificate if the opt-out is new.
>
> **Section 242(d) after the 2026 amendments.** Section 242(d) (added in 2023) lets a corporation make certain amendments with no stockholder vote or with a reduced "votes cast" standard. Paragraph (d)(1) covers name changes, deletion of historical provisions, and forward splits — but forward splits only if the corporation has a single class of stock not divided into series, so it will rarely apply to a venture-backed company with Preferred Stock outstanding. Paragraph (d)(2) covers changes in authorized shares and reverse splits but only for a class listed on a national securities exchange, so it does not apply while the Corporation is private. The 2026 amendments (H.B. 353, effective August 1, 2026) confirm that a standard Section 242(b)(2) opt-out like the one above does not by itself opt out of Section 242(d); an opt-out of Section 242(d) must say expressly that the corporation is not to be governed by Section 242(d)(1) or (2), or must require a vote greater than the one the last sentence of Section 242(b)(2) contemplates. This codifies and extends *Salama v. Simon*, 328 A.3d 356 (Del. Ch. 2024), *aff'd*, 342 A.3d 373 (Del. 2025). Alternative A (default) preserves Section 242(d) — company-favorable and the practical norm because the charter will be replaced at the IPO. Alternative B opts out — investor-favorable but of little practical effect for a private company. Because Part C, Section 6.1(b) requires the consent of the Requisite Preferred Holders for any charter amendment, that separate Preferred vote applies regardless of Section 242(d).

**4.3.2 Redemption Following Certain Deemed Liquidation Events.**

(a) *Notice and request.* This Section 4.3.2 applies if a Deemed Liquidation Event described in clause (a)(ii) or clause (b) of the definition of Deemed Liquidation Event occurs and the Corporation does not effect its dissolution under the DGCL within ninety (90) days afterward. In that case, (i) by the ninetieth (90th) day after the Deemed Liquidation Event, the Corporation shall send each holder of Preferred Stock written notice of the holder's right under clause (ii) of this Section 4.3.2(a) to require the redemption of its shares of Preferred Stock, describing what the holder must do to exercise that right; and (ii) if, within one hundred twenty (120) days after the Deemed Liquidation Event, the Requisite Preferred Holders deliver to the Corporation a written instrument requesting redemption, then on the one hundred fiftieth (150th) day after the Deemed Liquidation Event (the "**DLE Redemption Date**") the Corporation shall apply the consideration it received for the Deemed Liquidation Event, less any retained liabilities relating to the assets sold or the technology licensed (as the Board of Directors determines in good faith), together with its other assets available for distribution to its stockholders, in each case only to the extent that Delaware law governing distributions to stockholders permits (the "**Available Proceeds**"), to the redemption of all outstanding shares of Preferred Stock, for a price per share equal to the Liquidation Amount (or, if Section 4.2, Alternative B or Alternative C, applies, the amount that would be distributable on that share under Sections 4.1 and 4.2 if the Available Proceeds were distributed in a liquidation) (the "**DLE Redemption Price**").

(b) *Insufficient Available Proceeds.* If the Available Proceeds are too small to redeem every outstanding share of Preferred Stock, the Corporation shall use all of the Available Proceeds to redeem a ratable portion of each holder's shares, following the order of priority in Section 4.1(a) and, within each level of priority, in proportion to the full DLE Redemption Price otherwise payable on the shares to be redeemed. The Corporation shall redeem the rest of those shares as soon as Delaware law governing distributions to stockholders permits it to do so.

(c) *Use of Available Proceeds pending redemption.* Before the distribution or redemption provided for in this Section 4.3.2, the Corporation shall not expend or dissipate the consideration received for the Deemed Liquidation Event, except to discharge expenses incurred in connection with the Deemed Liquidation Event or in the ordinary course of business.

(d) *Redemption notice.* At least twenty (20) days before the DLE Redemption Date, the Corporation shall send each holder of record of Preferred Stock a written notice of the redemption stating (i) how many of the holder's shares of Preferred Stock the Corporation will redeem on the DLE Redemption Date, (ii) the DLE Redemption Date and the DLE Redemption Price, (iii) the date on which the holder's right to convert those shares terminates (as determined under Section 7.1(b)), and (iv) that the holder must deliver any certificate representing the shares to be redeemed to the Corporation, at the place and in the manner specified in the notice (or, if the holder alleges that a certificate has been lost, stolen or destroyed, a lost-certificate affidavit and agreement reasonably acceptable to the Corporation to indemnify the Corporation against any claim made against it on account of the alleged loss, theft or destruction).

(e) *Surrender and payment.* On or before the DLE Redemption Date, each holder whose shares of Preferred Stock are to be redeemed (unless the holder has exercised its right to convert those shares under Section 7 (Optional Conversion)) shall surrender to the Corporation any certificate representing those shares (or deliver the affidavit and agreement described in Section 4.3.2(d)), and the DLE Redemption Price for those shares then becomes payable to the order of the Person shown as their owner on the certificate or in the Corporation's book-entry records. If less than all of the shares represented by a certificate are redeemed, a new certificate (or a book-entry notice under Section 7.3(b)) representing the unredeemed shares shall promptly be issued to the holder.

(f) *Rights after the DLE Redemption Date.* If, on the DLE Redemption Date, the Corporation pays or tenders the DLE Redemption Price for the shares then to be redeemed, or deposits that amount with an independent payment agent so that the funds will be timely available for payment, then, even if a certificate representing any of those shares has not been surrendered, after the DLE Redemption Date dividends cease to accrue on those shares and all rights with respect to them terminate, other than the holders' right to receive the DLE Redemption Price, without interest, upon surrendering their certificates (or delivering the affidavit and agreement).

> **Drafting note:** Section 4.3.2 addresses the case where the Corporation itself sells its assets (or exclusively licenses its key technology) and keeps the cash. Without it, the Preferred Stock would have to wait for a dissolution that the Board might never approve. The redemption right is subject to Delaware's capital-impairment rule in Section 160 of the DGCL (a corporation may not redeem shares when its capital is impaired or the redemption would impair it, subject to limited exceptions), and directors who approve an unlawful redemption are exposed under Section 174. Section 4.3.2(b) builds in that limitation. The ninety/one-hundred-twenty/one-hundred-fifty-day sequence is the market convention; shorten it only if the parties accept the Board's reduced time to decide on a dissolution.

**4.3.3 Amount Deemed Paid or Distributed.** Upon any liquidation, dissolution or winding up of the Corporation or any Deemed Liquidation Event, the amount treated as paid or distributed to the holders of the Corporation's capital stock equals the cash, securities or other property that the Corporation or the acquiring Person pays or distributes to them. The value of any property, rights or securities other than cash shall be determined as follows:

(a) securities not subject to an investment letter or other similar restriction on free marketability: (i) if traded on a national securities exchange, the average of the closing prices of the securities on the exchange over the twenty (20) trading-day period ending three (3) trading days before the closing of the event; (ii) if actively traded over-the-counter, the average of the closing bid or sale prices (whichever is applicable) over the same period; and (iii) if there is no active public market, their fair market value as the Board of Directors determines it in good faith [[Optional: , including the approval of at least one (1) Series Director]];

(b) securities subject to an investment letter or other similar restriction on free marketability (other than restrictions arising solely by virtue of a stockholder's status as an affiliate or former affiliate): the value determined under clause (a), reduced by a discount that reflects the approximate fair market value of the restricted securities, as the Board of Directors determines it in good faith [[Optional: , including the approval of at least one (1) Series Director]]; and

(c) all other property: its fair market value as the Board of Directors determines it in good faith [[Optional: , including the approval of at least one (1) Series Director]].

In a Deemed Liquidation Event referred to in clause (a) of the definition of Deemed Liquidation Event, if the Merger Agreement provides a value for any consideration, that value controls for purposes of this Section 4.3.3.

[[Optional: Any amount paid or payable to employees, directors or consultants of the Corporation under a management carve-out, transaction bonus or retention plan approved by the Board of Directors in connection with a Deemed Liquidation Event shall be treated as a transaction expense of the Corporation and not as an amount paid or distributed to the holders of capital stock for purposes of Sections 4.1 and 4.2.]]

**4.3.4 Allocation of Escrow and Contingent Consideration.** If, in a Deemed Liquidation Event described in clause (a) of the definition of Deemed Liquidation Event, any part of the consideration payable to the Corporation's stockholders will be paid only if one or more contingencies are satisfied (the "**Additional Consideration**"), the Merger Agreement shall require that (a) the consideration other than the Additional Consideration (the "**Initial Consideration**") be allocated among the holders of the Corporation's capital stock under Sections 4.1 and 4.2 as though it were the only consideration payable in the Deemed Liquidation Event, and (b) any Additional Consideration that becomes payable to the Corporation's stockholders when the contingencies are satisfied be allocated among those holders under Sections 4.1 and 4.2, taking into account that the Initial Consideration was already paid as part of the same transaction. Any consideration held in escrow or retained as a holdback to secure indemnification or similar obligations relating to the Deemed Liquidation Event, and any earnout, milestone or similar contingent payment, is Additional Consideration for purposes of this Section 4.3.4.

> **Drafting note:** Section 4.3.4 prevents an escrow release or earnout from being allocated as if it were a separate, smaller transaction (which would let the Preferred Stock take its preference twice, or cause the Common Stock to bear all escrow losses). The same rule should appear in the merger agreement's allocation schedule. The optional management carve-out paragraph in Section 4.3.3 confirms that a Board-approved carve-out plan (common when the Preferred Stock preference would otherwise leave nothing for the team) is paid ahead of the waterfall as a transaction expense; if it is kept, consider requiring Series Director approval of the plan. Carve-out plans also raise fiduciary-duty and Section 280G questions; see references/venture-financing-terms.md (Trados discussion).

**4.4 Notice of Liquidation.** The Corporation shall give each holder of record of Preferred Stock written notice of any proposed liquidation, dissolution or winding up of the Corporation, or of any proposed Deemed Liquidation Event, in accordance with Section 8.12.

> **Drafting note — scope and drafting technique.** The lead-in phrase "directly or indirectly by amendment, merger, consolidation … or otherwise" is essential. Delaware courts read class-vote rights narrowly: in *Elliott Associates, L.P. v. Avatex Corp.*, 715 A.2d 843 (Del. 1998), the protective provision reached a merger because it covered changes made "whether by merger, consolidation or otherwise," while in *Benchmark Capital Partners IV, L.P. v. Vague*, C.A. No. 19719 (Del. Ch. July 15, 2002), *aff'd*, 822 A.2d 396 (Del. 2003), a provision without merger language did not stop the company from creating senior stock through a merger with a subsidiary. Keep the list short: protective provisions are investor vetoes over fundamental changes; operational controls (budgets, executive hiring, related-party transactions, large contracts) belong, if anywhere, in the Investors' Rights Agreement's "Matters Requiring Series Director Approval," where the ancillary drafter will place them.
>
> **Market positions.** Company-favorable: the Series Seed list (adverse charter changes, authorized-share changes, senior or parity stock, redemptions and dividends, board size, liquidation or change of control). Market (NVCA-style, used here): that list plus debt, subsidiary equity and asset transfers. Investor-favorable: adding the pool increase (clause (i)), principal business changes (clause (k)) and series-specific vetoes (Section 6.2). The "null and void *ab initio*" sentence is standard and supported by the general rule that acts in violation of the charter are void or voidable; after *W. Palm Beach Firefighters' Pension Fund v. Moelis & Co.*, C.A. No. 2023-0309-JTL (Del. Ch. Feb. 23, 2024), *rev'd on other grounds (laches)*, *Moelis & Co. v. W. Palm Beach Firefighters' Pension Fund*, 2026 WL 184868 (Del. #### Section 7. Optional Conversion

The holders of Preferred Stock have the following conversion rights (the "**Conversion Rights**"):

**7.1 Right to Convert.**

(a) *Conversion ratio.* Each share of Preferred Stock is convertible, at the option of its holder, at any time and from time to time, and without the payment of additional consideration by the holder, into a number of shares of Common Stock, fully paid and non-assessable, equal to the Original Issue Price of the applicable series divided by the Conversion Price of that series in effect at the time of conversion.

(b) *Termination of Conversion Rights.* If the Corporation liquidates, dissolves or winds up, or a Deemed Liquidation Event occurs, the Conversion Rights terminate at the close of business on the last full day before the date set for paying the holders of Preferred Stock any amounts distributable to them on that event; *provided* that, if the Corporation defaults in that payment, the Conversion Rights do not terminate. For any share of Preferred Stock called for redemption under Section 4.3.2 or Section 10 (Redemption), the Conversion Rights terminate at the close of business on the business day preceding the applicable redemption date, unless the redemption price for that share is not fully paid on that redemption date, in which case the Conversion Rights for that share continue until the price is paid in full.

**7.2 Fractional Shares.** No fractional shares of Common Stock shall be issued upon conversion of the Preferred Stock. In lieu of any fractional share to which the holder would otherwise be entitled, the Corporation shall pay cash equal to the fraction multiplied by the fair market value of a share of Common Stock, as the Board of Directors determines it in good faith. Whether the conversion would result in a fractional share shall be determined on the basis of the total number of shares of Preferred Stock the holder is converting at the time and the aggregate number of shares of Common Stock issuable upon that conversion.

**7.3 Mechanics of Conversion.**

(a) *Notice of conversion.* To voluntarily convert shares of Preferred Stock into shares of Common Stock, a holder shall (i) provide written notice to the Corporation's transfer agent at its office (or, if the Corporation serves as its own transfer agent, to the Corporation at its principal office) that the holder elects to convert all or a specified number of its shares of Preferred Stock, identifying any event on which the conversion is contingent, and (ii) if the shares are certificated, surrender the certificate or certificates for those shares (or, if the holder alleges that a certificate has been lost, stolen or destroyed, a lost-certificate affidavit and agreement reasonably acceptable to the Corporation to indemnify the Corporation against any claim that may be made against it on account of the alleged loss, theft or destruction) at that office. The notice shall state the holder's name or the names of the nominees in which the holder wishes the shares of Common Stock to be issued. If required by the Corporation, any certificate surrendered for conversion shall be endorsed or accompanied by a written instrument of transfer, in form reasonably satisfactory to the Corporation, duly executed by the registered holder or its attorney duly authorized in writing. The close of business on the date of receipt by the transfer agent (or by the Corporation) of the notice and, if applicable, the certificates (or affidavit and agreement), or the time the contingent event occurs, is the time of conversion (the "**Conversion Time**"), and the Common Stock issuable on conversion of the shares so specified is deemed to be outstanding of record from that time.

(b) *Delivery of Common Stock.* As soon as practicable after the Conversion Time, the Corporation shall (i) issue and deliver to the holder (or its nominees) one or more certificates representing the full shares of Common Stock issuable on the conversion (or, if the shares are uncertificated, a notice of issuance in book-entry form as contemplated by Section 151(f) of the DGCL), and a certificate or notice for any shares of Preferred Stock that a surrendered certificate represented but that were not converted, (ii) pay in cash any amount payable under Section 7.2 in lieu of a fractional share, and (iii) pay all declared but unpaid dividends on the shares of Preferred Stock converted.

(c) *Par value.* Before taking any action that would cause an adjustment reducing a series' Conversion Price below the par value, at that time, of the Common Stock issuable upon conversion of that series, the Corporation shall take any corporate action that is, in the opinion of its counsel, necessary so that the Corporation may validly and legally issue fully paid and non-assessable shares of Common Stock at the adjusted Conversion Price.

(d) *Effect of conversion.* All shares of Preferred Stock converted as provided in this Section 7 cease to be outstanding, and all rights with respect to those shares immediately cease and terminate at the Conversion Time, except only the right of the holders to receive shares of Common Stock in exchange for them, to receive payment in lieu of any fractional share under Section 7.2, and to receive payment of any dividends declared but unpaid on them.

(e) *No further adjustment.* A conversion does not result in any adjustment to the Conversion Price of the applicable series on account of declared but unpaid dividends on the converted shares or on the Common Stock delivered on conversion.

(f) *Taxes.* The Corporation shall pay all issue taxes and similar taxes payable on the issuance or delivery of Common Stock when Preferred Stock is converted under this Section 7. It is not required, however, to pay any transfer tax that arises because Common Stock is to be issued and delivered in a name other than the one in which the converted Preferred Stock was registered, and the Corporation need not make any such issuance or delivery until the Person requesting it has paid that tax to the Corporation or shown to the Corporation's satisfaction that it has been paid.

#### Section 8. Anti-Dilution Adjustments

**8.1 Application.** The Conversion Price of each series of Preferred Stock is subject to adjustment from time to time as provided in this Section 8. Each adjustment is determined separately for each series by reference to the Conversion Price of that series, so that an issuance may result in an adjustment for one series and not for another.

**8.2 No Adjustment of Conversion Price.** The Conversion Price of a series of Preferred Stock shall not be adjusted on account of an issuance or deemed issuance of Additional Shares of Common Stock as to which the Corporation receives, before or within thirty (30) days after that issuance or deemed issuance, written notice from [[Alternative A: the holders of at least a majority of the shares of that series then outstanding]] [[Alternative B: the Requisite Preferred Holders, provided that the waiver applies to every series of Preferred Stock whose Conversion Price would otherwise be adjusted]] agreeing that no adjustment will be made on account of it.

> **Drafting note:** Alternative A (series-by-series waiver) protects each series from having its anti-dilution protection waived by holders of other series; Alternative B is simpler and is acceptable where the same investors hold most of every series or where the waiver must apply uniformly. In a down round led by existing investors, obtaining this waiver (often in exchange for a pay-to-play or a recapitalization) is a key closing deliverable; coordinate with Section 13 (Waiver).

**8.3 Deemed Issue of Additional Shares of Common Stock.**

(a) *Options and Convertible Securities.* Whenever, after the Original Issue Date, the Corporation issues Options or Convertible Securities (other than Options or Convertible Securities that are themselves Exempted Securities), or fixes a record date to determine which holders of any class of its securities are entitled to receive them, the Corporation is deemed to have issued, as Additional Shares of Common Stock, the maximum number of shares of Common Stock that may be issued on exercise of those Options or, for Convertible Securities and Options to acquire them, on conversion or exchange of those Convertible Securities. That maximum is the number stated in the governing instrument, assuming that every condition to exercise, conversion or exchange has been met and disregarding any provision for later adjustment of that number. The deemed issuance occurs when the Options or Convertible Securities are issued or, if a record date has been fixed, at the close of business on that record date.

(b) *Revision of terms after an adjustment.* This clause (b) applies to any Option or Convertible Security whose issuance caused the Conversion Price of a series to be adjusted under Section 8.4. If an amendment to its terms, or the operation of any of its provisions (other than an automatic adjustment under its anti-dilution or similar provisions), changes, upward or downward, either the number of shares of Common Stock issuable on its exercise, conversion or exchange or the consideration payable to the Corporation on its exercise, conversion or exchange, then, effective when the change takes effect, the Conversion Price of that series as computed on the original issuance of the Option or Convertible Security (or on the record date for it) shall be readjusted to the Conversion Price that would have resulted had the changed terms applied from the original issuance date; *provided, however*, that a readjustment under this clause (b) may not raise the Conversion Price of a series above the lower of (A) that series' Conversion Price in effect immediately before the adjustment originally caused by issuing the Option or Convertible Security, and (B) the Conversion Price that series would have had as a result of any issuances of Additional Shares of Common Stock made between the original adjustment date and the readjustment date (disregarding the deemed issuance attributable to that Option or Convertible Security).

(c) *Revision of terms of instruments that did not cause an adjustment.* This clause (c) applies to any Option or Convertible Security (other than one that is itself an Exempted Security) whose issuance did not cause an adjustment to the Conversion Price of a series under Section 8.4, whether because the consideration per share for the Additional Shares of Common Stock underlying it was at least equal to that series' Conversion Price then in effect or because it was issued before the Original Issue Date. If, after the Original Issue Date, an amendment to its terms, or the operation of any of its provisions (other than an automatic adjustment under anti-dilution or similar provisions), increases the number of shares of Common Stock issuable on its exercise, conversion or exchange or decreases the consideration payable to the Corporation on its exercise, conversion or exchange, then the Option or Convertible Security as so revised, together with the Additional Shares of Common Stock underlying it (determined as provided in Section 8.3(a)), is deemed to have been issued when the revision takes effect.

(d) *Expiration.* If an Option or Convertible Security whose original issuance, or the revision of whose terms, caused an adjustment to the Conversion Price of a series under Section 8.4 expires or terminates, in whole or in part, without having been exercised, converted or exchanged, the Conversion Price of that series shall be readjusted to the Conversion Price that would have been in effect had the expired or terminated Option or Convertible Security (or part of it) never been issued.

(e) *Instruments with adjustable or undetermined terms.* Where the number of shares of Common Stock issuable on exercise, conversion or exchange of an Option or Convertible Security, or the amount of consideration the Corporation is to receive on that exercise, conversion or exchange, can be calculated when the instrument is issued or amended but may change based on later events, any adjustment to the Conversion Price under this Section 8.3 shall be made at the time of that issuance or amendment, using the number or amount as then calculated and disregarding any provision for later adjustment (and Sections 8.3(b) and 8.3(c) govern any later change). Where the number of shares or the consideration cannot be calculated at all when the instrument is issued or amended (as with a simple agreement for future equity or a convertible note without a fixed conversion price), any adjustment to the Conversion Price that this Section 8.3 would require shall instead be made when that number or amount first becomes calculable, even if it remains subject to later adjustment, and the calculation shall assume that the issuance or amendment occurred at that time.

**8.4 Adjustment of Conversion Price upon Issuance of Additional Shares of Common Stock.** Whenever, after the Original Issue Date, the Corporation issues (or under Section 8.3 is deemed to issue) Additional Shares of Common Stock for no consideration, or for consideration per share below the Conversion Price, in effect immediately before that issuance or deemed issuance, of a series of Preferred Stock, the Conversion Price of that series shall be reduced, at the same time as the issuance or deemed issuance, as follows:

[[Alternative A: (broad-based weighted average) to the price, rounded to the nearest one-hundredth of a cent, given by this formula:

CP2 = CP1 × (A + B) ÷ (A + C)

where:

(i) "CP2" is the Conversion Price of that series in effect immediately after that issuance or deemed issuance;

(ii) "CP1" is the Conversion Price of that series in effect immediately before that issuance or deemed issuance;

(iii) "A" is the number of shares of Common Stock outstanding immediately before that issuance or deemed issuance, counting as outstanding every share of Common Stock that would be issued if all Options outstanding at that time were exercised and all Convertible Securities (including the Preferred Stock) outstanding at that time were converted or exchanged (assuming the exercise of any outstanding Options to acquire those Convertible Securities);

(iv) "B" is the number of shares of Common Stock that the aggregate consideration received or deemed received by the Corporation for that issuance would have purchased at a price per share equal to CP1 (that is, that aggregate consideration divided by CP1); and

(v) "C" is the number of Additional Shares of Common Stock issued or deemed issued in the transaction.]]

[[Alternative B: (full ratchet) to the consideration per share that the Corporation received for that issuance or deemed issuance, rounded to the nearest one-hundredth of a cent.]]

In no event shall any adjustment under this Section 8.4 increase the Conversion Price of a series, except as provided in Sections 8.3(b) and 8.3(d).

> **Drafting note — choosing the formula.** Alternative A (broad-based weighted average) is the overwhelming market standard: Wilson Sonsini reports broad-based weighted average anti-dilution in 98% of its financings in 1H 2026, full ratchet in 1%, and narrow-based weighted average in 0% (Entrepreneurs Report Q2 2026, Appendix). "Broad-based" refers to the size of "A": counting all outstanding Common Stock plus all shares underlying outstanding Options and Convertible Securities (including the Preferred Stock) dilutes the adjustment and is company-favorable. A "broader-based" company-favorable variant adds the unallocated pool to "A" — to use it, insert at the end of clause (iii) ", and all shares of Common Stock reserved and available for future grant under any equity incentive plan of the Corporation approved by the Board of Directors"; investors often resist it. A "narrow-based" formula (counting only outstanding Common Stock, or only Preferred Stock) is investor-favorable and rare. Alternative B (full ratchet) reprices every share of the series to the new low price regardless of how few shares are sold; it is highly investor-favorable and is seen mainly in distressed or structured financings, sometimes limited to a period (for example, twelve months after the Original Issue Date) after which Alternative A applies — add that limitation expressly if negotiated. See references/venture-financing-terms.md for a worked example.

#### Section 12. Redeemed or Otherwise Acquired Shares

Shares of Preferred Stock that are converted or redeemed, or that the Corporation or any of its Subsidiaries otherwise acquires, are automatically and immediately cancelled and retired upon that conversion, redemption or acquisition, and may not be reissued, sold or transferred. #### Section 13. Waiver

The Requisite Preferred Holders, by affirmative written consent or vote, may waive on behalf of all holders of Preferred Stock any right, power, preference or other term of the Preferred Stock that this Restated Certificate sets out; *provided, however*, that (a) a waiver of any right, power, preference or other term of a series of Preferred Stock that affects the holders of that series adversely and differently from the holders of the other series also requires the affirmative written consent or vote of the holders of at least a majority of the shares of that series then outstanding; (b) the right of the holders of a class or series to elect directors under Section 5.2 may be waived only if holders of a majority of the shares of that class or series then outstanding consent in writing or vote in favor; and (c) Section 8.2 governs any waiver of an adjustment under Section 8 (Anti-Dilution Adjustments).

Any notice that this Part C requires or permits to be given to a holder of Preferred Stock shall be either mailed, postage prepaid, to the holder's mailing address as last shown in the Corporation's records or given by electronic transmission in accordance with Section 232 of the DGCL, and is deemed sent when mailed or electronically transmitted.

## ARTICLE V — BOARD OF DIRECTORS

**A. Number of Directors.** Subject to Part C, Section 5 (Voting) and Part C, Section 6 (Protective Provisions) of Article IV, the number of directors of the Corporation shall be fixed in the manner that the Bylaws of the Corporation provide.

**B. Ballots.** Directors need not be elected by written ballot except as the Bylaws of the Corporation may require.

**C. Management.** The business and affairs of the Corporation shall be managed by or under the direction of the Board of Directors, except as otherwise provided in this Restated Certificate or the DGCL.

## ARTICLE VI — BYLAWS

Subject to any additional vote required by this Restated Certificate (including Part C, Section 6 (Protective Provisions) of Article IV) or the Bylaws of the Corporation, the Board of Directors is expressly authorized to adopt, amend or repeal the Bylaws of the Corporation, consistent with Section 109(a) of the DGCL. The stockholders also have the power to adopt, amend or repeal the Bylaws of the Corporation.

## ARTICLE VII — LIMITATION OF LIABILITY OF DIRECTORS AND OFFICERS

**A. Elimination of Liability.** To the fullest extent permitted by the DGCL as it now exists or may hereafter be amended, no director or officer of the Corporation shall have personal liability to the Corporation or its stockholders for monetary damages for any breach of fiduciary duty in that person's capacity as a director or officer, as applicable. If, after this Restated Certificate is filed, the DGCL is amended to permit corporate action that further eliminates or limits the personal liability of directors or officers, the liability of each director and officer of the Corporation is eliminated or limited to the fullest extent that the DGCL, as so amended, permits.

**B. Officers.** For purposes of this Article VII, "officer" has the meaning given in Section 102(b)(7) of the DGCL.

**C. Effect of Amendment.** No repeal or modification of this Article VII by the stockholders of the Corporation, and no amendment of the DGCL, shall (a) adversely affect any right or protection that a director or officer of the Corporation has at the time of that repeal, modification or amendment, or (b) increase the liability of any director or officer of the Corporation for acts or omissions that occurred before it.

> **Drafting note:** Section 102(b)(7) permits exculpation of directors and (since the 2022 amendments) officers for monetary damages for breach of the duty of care, but not for breaches of the duty of loyalty, acts or omissions not in good faith or involving intentional misconduct or a knowing violation of law, transactions from which the director or officer derived an improper personal benefit, a director's liability under Section 174 (unlawful dividends and redemptions), or — for officers only — any action by or in the right of the corporation (derivative claims). Officer exculpation applies only if the certificate of incorporation provides for it, so a company formed with a director-only clause must add officers by amendment; the Restated Certificate is the natural place to do it, and the stockholder approval of the Restated Certificate supplies the required vote. "Officer" for this purpose means a person who, at the time of the act or omission, is deemed to have consented to service of process under 10 Del. C. § 3114(b).

## ARTICLE VIII — INDEMNIFICATION AND ADVANCEMENT OF EXPENSES

**A. Indemnification.** The Corporation shall indemnify and hold harmless, to the fullest extent that applicable law (as in effect now or as later amended) permits, each Person (each, a "**Covered Indemnitee**") who has been or is made, or is threatened to be made, a party to, or is otherwise involved in, any threatened, pending or completed action, suit or proceeding, whether civil, criminal, administrative or investigative (a "**Proceeding**"), because that Person (or a Person of whom that Person is the legal representative) holds or has held office as a director or officer of the Corporation or, while holding such an office, serves or has served at the Corporation's request as a director, officer, employee, fiduciary or agent of another corporation or of a partnership, limited liability company, joint venture, trust, nonprofit entity or other enterprise, including service with respect to employee benefit plans. The indemnity covers all liability and loss suffered, and all expenses (including attorneys' fees), judgments, fines, ERISA excise taxes and amounts paid in settlement, that the Covered Indemnitee actually and reasonably incurs in connection with the Proceeding. Notwithstanding the preceding sentence, except as provided in Section C of this Article VIII, the Corporation is required to indemnify a Covered Indemnitee in connection with a Proceeding (or part of a Proceeding) commenced by the Covered Indemnitee only if the commencement of the Proceeding (or part) by the Covered Indemnitee was authorized in advance by the Board of Directors.

**B. Advancement of Expenses.** To the fullest extent applicable law permits, the Corporation shall advance to each Covered Indemnitee, before the final disposition of any Proceeding, the expenses (including attorneys' fees) that the Covered Indemnitee incurs in defending it, upon receipt of an undertaking by or on behalf of the Covered Indemnitee to repay those amounts if it is ultimately determined that the Covered Indemnitee is not entitled to be indemnified under this Article VIII or otherwise, as contemplated by Section 145(e) of the DGCL.

**C. Claims.** If the Corporation does not pay in full, within thirty (30) days after receiving a written claim, any amount claimed as indemnification or advancement of expenses under this Article VIII, the Covered Indemnitee may file suit to recover the unpaid amount and, if successful in whole or in part, is entitled to be paid the expense of prosecuting the claim to the fullest extent permitted by law. In any such action, the Corporation bears the burden of proving that applicable law does not entitle the Covered Indemnitee to the indemnification or advancement of expenses requested.

**D. Non-Exclusivity of Rights.** The rights conferred on any Covered Indemnitee by this Article VIII are not exclusive of any other rights that the Covered Indemnitee has or may later acquire under any statute, this Restated Certificate, the Bylaws, any agreement, any vote of stockholders or disinterested directors, or otherwise. The Corporation is authorized to enter into indemnification agreements with any director, officer, employee or agent of the Corporation providing rights to indemnification and advancement of expenses broader than those provided in this Article VIII.

**E. Other Sources.** [[Alternative A: The Corporation's obligation, if any, to indemnify or to advance expenses to a Covered Indemnitee who serves or served, at the Corporation's request, as a director, officer, employee, fiduciary or agent of any other enterprise (including another corporation or a partnership, limited liability company, joint venture, trust or nonprofit entity) shall be reduced by any amount the Covered Indemnitee actually collects as indemnification or advancement of expenses from that other enterprise.]] [[Alternative B: The Corporation acknowledges that certain Covered Indemnitees may have rights to indemnification, advancement of expenses or insurance provided by one or more investment funds or their Affiliates (collectively, "**Fund Indemnitors**"). The Corporation is the indemnitor of first resort, its obligations to those Covered Indemnitees are primary, and any obligation of a Fund Indemnitor to advance or indemnify the same expenses or liabilities is secondary. No advancement or payment by a Fund Indemnitor relieves the Corporation of its obligations under this Article VIII, and each Fund Indemnitor that makes such an advancement or payment is subrogated, to the extent of that advancement or payment, to all of the Covered Indemnitee's rights of recovery against the Corporation.]]

**F. Insurance.** The Corporation may purchase and maintain insurance, at its expense, to protect itself and any Person who is or was a director, officer, employee or agent of the Corporation, or who serves or served at the Corporation's request in any of the capacities described in Section A of this Article VIII, against any expense, liability or loss, whether or not the Corporation would have the power to indemnify the Person against that expense, liability or loss under the DGCL, as permitted by Section 145(g) of the DGCL.

**G. Employees and Agents.** The Corporation may, to the extent authorized from time to time by the Board of Directors, grant rights to indemnification and to the advancement of expenses to any employee or agent of the Corporation to the fullest extent of the provisions of this Article VIII with respect to the indemnification and advancement of expenses of directors and officers of the Corporation.

**H. Amendment or Repeal.** Any right to indemnification or to advancement of expenses of any Covered Indemnitee arising under this Article VIII is a contract right, vests when the Covered Indemnitee first becomes a director or officer, and shall not be eliminated or impaired by an amendment to or repeal of this Article VIII after the occurrence of the act or omission that is the subject of the Proceeding for which indemnification or advancement of expenses is sought.

> **Drafting note:** Section 145 permits (and, for successful defenses by present and former directors and officers, requires under Section 145(c)) indemnification; this Article makes indemnification and advancement mandatory to the fullest extent permitted. Alternative B in Section E (the "fund indemnitor" or "first resort" clause) is customary when venture-fund partners serve as directors; it responds to the contribution disputes illustrated in *Levy v. HLI Operating Co.*, 924 A.2d 210 (Del. Ch. 2007), and is usually repeated in the director's Indemnification Agreement (a Transaction Agreement if used). Company counsel sometimes accepts Alternative B only for Series Directors.

## ARTICLE IX — CORPORATE OPPORTUNITIES

The Corporation renounces, to the fullest extent permitted by Section 122(17) of the DGCL, any interest or expectancy that the Corporation has in any Excluded Opportunity, or in being offered an opportunity to participate in one. "**Excluded Opportunity**" means any matter, transaction or interest that is presented to, or acquired, created or developed by, or which otherwise comes into the possession of, (a) any director of the Corporation who is not an employee of the Corporation or any of its Subsidiaries, or (b) any holder of Preferred Stock, or any of that holder's partners, members, directors, stockholders, employees, Affiliates or agents, unless that Person is also an employee of the Corporation or any of its Subsidiaries (the Persons described in clauses (a) and (b), collectively, "**Covered Persons**"), unless the matter, transaction or interest is presented to, or acquired, created or developed by, or otherwise comes into the possession of, a Covered Person expressly and solely in that Covered Person's capacity as a director of the Corporation and while performing services in that capacity. A repeal or modification of this Article IX operates only prospectively and does not affect rights under this Article IX as in effect when any act or omission giving rise to liability occurred. Despite any other provision of this Restated Certificate, amending or repealing this Article IX, or adopting any provision inconsistent with it, requires the affirmative vote of the Requisite Preferred Holders.

> **Drafting note:** Section 122(17) permits a renunciation of "specified business opportunities or specified classes or categories of business opportunities," so the clause must define the class of opportunities (here, opportunities that come to non-employee directors and investor-affiliated Persons other than in their capacity as directors). The renunciation protects venture investors whose funds invest in related companies; it does not authorize misuse of the Corporation's confidential information, which remains governed by fiduciary duties and the confidentiality covenants in the Investors' Rights Agreement and Management Rights Letter. Founders and other employees are deliberately excluded. Company-favorable alternative: limit Covered Persons to Series Directors and their funds.

## ARTICLE X — EXCLUSIVE FORUM

**A. Internal Corporate Claims.** Unless the Corporation gives its written consent to an alternative forum, the Court of Chancery of the State of Delaware (or, if and only if the Court of Chancery lacks subject matter jurisdiction, another state court located within the State of Delaware or, if and only if no state court located within the State of Delaware has subject matter jurisdiction, the federal district court for the District of Delaware) shall be, to the fullest extent permitted by law, the sole and exclusive forum for (1) any action or proceeding brought derivatively on behalf of the Corporation; (2) any action asserting a claim of breach of a fiduciary duty owed by any current or former director, officer, stockholder, employee or agent of the Corporation to the Corporation or its stockholders; (3) any action asserting a claim against the Corporation or any current or former director, officer, stockholder, employee or agent of the Corporation arising under any provision of the DGCL, this Restated Certificate or the Bylaws of the Corporation (each as it may be amended from time to time), or as to which the DGCL confers jurisdiction on the Court of Chancery of the State of Delaware; (4) any action to interpret, apply, enforce or determine the validity of this Restated Certificate or the Bylaws of the Corporation; or (5) any action asserting a claim governed by the internal affairs doctrine, in each case to the extent it is an "internal corporate claim" within the meaning of Section 115 of the DGCL.

**B. Securities Act Claims.** Unless the Corporation gives its written consent to an alternative forum, the federal district courts of the United States of America (including the United States District Court for the District of Delaware) shall be, to the fullest extent permitted by law, the exclusive forum for the resolution of any complaint asserting a cause of action arising under the Securities Act.

**C. Exchange Act Claims.** Section A of this Article X does not apply to claims arising under the Securities Exchange Act of 1934, as amended, or to any other claim for which the federal courts have exclusive jurisdiction.

**D. Notice and Consent.** Any Person purchasing or otherwise acquiring or holding any interest in any security of the Corporation is deemed to have notice of and to have consented to the provisions of this Article X.

**E. Severability.** If, for any reason, any provision of this Article X is held invalid, illegal or unenforceable as applied to any Person or circumstance, then, to the fullest extent the law permits, that holding shall not affect or impair (a) the validity, legality or enforceability of that provision in any other circumstance or as applied to any other Person, or (b) the remaining provisions of this Article X, including any part of a sentence containing the affected provision, to the extent that part is not itself held invalid, illegal or unenforceable.

> **Drafting note:** Section 115(a) authorizes a charter or bylaw provision requiring internal corporate claims (defined in Section 115(b)) to be brought exclusively in Delaware courts and prohibits any provision that bars bringing them in Delaware. Section 115(c), added by the 2025 amendments (S.B. 95, generally effective August 1, 2025), permits forum provisions for claims that are not internal corporate claims only if they are consistent with applicable jurisdictional requirements and allow the stockholder to bring the claim in at least one court in Delaware that has jurisdiction. Section B (the federal forum provision upheld in *Salzberg v. Sciabacucchi*, 227 A.3d 102 (Del. 2020)) designates the federal district courts "of the United States," which include the District of Delaware; the express parenthetical is included to make the Section 115(c) compliance visible. Do not add fee-shifting language: Section 102(f) prohibits charter provisions that impose attorneys' fees on stockholders in connection with internal corporate claims.

> **Verify:** That Article X, Section B, as drafted satisfies Section 115(c) of the DGCL as added in 2025 — in particular whether designating "the federal district courts of the United States" (rather than the District of Delaware alone) is sufficient. Confirm against the enacted text and current Delaware commentary before filing.
