## About this template

**What it does.** This is the board's single approval of the whole financing. It (1) adopts and declares advisable the Restated Certificate and recommends it to the stockholders, as § 242(b)(1) requires before any stockholder vote; (2) approves the Stock Purchase Agreement and every other Transaction Agreement; (3) authorizes the issuance of the new series at the Initial Closing and any Additional Closings and fixes the consideration under § 152; (4) authorizes the conversion of outstanding SAFEs and convertible notes into the shadow series; (5) reserves Common Stock for conversion; (6) increases the option pool and calls for stockholder approval of the increase; (7) approves indemnification agreements and D&O insurance; (8) directs the Form D and blue-sky filings; (9) records the § 144 disclosures and disinterested-director approval; and (10) authorizes the officers to close.

**Sequence.** Board consent (this form) → stockholder consent (separate template) → file the Restated Certificate with the Delaware Secretary of State → sign and release the Transaction Agreements → fund → issue shares. The Restated Certificate must be effective **before** the shares are issued, because the series does not exist until it is. If the consent is signed before final documents are settled, attach the final forms or use a pricing committee; a board cannot approve a document it has not seen in substantially final form without risking a challenge to the authorization.

**Insider-led and down rounds.** A round led or substantially funded by existing investors whose designees sit on the board is an interested transaction. Under the 2025 amendments, § 144(a) protects directors from equitable relief and damages if the material facts are disclosed and a majority of the disinterested directors approves (or a committee of at least two disinterested directors if most of the board is interested), if informed disinterested stockholders approve, or if the transaction is fair. If an investor or group can elect a board majority or holds a majority of the voting power, the financing may be a "controlling stockholder transaction" requiring the § 144(b) committee and disinterested-stockholder procedures. The safest practice for insider rounds is a market check (documented outreach to new investors) and a rights offering that lets every accredited stockholder participate pro rata. Delaware courts have scrutinized dilutive insider financings closely (for example, *Carsanaro v. Bloodhound Technologies, Inc.*, 65 A.3d 618 (Del. Ch. 2013)).

**Option pool.** The term sheet usually sets a post-closing available pool percentage (`{{post_closing_pool_percent}}`), and the pool increase is counted in the pre-money capitalization. Confirm the increase number with the cap table model that the investors approved. Under the ISO rules, any increase in the maximum number of plan shares (other than for splits and similar adjustments) is treated as a new plan that stockholders must approve within twelve months before or after board adoption.

**SAFE and note conversion.** Post-money SAFEs typically convert into a shadow series (for example, "{{series_name}}-1 Preferred Stock") with a liquidation preference equal to the SAFE's conversion price rather than the new money price. The board should approve the conversion shares and cancellation of the instruments, confirm each holder's conversion computation against the instrument, and rely on § 3(a)(9) (exchange with existing security holders, no paid solicitation) or § 4(a)(2) for the issuance.

**After the consent.** Within 15 calendar days after the first sale, file Form D on EDGAR (obtain EDGAR access codes in advance). State notice filings usually follow the same 15-day timing through NASAA's Electronic Filing Depository (including New York since December 2020); confirm each state's rule. Refresh the 409A valuation before new option grants: a priced round is information that materially affects value under Treas. Reg. § 1.409A-1(b)(5)(iv)(B)(2)(i).

# ACTION BY UNANIMOUS WRITTEN CONSENT OF THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS OF {{company_name}}

**Effective {{consent_effective_date}}**

The undersigned, being all of the members of the Board of Directors (the "**Board**") of {{company_name}}, a Delaware corporation (the "**Company**"), act by unanimous written consent without a meeting under Section 141(f) of the General Corporation Law of the State of Delaware (the "**DGCL**") and the Company's Bylaws, and adopt the resolutions below. Each director directs that this consent become effective at 12:01 a.m. Eastern Time on {{consent_effective_date}} (the "**Effective Time**"), and that it be filed with the minutes of the Board's proceedings. Any director may revoke that director's consent before the Effective Time.

> **Drafting note:** § 141(f) lets a director give a consent that becomes effective at a later time (including on the happening of an event) so long as it is no more than 60 days after the instruction is given, and the person is a director at the effective time. If a director will resign at closing, have that director sign a consent that is effective before the resignation.

> **Drafting note:** The Restated Certificate's introductory paragraph must state the Company's current name, any former name, the date its original certificate was filed, and that the restatement "was duly adopted in accordance with" §§ 242 and 245 (§ 245(c)). Because stockholders act by consent, § 228(e) requires the filed certificate to state that consent was given under § 228 in place of any statement about a stockholder vote; the usual recital is "duly adopted in accordance with Sections 228, 242 and 245 of the DGCL." Coordinate this wording with the charter drafter. Ask the Delaware Division of Corporations for pre-clearance of a complex charter on a tight timeline, and order expedited filing.

> **Drafting note:** Under § 144(e)(4), a "disinterested director" is one who is not a party to the transaction and has no material interest in it or material relationship with a person that does. A director affiliated with a fund buying in the round is interested. A designee of an existing investor that is not buying is usually disinterested, but check for side arrangements. If the interested investor is a "controlling stockholder" (majority voting power, a contractual right to elect a board majority, or at least one-third of the voting power plus managerial control, § 144(e)(2)), § 144(b) requires a committee of at least two disinterested directors with authority to negotiate and reject the deal, or a disinterested-stockholder vote conditioned in advance, to obtain the safe harbor; otherwise fairness review applies.
