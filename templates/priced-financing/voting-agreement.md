## About this template

**What it does.** The Voting Agreement is a contract among stockholders (and the Company) that (1) fixes how the parties will vote their shares to elect the board negotiated in the term sheet; (2) commits them to vote for increases in authorized Common Stock needed to permit conversion of the Preferred Stock; and (3) contains the drag-along, which obligates the parties to vote for, and if necessary sell their shares in, a "Sale of the Company" approved by the specified constituencies, subject to protective conditions for dragged holders. An irrevocable proxy backs each covenant. It is a "Transaction Agreement" signed at the Initial Closing, after the Restated Certificate is filed.

**How it relates to the charter.** The Restated Certificate creates the voting **constituencies** (holders of Preferred Stock elect the Series Directors; holders of Common Stock elect the Common Directors; all holders together elect the rest). The Voting Agreement decides **who** fills each seat (for example, "one Series Director designated by the lead investor"). Keep the seat counts identical in both documents and in the bylaws' authorized number of directors (8 Del. C. § 141(b): the number is fixed by the bylaws unless the charter fixes it).

**Statutory frame.**

- *§ 218(c).* A written agreement signed by two or more stockholders "may provide that in exercising any voting rights, the shares held by them shall be voted as provided by the agreement, or as the parties may agree, or as determined in accordance with a procedure agreed upon by them." § 218(d) preserves any voting agreement or irrevocable proxy that is not otherwise illegal. Stockholder-to-stockholder voting covenants (Articles 2–4) were never in doubt after *Moelis*.
- *§ 212(e).* A proxy is irrevocable if it says so and is "coupled with an interest sufficient in law to support an irrevocable power," and the interest may be "in the stock itself or an interest in the corporation generally." The parties' mutual voting obligations under this Agreement supply the interest.
- *§ 122(18) and Moelis.* The Company is a party and gives covenants (to nominate and seat designees, to enforce the agreement). Since August 1, 2024, § 122(18) validates corporate covenants of this kind if they are not contrary to the charter. The Court of Chancery's 2024 *Moelis* decision (holding several stockholder-agreement approval rights facially invalid under § 141(a)) prompted the statute; on January 20, 2026, the Delaware Supreme Court reversed *Moelis*, holding the provisions at most voidable and the challenge barred by laches. Board-composition covenants and drag-alongs of the kind in this form are standard and were not at issue, but keep every Company covenant consistent with the Restated Certificate.
- *§ 144 (2025).* A holder that "[h]as the right, by contract or otherwise, to cause the election of nominees who are selected at the discretion of such person and who constitute either a majority of the members of the board" is a "controlling stockholder" (§ 144(e)(2)b). If one investor (or a group acting together) can designate a board majority under this Agreement, transactions with that investor are "controlling stockholder transactions" under § 144(b)–(c). Most venture boards are deliberately balanced to avoid this; if not, flag it in the board consent.
- *§ 141(k) and § 223.* A director elected by the holders of a class or series can be removed without cause only by that class or series; class-elected vacancies may be filled by the remaining directors elected by that class (§ 223(a)(2)) unless the charter or bylaws provide otherwise. Sections 2.4 and 2.5 overlay those defaults with the designation mechanics.
- *§ 202.* A drag-along is a transfer restriction of the kind authorized by § 202(c)(4) (obligating a holder to sell). It binds transferees only if noted conspicuously on the certificate or in the § 151(f) notice for uncertificated shares (§ 202(a)), and it binds previously issued shares only if the holder is a party or voted for it (§ 202(b)). Section 6.12 provides the legend.

**Drag-along: the key business decisions.**

| Issue | Company/founder-favorable | Market / neutral (this form's default in bold) | Investor-favorable |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who triggers | Board + Requisite Preferred Holders + majority of Common held by founders/service providers | **Board + Requisite Preferred Holders; optional founder/Common approval** | Requisite Preferred Holders alone |
| Price floor | Drag only if Common receives at least a set amount or the Preferred receives at least a multiple | **Optional floor (Section 4.3(i))** | None |
| Dragged holder reps | Title, authority and capacity only | **Title, authority, capacity, enforceability, no conflicts** | Pro rata share of business reps |
| Liability | Several, capped at own proceeds, pro rata of any escrow; fraud carve-out limited to own fraud | **Same** | Joint and several for escrow |
| Consideration | Same form and amount per share, with same elections | **Same form and amount per share within a class or series, allocated by the charter waterfall; cash for non-accredited holders** | Investors may receive different forms |
| Restrictive covenants | None | **None unless the holder is an employee/officer and similar holders give the same** | Non-competes from all Key Holders |
| Appraisal | Not waived | **Express waiver of appraisal and dissenters' rights (Manti)** | Plus covenant not to sue |
| Board approval required? | Yes | **Yes, for a merger (required by § 251 anyway); bracketed for a Stock Sale** | No |

**Trados and the board trigger.** In *In re Trados Inc. Shareholder Litigation*, 73 A.3d 17 (Del. Ch. 2013), directors designated by preferred holders who approved a sale in which common received nothing were held to owe duties to the common as residual claimants; the sale survived only because it was found entirely fair. A merger always needs board approval (§ 251(b)), so a board-approval drag trigger adds no new risk for mergers. For a stock purchase structured among stockholders, the NVCA's October 2025 commentary notes that making the board one of the triggering parties can import fiduciary-duty scrutiny into what would otherwise be a contractual stockholder decision. The form keeps the board trigger bracketed for Stock Sales.

**Appraisal and fiduciary claims.** *Manti Holdings, LLC v. Authentix Acquisition Co.*, 261 A.3d 1199 (Del. 2021), enforced an advance agreement by sophisticated, counseled stockholders to "refrain" from exercising appraisal rights. The waiver must be express: general waivers have failed (*In re Altor BioScience Corp.*, 2019 WL 2567853 (Del. Ch. June 20, 2019); *Halpin v. Riverstone National, Inc.*, 2015 WL 854724 (Del. Ch. Feb. 26, 2015) (no express waiver; the drag-along operated only prospectively and was invoked only after the merger had been approved)). A covenant not to sue for breach of fiduciary duty in a drag-along sale was held facially valid and reasonable in *New Enterprise Associates 14, L.P. v. Rich*, 295 A.3d 520 (Del. Ch. 2023), but the court held it could not bar claims of intentional wrongdoing and denied the motion to dismiss because such claims were pleaded; a promise to "raise no objection" was not enough to waive fiduciary claims in *Manti Holdings, LLC v. Carlyle Group Inc.*, 2022 WL 444272 (Del. Ch. Feb. 14, 2022); see also *In re Good Technology Corp. Stockholder Litigation*, 2017 WL 2537347 (Del. Ch. May 12, 2017) (procedural ruling denying summary judgment to enforce a voting agreement's covenant to "vote in favor of, and take no actions to oppose" the sale, leaving the holders' defenses for trial; the claims then settled, so there is no merits ruling). Sections 4.1(e) and 4.2 implement these rules. Waivers are most defensible against sophisticated, counseled holders, so do not rely on them for employees who join by a short joinder without counsel.

> **Verify:** The Westlaw cites for *Chordia v. Lee* (2024 WL 49850; C.A. No. 2023-0382-NAC, Jan. 4, 2024, *aff'd*, No. 315, 2024 (Del. Mar. 10, 2025) (ORDER)), *In re Altor BioScience Corp.* (2019 WL 2567853; the ruling was delivered from the bench on May 15, 2019 and implemented by a later written order) and *Good Technology* (2017 WL 2537347) were taken from the NVCA model voting agreement's footnotes and have not been confirmed against Westlaw; check them before use in client work.

**Company covenants and *Chordia v. Lee*.** In *Chordia v. Lee*, 2024 WL 49850 (Del. Ch. Jan. 4, 2024), the court enforced a company's covenant to use efforts to make stockholder-agreement rights effective against the company's later actions that stripped minority holders of their board rights. The form's Company covenant (Section 5.1) is drafted so the Company's obligations are clear and administrable, with a bracketed clarification that ordinary employment decisions do not breach it.

**State-specific points.** *California.* If the Company is subject to Cal. Corp. Code § 2115 (a foreign corporation with more than half its business and voting shareholders in California and not listed), certain California provisions, including cumulative voting (§ 708), apply; the voting covenants still bind the parties, but model the outcome before relying on designation math. Jury waivers are unenforceable in California state court (*Grafton Partners*); see Section 6.5. *Community property states* (Arizona, California, Idaho, Louisiana, Nevada, New Mexico, Texas, Washington and Wisconsin): obtain a spousal consent (Exhibit B) from each married Key Holder so the spouse's community interest is bound.

**Common mistakes this form avoids.** Seat counts that do not match the charter or bylaws; designation rights that "travel" with shares on transfer (Section 6.2 prevents it); a drag-along that lets the majority sell for non-cash consideration to some and cash to others; a drag-along that is silent on appraisal; a voting covenant to increase authorized Common that is unnecessary because the charter already opts out of § 242(b)(2) (keep it anyway as backup); forgetting the § 202(a) legend on uncertificated-share notices.

**Deadlines.** None imposed by statute on signing. Any stockholder consent electing directors after closing that is not unanimous triggers prompt § 228(e) notice to non-consenting holders.

## ARTICLE 2 — ELECTION OF DIRECTORS

**2.1 Board Size.** Each Stockholder shall vote all Shares it owns or controls the vote of, at all times and in whatever way is needed, to set and keep the number of directors at {{board_size}}, and shall not vote for any change to the Restated Certificate or the Company's Bylaws that would change that number except as Section 6.9 permits.

**2.2 Board Composition.** At every annual or special meeting of stockholders at which directors are elected, and in every written consent of stockholders electing directors, each Stockholder shall vote all Shares it owns or controls the vote of, in whatever way is needed, to elect the following individuals:

(a) *Series Directors:*

(i) one (1) individual whom {{lead_investor_name}} designates from time to time, for as long as {{lead_investor_name}} and its Affiliates together beneficially own at least {{lead_designation_threshold}} (counting Common Stock issued on conversion of Preferred Stock), initially {{initial_lead_director}}; [[Optional: and

(ii) one (1) individual whom the holders of a majority of the outstanding {{prior_series_name}} Preferred Stock designate from time to time, for as long as at least {{prior_series_threshold}} remain outstanding, initially {{initial_prior_series_director}};]]

(b) *Common Directors:*

(i) the individual then serving as the Company's Chief Executive Officer (the "**CEO Director**"), currently {{ceo_name}}; if the CEO Director stops serving as Chief Executive Officer for any reason, each Stockholder shall promptly vote its Shares to remove that individual from the Board (if the individual has not resigned) and to elect the new Chief Executive Officer as CEO Director; [[Optional: and

(ii) one (1) individual whom the Key Holders holding a majority of the Common Stock then held by all Qualified Key Holders designate from time to time, for as long as the Qualified Key Holders and their permitted transferees together beneficially own at least {{key_holder_threshold}}, initially {{initial_key_holder_director}};]]

[[Optional: (c) *Independent Directors:* {{independent_director_count}} individual(s), none of whom is an employee, officer or Affiliate of the Company or of any Investor, designated from time to time by [[Alternative A: a majority of the other directors in office, including at least one (1) Series Director and at least one (1) Common Director]] [[Alternative B: the Requisite Preferred Holders, with the consent (which may not be unreasonably withheld, conditioned or delayed) of the Key Holders holding a majority of the Common Stock then held by all Qualified Key Holders]] [[Alternative C: {{lead_investor_name}} and the CEO Director, acting together]].]]

When a designation right under clause (a), (b) or (c) ends because its holder no longer meets the applicable threshold, the related seat stops being subject to designation under this Section 2.2, and the Stockholders shall vote to fill it with an individual elected by the stockholders who are entitled to fill it under the Restated Certificate. A designation right cannot be exercised after its holder falls below its threshold, and it cannot be transferred except as Section 6.2 permits.

> **Drafting note:** *Market Series A board.* Five seats (two Series Directors, the CEO Director, one Key Holder designee, one Independent) or three seats (one Series Director, CEO Director, one Key Holder designee or Independent) are the most common configurations. A board on which the investors' designees are a majority makes that group a potential "controlling stockholder" under § 144(e)(2)b if they act together; if one investor alone designates a majority, it is one. *Thresholds.* Tie each designation right to a share threshold so the right lapses if the designator sells down; express the threshold in shares (adjusted for splits under Section 6.13), not percentages, so later dilution does not trigger an unintended lapse. *Charter consistency.* The number of Series Directors here must equal the number the Restated Certificate entitles the holders of Preferred Stock to elect, and likewise for Common Directors; otherwise the charter controls and this Agreement cannot deliver the seat.

**2.3 No Designation Made.** If the Person entitled to designate a director under Section 2.2 makes no designation, the director that Person last designated who is then serving shall be re-elected, if still eligible and willing to serve under this Agreement; otherwise the seat stays vacant until filled as Section 2.2 provides.

**2.4 Removal.** Each Stockholder shall vote all Shares it owns or controls the vote of, at all times and in whatever way is needed, so that:

(a) a director elected under Section 2.2 or 2.3 is removed without cause only if (i) the Person entitled under Section 2.2 to designate that director (or the holders of a majority of the shares that hold that right) directs or approves the removal, or (ii) the Person or holders who designated or approved that director have lost the right to do so under Section 2.2;

(b) a vacancy caused by the resignation, removal or death of a director elected under Section 2.2 or 2.3 is filled as Section 2.2 provides; and

(c) a director is removed whenever the Person entitled to designate that director under Section 2.2 asks for removal.

Each Stockholder shall sign any written consent needed to carry out this Section 2.4, and the Company shall call a special meeting of stockholders to elect directors whenever a Person with a designation right asks it to.

> **Drafting note:** Under 8 Del. C. § 141(k), a director elected by the holders of a class or series may be removed without cause only by the vote of that class or series. > **Drafting note:** *Clause (a)* is the core of a "merger drag": once the Approving Parties approve, everyone votes yes. Voting all shares for a merger by written consent extinguishes appraisal rights by operation of § 262(b) (appraisal is available only to holders who did not vote in favor or consent), which is why the proxy in Section 5.2 matters — but the drag-along (and the proxy under it) must actually be invoked before the merger is approved, because a drag-along operates only prospectively (*Halpin*). *Clause (e)(i)* is the express appraisal waiver that *Manti* requires. *Clause (e)(ii)* is the kind of covenant not to sue held facially valid in *NEA v. Rich* (with the intentional-misconduct carve-out the court required); delete it if the Key Holders will not accept it, but understand that common stockholders who are cashed out for little or nothing increasingly sue directors under *Trados* theories. *Clause (f)* lets the Company cash out non-accredited holders so that stock consideration does not require registration or a Rule 506(b) disclosure package.

Confirm with Delaware counsel before omitting them.
