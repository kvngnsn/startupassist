## About this template

**What it does.** The Investors' Rights Agreement ("IRA") is the contract that carries the investors' rights that do not belong in the charter: (1) registration rights (demand, Form S-3 and piggyback), with the indemnification, Rule 144 and market stand-off provisions that make them work; (2) information, inspection and (optionally) board observer rights; (3) a right of first offer (pro rata or preemptive right) on future issuances; and (4) affirmative and negative company covenants (insurance, employee agreements and vesting, compliance, optional national-security, QSBS and cybersecurity covenants). It is one of the "Transaction Agreements" under the Stock Purchase Agreement and is signed at the Initial Closing; it is a closing condition in the SPA. Investors at later Additional Closings join by signing a counterpart signature page (Section 6.10), and Schedule A is updated by the Company without further consent.

**Where it sits.** Sign order at closing: (1) board consent and stockholder consent are effective; (2) the Restated Certificate is filed with the Delaware Secretary of State and effective; (3) the SPA, this IRA, the Voting Agreement, the ROFR and Co-Sale Agreement, Management Rights Letters and any Indemnification Agreements are released from escrow together; (4) funds are wired and shares are issued. If there is an existing IRA from a prior round, this form is an **amended and restated** IRA: the prior agreement can be amended only by the vote specified in it, so confirm that the existing investors signing satisfy the prior amendment provision (Section 6.11 and Recital C).

**Statutory frame — read before negotiating covenants.**

- **§ 122(18).** Since August 1, 2024, 8 Del. C. § 122(18) expressly permits a corporation, "[n]otwithstanding § 141(a)," to contract with current or prospective stockholders, in exchange for minimum consideration determined by the board, and to agree to restrict itself from specified actions, to require the approval of specified persons (including stockholders or directors) before acting, and to covenant that specified persons will take or refrain from actions. Limits: a provision is unenforceable against the corporation to the extent it is contrary to the certificate of incorporation or would be contrary to Delaware law (other than § 115) if placed in the certificate. The amendment was the legislature's response to *West Palm Beach Firefighters' Pension Fund v. Moelis & Co.* (Del. Ch. Feb. 23, 2024), which held several stockholder-agreement approval rights facially invalid under § 141(a). On January 20, 2026 the Delaware Supreme Court reversed *Moelis* on other grounds — holding that such provisions are at most **voidable** (not void) and the challenge was barred by **laches** — without deciding validity. The 2024 statute applies to existing contracts except in litigation pending on August 1, 2024. Practical consequence: the Company covenants in Article 4 and Article 5 (and the optional Series Director approval list in Section 5.4) are § 122(18) contracts. Keep them consistent with the Restated Certificate, and have the board consent recite the § 122(18) consideration determination (the board-consent template does).
- **§ 220(a)(1)h.** After the 2025 amendments (SB 21, 85 Del. Laws c. 6), "books and records" available to any stockholder with a proper purpose include "[a]ny agreement entered into under § 122(18)." Assume every stockholder can obtain this IRA. Do not put anything in it (side economics, investor-specific promises) that the Company would not want a common stockholder to read; put genuinely confidential terms in a side letter and assess whether the side letter is itself a § 122(18) agreement.
- **§ 144.** As amended in 2025, § 144(a) gives directors and officers a safe harbor from equitable relief and damages for interested transactions approved by a majority of disinterested directors (or a committee of at least two disinterested directors if a majority of the board is not disinterested), by informed disinterested stockholders, or that are fair. A stockholder that "has the right, by contract or otherwise, to cause the election of nominees" constituting a majority of the board is a "controlling stockholder" (§ 144(e)(2)b), and controlling-stockholder transactions have their own safe harbor in § 144(b). Insider-led rounds and IRA amendments that benefit a director's fund are interested transactions; route them through the board-consent template's § 144 resolutions.

**Current developments to reflect (as of November 2026).**

1. *NVCA October 2024 and October 2025 updates (IRA).* The October 24, 2024 update raised the S-1 demand minimum from $15M to $20M; added large-fund carve-outs from "Competitor"; clarified that the information covenant does not require the Company to create new information; added an optional restriction on severance arrangements above $200,000; and moved board-level ("Preferred Director") approval matters from the IRA into the model charter. The October 2, 2025 update excluded Sanctioned Parties from the consent calculation, consolidated governance policies as "Required Governance Policies," added outbound-investment monitoring covenants, and broadened observer exclusions (summarized by Nixon Peabody and Foley; Nixon Peabody attributes some of the 2024 changes to 2025, but NVCA's own October 2024 forms already contain them). This form follows the substance of those changes in original language.
2. *Outbound investment.* 31 C.F.R. Part 850 has applied since January 2, 2025. The COINS Act (enacted December 18, 2025 in the FY 2026 NDAA) codified and expanded the program; Treasury issued a proposed rule on October 22, 2026 that would replace Part 850 with a new Part 851. Until final rules issue, Part 850 governs. Section 5.11 is drafted to track "applicable Outbound Investment Rules" so it survives the transition.
3. *CFIUS.* A non-controlling foreign investment in a "TID U.S. business" becomes a "covered investment" if it affords the foreign person access to material nonpublic technical information, **board membership or observer rights or a right to nominate a director**, or involvement in substantive decisionmaking (31 C.F.R. § 800.211(b)). Observer rights in Section 3.4 can therefore by themselves create CFIUS jurisdiction; Section 3.5(c) and Section 5.10 keep "DPA Triggering Rights" away from Foreign Persons unless the Board approves.
4. *QSBS.* For stock issued after July 4, 2025, § 1202 as amended by Pub. L. 119-21 excludes 50%, 75% or 100% of gain after 3, 4 or 5 years, with a $15M per-issuer cap and a $75M aggregate gross asset test (both indexed for inflation after 2026). The QSBS covenant (Section 5.12) is optional and limited to commercially reasonable efforts and information delivery.
5. *§ 220 waivers.* Delaware enforces a waiver of statutory rights only if it is clearly and affirmatively expressed, and courts scrutinize waivers of § 220 inspection rights closely: in *Keesling v. World Class Health, Inc.*, C.A. No. 2026-0140-LM (Del. Ch. Aug. 14, 2026) (Magistrate in Chancery's post-trial final report), a waiver in an option agreement did not bar the holder's statutory inspection demand. Expect a waiver to hold up best when it is explicit and given by a represented, sophisticated investor in exchange for contractual information rights. A waiver in the charter or bylaws alone is not effective. The optional waiver in Section 3.8 is drafted for represented, institutional Investors.

**State-specific points.**

- *California.* A pre-dispute jury-trial waiver is unenforceable in California state court (*Grafton Partners L.P. v. Superior Court*, 36 Cal. 4th 944 (2005)); Section 6.3 includes optional judicial-reference fallback under Cal. Code Civ. Proc. § 638. Employee covenants in Section 5.2 must respect Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code §§ 16600–16600.5 and Cal. Lab. Code §§ 925 and 2870 (no non-competes for California employees; § 2870 notice in invention assignment agreements).
- *Washington, Minnesota, Massachusetts, Illinois, New York and others* restrict employee non-competes or require notice periods; Section 5.2 requires non-competes only "to the extent permitted by applicable law."
- *Blue sky.* Not addressed in the IRA; see the closing checklist.

**Common mistakes this form avoids.**

- Defining "Registrable Securities" only as Preferred Stock (it must be the Common Stock issued or issuable on conversion, because Preferred is not sold in an IPO).
- Leaving the IPO lock-up without a most-favored-nation release clause or applying it to shares bought in or after the IPO.
- A right of first offer that does not carve out "Exempted Securities" by reference to the Restated Certificate, so that option grants and conversion shares trigger offers. Section 4.1(e) incorporates the charter definition by reference so the two lists never diverge.
- Amendment provisions that let the majority strip a single investor's specifically negotiated right (Section 6.7(b)).
- Covenants that conflict with the charter, which § 122(18) makes unenforceable against the Company.
- Forgetting that Additional Closing purchasers must sign a counterpart to receive rights (Section 6.10).

**Deadlines this agreement triggers.** D&O insurance within `{{do_deadline_days}}` days after the date of this Agreement (Section 5.1). Cybersecurity program within `{{cybersecurity_days}}` days (Section 5.13, if used). Annual budget at least `{{budget_days_before_fye}}` days before fiscal year end. Quarterly financials within `{{quarterly_fs_days}}` days of quarter end; annual within `{{annual_fs_days}}` days of year end. The SPA (not this agreement) triggers Form D within 15 calendar days after the first sale (17 C.F.R. § 230.503(a)(1)).

## ARTICLE 1 — DEFINITIONS AND INTERPRETATION

**1.1 Definitions.** Capitalized terms used in this Agreement have the following meanings.

"**Affiliate**" of a Person means any other Person that, directly or indirectly, controls it, is controlled by it, or shares a common controlling Person with it. Without limiting that test: (a) a general partner, managing member, officer, director or trustee of a Person is its Affiliate; (b) an investment fund is an Affiliate of every other fund, existing now or formed later, that is controlled by the same Person or advised or managed by the same investment adviser or management company or by one of its affiliates; and (c) an individual's Affiliates include the individual's Immediate Family Members and any trust or estate-planning vehicle for their benefit.

"**Board of Directors**" or "**Board**" means the Company's board of directors.

"**Business Day**" means a day other than a Saturday, a Sunday or a day on which banks in New York, New York or Wilmington, Delaware may close by law.

"**Code**" means the Internal Revenue Code of 1986, as amended.

"**Common Stock**" means the Company's common stock, par value {{par_value}} per share.

"**Competitor**" means a Person that the Board determines in good faith competes with the Company's business, whether directly or indirectly, including through a controlled Affiliate or through an equity interest in another business. None of the following is a Competitor: (a) any Investor listed on Schedule A on the date of this Agreement or any of its Affiliates; (b) a Person whose only connection to a competing business is a passive, non-controlling equity position below [[Optional: twenty percent (20%)]] of that business's outstanding equity, held primarily as a financial investment; [[Optional: and (c) {{competitor_carve_out_names}}]].

Corporate strategic investors are the opposite case: they often are competitors in fact, and companies commonly refuse them information and observer rights on competitive matters.

"**Damages**" means losses, claims, damages, liabilities, costs and expenses (including reasonable attorneys' fees), whether joint or several, that a party incurs under the Securities Act, the Exchange Act or any other federal or state law, to the extent arising out of or based on a Violation.

"**Deemed Liquidation Event**" has the meaning given in the Restated Certificate.

"**Derivative Securities**" means options, warrants and other securities or rights that are convertible into, or exercisable or exchangeable for, Common Stock, directly or indirectly.

"**DGCL**" means the General Corporation Law of the State of Delaware.
