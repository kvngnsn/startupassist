# Term Sheet for Series Seed or Series A Preferred Stock Financing

## About this template

**What it does.** The term sheet records the business deal for a priced preferred stock round so that counsel can draft the definitive documents: the Amended and Restated Certificate of Incorporation (the "Restated Certificate"), the Stock Purchase Agreement, the Investors' Rights Agreement, the Voting Agreement, the Right of First Refusal and Co-Sale Agreement, and, where requested, Management Rights Letters (together, the "Transaction Agreements"). Almost all of it is a non-binding statement of intent. A short list of provisions — exclusivity, confidentiality, expenses, governing law, expiration and the non-binding statement itself — is binding when signed. The form labels each binding provision "[Binding]" and gathers them in Part 7. A term sheet that is silent on which provisions bind invites an argument that the parties had an enforceable agreement to negotiate in good faith, which Delaware courts have enforced in some circumstances (*SIGA Technologies, Inc. v. PharmAthene, Inc.*, 67 A.3d 330 (Del. 2013), where a term sheet was attached to an agreement that required good-faith negotiation). The form's preamble therefore disclaims any obligation to negotiate or close.

**Where it sits.** Sequence: (1) term sheet signed; (2) exclusivity period begins; (3) due diligence and drafting (lead investor's counsel traditionally drafts the Restated Certificate and investor agreements; company counsel drafts the disclosure schedule and consents, although company-counsel-drafts is increasingly common at seed); (4) Board consent approving the round, the Restated Certificate and any pool increase (8 Del. C. §§ 141(f), 242(b)(1)); (5) stockholder consent (§§ 228, 242(b)); (6) Restated Certificate filed and effective; (7) signature pages released and funds wired; (8) post-closing § 228(e) notice, Form D and state notices, stock ledger update. The term sheet itself requires no board approval, but many companies have the Board approve signing it — prudent if the term sheet contains a binding exclusivity covenant that limits the Company's options.

**Pricing — the most consequential drafting point.** The price per share is the pre-money valuation divided by a defined pre-money share count. Every word of that definition moves value between the founders and the new investors. The form defines "Fully Diluted Capitalization" to include (a) all outstanding shares, (b) all outstanding options, RSUs and warrants, (c) the unallocated pool, (d) the pool increase needed to reach the agreed post-closing pool percentage, and (e) the shares issuable on conversion of all SAFEs and notes. Items (d) and (e) are the two negotiated points:

- *Option pool in the pre-money.* Market, but founder-unfavorable: the new pool shares dilute only the existing holders (the "option pool shuffle"). The company-favorable alternative puts the increase in the post-money. Negotiate the pool size against a written hiring plan for the next 12 to 18 months rather than a round number.
- *SAFE and note conversion shares in the pre-money.* Market: investors expect a pre-money that is "fully loaded" with converting securities, so that new money buys exactly new money ÷ post-money of the company. Post-money SAFEs fix their own ownership; the term sheet must say where their shares sit for pricing purposes.

Run `python3 scripts/conversion_calculator.py` for every term sheet with converting securities and attach the output as Exhibit A. The worked example in [references/venture-financing-terms.md](../../references/venture-financing-terms.md) (Northwind Robotics: $24,000,000 pre-money, $6,000,000 new money, $1,500,000 of post-money SAFEs at a $12,000,000 cap, 10% post-closing pool in the pre-money) produces an Original Issue Price of $2.0276 and a {{shadow_series_name}} price of $1.1053, the figures used in the Restated Certificate template.

**Shadow series.** Each SAFE or note conversion price normally becomes its own series ("Series A-1," "Series A-2") whose Original Issue Price equals the conversion price, so that converting holders get a preference equal to the money they invested rather than a multiple of it. The post-money SAFE requires this ("Safe Preferred Stock"); many notes and older pre-money SAFEs do not. The form includes the shadow series by default.

**Approvals the term sheet must anticipate.** Draft the term sheet so the closing approvals are achievable:

- *Board.* Approves the Restated Certificate (declaring it advisable), the sale of shares, the reservation of conversion shares, the pool increase and the Transaction Agreements (§§ 141(f), 242(b)(1), 245). If an existing director's fund is buying, see "Insider rounds" below.
- *Stockholders.* A majority of the outstanding voting power approves the Restated Certificate (§ 242(b)(1)). Any class or series whose authorized shares are increased or decreased, or whose powers, preferences or special rights are adversely altered, votes separately (§ 242(b)(2)), unless the charter validly opted out of the class vote on authorized-share changes. The 2026 amendments (effective Aug. 1, 2026) confirm that a § 242(b)(2) opt-out does not by itself opt out of § 242(d); § 242(d) matters mainly to listed companies but should be considered when drafting the opt-out in Section A.4 of the Restated Certificate. Existing Preferred Stock protective provisions add their own approval thresholds.
- *Written consent.* Effective when consents from holders of the required votes are delivered within 60 days of the first (§ 228(a), (c)); non-consenting stockholders entitled to vote must receive prompt notice (§ 228(e)).
- *Contractual waivers.* Existing pro rata (preemptive) rights, SAFE side letters, MFN clauses and ROFR rights must be waived or satisfied.

**Securities law.** The round is almost always a Rule 506(b) offering to accredited investors (17 C.F.R. § 230.506(b)); confirm accreditation in the investor questionnaire. A Form D is due within 15 calendar days after the first sale (17 C.F.R. § 230.503(a)(1)), and states require notice filings and fees for the purchasers' states of residence. Do not let the lead investor or company announce the round before closing in a way that could be general solicitation. See [references/securities-law.md](../../references/securities-law.md).

**Foreign investors (CFIUS and outbound).** If any Investor is a foreign person and the Company is a "TID U.S. business" (critical technologies, covered critical infrastructure, or sensitive personal data), a non-controlling investment becomes a CFIUS "covered investment" if it gives the foreign person access to material nonpublic technical information, board membership or observer rights or the right to nominate a director, or involvement in substantive decisionmaking on those matters (31 C.F.R. § 800.211(b)). Some covered transactions require a mandatory declaration at least 30 days before completion (31 C.F.R. § 800.401(g)), which then becomes a closing condition. The form withholds these rights from foreign persons unless the Board approves after a CFIUS analysis. Separately, U.S. investors need comfort that the Company is not a "covered foreign person" under the Outbound Investment Security Program (31 C.F.R. §§ 850.209–850.210), relevant if the Company has subsidiaries, joint ventures or significant operations in a country of concern. The COINS Act (enacted in the FY 2026 NDAA on Dec. 18, 2025) codified and expanded the program; Treasury proposed a replacement rule at a new 31 C.F.R. Part 851 on Oct. 22, 2026 (comments due Dec. 7, 2026). Part 850 governs until a final rule takes effect.

> **Verify:** Before each use, confirm the status of the proposed outbound rule (Part 851), its effective date and any expanded sector coverage, and update the outbound representation in Part 3.

**QSBS (26 U.S.C. § 1202).** For stock issued after July 4, 2025, § 1202 as amended by Pub. L. 119-21 excludes 50%, 75% or 100% of gain on stock held at least three, four or five years, with a $15 million per-issuer cap (or 10 times basis, if greater) and a $75 million aggregate gross assets limit measured before and immediately after the issuance (§ 1202(a)(5), (b), (d)(1)); both dollar figures are indexed for inflation for taxable years beginning after 2026. Investors commonly ask for a representation that the Company believes the shares qualify and a covenant to deliver information and avoid avoidable disqualifications. The most common self-inflicted problem in a round is a stock repurchase: buying back founder or employee shares with round proceeds can disqualify stock under the redemption rules of § 1202(c)(3). Route every proposed secondary through the Company counsel's QSBS review.

**Insider rounds (§ 144, as amended in 2025).** If existing investors that designate directors are leading or participating, the round is an interested transaction. Approval by a majority of the disinterested directors (or a committee of at least two if a majority of the Board is not disinterested), approval by a majority of the votes cast by informed disinterested stockholders, or fairness gives the directors a safe harbor (§ 144(a)). If the insiders are a "controlling stockholder" or "control group" — including a holder with a contractual right to elect a board majority (§ 144(e)) — the stricter § 144(b) process applies. The Delaware Supreme Court upheld the 2025 § 144 amendments against constitutional challenge in *Rutledge v. Clearway Energy Group LLC* (Del. Feb. 27, 2026). Offering the round pro rata to all existing holders (a rights offering) and obtaining an independent view on price reduce risk; see *Carsanaro v. Bloodhound Technologies, Inc.*, 65 A.3d 618 (Del. Ch. 2013), and *In re Nine Systems Corp. Shareholders Litigation*, C.A. No. 3940-VCN (Del. Ch. Sept. 4, 2014) (unfair process found even though the price was fair).

**State-specific points.**

- *California.* No-shop and exclusivity clauses bind the Company, not employees, and raise no § 16600 issue; founder non-competes requested as "Key Holder" covenants are void for California employees (Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code §§ 16600–16600.5) and should be limited to non-solicitation of employees only where lawful and to confidentiality. A Delaware company with most of its business and voting stockholders in California may be a "quasi-California corporation" (Cal. Corp. Code § 2115), which affects cumulative voting and board designation math. State securities notice for California purchasers: Cal. Corp. Code § 25102.1(d) notice for Rule 506 offerings.
- *Other non-compete states.* Minnesota, North Dakota, Oklahoma and others bar or sharply limit employee non-competes; Washington, Massachusetts, Illinois, Colorado and others impose income thresholds and notice rules. Keep the employee covenant "to the extent permitted by applicable law."
- *Community property states.* Ask for spousal consents from married Key Holders in Arizona, California, Idaho, Louisiana, Nevada, New Mexico, Texas, Washington and Wisconsin for the Voting Agreement and ROFR Agreement.

**Common mistakes this form avoids.**

- A price per share stated as a number with no stated pre-money share count, so the capitalization basis is negotiated again in the purchase agreement.
- Silence on whether the pool increase and converting securities sit in the pre-money.
- Forgetting the shadow series, giving SAFE holders a preference multiple of their investment.
- A Major Investor threshold that excludes SAFE holders who have pro rata side letters, creating a conflict between the side letter and the Investors' Rights Agreement.
- A drag-along without the protective conditions dragged holders will ask for (several, capped liability; same consideration; limited representations), which then becomes a closing-week fight.
- Granting a foreign investor a board seat or observer right before the CFIUS analysis.
- Binding provisions that are not labeled, or a no-shop with no end date.
- Board composition that gives one investor a contractual right to elect a majority of the Board without anyone noticing the § 144(e) "controlling stockholder" consequence.

**Deadlines the signing triggers.** Exclusivity runs from signing for `{{exclusivity_days}}` days. The term sheet expires if not countersigned by `{{term_sheet_expiration_date}}`. No statutory deadline runs from the term sheet itself; the closing triggers the § 228(e) notice, Form D (15 calendar days after the first sale) and state notice filings, and the stock ledger and § 151(f) notices.

## Form

---

# SUMMARY OF PROPOSED TERMS
# {{series_name}} PREFERRED STOCK FINANCING OF {{company_name}}

{{term_sheet_date}}

This summary describes the principal terms on which {{lead_investor_name}} (the "**Lead Investor**") proposes to invest in {{company_name}}, a Delaware corporation (the "**Company**"). It is an outline for negotiation, not a contract. Only the provisions marked "[Binding]" create legal obligations, and they do so when this summary is signed by the Company and the Lead Investor. No party is obligated to negotiate further, to sign definitive agreements or to complete the financing unless and until definitive agreements are signed and delivered, and then only on their terms. Each party may end discussions at any time for any reason, subject only to the binding provisions.

> **Drafting note:** Keep the preamble's disclaimer of any duty to negotiate. Delaware will enforce an express agreement to negotiate in good faith (*SIGA Technologies, Inc. v. PharmAthene, Inc.*, 67 A.3d 330 (Del. 2013)), so do not add "the parties shall negotiate in good faith" unless the client wants that exposure.

## 1. Offering Terms

**1.1 Securities.** Shares of the Company's {{series_name}} Preferred Stock (the "**{{series_name}} Preferred Stock**") [[Optional: and, solely for issuance on conversion of the Converting Securities described in Section 1.8, shares of the Company's {{shadow_series_name}} Preferred Stock (the "**{{shadow_series_name}} Preferred Stock**")]]. The {{series_name}} Preferred Stock[[Optional: , the {{shadow_series_name}} Preferred Stock]] [[Optional: and the Company's outstanding {{prior_series_name}} Preferred Stock]] are together called the "**Preferred Stock**." The Company's common stock is called the "**Common Stock**."

**1.2 Amount of the Round.** Up to {{round_size}} of new money in the aggregate, of which the Lead Investor will invest {{lead_investor_amount}}. In addition, the Converting Securities described in Section 1.8 (aggregate purchase amount and, for notes, accrued interest of approximately {{converting_securities_amount}}) will convert at the Initial Closing and do not count toward the new money.

**1.3 Purchasers.** The Lead Investor and other accredited investors that the Company and the Lead Investor approve (with the holders of Converting Securities, the "**Purchasers**"; the Purchasers and the existing holders of Preferred Stock that become parties to the investor agreements, the "**Investors**").

**1.4 Closings.** An initial closing (the "**Initial Closing**") targeted for {{initial_closing_date}}, at which the Company must sell at least {{minimum_initial_closing_amount}} of new money and the Converting Securities will convert. The Company may sell the balance of the round at one or more additional closings on the same terms and at the same price within {{additional_closing_deadline}} (each, an "**Additional Closing**"); investors in an Additional Closing must sign the same Transaction Agreements as Purchasers at the Initial Closing.

> **Drafting note:** Keep the Additional Closing window short and price-protected. Shares sold later at the same price are Exempted Securities and do not trigger anti-dilution, but a long window lets the Company sell stock at a stale price after the business has changed. Milestone (tranched) closings are a different structure: they require stated milestones, an investor's obligation to fund, and a remedy for a failure to fund (often conversion of the defaulting investor's shares to Common Stock). Do not use this form's Additional Closing language for them.

**1.5 Price per Share.** The purchase price per share of {{series_name}} Preferred Stock (the "**Original Issue Price**") equals {{pre_money_valuation}} (the "**Pre-Money Valuation**") divided by the Fully Diluted Capitalization, rounded to four decimal places. Based on the Company's capitalization as of the date of this summary, the Original Issue Price is expected to be approximately {{original_issue_price}} per share. The implied post-money valuation, before giving effect to any rounding, is {{post_money_valuation}}.

> **Drafting note:** These covenants are contracts under 8 Del. C. § 122(18) (effective Aug. 1, 2024). The provision responds to *West Palm Beach Firefighters' Pension Fund v. **5.1 Board Composition.** The Investors, the Key Holders and the Company will agree to vote their shares to elect a Board consisting of: (a) {{series_director_count}} Series Director(s), designated by [[Alternative A: the Lead Investor, for so long as it and its affiliates hold at least an agreed number of shares of Preferred Stock]] [[Alternative B: the holders of a majority of the {{series_name}} Preferred Stock (and, if applicable, one designated by the holders of a majority of the {{prior_series_name}} Preferred Stock)]]; (b) {{common_director_count}} Common Director(s), one of whom will be the Company's then-current Chief Executive Officer and the other designated by the Key Holders holding a majority of the Common Stock held by all Key Holders then providing services to the Company; and [[Optional: (c) {{independent_director_count}} Independent Director(s), designated by the Common Directors and approved by the Series Directors]]. > **Drafting note:** Count the votes. If one Investor, or Investors who agree to act together, can designate a majority of the Board, that Investor or group is a "controlling stockholder" or "control group" under 8 Del. C. § 144(e), and its later transactions with the Company (including insider financings) become controlling stockholder transactions under § 144(b). Seed boards are commonly two Common Directors and one Series Director; Series A boards are commonly two Common, two Series and one Independent, or two Common and one Series. The Independent Director seat often stays vacant for a year; say who fills it and by when.

**5.2 Drag-Along.** If the Board and the Requisite Preferred Holders [[Optional: and the holders of a majority of the Common Stock then held by Key Holders who are providing services to the Company]] approve a Deemed Liquidation Event or a sale of a majority of the Company's voting stock (a "**Sale of the Company**"), each Investor and Key Holder will vote for it, will not exercise appraisal or dissenters' rights under 8 Del. C. § 262, and, if it is structured as a stock sale, will sell its shares on the same terms, subject to the following conditions for each dragged holder:

(a) the holder's representations are limited to its own title to its shares, authority, capacity, enforceability and absence of conflicts, and the holder is not required to make representations about the Company;

(b) the holder's liability is several and not joint, is in proportion to its share of the total consideration (subject to the escrow pro ration in clause (d)), and does not exceed the consideration it actually receives, except for its own fraud;

(d) indemnity obligations for breaches of Company representations are satisfied first from any escrow or holdback, borne by holders in proportion to their consideration;

(e) the holder is not required to agree to a non-competition, non-solicitation or similar covenant, except in its capacity as an employee or officer on terms that similarly situated persons also accept, or to release claims other than claims as a stockholder;

[[Optional: (g) the consideration payable to holders of Common Stock in the sale is at least {{drag_minimum_amount}} per share.]]

> **Drafting note:** The appraisal waiver must be express to bind sophisticated, counseled holders (*Manti Holdings, LLC v. Authentix Acquisition Co.*, 261 A.3d 1199 (Del. 2021)); do not rely on it against unrepresented employees who sign a short joinder. When the Board approves a sale in which the Preferred's preference absorbs most of the proceeds, investor-designated directors face entire-fairness risk (*In re Trados Inc. Shareholder Litigation*, 73 A.3d 17 (Del. Ch. 2013)); a drag-along does not cure that risk, so pair it with an independent process. A drag-along is a transfer restriction under 8 Del. C. § 202(c)(4) and binds transferees only if it is noted on the certificate or in the uncertificated-shares notice (§ 202(a)).
