# Disclosure Schedule (Series Preferred Stock Purchase Agreement)

## About this template

**What it does.** The Disclosure Schedule is the Company's written statement of (1) the lists that the representations in Article 3 of the Stock Purchase Agreement ask for (registered IP, material contracts, option grants, employees, insurance) and (2) the exceptions to representations that would otherwise be untrue (a pending demand letter, a missing invention assignment, a late 83(b) election). It is Exhibit B to the Stock Purchase Agreement, and the lead-in to Article 3 makes the representations true "except as disclosed" in it. A fact properly disclosed here cannot support a contract claim for breach of the corresponding representation; a fact omitted can. The schedule is also the investors' best diligence document, and, because Rule 10b-5 applies to private placements, a misleading or incomplete schedule can create antifraud exposure that no contractual limitation removes.

**Who prepares it and when.** Company counsel drafts it with the CEO, CFO (or outside accountant) and CTO, working from the due-diligence request list and the data room. Investor counsel comments; expect two or three rounds. The final version is delivered at signing, which is usually the Initial Closing. If the Company holds Additional Closings and the Stock Purchase Agreement uses Alternative B of Section 2.3(d), the Company may deliver a Disclosure Supplement for later events; that supplement qualifies only the representations made at that Additional Closing and does not cure anything earlier.

**Standards of disclosure (how to write each entry).**

1. *Number it to the representation.* Use the exact section and subsection numbers of Article 3 of the Stock Purchase Agreement. The Agreement treats a disclosure as qualifying its own numbered section and any other section where its relevance is "reasonably apparent" from the text of the disclosure itself. Do not rely on that standard if you can avoid it: add an express cross-reference ("See also Section 3.11(a), item 4") wherever an item is relevant to more than one representation.
2. *List sections versus exception sections.* Some representations require a list (Sections 3.1, 3.2(a), 3.2(d), 3.2(h), 3.8(b), 3.8(d), 3.8(g), 3.9(b), 3.11(a), 3.14, 3.17(a), 3.18(a), 3.18(b), 3.21, 3.27(c), 3.29 and 3.30). Others are true unless an exception is disclosed (Sections 3.7, 3.10, 3.16, 3.17(g), 3.18(c), 3.24, 3.25(c), 3.26 and 3.31). For a list section, the list is the representation: a missing item is a breach. For an exception section, write "None." when there is nothing to disclose, so the reader knows the section was considered.
3. *Be specific.* Identify the document (title, parties, date, and any amendment), the fact (who, what, when, amount), and the consequence the Company knows of. "Various customer agreements" or "matters described in the data room" discloses nothing. A reference to a document in the data room is useful to identify it but is not a substitute for describing the exception, particularly if the Stock Purchase Agreement includes its optional sentence that mere listing of a document does not disclose an exception.
4. *Materiality.* Disclose what the representation asks for, at the thresholds the Agreement sets (for example, the dollar threshold for Material Contracts in Section 3.11(a)). Including an item is not an admission that it is material (Preliminary Statement 4 says so), so err on the side of disclosure for close calls. Do not, however, "over-disclose" with general disclaimers ("the Company may be subject to claims in the ordinary course"); courts and investors treat them as meaningless, and they hide the real exceptions.
5. *No blanket "deemed disclosed" language.* Company-favorable schedules sometimes say that every document in the data room is deemed disclosed against every representation. Institutional investors reject this, and it conflicts with the Agreement's cross-referencing standard. This form does not include it.
6. *Knowledge-qualified representations still need disclosure.* If a founder knows of a problem, the Knowledge qualifier does not protect the Company; disclose it.
7. *Facts, not legal analysis.* Describe facts, not counsel's assessment of the merits, so as not to waive privilege. For litigation, give parties, forum, case number, claims, amount at issue and status.
8. *Personal Information.* Identify employees by employee number where possible, give compensation and personal details only to the lead's counsel under confidentiality, and keep health, immigration and other sensitive data out of the schedule entirely.
9. *Consistency.* Every number here (shares, prices, option grants, pool size) must match the Restated Certificate, Schedule A to the Stock Purchase Agreement, the Investors' Rights Agreement schedules, the board consents, the stock ledger and the capitalization table. Run the conversion arithmetic for SAFEs and notes with scripts/conversion_calculator.py and attach the result as the Conversion Calculation.
10. *Post-closing fixes.* If an exception will be cured after closing (a confirmatory IP assignment, a foreign qualification, a § 204 ratification), say so here and list it as a post-closing deliverable under Section 6.3 of the Stock Purchase Agreement.

**Market positions on the general terms.**

| Term | Company-favorable | Market / neutral | Investor-favorable | This form |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cross-referencing | Every disclosure qualifies every representation | Qualifies other sections where relevance is reasonably apparent on its face | Qualifies only the section expressly referenced | Market (Preliminary Statement 3) |
| Data room | All data room documents deemed disclosed | Documents may be referenced to identify them; exception must be described | No references to data room | Market (Preliminary Statement 5) |
| Summaries of documents | Qualified by the full text of the document | Qualified by the full text if the document was made available | Summary must stand on its own | Alternatives in Preliminary Statement 5 |
| Materiality disclaimer | Broad | Inclusion is not an admission of materiality | None | Market (Preliminary Statement 4) |
| Supplements | Supplements cure breaches for all purposes | Supplements qualify only later Closings | No supplements | Market (Preliminary Statement 9) |

**Common mistakes this template avoids.**

- Capitalization schedules that omit promised but ungranted equity, side-letter rights (pro rata, MFN, information), warrants to lenders or landlords, and SAFEs or notes signed but not yet funded.
- Option schedules that do not show acceleration, early-exercise rights and the 409A valuation used for each grant.
- Open-source lists that show only libraries and not how they are used (linking, modification, distribution, or network use), which is what determines Copyleft risk.
- Missing invention assignments from early contractors, university-affiliated founders or offshore developers (Section 3.8(e) and (h)).
- National-security sections left blank; investors now expect an express statement on TID U.S. Business status, country-of-concern nexus and bulk sensitive data (Sections 3.28–3.30).
- Disclosure that contradicts the Restated Certificate or Schedule A by a few shares because of rounding; reconcile before signing.

**Deadlines.** None arises from the schedule itself. Items it identifies for cure feed the post-closing deadlines in Section 6.3 of the Stock Purchase Agreement, and any Rule 506(e) disclosure in Section 3.31 must reach each Purchaser a reasonable time before the sale.

# {{company_name}}

# DISCLOSURE SCHEDULE

**Exhibit B to the {{series_name}} Preferred Stock Purchase Agreement dated {{agreement_date}}**

## PRELIMINARY STATEMENTS

1. **Delivery.** {{company_name}}, a Delaware corporation (the "**Company**"), delivers this Disclosure Schedule under Article 3 of the {{series_name}} Preferred Stock Purchase Agreement dated {{agreement_date}} (the "**Agreement**") between the Company and the Purchasers named in it. Capitalized terms used but not defined in this Disclosure Schedule have the meanings given in the Agreement.

2. **Numbering.** The sections and subsections of this Disclosure Schedule are numbered to correspond to the sections and subsections of Article 3 of the Agreement. Headings are included for convenience only and do not affect the meaning of any disclosure.

3. **Cross-references.** A disclosure in any section or subsection of this Disclosure Schedule qualifies the correspondingly numbered section or subsection of Article 3 of the Agreement, and qualifies any other section or subsection of Article 3 only to the extent that its relevance to that other section or subsection is reasonably apparent from the text of the disclosure itself. Express cross-references are included for convenience and do not limit the preceding sentence.

4. **No admission.** The inclusion of any item in this Disclosure Schedule (a) is not an admission or indication that the item is material, is required to be disclosed, or is outside the ordinary course of business; (b) does not establish any standard of materiality; and (c) is not an admission to any third party of any liability, obligation, breach or violation of any contract or law.

5. **Documents.** Where this Disclosure Schedule identifies a document, the document has been made available to the Purchasers. [[Alternative A: Each summary of a document in this Disclosure Schedule is qualified by the full text of that document.]] [[Alternative B: Each summary of a document in this Disclosure Schedule is intended to describe the terms of that document that are relevant to the representation it qualifies; the identification of a document does not by itself disclose any exception that is not described in the summary.]]

6. **"None."** The word "None" in any section means that the Company has no list or exception to disclose against the corresponding representation.

7. **Representations not expanded.** Nothing in this Disclosure Schedule constitutes a representation or warranty of the Company, or expands the scope of any representation or warranty in the Agreement, except to the extent the Agreement expressly so provides.

8. **Personal Information.** Individuals are identified in this Disclosure Schedule by employee or contractor number where practicable. A key to those numbers, and any compensation details withheld from this Disclosure Schedule, have been provided to Purchaser Counsel on a confidential basis and form part of this Disclosure Schedule for purposes of the Agreement.

9. **Supplements.** Any Disclosure Supplement delivered under Section 2.3(d) of the Agreement qualifies only the representations and warranties made at the Additional Closing to which it relates.

> **Drafting note:** Preliminary Statement 3 must say exactly what the lead-in to Article 3 of the Agreement says; if the parties negotiate a different cross-referencing standard there, conform it here. Choose Alternative B in Statement 5 when the Agreement includes its optional sentence that mere listing of a document does not disclose an exception; Alternative A is company-favorable and conflicts with that sentence. Statement 8 lets the Company limit personal data in the document that circulates to every Purchaser.
