# Series Preferred Stock Purchase Agreement

## About this template

**What it does.** The Stock Purchase Agreement ("SPA," and in the other Transaction Agreements the "Purchase Agreement") is the contract under which the Company sells the new series of Preferred Stock. It does five jobs: (1) commits each Purchaser, severally, to buy a fixed number of shares at the Original Issue Price at the Initial Closing, and lets the Company sell the rest of the authorized round at Additional Closings; (2) converts outstanding SAFEs and convertible notes into a shadow series at the Initial Closing and cancels them; (3) carries the Company's representations and warranties, qualified by the Disclosure Schedule, which are the investors' main diligence tool and their only contractual remedy if the facts turn out to be wrong; (4) carries each Purchaser's securities-law and national-security representations, which support the Rule 506(b) exemption and the CFIUS, outbound-investment and sanctions analysis; and (5) sets the closing conditions that tie the round together (Restated Certificate filed, ancillary agreements signed, board seated). Charter rights live in the Restated Certificate; contractual investor rights live in the Investors' Rights Agreement, Voting Agreement and Right of First Refusal and Co-Sale Agreement. The SPA does not repeat those rights.

**Where it sits in the closing.** (1) Term sheet signed. (2) Diligence; Disclosure Schedule drafted by company counsel and reviewed by investor counsel. (3) Board approves the Restated Certificate, this Agreement, the other Transaction Agreements, the issuance of the Shares and of the Underlying Common Stock, any pool increase and the SAFE conversions (8 Del. C. §§ 141(f), 152, 153, 161; § 144 resolutions if insiders are buying). (4) Stockholders approve the Restated Certificate (and any plan increase) by written consent under § 228, including every class and series vote under § 242(b)(2). (5) Signature pages are held in escrow. (6) The Restated Certificate is filed and becomes effective (§ 103(d)); the shares do not exist until then. (7) Company counsel confirms filing, the Purchasers wire funds, signature pages are released, and the Company records the issuance in its stock ledger. (8) Post-closing: § 151(f) notices, § 228(e) notice, Form D and state notice filings, and the deliverables in Section 6.3. Additional Closings repeat steps 5, 7 and 8 for new Purchasers, who sign the Joinder in Annex 1.

**Securities-law frame.** This form assumes Rule 506(b): every Purchaser is an accredited investor, there is no general solicitation, and the Company files Form D within 15 calendar days after the first sale (17 C.F.R. § 230.503(a)(1)); the SEC staff treats the date of first sale as the date on which the first investor is irrevocably contractually committed to invest. When this Agreement is signed at the Initial Closing (the usual practice), that date is the Initial Closing; if signing precedes closing, calendar the filing from signing unless counsel concludes that the conditions left the investors uncommitted. Rule 506 securities are "covered securities" (Securities Act § 18(b)(4)(F)), so states may require only notice filings and fees; use the state table in references/securities-law.md § 9. If the founders have publicly marketed the round, use Rule 506(c) instead, verify accredited status (Rule 506(c)(2)(ii)) and use the Section 4.11 alternative. Non-accredited purchasers are not permitted under this form: they trigger the Rule 502(b) disclosure requirements, which a startup cannot practically meet. The SAFE and note conversions at the Initial Closing are exchanges of one Company security for another with no solicitation fee and rely on § 3(a)(9); the converted shares tack the holding period of the SAFE or note for Rule 144 purposes (Rule 144(d)(3)(ii)). Bad-actor diligence (Rule 506(d)) must cover the Company's directors, executive officers, officers participating in the offering, any 20% voting holder (on a post-closing basis for the lead) and any paid solicitor; the reasonable-care exception in Rule 506(d)(2)(iv) is available only if the Company actually made a factual inquiry, which is why both Section 3.31 and Section 4.14 exist and why the closing checklist calls for questionnaires.

**Seed versus Series A.** A seed round led by an institutional fund now usually uses a short form (the Series Seed documents or a law-firm seed form) with fewer representations, no legal opinion, no Additional Closing lead consent, and an investor-counsel cap that reflects the round size. To produce a seed version from this template: keep Sections 3.1–3.8, 3.10–3.13, 3.15 (Alternative B, management statements), 3.17, 3.19, 3.20 (Alternative B), 3.23 and 3.26–3.33; make 3.9, 3.14, 3.16, 3.18, 3.21, 3.22, 3.24 and 3.25 optional or shorten them; omit Exhibit H; and use the 12-month survival alternative. A Series A led by an institutional fund typically uses the full set, with the national-security representations (Sections 3.28–3.30 and 4.8–4.9) because those funds now ask for them as a matter of course after the NVCA's October 2025 update added outbound-investment and data-security representations to its model SPA (as summarized by Foley & Lardner).

**SAFE and note conversions.** Read every outstanding SAFE, note and side letter before drafting Section 2.6. Under the post-money SAFE, the holder receives the greater number of shares from the standard-price and cap-price calculations, issued as "Safe Preferred Stock" whose price-based terms (liquidation amount, initial Conversion Price, dividend rate) are keyed to the conversion price; the Restated Certificate creates that shadow series (here, `{{shadow_series_name}}` Preferred Stock) so that each holder's liquidation preference equals the cash invested. Each distinct conversion price can require its own shadow series; most deals with several SAFE caps create one series per price (A-1, A-2) or issue a mix of shadow-series Preferred and Common Stock to hit the right preference (see references/convertible-instruments.md § 5.5). Notes add accrued interest to the conversion amount; confirm whether the note requires a majority-holder amendment to convert in this round (for example, if the round is smaller than the note's qualified-financing threshold). The SAFE obligates its holder to sign the round documents; this form makes converting holders "Purchasers" so they sign once and receive the same IRA, Voting Agreement and ROFR rights as cash Purchasers, and Section 2.6 contains the cancellation, calculation acknowledgment and limited release. Pro rata side letters attached to SAFEs give the holder a right to buy cash Shares; give notice early and record exercises in Part 1 of Schedule A. The Restated Certificate's "Exempted Securities" definition (Part C, Section 1, clause (j)) already exempts Shares issued under this Agreement, including SAFE conversion shares, from anti-dilution adjustment.

**Representations: what changed recently and why it matters.**

- *National security (Sections 3.28–3.30; 4.8–4.9).* A non-controlling foreign investment in a "TID U.S. business" becomes a CFIUS "covered investment" if the foreign investor gets access to material nonpublic technical information, board membership or observer rights or a nomination right, or involvement in substantive decisionmaking (31 C.F.R. § 800.211(b)). A mandatory declaration is required, at least 30 days before completion, for certain investments in businesses whose critical technology would need a U.S. export authorization to reach the investor and for certain foreign-government-linked investors (31 C.F.R. § 800.401(b), (c), (g)). The Outbound Investment Security Program (31 C.F.R. Part 850, effective January 2, 2025) can make a U.S. startup a "covered foreign person" if, among other things, it holds an interest in a country-of-concern person engaged in a covered activity and more than 50% of its revenue, net income, capital expenditure or operating expenses comes from such persons (§ 850.209(a)(2)); its U.S. investors then cannot invest or must notify Treasury. The COINS Act (enacted December 18, 2025, in the FY 2026 NDAA) codified and expanded that program; Treasury's October 22, 2026 proposed rule would add a new 31 C.F.R. Part 851, expand the countries of concern beyond China and the covered sectors, and convert covered AI investments to outright prohibitions; comments are due December 7, 2026 and Part 850 remains in force until a final rule takes effect. This form therefore defines "Outbound Investment Rules" to include successor regulations. The DOJ Data Security Program (28 C.F.R. Part 202, effective April 8, 2025, with due-diligence, audit and reporting provisions effective October 6, 2025) prohibits or restricts transactions giving countries of concern or "covered persons" (§ 202.211) access to bulk U.S. sensitive personal data or government-related data, including through investment agreements and vendor or employment arrangements.
- *Artificial intelligence (Section 3.9).* Investors now ask whether the Company trains or fine-tunes models, on what data, under what licenses, and whether customer data or confidential information is used for training. The representation is optional and knowledge-qualified in the parts that depend on third parties' conduct.
- *QSBS (Section 3.20 or Section 6.4).* For stock issued after July 4, 2025, § 1202 excludes 50%, 75% or 100% of gain after three, four or five years, with a per-issuer cap of the greater of $15 million (indexed for taxable years beginning after 2026) or ten times basis, and requires aggregate gross assets of no more than $75 million (indexed after 2026) at all times before and immediately after the issuance (26 U.S.C. § 1202(a)(5), (b), (d)(1)). A round that pushes gross assets over $75 million can disqualify shares issued at a later Additional Closing; measure at each Closing. The rep should be limited to facts the Company controls; the active-business test (§ 1202(e)) and redemption look-back (§ 1202(c)(3)) are partly forward-looking, so the covenant alternative is often the better tool.
- *Beneficial ownership reporting.* FinCEN's final rule (effective August 14, 2026) exempts domestic companies and U.S. persons from Corporate Transparency Act reporting, so this form does not include a CTA representation. Keep it for a Company with a foreign-formed subsidiary registered to do business in the United States.

> **Verify:** Before signing, check (1) the status and effective date of Treasury's COINS Act rule (proposed 31 C.F.R. Part 851) and the countries and sectors it covers; (2) any amendments to 28 C.F.R. Part 202 or DOJ guidance; (3) any CFIUS amendments to 31 C.F.R. Part 800 after November 2, 2026; and (4) the inflation-adjusted § 1202 figures for the year of issuance once published.

**Insider rounds.** If an existing investor with a Board designee, or a group that together has the contractual right to elect a majority of the Board, buys in the round, the financing is an interested transaction under 8 Del. C. § 144 as amended in 2025. Obtain approval by a majority of the disinterested directors (or a committee of at least two disinterested directors), or by a disinterested stockholder vote, and consider offering the round to all existing holders pro rata. The board-consent template contains the § 144 resolutions.

**State-specific variations.**

- *California.* A pre-dispute jury-trial waiver is unenforceable in California state court (*Grafton Partners L.P. v. Superior Court*, 36 Cal. 4th 944 (2005)); Section 7.5 contains an optional judicial-reference fallback under Cal. Civ. Proc. Code § 638. California residents' purchases need a notice filing under Cal. Corp. Code § 25102.1(d) within 15 days after the first sale in California. Section 3.17 must not imply that California employees signed non-competes (Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code §§ 16600–16600.5), and invention-assignment agreements must carry the Cal. Lab. Code § 2870 notice. A Delaware company with most of its business and stockholders in California may be a quasi-California corporation (Cal. Corp. Code § 2115).
- *New York, Texas, Washington and others.* State notice filings for Rule 506 offerings vary in fee and deadline; see references/securities-law.md § 9.2. Washington, Massachusetts, Illinois, Minnesota and others restrict employee non-competes; Section 3.17 represents compliance only "to the extent enforceable under applicable law."
- *Choice of law.* 6 Del. C. § 2708 makes a Delaware choice of law in a contract involving at least $100,000 presumptively valid; most rounds clear that threshold. If a Purchaser insists on another state's law, keep Delaware law for matters of corporate law in any event.

**Common mistakes this form avoids.**

- A Disclosure Schedule that says "see data room" instead of listing items. Section 3 lead-in and the disclosure-schedule template require specific listing, with cross-referencing only where relevance is reasonably apparent.
- A capitalization rep that omits SAFEs, notes, side letters with pro rata or MFN rights, promised but ungranted options (offer letters), warrants to lenders, and accelerated vesting. Section 3.2 requires each.
- Closing before the Restated Certificate is effective (Section 5.1(d) conditions the Initial Closing on it).
- Forgetting the § 151(f) notice for uncertificated shares, or omitting the transfer-restriction legends from it; under § 202(a), a restriction on uncertificated shares that is not in the § 151(f) notice is ineffective against a holder without actual knowledge (Section 2.5(c) and Section 4.5).
- Additional Closings that are open-ended or on different terms, or that never get a Joinder signed (Section 2.3).
- Converting SAFEs without a written conversion calculation the holders acknowledge (Section 2.6(c)).
- Counsel-fee and finder provisions that ignore an unregistered finder's role in the round (Section 7.9; Exchange Act § 15(a)).
- Missing Form D or state notice filings (Section 6.1).
- Deleting an optional Section (2.4, 2.6, 3.9, 3.32, 6.6 or 7.21) or an optional lettered condition in Section 5.1 without renumbering and conforming every cross-reference, including the references in the other Transaction Agreements and in the Disclosure Schedule.
- Letting the Disclosure Schedule's numbering drift from Article 3. The Disclosure Schedule template uses exactly the section numbers of this form.
