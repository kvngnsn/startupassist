## About this template

**What it does.** The consulting agreement engages an independent contractor to perform services described in one or more statements of work ("SOWs"), pays the contractor, and, most importantly for a startup, (1) presently assigns to the Company everything the contractor creates for it, (2) licenses any pre-existing contractor materials embedded in the deliverables, (3) imposes confidentiality and data-security obligations, and (4) documents the facts that support independent-contractor status. It also allocates commercial risk (warranties, indemnities, limitation of liability, insurance) in proportion to the engagement.

**Where it sits.** Sign before any work begins (a contractor owns copyright in pre-signature work until assigned). For each new project, sign a new SOW under the same master agreement. If equity is part of the compensation, the Board approves an NSO grant under the plan after signing. If the contractor is a firm, each individual who performs the services signs the joinder in Exhibit C (a firm cannot convey rights its personnel never assigned to it).

**Worker classification — the threshold question.** Labeling someone a contractor does not make them one. Three federal tests and many state tests apply, and the strictest one governs in practice.
- *IRS (employment taxes).* Common-law control test, with evidence grouped into behavioral control, financial control, and the relationship of the parties; no single factor decides it. Either party may ask the IRS for a determination on Form SS-8 (six months or more). Section 530 of the Revenue Act of 1978 relieves employers of federal employment-tax liability for misclassification if they filed all required Forms 1099, treated all similar workers consistently, and had a reasonable basis (case law, a prior audit, long-standing industry practice, or another reasonable basis). The Voluntary Classification Settlement Program (Form 8952) lets a business that has treated workers as contractors (with Forms 1099 filed for three years and no current audit) prospectively reclassify them, paying 10% of one year's employment tax computed at reduced rates; apply at least 120 days before the reclassification date.
- *FLSA (minimum wage and overtime).* "Economic reality" test: whether the worker is economically dependent on the business or in business for themselves. The 2024 DOL rule (29 C.F.R. part 795, six-factor totality-of-the-circumstances analysis) remains in the Code of Federal Regulations, but WHD Field Assistance Bulletin 2025-1 (May 1, 2025) directs investigators to apply instead Fact Sheet #13 (July 2008) as informed by Opinion Letter FLSA2019-6, while noting that the 2024 rule still applies in private litigation. On February 27, 2026, DOL proposed to rescind the 2024 rule and restore a version of the 2021 test, with two "core" factors (control over the work and opportunity for profit or loss) and three guideposts (skill, permanence, and integration), and with actual practice weighing more than contract terms (91 Fed. Reg. 9932). No final rule had been published as of November 2, 2026.
- *NLRA.* The Board's test (Atlanta Opera, 372 NLRB No. 95 (2023), restoring the common-law agency test from FedEx Home Delivery II) remains Board law as of November 2026, but the Board now has a three-member Republican majority that could revisit it.
- *California (and other ABC-test states).* Under Lab. Code § 2775 (AB 5 as revised by AB 2257), a worker is an employee unless the hiring entity proves all three: (A) the worker is free from control and direction in performing the work, under contract and in fact; (B) the work is outside the usual course of the hiring entity's business; and (C) the worker is customarily engaged in an independently established trade, occupation, or business of the same nature. Prong B makes it nearly impossible to classify an individual software engineer at a software startup as a contractor. Exemptions fall back to the Borello multi-factor test: the business-to-business exemption (§ 2776) requires all twelve criteria (free from control; services provided to the business, not its customers; written contract specifying payment amount, rate, and due date; any required business license; a business location separate from the hiring entity's (which may be the contractor's residence); customarily engaged in an independent business; may contract with other businesses; advertises to the public; provides its own tools; negotiates its own rates; sets its own hours and location; and is not performing work requiring a contractor's license), and the professional-services exemption (§ 2778) covers listed occupations (marketing, human resources administration, graphic design, grant writing, fine arts, enrolled agents, photography and videography, freelance writing and editing, and others) if six conditions are met. Other ABC-test states include Massachusetts for wage-and-hour purposes (M.G.L. c. 149, § 148B, a strict three-prong test), New Jersey (unemployment and wage laws; new N.J.A.C. 12:11 rules effective June 1, 2026 and operative October 1, 2026), Illinois (Wage Payment and Collection Act and unemployment), and Connecticut and many other states for unemployment insurance.
- *Consequences.* Back payroll taxes and penalties, unpaid overtime and minimum wage, expense reimbursement (California Lab. Code § 2802), benefits-plan eligibility claims, state penalties (California Lab. Code § 226.8: $5,000–$25,000 per violation for willful misclassification), and PAGA exposure.

The form builds in the factual indicators of independence (Article 4) and the B2B criteria (Section 4.2) because the agreement is evidence, but **the facts control**. Use this form only when the relationship really is independent; otherwise hire the person as an employee (part-time or temporary employment is often the right answer).

**Copyright: the "work made for hire" trap.** For non-employees, a work is a "work made for hire" only if it is specially ordered or commissioned, falls within one of nine categories (a contribution to a collective work, part of a motion picture or other audiovisual work, a translation, a supplementary work, a compilation, an instructional text, a test, answer material for a test, or an atlas), *and* the parties expressly agree in a signed writing that it is a work made for hire (17 U.S.C. § 101). Software, product designs, and most startup deliverables do not fit. That is why Section 5.2 uses a present assignment ("hereby assigns"), which works for every category of IP. Do **not** add "work made for hire" language to a contractor agreement governed by or performed in California: under Cal. Lab. Code § 3351.5(c) and Cal. Unemp. Ins. Code §§ 686 and 621(d), a person who commissions a work under a written agreement stating that it is a work made for hire (and obtains all copyright rights) is the creator's *employer* for workers' compensation and unemployment insurance purposes, and the creator is the commissioning party's employee for those purposes. Alternative B of Section 5.2 is offered only for non-California engagements where an audiovisual, compilation, or other enumerated-category work makes the designation useful.

**Freelance-worker statutes.** Several jurisdictions require a written contract with specified content and timely payment for individual freelancers: California's Freelance Worker Protection Act (Bus. & Prof. Code §§ 18100–18108, effective January 1, 2025; contracts of $250 or more with an individual freelancer providing professional services; payment by the contract date or, if none, within 30 days after completion); New York State's Freelance Isn't Free Act (Gen. Bus. Law art. 44-A, effective August 28, 2024; $800 or more alone or aggregated over 120 days; payment within 30 days after completion if no date is set) and New York City's law; and Illinois's Freelance Worker Protection Act (820 ILCS 193, effective July 1, 2024; $500 or more). The form's Exhibit A captures the required content (names and addresses, itemized services, value and rate and method of compensation, payment date or the method for determining it, and the date by which the freelancer must submit a list of services rendered), and Section 3.3 sets payment within 30 days.

> **Verify:** Confirm the current thresholds, content requirements, and record-retention obligations of the California, New York State, New York City, and Illinois freelance statutes (and any other jurisdiction where the Consultant is located) before use; these were drafted from knowledge as of November 2026 rather than a fresh read of each statute.

**Equity for consultants.** An NSO (never an ISO, which is limited to employees, I.R.C. § 422(a)(2)) at fair market value on the grant date (Treas. Reg. § 1.409A-1(b)(5)(i)(A)). Rule 701 covers consultants only if they are natural persons who provide bona fide services not connected with capital-raising (17 C.F.R. § 230.701(c)(1)); grant to the individual, not the individual's LLC. Equity paid to a consultant is reported as nonemployee compensation (Form 1099-NEC) at exercise.

> **Verify:** If the Consultant is an entity, confirm with securities counsel whether an option may be granted under Rule 701 to the individual who personally performs the services through that entity, or whether another exemption is needed.

**State-specific notes on restrictive covenants.** Minnesota's non-compete ban applies to independent contractors (Minn. Stat. § 181.988). Washington's non-compete law covers independent contractors (current 2026 threshold: $317,147.09 in annual earnings) and becomes a full ban on June 30, 2027 (ESHB 1155). California voids restraints on any person's lawful profession (Bus. & Prof. Code § 16600), but applies a rule of reason to some business-to-business restraints (Ixchel Pharma, LLC v. Biogen, Inc., 9 Cal. 5th 1130 (2020)); do not include a non-compete or customer non-solicit for an individual California consultant. This form includes no non-compete; the employee non-solicit (Section 13.2) is optional.

**Common mistakes the form avoids.** "Work made for hire" as the only IP clause; no license to the consultant's pre-existing code; no joinder from a consulting firm's engineers; open-source code introduced without review; deliverables containing AI-generated material of uncertain ownership; no DTSA notice; an "agreement" that reads like an employment offer (hours, supervision, benefits, indefinite term); and paying a California freelancer late.

**Deadlines triggered by signing.** Collect Form W-9 (or W-8BEN/W-8BEN-E for a foreign consultant, with treaty and withholding analysis) before first payment; file Form 1099-NEC by January 31 of the following year for nonemployee compensation to a U.S. non-corporate payee that equals or exceeds the threshold in I.R.C. § 6041(a) (by cross-reference in § 6041A(a)(2)), which Pub. L. 119-21, § 70433, raised from $600 to $2,000 for payments made after December 31, 2025, indexed for inflation after 2026; report independent contractors to the state where required (California requires reporting of independent contractors paid $600 or more within 20 days, on Form DE 542); pay freelancers within the statutory period.

# CONSULTING AGREEMENT

This Consulting Agreement (this "**Agreement**") is entered into as of {{effective_date}} (the "**Effective Date**") by and between {{company_name}}, a Delaware corporation (the "**Company**"), and {{consultant_name}}, {{consultant_description}} ("**Consultant**").

The Company desires to engage Consultant to provide certain services, and Consultant desires to provide those services as an independent contractor, on the terms of this Agreement. In consideration of the mutual covenants in this Agreement, the Company and Consultant agree as follows:

## 1. Definitions

**1.1 "Background IP"** means all inventions, works of authorship, software, data, tools, methods, know-how, and other materials, and all Intellectual Property Rights in them, that Consultant (a) owned or controlled before the Effective Date, or (b) develops after the Effective Date independently of the Services and without use of Confidential Information, including the items listed in Exhibit B.

**1.2 "Confidential Information"** has the meaning given in Section 6.1.

**1.3 "Deliverables"** means all documents, software (in source and object code form), designs, data, reports, and other materials that Consultant delivers or is required to deliver to the Company under any SOW.

**1.4 "Intellectual Property Rights"** means all rights, anywhere in the world, in and to patents, patent applications, utility models, and statutory invention registrations (including provisionals, continuations, continuations-in-part, divisionals, reissues, reexaminations, and extensions); copyrights, works of authorship, and mask works, whether or not registered; trade secrets and know-how; trademarks, service marks, trade names, trade dress, logos, and domain names, together with the associated goodwill; moral rights; rights in designs, databases, and data; and all other intellectual or industrial property rights, together with all registrations, applications, renewals, and extensions of any of them and all rights to sue for past, present, and future infringement or misappropriation.

**1.5 "Open Source Software"** means any software that is distributed under a license approved by the Open Source Initiative or that is otherwise distributed as "free software," "open source software," or under similar licensing or distribution terms, including any license that requires, as a condition of use, modification, or distribution, that the software or other software combined or distributed with it be disclosed or distributed in source code form, licensed for the purpose of making derivative works, or redistributable at no charge (a "**Copyleft License**").

**1.6 "Personnel"** means Consultant and each employee, contractor, and agent of Consultant who performs any Services.

**1.7 "Personal Data"** means any information that identifies, relates to, describes, or is reasonably capable of being associated with an identified or identifiable individual or household, and any other information defined as "personal data," "personal information," or a similar term under applicable data protection law, that Consultant receives, accesses, or processes in connection with the Services.

**1.8 "Services"** means the services described in each SOW and any related services Consultant performs for the Company.

**1.9 "SOW"** means a statement of work substantially in the form of Exhibit A that is signed by both parties and refers to this Agreement.

**1.10 "Work Product"** means all Deliverables and all other inventions (whether or not patentable), discoveries, improvements, ideas, designs, processes, methods, software, data, works of authorship, and other materials, and all Intellectual Property Rights in them, that Personnel, alone or jointly with others, conceive, develop, create, reduce to practice, or deliver (a) in performing the Services, (b) with use of Confidential Information or the Company's equipment, systems, or facilities, or (c) that result from or are suggested by any Services, in each case during the term of this Agreement, excluding Background IP.

## 2. Services

**2.1 Performance.** Consultant shall perform the Services described in each SOW in accordance with this Agreement and that SOW. Each SOW is incorporated into and governed by this Agreement. If an SOW conflicts with this Agreement, this Agreement controls unless the SOW expressly identifies the provision of this Agreement it modifies and states that it controls.

**2.2 Changes.** Either party may request a change to the scope, schedule, or fees of an SOW. No change is effective unless set out in a written change order or amended SOW signed by both parties.

**2.3 Acceptance.** Unless the SOW provides otherwise, the Company shall have ten (10) business days after delivery of each Deliverable to test it and notify Consultant in writing whether it materially conforms to the requirements of the SOW. If the Company notifies Consultant of a nonconformity, Consultant shall correct it and redeliver the Deliverable within ten (10) business days, at no additional charge, and the acceptance procedure shall be repeated. If a Deliverable fails to conform after two (2) correction attempts, the Company may terminate the applicable SOW and receive a refund of the fees paid for the nonconforming Deliverable. A Deliverable is accepted when the Company notifies Consultant of acceptance or the acceptance period expires without notice of a nonconformity.

**2.4 Personnel.** Consultant is solely responsible for its Personnel, including their selection, supervision, compensation, benefits, taxes, and compliance with this Agreement. Consultant shall not subcontract or delegate any Services to any person other than its own employees without the Company's prior written consent. Before any Personnel (other than Consultant, if Consultant is an individual) perform any Services, Consultant shall cause that Personnel to sign the joinder attached as Exhibit C (or a written agreement with Consultant that assigns to Consultant, for further assignment to the Company, all Work Product and protects Confidential Information on terms at least as protective as this Agreement), and shall deliver a copy to the Company on request. Consultant is responsible for any breach of this Agreement by its Personnel.

**2.5 Company Cooperation.** The Company shall provide Consultant with the information and access reasonably necessary to perform the Services, as described in the applicable SOW. Consultant's sole remedy for the Company's failure to do so is an equitable extension of the affected schedule.

## 3. Compensation

**3.1 Fees.** The Company shall pay Consultant the fees set out in each SOW. Unless the SOW provides otherwise, fees for time-and-materials Services are payable monthly in arrears based on hours actually worked and accurately recorded, and fees for fixed-fee Services are payable on acceptance of the applicable Deliverables or milestones.

**3.2 Expenses.** The Company shall reimburse Consultant for reasonable, documented out-of-pocket expenses incurred in performing the Services, if the expense is described in the applicable SOW or the Company approved it in writing in advance (email sufficing) and, for any single expense exceeding {{expense_cap}}, the approval specifically identified the expense. Consultant shall bear all other costs of performing the Services, including its own equipment, software, workspace, and administrative costs.

**3.3 Invoices and Payment.** Consultant shall invoice the Company monthly (or as the SOW provides), itemizing the Services performed, the hours worked (for time-and-materials Services), and reimbursable expenses with receipts. The Company shall pay each undisputed invoice within {{payment_days}} days after receipt. If the Company disputes any portion of an invoice in good faith, it shall pay the undisputed portion and notify Consultant of the dispute in writing within that period, and the parties shall work in good faith to resolve it. The Company shall not require Consultant to accept less compensation than is due, or to provide additional services, as a condition of timely payment.

**3.4 Equity.** [[Optional: Subject to approval by the Company's Board of Directors, Company management shall recommend that the Board grant Consultant (or, if Consultant is an entity, the individual Personnel identified in the applicable SOW, if permitted by applicable securities laws) a non-qualified stock option to purchase {{consultant_shares}} shares of the Company's Common Stock under the Company's {{plan_name}} (the "**Plan**"), with an exercise price per share equal to the fair market value of a share of Common Stock on the date of grant, as determined by the Board. The option shall vest in equal monthly installments over {{vesting_months}} months beginning on the Effective Date, subject to the continued provision of services to the Company through each vesting date, and the vested portion shall remain exercisable for {{ptep_months}} months after services end, subject to the Plan and the stock option agreement, which govern the option in all respects. Nothing in this Agreement is a promise of any particular exercise price or value.]]

**3.5 Taxes.** Consultant is solely responsible for all taxes, assessments, and contributions arising from compensation paid under this Agreement, including federal, state, and local income, self-employment, and other taxes, and for any taxes on compensation paid by Consultant to its Personnel. The Company will not withhold taxes from payments to Consultant, except as required by law (including backup withholding if Consultant fails to provide a valid taxpayer identification number), and will report payments on Internal Revenue Service Form 1099 or as otherwise required by law. Consultant shall provide a completed Internal Revenue Service Form W-9 (or the applicable Form W-8) before the first payment.

**3.6 No Other Compensation.** The compensation described in this Article 3 and the SOWs is Consultant's entire compensation for the Services and the rights granted in this Agreement.

## 4. Independent Contractor Relationship

**4.1 Status.** Consultant is an independent contractor. Nothing in this Agreement creates an employment, agency, partnership, or joint-venture relationship between the Company and Consultant or any Personnel. Neither Consultant nor any Personnel is entitled to participate in any employee benefit plan, program, or arrangement of the Company, including any health, retirement, equity (except as provided in Section 3.4), vacation, or sick-leave plan, and Consultant waives any such participation even if Consultant or any Personnel is later reclassified as an employee by any court or agency for any purpose.

**4.2 Control and Manner of Performance.** Consultant controls the manner, means, methods, and sequence by which the Services are performed, subject only to the requirement that the results conform to the applicable SOW. Without limiting the foregoing:

(a) Consultant determines its own hours and place of work, except that Consultant shall attend scheduled meetings and comply with reasonable deadlines in the applicable SOW;

(b) Consultant provides its own equipment, tools, software, and workspace, except for access to Company systems and data necessary to perform the Services;

(c) Consultant is free to provide services to other clients, including other companies in the Company's industry, subject to Articles 5 and 6 and Section 13.1;

(d) Consultant negotiated, and sets, its own rates;

(e) Consultant is customarily engaged in an independently established trade, occupation, or business of the same nature as the Services, maintains its own business location (which may be Consultant's residence), holds any business license or tax registration required by its jurisdiction, and advertises or otherwise holds itself out to the public as available to provide services of the same nature;

(f) Consultant provides the Services to the Company itself and not to the Company's customers; and

(g) the Company will not provide Consultant with training (other than orientation concerning Company systems, security policies, and the subject matter of the Services), a Company email title implying employment, or business cards identifying Consultant as an employee.

> **Drafting note:** Clauses (a)–(f) track the factual indicators of independence under the IRS common-law test, the FLSA economic-reality analysis, and California's business-to-business exemption (Lab. Code § 2776). They help only if they are true; if a clause does not describe the actual relationship, the right answer is usually to hire the person as an employee, not to delete the clause. For an individual consultant in California, also confirm prong B of the ABC test (work outside the usual course of the Company's business) or an applicable exemption.

**4.3 No Authority.** Consultant has no authority to enter into any contract, incur any obligation, or make any representation on the Company's behalf, and shall not hold itself out as having that authority.

**4.4 Workers' Compensation and Insurance.** Consultant is responsible for providing workers' compensation, unemployment, and disability insurance for its Personnel as required by law. Neither Consultant nor its Personnel is covered by the Company's workers' compensation, unemployment, or disability insurance.
