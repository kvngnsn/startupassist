## About this template

**What it does.** The offer letter states the job, pay, equity recommendation, benefits, and conditions of employment, confirms at-will status, and makes the CIIAA a condition of employment. It should be short enough for a candidate to read in one sitting and precise enough that it does not promise anything the Company cannot deliver (a fixed equity percentage, an exercise price, a guaranteed bonus, or employment for a term). Everything binding about IP and confidentiality lives in the CIIAA, and everything about equity lives in the plan and award agreement.

**Where it sits.** Issue after the hiring decision and after any required pay-range disclosure; the candidate signs it and the CIIAA (and any arbitration agreement) before the start date. On or before day one: Form I-9 Section 1. Within three business days after the first day of work: I-9 Section 2 (8 C.F.R. § 274a.2(b)(1)(ii)). After the start date: the Board approves the option grant at the next scheduled grant date using a current 409A valuation.

**Exempt classification.** Exemption requires both a salary test and a duties test.
- *Federal.* After Texas v. U.S. Department of Labor (E.D. Tex. Nov. 15, 2024) vacated the 2024 overtime rule, the Fifth Circuit dismissed the government's appeals in May 2026, and the Department of Labor restored the 2019 rule by technical amendment (91 Fed. Reg. 27833, May 15, 2026). The federal salary level is **$684 per week ($35,568 per year)** (29 C.F.R. § 541.600); the highly compensated employee total-compensation level is **$107,432** (§ 541.601); computer employees may instead be paid at least **$27.63 per hour** (§ 541.400(b)). The DOL withdrew its pending overtime rulemaking from the regulatory agenda on December 15, 2025; no new proposal had been published as of November 2, 2026.
- *State minimums for 2026 (higher than federal):* **California** $70,304 per year (two times the $16.90 state minimum wage for full-time work, Lab. Code § 515(a)); computer professionals $58.85 per hour or $122,573.13 per year (Lab. Code § 515.5). The 2027 figures are $72,384 and $61.03 per hour or $127,108.34 per year. **New York** executive and administrative exemptions: $1,275 per week ($66,300) in New York City, Nassau, Suffolk, and Westchester; $1,199.10 per week ($62,353.20) elsewhere (12 NYCRR § 142-2.14); the professional exemption has no state salary test. **Washington** $1,541.70 per week ($80,168.40), computer professionals $59.96 per hour; in 2027 the threshold splits by employer size ($1,595.70 per week for 1–50 employees; $1,773 per week for 51 or more). **Colorado** $1,111.23 per week ($57,784).
- Paying a startup engineer below the state threshold "because they have equity" does not work; equity does not count toward the salary test.

> **Verify:** The Colorado 2026 figures were confirmed against the 2026 PAY CALC Order (7 CCR 1103-14, effective February 1, 2026); check for the 2027 order before using them after 2026. Confirm that no new federal salary-level rule has been proposed or finalized, and check any other state in which the employee will work (for example, Alaska and Maine also set salary thresholds above the federal level).

**At will.** Every state except Montana presumes at-will employment. Montana's Wrongful Discharge from Employment Act makes a discharge after the probationary period wrongful unless for good cause (Mont. Code Ann. § 39-2-904(1)(b)); the default probationary period is 12 months and may be extended to up to 18 months in total (§ 39-2-910). Use the Montana alternative in Section 7 for Montana employees. Avoid language implying job security ("permanent position," "annual salary of $X for the year").

**Equity.** State a share number, not a percentage. Say that management will *recommend* the grant to the Board, that the exercise price will equal fair market value on the grant date (Treas. Reg. § 1.409A-1(b)(5)(i)(A)), and that the plan and award agreement govern. Never promise an exercise price: the 409A value may increase before the grant is approved. Options granted to employees can be ISOs to the extent of the $100,000 first-exercisable-per-year limit (I.R.C. § 422(d)). For California employees, the grant must be exempt under Cal. Corp. Code § 25102(o) (plan drafter handles it).

**Background checks and fair chance.** Under the FCRA, an employer using a consumer reporting agency must give a clear and conspicuous disclosure "in a document that consists solely of the disclosure," obtain written authorization (15 U.S.C. § 1681b(b)(2)), and give a pre-adverse action notice with a copy of the report and the summary of rights before acting on it (§ 1681b(b)(3)). Do not put the disclosure in the offer letter. Fair-chance laws restrict when and how criminal history may be considered: California (Gov. Code § 12952: employers with five or more employees may not ask about conviction history before a conditional offer and must follow an individualized-assessment and notice process); New York City (Fair Chance Act: only after a conditional offer, with an Article 23-A analysis and at least five business days to respond); Washington (RCW ch. 49.94, as amended in 2025: inquiries only after a conditional offer, effective July 1, 2026 for employers with 15 or more employees and January 1, 2027 for smaller employers); Illinois, Los Angeles, and many other jurisdictions. New York's Clean Slate Act (Crim. Proc. Law § 160.57, effective November 16, 2024) seals older convictions. That is why the form makes the background check a condition of a *conditional* offer.

**I-9 and E-Verify.** The current Form I-9 edition is dated 01/20/25 and expires 05/31/2027; the prior edition may no longer be used. E-Verify is voluntary federally but required for some or all private employers in Alabama, Arizona, Mississippi, and South Carolina, and above size thresholds in Georgia, North Carolina, Florida, Tennessee, and Utah. California prohibits using E-Verify to check an applicant before an offer or to recheck current employees except as required by federal law (Lab. Code § 2814). Illinois imposes notice and procedure requirements on E-Verify users (Right to Privacy in the Workplace Act, as amended effective January 1, 2025). E-Verify employers in good standing may examine I-9 documents remotely under the DHS alternative procedure (8 C.F.R. § 274a.2(b)(1)(ix)).

> **Verify:** State E-Verify mandates and thresholds come from secondary compilations and change often; confirm the employee's state statute before relying on Section 8.2.

**Pay transparency.** Many jurisdictions require a good-faith salary range in job postings, and some require disclosure to applicants on request or before discussing pay: California (Lab. Code § 432.3; employers with 15+ employees for postings, all employers on request; SB 642 defines "pay scale" as a good-faith estimate of the range the employer reasonably expects to pay, effective January 1, 2026); Colorado (C.R.S. § 8-5-201; all employers); Washington (RCW 49.58.110; 15+ employees, range plus benefits; 2025 amendments add a cure period); New York State (Lab. Law § 194-b; 4+ employees) and New York City; Illinois (15+ employees, January 1, 2025); Minnesota (30+ employees, January 1, 2025); Maryland (October 1, 2024); Hawaii (50+); District of Columbia; Vermont (5+, July 1, 2025); New Jersey (10+, June 1, 2025); Massachusetts (25+, October 29, 2025); Virginia (July 1, 2026); Maine (10+, July 29, 2026); Connecticut (October 1, 2026); Cleveland (October 27, 2025); with Delaware (September 26, 2027) and Columbus (enforced January 1, 2027) to come. The offer letter does not itself satisfy posting requirements, but the optional pay-range sentence in Section 3.1 helps document compliance and is required on request in several states. Most of these states and several cities also ban salary-history inquiries; do not ask, and do not reference prior pay in the letter.

> **Verify:** Pay-transparency coverage thresholds, effective dates, and the list of states were compiled on November 2, 2026, partly from secondary sources (Colorado benefits-disclosure details, Hawaii, District of Columbia effective date, Maine and Delaware code sections). Confirm for the employee's location before issuing the letter.

**Signing bonuses and repayment ("stay-or-pay").** California Bus. & Prof. Code § 16608 and Lab. Code § 926 (AB 692), as amended by AB 1697 (2026), apply to contracts entered into on or after **January 1, 2027**; they prohibit most repayment obligations on separation, with an exception for discretionary payments such as sign-on bonuses (§ 16608(b)(2)(D), which after AB 1697 is no longer limited to payments at the outset of employment) that requires a separate agreement, notice of the right to consult an attorney with at least five business days, no interest, proration over a retention period of no more than two years, an option to defer receipt to the end of the retention period, and repayment only on voluntary resignation or termination for misconduct. Connecticut bans required employment promissory notes signed on or after October 1, 2026 (Public Act 26-12). Washington will treat any repayment triggered by joining a competitor as a prohibited non-compete from June 30, 2027 (ESHB 1155). The optional signing-bonus clause in Section 3.3 is drafted to fit inside the California exception when used with a separate repayment agreement.

**Arbitration.** Optional (Section 10). The Federal Arbitration Act generally enforces employment arbitration agreements, including class and collective action waivers (Epic Systems Corp. v. Lewis, 584 U.S. 497 (2018)). Limits: the Ending Forced Arbitration of Sexual Assault and Sexual Harassment Act of 2021 lets the claimant void a pre-dispute arbitration agreement or joint-action waiver for any case that "relates to" a sexual harassment or sexual assault dispute (9 U.S.C. § 402(a)), with the court, not the arbitrator, deciding applicability (§ 402(b)), and a number of courts have held that the entire case leaves arbitration; Sarbanes-Oxley and CFPA whistleblower claims are non-arbitrable by statute (18 U.S.C. § 1514A(e); 12 U.S.C. § 5567(d)). California: Lab. Code § 432.6 (AB 51) is preempted as applied to FAA-covered agreements (Chamber of Commerce v. Bonta, 62 F.4th 473 (9th Cir. 2023); permanent injunction entered January 2024); Armendariz v. Foundation Health Psychcare Services, Inc., 24 Cal. 4th 83 (2000) minimum requirements apply; the employer must pay arbitration fees within 30 days after the due date (Code Civ. Proc. §§ 1281.97–1281.98), although Hohenshelt v. Superior Court (Cal. Aug. 11, 2025) held that forfeiture is not automatic where nonpayment was not willful, grossly negligent, or fraudulent; and non-individual PAGA claims cannot be compelled to arbitration (Adolph v. Uber Technologies, Inc., 14 Cal. 5th 1104 (2023)). Many startups put arbitration in a separate stand-alone agreement; this form includes a complete optional clause.

**State-specific alternatives.** Section 13 collects state-specific text for California, New York, Washington, Massachusetts, Illinois, and Colorado; include the paragraph for the employee's work state and delete the rest.

**Common mistakes the form avoids.** Promising a percentage or exercise price; promising an annual salary "for the year" (implies a term); omitting the CIIAA condition; embedding the FCRA disclosure; asking about salary history; using a Delaware choice of law for a California employee (Lab. Code § 925); conditioning the offer on a background check without following fair-chance procedures; and stating "exempt" for a salary below the state threshold.

**Deadlines triggered by signing.** I-9 Section 1 by the end of the first day; Section 2 within three business days; E-Verify case (if used) by the third business day; New York pay notice (Lab. Law § 195(1)) at hire; California SB 294 Workplace Know Your Rights notice to new hires and the emergency-contact designation at hire (Lab. Code §§ 1550–1559); California Wage Theft Prevention Act notice (Lab. Code § 2810.5) for non-exempt hires; state new-hire reporting within the state's deadline (generally 20 days, 42 U.S.C. § 653a(b)(2)); Board approval of the equity grant.

[[Optional: The Company's good-faith pay range for this position at the time of this offer is {{pay_range}}.]]

**4.2 Paid Time Off.** You will be eligible for paid time off and paid sick leave in accordance with the Company's policies and applicable law.

**4.3 Expenses.** The Company will reimburse you for reasonable, necessary business expenses you incur in performing your duties, in accordance with the Company's expense reimbursement policy and applicable law.

## 5. Equity

**5.1 Option Recommendation.** Subject to approval by the Company's Board of Directors (the "**Board**"), Company management will recommend that the Board grant you an option to purchase {{grant_shares}} shares of the Company's Common Stock under the Company's {{plan_name}} (the "**Plan**"). The option will be an incentive stock option to the maximum extent permitted by Section 422 of the Internal Revenue Code of 1986, as amended (the "**Code**"), and otherwise a non-qualified stock option.

**5.2 Vesting.** Subject to your continuous service with the Company, {{cliff_months}}/{{vesting_months}} of the shares subject to the option will vest on the date that is {{cliff_months}} months after your vesting commencement date (which will be your Start Date, unless the Board determines otherwise), and 1/{{vesting_months}} of the shares will vest on each monthly anniversary of the vesting commencement date thereafter, so that the option is fully vested {{vesting_months}} months after the vesting commencement date.

**5.3 Governing Documents.** The option will be governed in all respects by the Plan and the stock option agreement you will be required to sign. No right to any equity is earned or accrued until the Board approves the grant, and nothing in this Agreement is a promise of any particular exercise price or value. [[Optional: For informational purposes only, as of the date of this letter the Company had approximately {{fully_diluted_shares}} shares outstanding on a fully diluted basis. > **Drafting note:** Candidates often ask what percentage their grant represents. If RSUs are granted instead, describe them and their (typically double-trigger) vesting and settlement in place of Sections 5.1 and 5.2.

**6.2 Company Policies.** You will be expected to comply with the Company's written policies and procedures, including its code of conduct and its policies on harassment, discrimination, information security, and acceptable use of technology, as in effect from time to time.

**6.3 Prior Obligations.** By signing this Agreement, you represent that your employment with the Company will not breach any agreement or obligation you have to any former or current employer or other person, including any non-competition, non-solicitation, or confidentiality obligation, except as you have disclosed to the Company in writing. Please provide the Company with a copy of any agreement that may restrict your activities for the Company before you sign this Agreement, to the extent you are permitted to do so.

## 7. At-Will Employment

[[Alternative B: Your employment will be subject to a probationary period of {{montana_probation_months}} months beginning on the Start Date, which the Company may extend in writing before it expires, for a total probationary period of not more than eighteen (18) months, as permitted by Montana Code Annotated § 39-2-910.
