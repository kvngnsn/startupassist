## About this template

**What it does.** An executive employment agreement sets the executive's role, pay, and equity, and, most importantly, what happens if the executive is pushed out: cash severance and equity acceleration on an "Involuntary Termination" (termination without Cause or resignation for Good Reason), enhanced "double-trigger" protection when the Involuntary Termination is connected to a Change in Control, all conditioned on a release of claims. It also handles the tax rules that most often trip up executive pay: Section 409A (timing of deferred compensation) and Section 280G (golden parachute excise tax). Employment remains at will; the severance is the price of terminating without Cause.

**Where it sits.** The Board (or its compensation committee) approves the agreement and the equity grant. The executive signs the agreement and the CIIAA (ciiaa-employee) at hire. The equity grant is made under the plan by Board action on or after the start date at the then-current 409A fair market value. Investor protective provisions and the voting agreement may require preferred-director or majority-investor approval of executive compensation, new officer hires, or severance arrangements; check the charter and investor agreements before signing. Officers are elected by the Board (8 Del. C. § 142); the agreement should be conditioned on the Board's appointment of the executive to the stated office.

Investors focus on two things: (1) single-trigger acceleration (vesting on the deal itself) reduces acquirers' retention leverage and is usually limited to a fraction (for example, 25%–50%) for founders, if any; and (2) cash severance large enough to burden a company with limited runway. Double-trigger 100% acceleration of time-based awards is widely accepted.

**Section 409A — design rules built into this form.**
- *Separation pay.* Severance paid only on an involuntary separation from service is exempt from § 409A to the extent it is (a) a short-term deferral, paid by March 15 of the year after the year of termination (Treas. Reg. § 1.409A-1(b)(4)), or (b) within the separation-pay-plan exception: no more than two times the lesser of the executive's annualized compensation for the prior year and the § 401(a)(17) limit for the year of separation ($360,000 for 2026, so a $720,000 cap), and paid by December 31 of the second year after the year of separation (Treas. Reg. § 1.409A-1(b)(9)(iii)). Resignation for a Good Reason that satisfies the safe harbor in Treas. Reg. § 1.409A-1(n)(2)(ii) counts as involuntary. Section 9 designates each installment as a separate payment so the exceptions can be stacked.
- *Good Reason safe harbor.* To be treated as involuntary, the Good Reason condition must be a material negative change, the executive must give notice within 90 days after its initial existence, the Company must have at least 30 days to cure, and the separation must occur within two years after the initial existence (Treas. Reg. § 1.409A-1(n)(2)(ii)). The Good Reason definition in Section 7.8 tracks that safe harbor.
- *Release timing.* When severance depends on a release, the executive cannot be allowed to choose the tax year of payment by timing the signature. If the consideration-plus-revocation period spans two calendar years, payment must be made in the second year (IRS Notice 2010-6 § VI.B.2, as modified by Notice 2010-80). Section 6.6 builds this in.
- *Separation from service.* Payment triggers use "separation from service" under Treas. Reg. § 1.409A-1(h) (a reasonably anticipated permanent decrease to no more than 20% of the average level of services over the prior 36 months), not merely "termination of employment."
- *Specified employees.* If the Company's stock is publicly traded on an established securities market when the executive separates, payments of deferred compensation to a "specified employee" (key employees under § 416(i)) must be delayed six months after separation (Treas. Reg. § 1.409A-3(i)(2)). Private companies are not subject to the delay; Section 9.4 applies only if the Company becomes public.
- *Change in Control payments.* A lump-sum CIC payment that is nonqualified deferred compensation can be accelerated on a CIC only if the deal is a "change in control event" under Treas. Reg. § 1.409A-3(i)(5). The definition in Section 7.3 uses the plan's definition, with a § 409A overlay where needed.
- *Reimbursements* (legal fees, expenses) must satisfy Treas. Reg. § 1.409A-3(i)(1)(iv): objectively determinable, paid by the end of the year after the year incurred, no exchange for another benefit, and amounts in one year do not affect another year.

**Section 280G.** If a change in control causes "parachute payments" to a "disqualified individual" equal to or exceeding three times the individual's "base amount" (average annual W-2 compensation for the five prior years), the excess over one times the base amount is non-deductible to the company (§ 280G) and subject to a 20% excise tax on the executive (§ 4999). Market practice is a "best-net" cutback (Section 10): payments are reduced to just below the threshold only if that leaves the executive better off after taxes. A private company can avoid § 280G entirely by obtaining approval of the payments by stockholders holding more than 75% of the voting power of outstanding stock (excluding the affected individuals' shares), after adequate disclosure, in a vote separate from the deal approval; the executive must first irrevocably waive the payments to the extent not approved (§ 280G(b)(5)(A)(ii), (B); Treas. Reg. § 1.280G-1, Q&A-7). Excise-tax gross-ups are now rare and disfavored by investors and proxy advisors.

**Clawback.** Listed issuers must maintain a no-fault clawback of erroneously awarded incentive compensation after an accounting restatement (17 C.F.R. § 240.10D-1). A private company is not subject to the rule, but the agreement should state that compensation is subject to any clawback policy the Company adopts to comply with law (so that adopting one at IPO is not Good Reason). Do not make severance forfeitable because the executive competes if the executive works in Washington: from June 30, 2027, any forfeiture, repayment, or clawback triggered by competition is treated as a prohibited non-compete (ESHB 1155, ch. 149, Laws of 2026). In California, forfeiture-for-competition provisions are void under § 16600.

**State-specific notes.**
- *California.* No non-compete or non-solicit covenants (Bus. & Prof. Code §§ 16600, 16600.5); governing law and forum must be California unless the executive is individually represented by counsel in negotiating those terms (Lab. Code § 925(e)) — the form's Section 13.12 recital documents representation. Final wages are due immediately on involuntary termination and within 72 hours on resignation without notice (Lab. Code §§ 201–202), with waiting-time penalties (§ 203); severance is not "wages" for this purpose but accrued PTO is. Expense reimbursement is mandatory (Lab. Code § 2802). Sign-on bonus repayment terms in contracts entered into on or after January 1, 2027 must satisfy Bus. & Prof. Code § 16608 (separate agreement, notice of right to consult counsel with at least five business days, no interest, proration over no more than two years, an option to defer receipt, and repayment only on voluntary resignation or termination for misconduct) as amended by AB 1697 (2026).
- *New York.* Non-competes are not banned by statute as of November 2026 (S9759/A10023 passed the Senate on June 3, 2026 and remains in the Assembly); New York City and State pay-transparency laws apply to the posting. Release requirements for discrimination claims (Gen. Oblig. Law § 5-336) apply at separation.
- *Washington.* Non-competes are void from June 30, 2027 regardless of when signed (ESHB 1155); until then, enforceable only above the 2026 threshold of $126,858.83 (RCW 49.62.020). Do not include one in a new agreement.
- *Massachusetts.* A non-compete requires garden leave (at least 50% of highest base salary for the restricted period) or other mutually agreed consideration, a 12-month cap, delivery by the earlier of the formal offer or 10 business days before start, and a statement of the right to counsel (M.G.L. c. 149, § 24L).
- *Montana.* After the probationary period (default 12 months, extendable to 18), discharge requires good cause (Mont. Code Ann. §§ 39-2-904, 39-2-910); the at-will statement must be adapted.

**Common mistakes the form avoids.** Severance timing tied to "termination of employment" rather than separation from service; a release that the executive can sign in either December or January; Good Reason without notice and cure (which fails the § 409A safe harbor and invites "constructive termination" claims); "Change in Control" definitions that include ordinary venture financings; CIC acceleration that cannot operate because unvested awards terminate at termination before a later CIC (Section 6.4(c)'s three-month hold solves this); cutback ordering that violates § 409A; and attaching no release form, which lets the Company add new covenants at termination and invites disputes.

**Deadlines triggered by signing.** None by statute at signing. At termination: deliver the release within 7 days after the separation (Section 6.6), allow 21 days (45 days in a group program) to consider and 7 days to revoke for an executive aged 40 or older (29 U.S.C. § 626(f)(1)(F)–(G)); the release must be effective by the 60th day after separation. At a change in control: run the § 280G calculations early (at the term-sheet stage) and, for a private company, solicit the stockholder vote before closing.

# EXECUTIVE EMPLOYMENT AGREEMENT

This Executive Employment Agreement (this "**Agreement**") is entered into as of {{effective_date}} (the "**Effective Date**") by and between {{company_name}}, a Delaware corporation (the "**Company**"), and {{employee_name}} (the "**Executive**").

The Company desires to employ the Executive, and the Executive desires to be employed by the Company, on the terms of this Agreement. In consideration of the mutual covenants in this Agreement, the Company and the Executive agree as follows:

## 1. Employment; Position and Duties

**1.1 Position.** Beginning on {{start_date}} (the "**Start Date**"), the Company shall employ the Executive as its {{employee_title}}, subject to the Board's election or appointment of the Executive to that office. The Executive shall report to {{reports_to}}.

**1.2 Duties.** The Executive shall have the duties, authority, and responsibilities customarily associated with the Executive's position in a company of the Company's size and stage, and such other duties consistent with that position as {{reports_to}} or the Board may assign from time to time. The Executive shall devote the Executive's full business time, attention, and efforts to the Company's business and shall comply with the Company's written policies as in effect from time to time.

**1.3 Outside Activities.** The Executive may (a) serve on the boards of directors of up to [[Alternative A: one (1)]] [[Alternative B: two (2)]] for-profit companies that do not compete with the Company, with the prior written consent of the Board, which shall not be unreasonably withheld; (b) serve on civic, charitable, educational, and professional boards and committees; and (c) manage the Executive's personal investments, in each case so long as those activities, individually and together, do not materially interfere with the Executive's duties, create a conflict of interest, or breach the CIIAA.

**1.4 Place of Employment.** The Executive's principal place of employment is {{work_location}}, subject to reasonable business travel.

## 2. At-Will Employment

**2.1 At-Will.** The Executive's employment is at will, which means that either the Company or the Executive may terminate it at any time, with or without Cause or Good Reason and with or without advance notice, subject only to the Executive's right to the payments and benefits expressly provided in Articles 5 and 6. This at-will relationship may be changed only by a written agreement that expressly states that it changes the at-will relationship and is signed by the Executive and an authorized member of the Board.

> **Drafting note:** For an Executive who works in Montana, replace Section 2.1 with a statement that the Executive's probationary period is twelve (12) months (or a different period of up to eighteen (18) months) and that after the probationary period the Company may terminate the Executive's employment for good cause as provided in Mont. Code Ann. § 39-2-903(5), or otherwise subject to the Montana Wrongful Discharge from Employment Act, and conform Section 6.

## 3. Compensation

**3.1 Base Salary.** The Company shall pay the Executive a base salary at the annual rate of {{base_salary}}, payable in accordance with the Company's regular payroll practices and subject to applicable withholdings (the "**Base Salary**"). The Board or its compensation committee shall review the Base Salary at least annually and may increase (but, except as permitted by Section 7.8(a), not decrease) it.

**3.2 Annual Bonus.** For each fiscal year of the Company ending during the Executive's employment, beginning with the fiscal year in which the Start Date occurs, the Executive is eligible to earn an annual performance bonus with a target amount equal to {{target_bonus_percent}} of the Base Salary paid during that fiscal year (the "**Target Bonus**"), based on the achievement of individual and Company performance objectives established by the Board or its compensation committee after consultation with the Executive. The Board or its compensation committee shall determine in good faith whether and to what extent the objectives have been achieved and the amount of any bonus earned, which may be more or less than the Target Bonus. Any bonus earned for a fiscal year shall be paid no later than March 15 of the following calendar year. Except as provided in Article 6, the Executive must remain employed through the payment date to earn any bonus.

> **Drafting note:** "Employed through the payment date" conditions are generally enforceable for discretionary bonuses, but in California and some other states a bonus that is earned by formula before the payment date may be treated as earned wages. If the bonus is formulaic, consider requiring employment only through the end of the fiscal year.

**3.3 Signing Bonus.** [[Optional: The Company shall pay the Executive a one-time signing bonus of {{signing_bonus}}, less applicable withholdings, on the first regular payroll date after the Start Date. If, within twelve (12) months after the Start Date, the Executive resigns without Good Reason or the Company terminates the Executive's employment for Cause, the Executive shall repay a pro-rata portion of the after-tax amount of the signing bonus, determined by multiplying that amount by a fraction, the numerator of which is the number of days remaining in that twelve-month period after the termination date and the denominator of which is 365, within thirty (30) days after the termination date, without interest.]]

> **Drafting note:** For a California Executive under an agreement entered into on or after January 1, 2027, any repayment obligation must satisfy Bus. & Prof. Code § 16608(b)(2)(D) as amended by AB 1697 (2026): place the repayment terms in a separate agreement, give written notice of the right to consult an attorney and at least five business days to do so, charge no interest, prorate over a retention period of no more than two years, let the Executive elect to defer receipt to the end of the retention period with no repayment obligation, and require repayment only on voluntary resignation or termination for misconduct. Connecticut prohibits required employment promissory notes for agreements signed on or after October 1, 2026 (Public Act 26-12). In Washington, never tie repayment to joining a competitor (ESHB 1155, effective June 30, 2027). Under the NLRA, the General Counsel's prior stay-or-pay memo (GC 25-01) was rescinded in February 2025.

**3.4 Equity Award.** Subject to approval by the Board, the Company shall grant the Executive, as soon as reasonably practicable after the Start Date, [[Alternative A: an option to purchase {{grant_shares}} shares of the Company's Common Stock, with a per-share exercise price equal to the fair market value of a share of Common Stock on the date of grant as determined by the Board (the "**Option**"), which shall be an incentive stock option to the maximum extent permitted by Section 422 of the Code and otherwise a non-qualified stock option]] [[Alternative B: restricted stock units covering {{grant_shares}} shares of the Company's Common Stock (the "**RSUs**")]] (the "**Initial Award**") under the Company's {{plan_name}} (the "**Plan**"). {{cliff_months}}/{{vesting_months}} of the shares subject to the Initial Award shall vest on the date that is {{cliff_months}} months after the Start Date, and 1/{{vesting_months}} of the shares shall vest on each monthly anniversary of the Start Date thereafter, so that the Initial Award is fully vested on the date that is {{vesting_months}} months after the Start Date, in each case subject to the Executive's continuous service through each vesting date and to accelerated vesting as provided in Article 6. The Initial Award is governed by the Plan and the award agreement, except as expressly provided in this Agreement. The Executive acknowledges that no particular exercise price has been promised.

> **Drafting note:** Grant at fair market value supported by a current 409A valuation (Treas. Reg. § 1.409A-1(b)(5)(iv)(B)(2)(i)). Do not state a price or percentage of the company. Private-company RSUs typically vest on a double trigger (service plus a liquidity event) to avoid taxation at service vesting; coordinate with the plan drafter's RSU form and § 409A short-term deferral design.

**3.5 Withholding.** All amounts payable under this Agreement are subject to applicable tax withholding and other deductions required by law or authorized by the Executive.

## 4. Benefits; Expenses; Indemnification

**4.1 Benefits.** The Executive is eligible to participate in the employee benefit plans and programs that the Company makes available to its similarly situated senior executives, subject to the terms of those plans and programs. The Company may amend or terminate any benefit plan or program at any time in accordance with its terms and applicable law.

**4.2 Paid Time Off.** The Executive is entitled to paid time off in accordance with the Company's policies applicable to senior executives [[Optional: , but not less than {{vacation_days}} days per year]]. Accrued, unused paid time off will be paid at termination where required by law or by the Company's policy.

**4.3 Business Expenses.** The Company shall reimburse the Executive for reasonable and necessary business expenses incurred in performing the Executive's duties, in accordance with the Company's expense reimbursement policy and Section 9.6, and as required by applicable law (including California Labor Code § 2802).

**4.4 Indemnification; Insurance.** The Company shall enter into its standard form of indemnification agreement for directors and officers with the Executive, and shall cover the Executive under its directors' and officers' liability insurance policy, both during and, while potential liability exists, after the Executive's employment, on terms no less favorable than those provided to any other officer or director of the Company. This Section 4.4 is in addition to any right to indemnification and advancement of expenses the Executive has under the Company's certificate of incorporation or bylaws or under Section 145 of the Delaware General Corporation Law.

## 5. Termination of Employment

**5.1 Accrued Obligations.** On any termination of the Executive's employment, the Company shall pay or provide to the Executive (a) all Base Salary earned through the termination date and, to the extent required by law or Company policy, all accrued and unused paid time off, in each case by the date required by applicable law; (b) reimbursement of all business expenses properly incurred before the termination date and submitted in accordance with Section 4.3 within thirty (30) days after the termination date; and (c) all vested benefits under the Company's employee benefit plans, in accordance with their terms (collectively, the "**Accrued Obligations**"). The Accrued Obligations are not conditioned on the Release.

**5.2 Resignation from Positions.** On any termination of the Executive's employment, the Executive shall be deemed to have resigned from all offices, directorships, committee memberships, and fiduciary positions with the Company, its affiliates, and its benefit plans, effective on the termination date, and shall sign any documents the Company reasonably requests to confirm those resignations.

**5.3 Return of Property; Transition.** On any termination of employment, the Executive shall comply with Article 5 of the CIIAA (return of property) and, if requested by the Company and at no additional compensation, shall provide reasonable transition assistance during any notice period.

**5.4 Notice of Termination.** Any termination by the Company or the Executive (other than by death) shall be communicated by written notice to the other party that states the termination provision relied on (if any) and the termination date. The Executive shall give the Company at least [[Alternative A: thirty (30)]] [[Alternative B: sixty (60)]] days' notice of a resignation without Good Reason, and the Company may waive all or part of the notice period and accelerate the termination date without that acceleration constituting a termination by the Company or giving rise to Good Reason.

## 6. Severance Benefits

**6.1 Involuntary Termination Outside the Change in Control Period.** If the Executive experiences an Involuntary Termination that does not occur during the Change in Control Period, then, subject to Sections 6.6 and 6.7, the Company shall provide the Executive with the following:

(a) *Salary Continuation.* Continued payment of the Executive's Base Salary, at the rate in effect immediately before the termination date (disregarding any reduction that constitutes Good Reason), for {{severance_months}} months after the termination date (the "**Severance Period**"), paid in installments on the Company's regular payroll dates in accordance with Section 6.6(c);

(b) *Prior-Year Bonus.* Any annual bonus earned under Section 3.2 for the fiscal year ended before the termination date that has not been paid, paid when it would have been paid had the Executive remained employed;

(c) *Pro-Rata Bonus.* [[Optional: A pro-rata portion of the Executive's annual bonus for the fiscal year in which the termination date occurs, determined based on actual performance for the full fiscal year and multiplied by a fraction, the numerator of which is the number of days the Executive was employed in that fiscal year and the denominator of which is 365, paid when annual bonuses are paid to other senior executives for that fiscal year and in any event by March 15 of the following calendar year;]]

(d) *COBRA.* The COBRA Benefits described in Section 6.5 for {{cobra_months}} months; and
