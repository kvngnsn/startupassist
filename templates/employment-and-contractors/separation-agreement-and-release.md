## About this template

**What it does.** The Company pays something the employee is not otherwise entitled to (severance pay, COBRA subsidy, equity acceleration, an extended option exercise period) in exchange for a general release of claims, a reaffirmation of continuing obligations, and related covenants. A release is enforceable only if it is supported by new consideration, knowing and voluntary, and limited to waivable claims. Federal and state laws now prescribe much of the content: the OWBPA for employees aged 40 and over, the SEC's whistleblower rule, the NLRA (for non-supervisory employees), the Speak Out Act, and a growing list of state statutes in California, New York, Illinois, Washington, Minnesota, and Colorado.

**Where it sits.** At an individual separation, present the agreement on or shortly before the separation date. For an executive with an employment agreement, use the release form attached to that agreement (see executive-employment-agreement, Exhibit A) and do not add new covenants. For a group reduction in force, (1) check federal WARN (100+ employees; 60 days' notice of a plant closing or mass layoff, 29 U.S.C. § 2102) and state mini-WARN laws (California, New York, New Jersey, Illinois, and others have lower thresholds or longer notice); (2) use the 45-day version with the decisional-unit disclosures in Exhibit A; and (3) have counsel review the selection criteria for disparate impact before the disclosures are generated.

**OWBPA (employees aged 40 or older).** A waiver of ADEA claims is valid only if all of the following are true (29 U.S.C. § 626(f)(1)):
- (A) it is written in a manner calculated to be understood by the employee (or the average individual eligible to participate);
- (B) it specifically refers to rights or claims under the ADEA;
- (C) it does not waive claims that arise after signing;
- (D) the consideration is in addition to anything of value to which the employee is already entitled;
- (E) the employee is advised in writing to consult an attorney before signing;
- (F) the employee has at least 21 days to consider the agreement, or at least 45 days if the separation is part of an "exit incentive or other employment termination program offered to a group or class of employees";
- (G) the employee has at least 7 days after signing to revoke, and the agreement does not become effective until the revocation period expires; and
- (H) for a group program, the employer discloses in writing, at the start of the consideration period, the class, unit, or group covered, the eligibility factors, the time limits, and the job titles and ages of all individuals selected and not selected in the same job classification or organizational unit (the "decisional unit," 29 C.F.R. § 1625.22(f)(3)).
Material changes to the offer restart the consideration period unless the parties agree otherwise (29 C.F.R. § 1625.22(e)(4)); the form states that changes do not restart it. The employee may sign before the period ends if not induced to do so by fraud, threats, or improved terms for early signing (§ 1625.22(e)(6)). The 7-day revocation period cannot be shortened (§ 1625.22(e)(5)). The employee cannot be required to tender back the consideration before challenging the ADEA waiver, and no penalty or fee-shifting may attach to such a challenge (29 C.F.R. § 1625.23).

**Age-based alternatives.** Section 7 has three alternatives: **A** (under 40; the form still gives a reasonable review period and a short revocation period, which helps show voluntariness and is required by state law in California, Illinois, and Minnesota for some claims); **B** (40 or older, individual: 21 days and 7 days); **C** (40 or older, group program: 45 days and 7 days, with Exhibit A). If in doubt whether a termination is part of a group program (two or more employees offered severance in the same time frame for related reasons), use Alternative C.

**Protected activity and gag-clause rules.**
- *SEC.* Rule 21F-17(a) prohibits any action to impede communications with the SEC, "including enforcing, or threatening to enforce, a confidentiality agreement." The SEC has repeatedly sanctioned separation agreements that required employees to waive SEC whistleblower awards, even where reporting itself was permitted (seven settled actions announced in Press Release 2024-118 (Sept. 9, 2024); In re Foot Locker, Inc., Exchange Act Rel. No. 105542 (May 22, 2026)). **Never waive the right to a government award.** Section 6 preserves it.
- *NLRA.* Under McLaren Macomb, 372 NLRB No. 58 (2023), offering a non-supervisory employee a severance agreement with broad confidentiality and non-disparagement clauses that restrict Section 7 rights is itself an unfair labor practice. As of November 2, 2026 McLaren remains Board law: the Board applied it in Prime Communications, LP, 374 NLRB No. 88 (Apr. 7, 2026), while two members signaled willingness to reconsider, and the General Counsel has asked the Board to overrule it (GC Memo 26-04, Aug. 26, 2026). Supervisors and managers are outside the NLRA (29 U.S.C. § 152(3), (11)).
- *Speak Out Act.* A nondisclosure or nondisparagement clause agreed to *before* a sexual assault or sexual harassment dispute arises is not judicially enforceable as to that dispute (42 U.S.C. § 19403(a)). A separation agreement signed when no such dispute exists is "pre-dispute" as to later-surfacing claims, so carve those disclosures out.
- *California.* A separation agreement may not prohibit disclosure of information about unlawful acts in the workplace and must contain the sentence in Gov. Code § 12964.5(b)(1)(B) (Section 6.2); the employer must notify the employee of the right to consult an attorney and give at least five business days to do so (§ 12964.5(b)(4)); the amount of severance may be kept confidential (§ 12964.5(e)). Settlement agreements of filed claims of sexual harassment, discrimination, and retaliation may not restrict disclosure of factual information about the claims (Code Civ. Proc. § 1001). Contract provisions waiving the right to testify about criminal conduct or sexual harassment when required or requested by legal process are void (Civ. Code § 1670.11).
- *New York.* Gen. Oblig. Law § 5-336 (as amended November 17, 2023) applies where the factual foundation of the claims involves discrimination, harassment, or retaliation: confidentiality of the underlying facts must be the complainant's preference, with up to 21 days to consider and 7 days to revoke; a release is unenforceable if it requires liquidated damages or forfeiture of consideration for violating a nondisclosure or nondisparagement clause, or an affirmative statement that the employee was not subjected to unlawful discrimination. Note that C.P.L.R. 5003-b, for settled litigation, was not amended and still says "twenty-one days."
- *Illinois.* As amended effective January 1, 2026 (P.A. 104-0320), the Workplace Transparency Act permits confidentiality about alleged unlawful employment practices in a separation agreement only if confidentiality is the employee's documented preference and mutually beneficial, the employee receives written notice of the right to have an attorney review the agreement, the confidentiality term is supported by **separate consideration**, no future claims are waived, the employee has 21 calendar days to consider and 7 calendar days to revoke (which the employee may knowingly and voluntarily waive), and the clause does not reach future concerted activity about workplace conditions (820 ILCS 96/1-30). The employer may not unilaterally insert a recital that confidentiality is the employee's preference.
- *Washington.* RCW 49.44.211 voids nondisclosure and nondisparagement provisions concerning conduct the employee reasonably believed to be illegal discrimination, harassment, retaliation, a wage-and-hour violation, or sexual assault, or against a clear mandate of public policy; the settlement amount may be confidential. Requesting or enforcing a prohibited provision carries the greater of actual damages or $10,000, plus fees.
- *Colorado.* C.R.S. § 24-34-407 (POWR Act) voids a provision limiting disclosure of an alleged discriminatory or unfair employment practice unless it applies equally to all parties, contains express carve-outs (family, religious advisor, medical and mental health providers, counsel, financial and tax advisors, government agencies, legal process, and as required by law), states that such disclosures do not constitute disparagement, limits liquidated damages, and attaches a signed addendum attesting compliance (Exhibit B). Penalty: actual damages plus $5,000 per violation.
- *Minnesota.* A release of Minnesota Human Rights Act claims may be rescinded within 15 calendar days after signing, and the employee must be informed of that right in writing (Minn. Stat. § 363A.31, subd. 2). The form's effective date accounts for it.
- *Massachusetts.* A release of claims under the Massachusetts Wage Act is effective only if stated in clear and unmistakable terms that specifically refer to the Wage Act (Crocker v. Townsend Oil Co., 464 Mass. 1 (2012)).

**Non-waivable claims.** A private release cannot waive claims for unemployment or workers' compensation benefits, vested ERISA benefits, claims arising after signing, the right to file an agency charge (though individual monetary recovery from a charge may generally be waived, other than government whistleblower awards), FLSA minimum wage and overtime claims absent court or DOL supervision (Lynn's Food Stores, Inc. v. United States, 679 F.2d 1350 (11th Cir. 1982); Cheeks v. Freeport Pancake House, Inc., 796 F.3d 199 (2d Cir. 2015)), California claims for unpaid wages unless the wages have been paid (Lab. Code § 206.5) and for business-expense reimbursement (Lab. Code § 2804), and indemnification rights. Section 5 lists them so the release is not overbroad.

**Equity treatment.** Vesting normally stops on the separation date. Options typically remain exercisable for three months (ISOs must be exercised within three months after employment ends to keep ISO treatment, I.R.C. § 422(a)(2)). Extending the post-termination exercise period is common severance consideration: an extension to a date no later than the earlier of the option's original maximum term and the tenth anniversary of the grant date is not treated as a new grant for § 409A purposes (Treas. Reg. § 1.409A-1(b)(5)(v)(C)(1)), but an ISO exercised more than three months after employment ends is taxed as a non-qualified option, and the extension may be a modification for ISO and accounting purposes. Section 3 includes the employee's acknowledgment. Early-exercised unvested shares remain subject to the Company's repurchase right. Any acceleration needs Board (or plan administrator) approval.

> **Verify:** Before offering an option-term extension, confirm with the plan drafter and the Company's accountants (a) whether the plan permits it, (b) its ISO-modification consequences under I.R.C. § 424(h) and Treas. Reg. § 1.424-1(e), and (c) the ASC 718 modification charge.

**Medicare.** Section 111 of the Medicare, Medicaid, and SCHIP Extension Act of 2007 (42 U.S.C. § 1395y(b)(8)) requires "applicable plans" (liability insurance, including self-insurance, no-fault insurance, and workers' compensation) to report settlements with Medicare beneficiaries that resolve claims for medical expenses or physical injury. A typical employment release does not trigger it. Include the optional Medicare representation (Section 15) when the employee is 65 or older or receives Social Security disability benefits, or when the release covers physical injury or medical-expense claims.

**Market terms.** Non-executive severance at venture-backed companies commonly runs 2–4 weeks of base salary per year of service (with a floor of 2–8 weeks), plus 1–3 months of COBRA subsidy; executives follow their employment agreements. Mutual non-disparagement (with the Company's obligation limited to instructing named officers and directors) is market for executives; one-way non-disparagement by the employee is common for others, subject to the NLRA and state limits.

**Common mistakes the form avoids.** Signing before the separation date without a bring-down; releasing claims that cannot be released (making the release look overbroad); a waiver of SEC awards; a confidentiality or non-disparagement clause that violates McLaren for a non-supervisory employee, or that lacks the California, Washington, New York, Illinois, or Colorado carve-outs; a forfeiture-of-severance remedy for breach of non-disparagement (barred in New York for discrimination-related clauses and fatal to ADEA tender-back rules if applied to the ADEA waiver); and reaffirming a void covenant (such as a non-compete or non-solicit for a California employee).

**Deadlines triggered by signing.** Final pay on the statutory schedule regardless of the release (for example, California: immediately on involuntary termination, Lab. Code § 201); COBRA notices (the plan administrator generally has 44 days after the qualifying event to send the election notice when the employer is also the administrator); the 21/45-day consideration and 7-day revocation periods (Minnesota: 15 days); payment timing under any § 409A-governed severance arrangement (see executive-employment-agreement).

# SEPARATION AGREEMENT AND GENERAL RELEASE

This Separation Agreement and General Release (this "**Agreement**") is entered into by and between {{company_name}}, a Delaware corporation (the "**Company**"), and {{employee_name}} ("**Employee**").

## 1. Separation; Final Pay; Accrued Benefits

**1.1 Separation Date.** Employee's employment with the Company ends (or ended) on {{separation_date}} (the "**Separation Date**"). Effective on the Separation Date, Employee resigns from all offices, directorships, and other positions Employee holds with the Company and its affiliates and benefit plans, and shall sign any document the Company reasonably requests to confirm those resignations.

**1.2 Final Pay and Accrued Benefits.** Whether or not Employee signs this Agreement, the Company shall pay Employee (a) all wages earned through the Separation Date and, to the extent required by law or Company policy, all accrued and unused paid time off, less applicable withholdings, by the date required by law; (b) reimbursement of business expenses properly incurred through the Separation Date and submitted with supporting documentation within thirty (30) days after the Separation Date, in accordance with Company policy; and (c) any vested benefits under the Company's employee benefit plans in accordance with their terms. Employee's group health coverage ends on [[Alternative A: the Separation Date]] [[Alternative B: the last day of the month in which the Separation Date occurs]], and Employee will receive a separate notice of Employee's right to elect continuation coverage under the Consolidated Omnibus Budget Reconciliation Act of 1985, as amended, or applicable state law ("**COBRA**").

**1.3 No Other Amounts Owed.** Employee acknowledges that, upon receipt of the amounts described in Section 1.2(a), Employee will have received all wages, bonuses, commissions, and other compensation owed to Employee by the Company through the Separation Date, except as provided in this Agreement.

> **Drafting note:** Do not condition anything in Section 1.2 on signing this Agreement. In California, final wages are due immediately on involuntary termination (Lab. Code § 201) and a release of a wage claim is void unless the wages have been paid (Lab. Code § 206.5). If commissions or bonuses are disputed, resolve them expressly (and for California, pay the undisputed amount first).

## 2. Severance Consideration

**2.1 Severance Benefits.** If Employee signs this Agreement within the period described in Article 7, does not revoke it, and complies with its terms, the Company shall provide Employee with the following (collectively, the "**Severance Benefits**"):

(a) *Severance Pay.* [[Alternative A: A lump-sum payment of {{severance_amount}}, less applicable withholdings, paid within ten (10) business days after the Effective Date.]] [[Alternative B: Continued payment of Employee's base salary at the rate in effect on the Separation Date for {{severance_weeks}} weeks (a total of {{severance_amount}}), less applicable withholdings, in installments on the Company's regular payroll dates, beginning on the first regular payroll date after the Effective Date, with the first installment including any amounts that would have been paid between the Separation Date and that payroll date.]]

(b) *COBRA Premiums.* If Employee timely elects COBRA continuation coverage, the Company shall pay directly to the carrier (or reimburse Employee for) the COBRA premiums for Employee and Employee's covered dependents, at the coverage levels in effect on the Separation Date, for {{cobra_months}} months after the Separation Date or, if earlier, until Employee becomes eligible for group health coverage through another employer (which Employee shall promptly report to the Company). If the Company determines that it cannot provide this benefit without potentially violating applicable law or incurring an excise tax or penalty, it shall instead pay Employee a taxable monthly amount equal to the monthly COBRA premium for the remainder of that period.

(c) *Equity Acceleration.* [[Optional: Subject to the approval of the Company's Board of Directors or the administrator of the Company's {{plan_name}} (the "**Plan**"), which the Company shall seek before the Effective Date, accelerated vesting of {{acceleration_shares}} of the unvested shares subject to Employee's outstanding equity awards, effective on the Effective Date.]]

(d) *Extended Exercise Period.* [[Optional: Subject to the approval of the Company's Board of Directors or the Plan administrator, an extension of the period during which Employee may exercise each of Employee's outstanding stock options, to the extent vested on the Separation Date (after giving effect to Section 2.1(c)), until the earlier of (i) {{ptep_months}} months after the Separation Date and (ii) the option's original expiration date, subject to earlier termination in connection with a corporate transaction as provided in the Plan.]]

(e) *Other.* [[Optional: Outplacement services through a provider selected by the Company, at a cost to the Company not to exceed the amount stated in a separate letter, if Employee begins using them within sixty (60) days after the Separation Date.]]

**2.2 Adequacy of Consideration.** Employee acknowledges that the Severance Benefits are in addition to anything of value to which Employee is already entitled, and that Employee would not receive them without signing and not revoking this Agreement.

**2.3 No Further Benefits.** Except as provided in this Agreement, Employee is not entitled to any further compensation, severance, equity, bonus, commission, or benefits from the Company.

## 3. Equity Awards

**3.1 Vesting and Exercise.** Except as provided in Section 2.1(c), vesting of Employee's equity awards ends on the Separation Date. Employee's rights with respect to Employee's equity awards and any shares acquired under them continue to be governed by the Plan, the applicable award agreements, and any stockholder agreements to which Employee is a party, as modified by Section 2.1. Any shares that Employee acquired by early exercise of an option and that are unvested on the Separation Date remain subject to the Company's repurchase right under the applicable agreement.

**3.2 Equity Summary.** [[Optional: As of the Separation Date (and after giving effect to Section 2.1(c)), Employee holds the vested equity awards and shares described in Schedule 1 to this Agreement. Schedule 1 is provided for convenience; the Company's records and the applicable agreements govern.]]

**3.3 Tax Acknowledgment.** [[Optional: Employee acknowledges that (a) any incentive stock option exercised more than three (3) months after the Separation Date will be treated as a non-qualified stock option for federal income tax purposes; (b) the extension described in Section 2.1(d) may cause an incentive stock option to be treated as a non-qualified stock option from the date of the extension; and (c) Employee has had the opportunity to consult Employee's own tax advisor about these consequences and is not relying on the Company for tax advice.]]

## 4. General Release of Claims

**4.1 Release.** In exchange for the Severance Benefits, Employee, on behalf of Employee and Employee's heirs, executors, administrators, representatives, and assigns, hereby fully and forever releases and discharges the Company and its current and former parents, subsidiaries, affiliates, predecessors, successors, and assigns, and each of their current and former directors, officers, managers, employees, stockholders, investors, agents, attorneys, insurers, and employee benefit plans (and the fiduciaries of those plans), in each case in their individual and official capacities (collectively, the "**Released Parties**"), from any and all claims, demands, causes of action, obligations, damages, and liabilities of any kind, known or unknown, suspected or unsuspected, that Employee has or may have against any Released Party arising out of any act, omission, event, or matter occurring on or before the date Employee signs this Agreement (collectively, "**Claims**"), including:

(a) Claims arising out of or relating to Employee's employment with the Company, the terms and conditions of that employment, or its termination;

(b) Claims for wages, bonuses, commissions, equity or other securities, severance, vacation pay, expense reimbursement, or other compensation or benefits, to the extent releasable by law;

(c) Claims for breach of contract (express or implied), breach of the covenant of good faith and fair dealing, promissory estoppel, wrongful discharge, fraud, misrepresentation, defamation, infliction of emotional distress, negligence, invasion of privacy, or any other tort or common-law claim;

(d) Claims of discrimination, harassment, retaliation, or failure to accommodate under any federal, state, or local law, including Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964; the Civil Rights Acts of 1866 and 1991 (including 42 U.S.C. § 1981); [[Optional: the Age Discrimination in Employment Act of 1967 and the Older Workers Benefit Protection Act;]] the Americans with Disabilities Act of 1990; the Rehabilitation Act of 1973; the Genetic Information Nondiscrimination Act of 2008; the Equal Pay Act of 1963; the Pregnant Workers Fairness Act; and the Uniformed Services Employment and Reemployment Rights Act (to the extent waivable);

(e) Claims under the Family and Medical Leave Act; the Employee Retirement Income Security Act of 1974 (other than claims for vested benefits); the Worker Adjustment and Retraining Notification Act and any similar state law; the National Labor Relations Act (to the extent waivable); the Fair Credit Reporting Act; and the Sarbanes-Oxley Act of 2002 and the Dodd-Frank Wall Street Reform and Consumer Protection Act (to the extent waivable);

(f) Claims under the laws of the state in which Employee worked and of every other state and locality, including the state-law claims listed in Article 8 to the extent applicable; and

(g) Claims for attorneys' fees, costs, or expenses.

**4.2 Unknown Claims.** Employee understands that this Agreement releases Claims that Employee does not know or suspect to exist, and that Employee may later discover facts different from or in addition to those Employee now knows or believes to be true. Employee nevertheless intends this release to be full and final as to all Claims, and assumes the risk of any such different or additional facts.

**4.3 Release by the Company.** [[Optional: The Company, on behalf of itself and its subsidiaries, releases Employee from all Claims, known or unknown, that the Company has against Employee arising out of Employee's employment as of the date the Company signs this Agreement, except (a) Claims arising from Employee's fraud, embezzlement, willful misconduct, or criminal conduct, (b) Claims under the CIIAA (as defined in Section 11.1) or this Agreement, and (c) Claims that the Company does not know of on the date it signs this Agreement.]]

## 5. Claims Not Released
