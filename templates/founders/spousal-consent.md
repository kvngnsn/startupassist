## About this template

**Why it matters.** In a community property jurisdiction, property acquired by either spouse during marriage is generally presumed to be community property owned equally by both, regardless of whose name is on the stock ledger. A founder's spouse who never signed the stock purchase agreement can argue that the spouse's one-half community interest is not bound by the vesting schedule, the Company's repurchase option, the right of first refusal, the market stand-off, or a voting agreement — and on divorce or death, the spouse (or the spouse's estate) may be awarded shares free of those restrictions. A signed spousal consent closes that gap. Investors check for it in diligence of founder stock.

**Which jurisdictions.**
- *Community property states (IRS Publication 555):* Arizona, California, Idaho, Louisiana, Nevada, New Mexico, Texas, Washington, and Wisconsin (Wisconsin's Marital Property Act is treated as community property). Puerto Rico also has a community property regime.
- *Registered domestic partners:* California (Cal. Fam. Code § 297.5), Nevada and Washington extend community property rules to registered domestic partners; treat them as spouses for this purpose.
- *Opt-in (elective) regimes:* Alaska (community property agreements and trusts, AS 34.77), Tennessee (community property trusts, effective July 1, 2010), South Dakota (special spousal trusts, 2016), Kentucky (community property trusts, 2020), Florida (community property trusts, effective July 1, 2021), and Kansas (community property trust act, approved April 9, 2026). Property becomes community property under these statutes only if the couple affirmatively opts in (typically by trust or agreement), so ask whether the founder has done so.
- *Moving matters.* Property acquired while domiciled in a community property state generally keeps its character after a move, and some states (notably California) treat "quasi-community property" acquired elsewhere as community property on divorce. Ask where the founder has lived during the marriage.

> **Verify:** Kansas's community property trust act (2026 Kan. Sess. Laws ch. 98) was approved April 9, 2026; secondary sources report an effective date of July 1, 2026, and Kansas statute section numbers had not been assigned when this template was last verified. Also check for any other state that has adopted an opt-in community property trust act since November 2026.

**Equitable distribution states.** Outside community property regimes, a court in divorce divides "marital property" equitably and can award a spouse an interest in stock acquired during marriage. A spousal consent is still useful there to bind the spouse to the restrictions; many companies obtain one from every married founder regardless of residence. This form works in any state.

**What the consent does and does not do.** It (1) acknowledges the spouse read the agreement; (2) agrees that any community or marital interest is bound by its terms; (3) appoints the stockholder as the spouse's attorney-in-fact to act on the shares; (4) agrees to the Company's purchase right if the spouse is awarded shares on divorce; and (5) confirms the spouse had the opportunity to obtain independent counsel. It does not transfer anything to the Company, does not convert separate property into community property or vice versa, and is not a marital agreement under the Uniform Premarital and Marital Agreements Act or similar statutes. It should not be drafted as one.

**Timing and execution.** Sign at the same time as the equity agreement. If the founder marries later, obtain a consent then (the stock purchase agreement obligates the founder to cooperate). Electronic signatures are effective (E-SIGN, 15 U.S.C. § 7001 et seq.). Notarization is not required for enforceability between the parties, but some practitioners notarize spousal consents for founders with large holdings to defeat later claims of non-execution.

**Independent counsel.** The spouse should be told in writing that the Company's counsel does not represent the spouse. In California, courts scrutinize transactions between spouses that advantage one spouse for undue influence (Cal. Fam. Code § 721); the consent's statement that the spouse had the opportunity to consult counsel and signs voluntarily helps.

# SPOUSAL CONSENT

**{{company_name}}**

I, {{founder_spouse_name}}, am the spouse or registered domestic partner of {{founder_name}} (the "**Stockholder**"). I acknowledge and agree as follows:

1. **Review of Agreement.** I have read the {{agreement_title}} dated {{agreement_date}} between {{company_name}}, a Delaware corporation (the "**Company**"), and the Stockholder (the "**Agreement**"), relating to {{founder_shares}} shares of the Company's Common Stock and any securities issued in respect of them (the "**Shares**"). I understand its contents, including that the Shares are subject to vesting, to an option of the Company to repurchase unvested Shares at a price that may be less than their value, to a right of first refusal under the Company's Bylaws or the Agreement, to restrictions on transfer, and to a market stand-off.

2. **Consent; Interest Bound.** I consent to the Agreement and to the Stockholder's execution and performance of it. I agree that any community property, quasi-community property, marital property or other interest I have or may acquire in the Shares, whether under the laws of any state or country, is subject to, and bound by, all of the terms of the Agreement, including the Company's repurchase option and right of first refusal and all restrictions on transfer, to the same extent as the Stockholder's interest.

3. **Appointment of Stockholder as Attorney-in-Fact.** I appoint the Stockholder as my attorney-in-fact, with full power of substitution, to act in my name and on my behalf with respect to any amendment, waiver or exercise of any right under the Agreement, and to take any action with respect to the Shares, including voting them and signing any document relating to a repurchase, transfer or Change in Control (as defined in the Agreement). This appointment is coupled with an interest and is irrevocable, and it survives my death, disability or incapacity to the extent permitted by law.

4. **No Separate Transfer.** I shall not sell, assign, pledge, or otherwise transfer or encumber any interest in the Shares, except in compliance with the Agreement.

5. **Dissolution of Marriage or Partnership; Death.** If my marriage or registered domestic partnership with the Stockholder is dissolved, or I die, and any Shares (or any interest in them) are awarded or pass to me or to my estate, heirs or legatees, those Shares remain subject to the Agreement, and I (or my estate, heirs or legatees) shall sell them to the Company (or, if the Company does not purchase them, to the Stockholder) upon written demand, at the prices and on the terms provided in the Agreement for that event, or, if the Agreement does not provide a price, at the repurchase price as to unvested Shares and at fair market value as determined in good faith by the Company's Board of Directors as to vested Shares.

6. **Amendments; Later Agreements.** I agree that this consent applies to the Agreement as it may be amended in accordance with its terms, and [[Optional: to any later agreement between the Company and the Stockholder relating to equity securities of the Company, including any stock option agreement, restricted stock agreement, voting agreement, right of first refusal and co-sale agreement, or investors' rights agreement, without the need for a further consent from me]].

7. **Advice of Counsel.** I have had the opportunity to obtain the advice of independent legal counsel of my choosing before signing this consent. I understand that counsel for the Company does not represent me. I sign this consent voluntarily, without duress or undue influence.

8. **Effect.** This consent does not transfer any property to the Company, does not by itself change the community, marital or separate character of any property, and is not a marital or premarital agreement. It is binding on my heirs, executors, administrators, legal representatives and assigns.

9. **Electronic Signature; Governing Law.** This consent may be signed electronically. It is governed by the laws of the State of Delaware, except to the extent the law of the jurisdiction of the marital domicile governs the characterization of marital property.

Dated: {{consent_date}}

______________________________
{{founder_spouse_name}}
