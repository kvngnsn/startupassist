## About this template

**What it does.** Transfers to the Company all of a founder's right, title and interest in the technology and intellectual property the founder created or acquired before formation that relates to the Company's business. Without it, the founder — not the Company — owns the pre-formation code, inventions, designs and brand, and every investor, acquirer and patent examiner will see a broken chain of title. The confidential information and invention assignment agreement (CIIAA) covers inventions **during** service; this agreement covers the time **before**.

**Why present-assignment language matters.** The Federal Circuit held in *Stanford v. Roche*, 583 F.3d 832, 841–42 (Fed. Cir. 2009), that an inventor's earlier "agree to assign" promise lost to a later "do hereby assign" to a third party. The Supreme Court affirmed on the Bayh-Dole question only and expressly did not pass on that contract construction (563 U.S. 776, 784 n.2 (2011)). Section 2.1 uses present-tense words of assignment ("hereby irrevocably assigns"), plus an obligation to assign anything that cannot presently be assigned, plus a power of attorney.

**Formalities.** A patent assignment must be in writing (35 U.S.C. § 261) and is void against a later bona fide purchaser for value without notice unless recorded in the USPTO within three months after its date or before the later purchase. A copyright transfer must be in a writing signed by the owner (17 U.S.C. § 204(a)); recordation with the Copyright Office gives constructive notice and priority (17 U.S.C. § 205). Trademarks (and domain names used as marks) are assigned with goodwill (15 U.S.C. § 1060(a)(1)). Exhibit 1 is a short-form confirmatory assignment for recording, so the full agreement (with consideration and schedules) need not be filed publicly.

**Consideration and tax.** Choose one:
- *Shares* (Alternative A of Section 2.2): the technology is all or part of the purchase price under the founder RSPA. The board must value it (§ 152) at not less than the aggregate par value of the shares (§ 153(a)), and the RSPA and board consent must state the same value. For tax, this is a transfer of property for stock, nonrecognized under § 351 only if the property-transferor group controls at least 80% of the Company immediately afterward; the founder's basis in the shares generally equals the founder's basis in the technology (often near zero). If the technology is later sold, how it was characterized matters; involve tax counsel where the technology is valuable or was developed in another entity.
- *No additional consideration* (Alternative B): the founder buys shares for cash and assigns the technology as a contribution to capital. Simpler; no valuation; but the RSPA should still reference the assignment.
- *Nominal cash* (Alternative C): rare; use only when the founder will not receive shares (for example, an early collaborator who leaves before formation).

**Diligence before signing (Schedule C).** Ask each founder about: (1) prior or current employers and any invention assignment or confidentiality agreements — many employers own inventions related to their business or made with their resources, and California Labor Code § 2870 protects only inventions developed entirely on the employee's own time without employer equipment, supplies, facilities or trade secrets and that do not relate to the employer's business or result from work for the employer; (2) university affiliations — university IP policies often claim inventions made with university resources, and federally funded inventions carry Bayh-Dole obligations (35 U.S.C. §§ 200–212); (3) open-source components and their licenses; (4) co-authors or co-inventors who are not founders; (5) work done through a prior LLC or partnership (that entity, not the individual, may own the IP — have it sign too). Where a conflict exists, solve it before closing (release from the employer or university, or a license) rather than papering over it with a representation.

**Excluded technology and license-back.** Founders sometimes hold technology unrelated to the business or want to keep personal projects. List those on Schedule B. Default: **no license back** to the founder of anything assigned (so the founder cannot compete with the Company's own technology after leaving). Optional Section 7.2 grants a narrow personal, non-commercial license if negotiated. Optional Section 3.2 gives the Company a license to any Excluded Technology that ends up incorporated in Company products — important, because otherwise the founder could later assert it against the Company.

**Survives repurchase.** The assignment is not reversed if the founder leaves and the Company repurchases unvested shares. Section 10.2 says so expressly; departing founders sometimes argue the contrary.

**Market positions.** Representations in this agreement are founder representations to the founder's own company; investors rely on them in diligence. Keep them to knowledge-qualified non-infringement and unqualified ownership and authority, which is market. Do not add indemnities by founders at formation; they will be revisited (if at all) in the financing.

# TECHNOLOGY ASSIGNMENT AGREEMENT

This Technology Assignment Agreement (this "**Agreement**") is entered into as of {{effective_date}} (the "**Effective Date**") between {{company_name}}, a Delaware corporation (the "**Company**"), and {{founder_name}} ("**Assignor**").

## RECITALS

A. Before the Effective Date, Assignor, alone or with others, conceived, developed, authored, reduced to practice or acquired technology and intellectual property relating to the Company's business, in anticipation of forming the Company.

B. Assignor desires to transfer all of that technology and intellectual property to the Company, and the Company desires to acquire it, on the terms of this Agreement.

NOW, THEREFORE, the parties agree as follows:

## 1. DEFINITIONS

"**Assigned Technology**" means (a) all Technology that Assignor, alone or jointly with others, conceived, developed, authored, created, reduced to practice or acquired before the Effective Date and that relates to the Business, including the Technology described on **Schedule A**; (b) all versions, modifications, improvements and derivative works of any of the foregoing made before the Effective Date; and (c) all Intellectual Property Rights in any of the foregoing; but excluding the Excluded Technology.

"**Business**" means {{business_description}}, and any business the Company conducts or demonstrably plans to conduct as of the Effective Date.

"**Excluded Technology**" means the Technology, if any, described on **Schedule B**, and the Intellectual Property Rights in it.

"**Intellectual Property Rights**" means all rights of every kind and nature, throughout the world, in, to and under: (a) patents, patent applications, invention disclosures and all related continuations, continuations-in-part, divisionals, reissues, re-examinations, extensions and foreign counterparts, and all inventions, whether or not patentable; (b) copyrights, works of authorship (including software in source and object code), mask works, and all registrations and applications for them; (c) trade secrets, know-how and confidential information; (d) trademarks, service marks, trade names, trade dress, logos, social media handles and Internet domain names, together with all goodwill associated with them, and all registrations and applications for them; (e) rights in data and databases; (f) Moral Rights; (g) all other intellectual and industrial property and proprietary rights; and (h) all rights to sue for and collect damages and other remedies for past, present and future infringement, misappropriation or other violation of any of the foregoing, and all rights of priority and protection of interests in them.

"**Moral Rights**" means any right to claim authorship of a work, to object to or prevent its modification, distortion or mutilation, to withdraw it from circulation, or any similar right, whether existing under the laws of any jurisdiction (including 17 U.S.C. § 106A) and whether called a "moral right" or otherwise.

"**Technology**" means all inventions, discoveries, ideas, processes, methods, formulas, algorithms, models (including machine-learning models and their weights and parameters), software, source code, object code, firmware, designs, schematics, drawings, prototypes, specifications, documentation, data, datasets, research, notes, business plans, product plans, customer and supplier lists, websites, content, and other tangible and intangible materials, in any form or medium.

## 2. ASSIGNMENT

**2.1 Assignment.** Assignor hereby irrevocably and unconditionally assigns, transfers and conveys to the Company, and the Company accepts, all of Assignor's right, title and interest in, to and under the Assigned Technology, free and clear of all liens, claims and encumbrances, together with the right to sue for and collect damages and other remedies for any past, present or future infringement, misappropriation or other violation, and the right to file and prosecute applications for any Intellectual Property Rights in the Company's name. To the extent any Assigned Technology or Intellectual Property Right cannot be assigned by this Agreement as of the Effective Date (including any right that arises after the Effective Date in Technology created before it), Assignor agrees to assign it to the Company immediately when it can be assigned and, until then, grants the Company an exclusive (including as to Assignor), perpetual, irrevocable, worldwide, fully paid-up, royalty-free, transferable license, with the right to sublicense through multiple tiers, to exercise all rights in it.

**2.2 Consideration.**

[[Alternative A: The assignment in Section 2.1 is made in consideration of the Company's issuance to Assignor of {{founder_shares}} shares of Common Stock (or, if the purchase price of those shares is paid partly in cash, of the portion of those shares attributable to the Assigned Technology) under the Founder Restricted Stock Purchase Agreement between the Company and Assignor dated as of the Effective Date (the "**Stock Purchase Agreement**"). The Board of Directors of the Company has determined that the value of the Assigned Technology is {{technology_value}}. Assignor acknowledges that this consideration is adequate and that no further consideration is owed.]]

[[Alternative B: Assignor is purchasing shares of the Company's Common Stock for cash under a Founder Restricted Stock Purchase Agreement dated as of the Effective Date and, as a founder and stockholder of the Company, makes the assignment in Section 2.1 as a contribution to the capital of the Company, for no additional consideration. Assignor acknowledges the receipt and sufficiency of the benefits Assignor derives from the Company's success and that no further consideration is owed.]]

[[Alternative C: The assignment in Section 2.1 is made in consideration of the Company's payment to Assignor of {{cash_consideration}}, the receipt and sufficiency of which Assignor acknowledges. No further consideration is owed.]]

**2.3 Delivery.** On or before the Effective Date (and thereafter promptly upon the Company's request), Assignor shall deliver to the Company all tangible embodiments of the Assigned Technology in Assignor's possession or control, including all source code (with build instructions, credentials for repositories, and version history), documentation, laboratory notebooks, files, models and data, and shall transfer to the Company's control all accounts, repositories, domain-name registrations, social media handles, and cloud resources that host or constitute Assigned Technology. Assignor shall not retain any copy of Assigned Technology except as permitted by Section 7 or as the Company authorizes in writing for performing services for the Company.

**2.4 Records.** Assignor shall, upon request, provide the Company with all information Assignor has regarding the conception, development, authorship and reduction to practice of the Assigned Technology, including the identity of any co-inventor or co-author and the dates and circumstances of creation.

## 3. EXCLUDED TECHNOLOGY

**3.1 Exclusion.** The Excluded Technology is not assigned by this Agreement. Assignor represents that the Excluded Technology does not relate to the Business, except as described on Schedule B.

[[Optional: **3.2 License of Excluded Technology Incorporated in Company Products.** If Assignor incorporates, or permits to be incorporated, any Excluded Technology into any product, service, process or Technology of the Company, Assignor hereby grants the Company a non-exclusive, perpetual, irrevocable, worldwide, fully paid-up, royalty-free, transferable license, with the right to sublicense through multiple tiers, to make, have made, use, sell, offer for sale, import, reproduce, distribute, modify, prepare derivative works of, display, perform and otherwise exploit that Excluded Technology as part of or in connection with that product, service, process or Technology. Assignor shall not incorporate any Excluded Technology into Company Technology without the Company's prior written consent.]]

## 4. MORAL RIGHTS

To the fullest extent permitted by applicable law, Assignor irrevocably waives, and agrees never to assert, any Moral Rights Assignor may have in the Assigned Technology against the Company, its successors, licensees or assigns. To the extent a waiver is not permitted, Assignor irrevocably consents to any act or omission by the Company, its successors, licensees or assigns that would otherwise infringe those Moral Rights.

## 5. FURTHER ASSURANCES; POWER OF ATTORNEY

**5.1 Further Assurances.** During and after Assignor's service with the Company, Assignor shall, at the Company's request and expense (but without further consideration to Assignor), sign all documents, give all testimony, and take all other lawful actions the Company reasonably requests to perfect, record, register, maintain, enforce or defend the Company's rights in the Assigned Technology in any country, including signing the confirmatory assignment in the form of **Exhibit 1** and any declarations, oaths, assignments, and powers of attorney required for patent, copyright, trademark or domain-name filings. The Company shall reimburse Assignor's reasonable out-of-pocket expenses and, after Assignor's service ends, compensate Assignor at a reasonable hourly rate for time spent at the Company's request.

**5.2 Power of Attorney.** Assignor irrevocably designates and appoints the Company and each of its duly authorized officers and agents as Assignor's agent and attorney-in-fact, with full power of substitution, to act for and on Assignor's behalf to sign, verify and file any document described in Section 5.1, and to do all other lawfully permitted acts to further the purposes of Section 5.1, with the same legal force and effect as if signed by Assignor, if the Company is unable, after reasonable effort, to obtain Assignor's signature within ten (10) business days after requesting it, whether because of Assignor's unavailability, incapacity, refusal or otherwise. This power of attorney is coupled with an interest, is irrevocable, and survives Assignor's death, disability or incapacity.

## 6. REPRESENTATIONS AND WARRANTIES OF ASSIGNOR

Assignor represents and warrants to the Company that, except as disclosed on **Schedule C**:

(a) *Ownership.* Assignor is the sole owner of the Assigned Technology (or, as to Assigned Technology created jointly with another founder who is simultaneously assigning that person's interest to the Company, a co-owner with that founder), and has the full right and power to assign it to the Company free and clear of all liens, claims and encumbrances.

(b) *No prior grants.* Assignor has not assigned, licensed, optioned, pledged or otherwise transferred, and has not agreed to transfer, any right in the Assigned Technology to any person, and no person has any right, license or option in it.

(c) *No employer, university or government rights.* No current or former employer, client, educational institution, research institution or governmental authority has or may claim any right in the Assigned Technology. None of the Assigned Technology was conceived, developed or reduced to practice (i) in the course of Assignor's employment or engagement by any other person, (ii) using the equipment, supplies, facilities, funding, personnel or confidential information of any other person, or (iii) with funding from any governmental authority, university or research institution.

(d) *No conflict.* The execution and performance of this Agreement do not breach any agreement, including any confidentiality, invention assignment, non-competition or non-solicitation agreement, to which Assignor is a party or by which Assignor is bound, and do not require the consent of any person.

(e) *Originality; no infringement.* To Assignor's knowledge, the Assigned Technology is original to Assignor (and any co-founder identified under clause (a)) and does not infringe, misappropriate or otherwise violate the Intellectual Property Rights of any person. Assignor has received no notice or claim of any such infringement, misappropriation or violation, and knows of no basis for one.

(f) *Confidentiality.* Assignor has taken reasonable measures to protect the confidentiality of the trade secrets included in the Assigned Technology and has not disclosed any of them to any person except under a written obligation of confidentiality or to another founder of the Company.

(g) *Open source and third-party components.* Schedule C identifies each item of open-source or other third-party software, model, dataset or content that is incorporated into, linked with, or distributed with the Assigned Technology, and the license governing it. No such item is used in a manner that requires the disclosure or licensing of any source code of the Assigned Technology, or grants any third party any right in it, by reason of the license terms.

(h) *Registrations.* Schedule A identifies every patent, patent application, copyright registration, trademark application or registration, and domain-name registration included in the Assigned Technology.

(i) *Personal data.* To Assignor's knowledge, any personal data included in the Assigned Technology was collected and processed in compliance with applicable law and any applicable privacy notice, and may lawfully be transferred to and used by the Company.

## 7. NO LICENSE BACK

**7.1 No License.** Except as provided in Section 7.2 (if included), Assignor retains no right or license, express or implied, in or to any Assigned Technology, and nothing in this Agreement or in any course of dealing grants Assignor any right to use any Assigned Technology except as required to perform services for the Company.

[[Optional: **7.2 Limited Personal License.** The Company grants Assignor a non-exclusive, non-transferable, non-sublicensable, royalty-free license to use the items of Assigned Technology identified on Schedule A as "Personal License Items" solely for Assignor's personal, non-commercial, academic or portfolio purposes, and in no event (a) in competition with the Company, (b) in any manner that discloses any trade secret or confidential information of the Company, or (c) after the Company gives Assignor written notice terminating this license because the Board determines in good faith that continued use is reasonably likely to harm the Company. The Company retains all ownership.]]

## 8. CONFIDENTIALITY

Assignor shall hold the Assigned Technology in strict confidence as confidential information of the Company and shall not use or disclose it except for the benefit of the Company, in accordance with Assignor's confidential information and invention assignment agreement with the Company (whose definitions and remedies apply to the Assigned Technology as if it were Company confidential information created during Assignor's service). This Section 8 does not limit any immunity under 18 U.S.C. § 1833(b) for disclosures of trade secrets made in confidence to a government official or an attorney solely for the purpose of reporting or investigating a suspected violation of law, or in a complaint or other document filed under seal in a lawsuit or other proceeding.

## 9. JOINTLY CREATED TECHNOLOGY

If any Assigned Technology was created jointly with any other person, (a) Assignor assigns all of Assignor's undivided interest in it under Section 2.1; (b) Assignor shall cooperate with the Company in obtaining an assignment from each other co-owner; and (c) Assignor waives, as against the Company and its successors and licensees, any right to an accounting from any co-owner or licensee for the exploitation of that Technology.

## 10. REMEDIES; NO RESCISSION

**10.1 Equitable Relief.** Assignor acknowledges that any breach of Sections 2, 5, 6 or 8 would cause the Company irreparable harm for which monetary damages would be inadequate, and that the Company may obtain specific performance and injunctive relief, without posting a bond, in addition to any other remedy.

**10.2 No Rescission or Reversion.** The assignment in Section 2.1 is absolute and irrevocable. It is not affected by, and no Assigned Technology reverts to Assignor upon, (a) the termination of Assignor's service with the Company for any reason; (b) the repurchase by the Company of any shares of capital stock held by Assignor (including under the Stock Purchase Agreement, if applicable); (c) any breach of this Agreement or any other agreement by the Company; or (d) the dissolution of the Company. Assignor's remedies for any breach by the Company are limited to monetary damages, and Assignor waives any right to rescind this Agreement.

**11.7 Severability.** If any provision is held invalid or unenforceable, it shall be enforced to the maximum extent permissible, and the remaining provisions remain in effect.

> **Drafting note:** Notarization is not required to record a patent assignment at the USPTO, but under 35 U.S.C. § 261 an acknowledgment before a notary is prima facie evidence of the execution of the assignment, and some foreign patent offices require it. Record patent and trademark assignments through the USPTO Assignment Center and copyright transfers with the Copyright Office. Record patent assignments within three months of signing to protect against later purchasers (35 U.S.C. § 261). Use "None" in any list that does not apply.
