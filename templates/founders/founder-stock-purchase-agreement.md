## About this template

**What it does.** The Company sells shares of Common Stock to a founder (the "Purchaser") at fair market value — normally par value or a fraction of a cent above it — paid in cash, by assigning pre-formation technology, or both. The shares are issued fully vested in the legal sense (the founder owns and votes them immediately) but are subject to a Company option to repurchase **unvested** shares at the lower of cost or fair market value if the founder's service ends. Vesting is time-based (usually four years with a one-year cliff). The agreement also restricts transfers, preserves the Company's right of first refusal, imposes a market stand-off, and documents the founder's investment representations and tax acknowledgments.

**Where it sits in the sequence.** The charter is filed; the incorporator adopts bylaws and elects the board; the board, in the initial board consent, elects officers, determines the value of any technology contributed, approves this form, and authorizes the issuance. Only then is this agreement signed and the shares issued. The date of issuance (the "Closing Date") is the "transfer" date for § 83 purposes and starts the **30-day 83(b) deadline**.

**Why founders buy their stock rather than receive it "for services."** Stock transferred in connection with services is taxed under § 83. If shares are simply issued "for services," the founder has ordinary compensation income equal to their fair market value, and — if they are unvested — that income is recognized as they vest, at then-current (possibly much higher) values, unless an 83(b) election is filed. Having the founder pay full fair market value (par, at formation, when the company has no assets) makes the income zero, and the 83(b) election then locks in zero income and starts the capital-gain and § 1202 holding periods. Section 152 permits any "benefit to the corporation" as consideration, but use of past or future services as consideration creates exactly the tax problem described; this form does not offer it.

**Consideration alternatives.**
- *Cash* (Alternative A): simplest; price per share × shares, paid by check or wire. A founder purchasing 4,000,000 shares at $0.00001 pays $40.
- *Technology* (Alternative B): the founder assigns pre-formation intellectual property under the technology assignment agreement in exchange for the shares. The board must determine the value of the technology (§ 152; conclusive absent fraud under § 152(d)) and that it is at least the aggregate par value (§ 153(a)). For tax, this is a transfer of property, potentially nonrecognized under § 351 **only if** the transferors of property, as a group, control at least 80% of the Company immediately afterward. Services are not property (§ 351(d)(1)), and stock issued for services does not count toward the 80% group unless the person also transfers property of more than nominal value. If one founder pays cash and the other contributes technology, both are property transferors (cash is property), so the group typically qualifies. Have tax counsel review if any founder contributes only services or if non-founders receive stock in the same transaction. Each "significant transferor" and the Company may have § 351 statement requirements on their returns (Treas. Reg. § 1.351-3).
- *Both* (Alternative C): common when founders want a modest cash payment plus a clean IP chain; the board values each component.
- If the founder assigns technology but the shares are paid entirely in cash, the technology assignment is a contribution to capital for no additional consideration; that is acceptable and simpler, but state it expressly in the technology assignment so it is not later characterized as an unpaid sale.

**Vesting (Section 3).** Market: 48 months, 12-month cliff, then monthly. Investors in the first priced round almost always require founder vesting; a founder who starts with none will often be asked to "re-vest" at the Series Seed or Series A. Credit for pre-formation work is common (Optional in Section 3.1(b)): set the Vesting Commencement Date earlier than the Closing Date, so part of the shares are vested at issuance. An 83(b) election is still essential for the unvested remainder.

**Acceleration (Section 3.3).** Market positions:
- *None* (Alternative A): company-favorable, still common at formation.
- *Single-trigger* (Alternative B): vesting accelerates on a Change in Control alone. Acquirers dislike it because the founder has no unvested equity to retain; investors often resist all but partial single-trigger (25%–50%).
- *Double-trigger* (Alternative C, this form's default): acceleration (often 100% for founders; 25%–50% in some deals) only on an Involuntary Termination within a window around a Change in Control (commonly 3 months before to 12 months after). Market-standard for founders.
- Definitions of Cause and Good Reason are founder-protective but conventional. Good Reason tracks the § 409A safe harbor procedure (notice within 90 days, 30-day cure, resignation shortly afterward), which also prevents stale claims. A release of claims is a condition of double-trigger acceleration (Section 3.4).
- Acceleration payments can be "parachute payments" under § 280G. Private companies can avoid the excise tax through a stockholder vote of more than 75% of disinterested holders under § 280G(b)(5)(A)(ii) and Treas. Reg. § 1.280G-1, Q&A-7; Optional Section 3.6 adds a "best-net" cutback.

**Repurchase option (Section 4).** Price: the lower of the original purchase price and current fair market value, per share — market. Exercise window: 90 days after termination, with Alternative B making exercise **automatic** unless the board elects otherwise, which avoids the frequent failure where nobody remembers to exercise and the departed founder keeps unvested shares by default. Payment by check, wire, or cancellation of indebtedness. Delaware § 160(a)(1) forbids repurchases that impair capital; at formation that is not an issue for par-value repurchases, but Section 4.8 handles it.

**Escrow vs. book-entry hold (Section 5).** Historically, certificates for unvested shares were held in escrow with a signed stock assignment. With uncertificated shares, a book-entry hold plus a pre-signed stock assignment achieves the same thing. Alternative A keeps the joint escrow instructions (Exhibit B); Alternative B uses a book-entry hold.

**Transfer restrictions and ROFR (Section 6).** Unvested shares cannot be transferred. Vested shares are subject to the bylaws' right of first refusal (Section 7.6 of the Bylaws) — Alternative A of Section 6.2 relies on it — or, if the Company's bylaws lack one, to a contractual ROFR in Alternative B. Under § 202(b), a bylaw ROFR binds shares issued before its adoption only if the holder agrees; Section 6.2 includes the Purchaser's express agreement to be bound, which cures any sequencing gap. The market stand-off (Section 6.4) mirrors the NVCA Investors' Rights Agreement lock-up so that the underwriters in an IPO can rely on it.

**Section 83(b) (Section 7).** The founder — not the Company — files the election. Deadline: within 30 days after the Closing Date (Treas. Reg. § 1.83-2(b)); if the 30th day falls on a weekend or legal holiday, the next business day (§ 7503). Use IRS Form 15620 (current revision April 2025) or a written statement meeting § 1.83-2(e); since mid-2025 the IRS also offers online submission of Form 15620 through an IRS Online Account (paper filing remains available). See the 83(b) election package template for the full procedure.

**Qualified small business stock (§ 1202).** Founder stock is often QSBS if the Company is a C corporation with aggregate gross assets of not more than $75 million (indexed after 2026) when the stock is issued and meets the active-business test during substantially all of the holding period. For stock issued after July 4, 2025, the exclusion is 50% after three years, 75% after four, 100% after five, subject to a per-issuer cap of the greater of $15 million (indexed after 2026) or ten times basis. With an 83(b) election, the holding period begins at issuance; without it, it starts only when shares vest (Treas. Reg. § 1.83-4(a); § 83(f)). The Company should not represent QSBS status; optional Section 9.5 adds a reasonable-efforts cooperation covenant founders often request.

**Securities law.** Founder sales rely on Securities Act § 4(a)(2) (no Form D needed for § 4(a)(2) alone; Form D is a Regulation D filing). State law also applies where the founder resides. In **California**, the DFPI's position is that a newly incorporated company issuing shares to founders in California must file a Limited Offering Exemption Notice under Corporations Code § 25102(f) — within 15 calendar days after the first sale in California (10 Cal. Code Regs. § 260.102.14(b)), electronically, with the § 25608(c) fee and, for a non-California issuer, a consent to service of process. Founders who are officers, directors or promoters are excluded from the 35-purchaser count (Rule 260.102.13), but the notice is still filed; failure to file does not destroy the exemption but triggers a late fee. Other states: check the founder's state of residence (many states exempt sales to officers and directors or isolated transactions without filing).

**State-specific contract points.** If the founder lives in a community property state (Arizona, California, Idaho, Louisiana, Nevada, New Mexico, Texas, Washington, Wisconsin) or has elected community property treatment under an opt-in statute, obtain the spousal consent (Exhibit C; see the stand-alone spousal-consent template for the state list). California courts do not enforce pre-dispute contractual jury-trial waivers (*Grafton Partners L.P. v. Superior Court*, 36 Cal. 4th 944 (2005)); Section 15.3 is drafted to survive that limitation through severability.

**Common mistakes this form avoids.** Issuing shares before the board was constituted and bylaws adopted; issuing shares "for services" with no price; a vesting schedule with no defined start date; a repurchase option with no exercise mechanics; no 83(b) election or a late one; no spousal consent; inconsistent share counts between the board consent, this agreement, the § 151(f) notice, and the cap table.

# FOUNDER RESTRICTED STOCK PURCHASE AGREEMENT

This Founder Restricted Stock Purchase Agreement (this "**Agreement**") is entered into as of {{effective_date}} (the "**Effective Date**") between {{company_name}}, a Delaware corporation (the "**Company**"), and {{founder_name}} (the "**Purchaser**").

## RECITALS

A. The Purchaser is a founder of the Company and is, or will be, providing services to the Company as an employee, officer, director or consultant.

B. The Board of Directors of the Company (the "**Board**") has approved the sale of shares of the Company's Common Stock to the Purchaser on the terms of this Agreement, has determined the fair market value of those shares, and, where the consideration includes the Assigned Technology, has determined the value of that consideration.

C. The Company requires, as a condition of the sale, that the shares be subject to vesting, a repurchase option, and restrictions on transfer, so that the Purchaser's ownership reflects continued service to the Company.

NOW, THEREFORE, the parties agree as follows:

## 1. DEFINITIONS

Capitalized terms used in this Agreement have the following meanings:

"**Assigned Technology**" has the meaning given in the Technology Assignment Agreement.

"**Bylaws**" means the Bylaws of the Company, as amended from time to time.

"**Cause**" means the occurrence of any of the following, as determined in good faith by the Board (excluding the Purchaser, if a director): (a) the Purchaser's conviction of, or plea of guilty or *nolo contendere* to, any felony, or any crime involving fraud, dishonesty or moral turpitude; (b) the Purchaser's commission of an act of fraud, embezzlement or material dishonesty against the Company; (c) the Purchaser's willful and material breach of any written agreement with the Company (including any confidential information and invention assignment agreement) or of any material written policy of the Company, which breach, if curable, is not cured within thirty (30) days after written notice from the Company describing it; (d) the Purchaser's willful misconduct or gross negligence that causes material harm to the Company; or (e) the Purchaser's willful and continued failure to perform the Purchaser's material duties (other than as a result of physical or mental incapacity), which failure is not cured within thirty (30) days after written notice from the Company describing it. No act or failure to act is "willful" if done, or omitted to be done, in good faith and with a reasonable belief that it was in the best interests of the Company.

"**Change in Control**" means the first to occur of the following: (a) any person or group of persons acting together becomes the beneficial owner, directly or indirectly, of securities of the Company representing more than fifty percent (50%) of the total voting power of the Company's then-outstanding securities, other than by acquiring securities directly from the Company in a transaction or series of related transactions whose primary purpose is to raise capital for the Company; (b) the consummation of a merger, consolidation or similar transaction involving the Company, unless the stockholders of the Company immediately before the transaction own, immediately after it, securities representing more than fifty percent (50%) of the total voting power of the surviving or resulting entity (or its parent) in substantially the same proportions as before; or (c) the consummation of a sale, lease, exclusive license or other disposition of all or substantially all of the assets of the Company and its subsidiaries taken as a whole, other than to an entity of which more than fifty percent (50%) of the voting power is owned by the Company's stockholders in substantially the same proportions as their ownership of the Company immediately before the disposition. A transaction is not a Change in Control if its sole purpose is to change the Company's jurisdiction of incorporation or to create a holding company owned in substantially the same proportions by the persons who held the Company's securities immediately before the transaction.

"**Closing**" and "**Closing Date**" have the meanings given in Section 2.3.

"**Code**" means the Internal Revenue Code of 1986, as amended, and the Treasury Regulations under it.

"**Common Stock**" means the Common Stock, par value {{par_value}} per share, of the Company.

"**Continuous Service Status**" means the absence of any interruption or termination of the Purchaser's service to the Company or any parent or subsidiary of the Company as an employee, officer, director or consultant. Continuous Service Status is not interrupted by (a) a change in the capacity in which the Purchaser renders service, or a transfer between the Company and any parent, subsidiary or successor, so long as service continues without interruption, or (b) a leave of absence approved in writing by the Company, subject to Section 3.5.

"**Fair Market Value**" means, as of any date, the fair market value of a share of Common Stock as determined in good faith by the Board, taking into account the most recent independent valuation of the Common Stock under Section 409A of the Code, if any, and any events since that valuation that the Board considers relevant.

"**Good Reason**" means the occurrence of any of the following without the Purchaser's written consent: (a) a material reduction in the Purchaser's base salary or base fees (other than a reduction of not more than ten percent (10%) that applies generally to all senior executives of the Company); (b) a material reduction in the Purchaser's duties, authority or responsibilities [[Optional: ; provided that a change in title or reporting relationship following a Change in Control solely because the Company becomes a subsidiary, division or business unit of a larger organization is not Good Reason if the Purchaser continues to have substantially the same duties and responsibilities with respect to the Company's business]]; (c) a relocation of the Purchaser's principal place of work by more than {{relocation_miles}} miles from its location immediately before the relocation (a change to or from remote work at the Purchaser's request is not a relocation); or (d) a material breach by the Company of this Agreement or any other material written agreement with the Purchaser. The Purchaser's resignation is for Good Reason only if (i) the Purchaser gives the Company written notice describing the condition within ninety (90) days after its initial existence, (ii) the Company fails to cure the condition within thirty (30) days after receiving that notice (the "**Cure Period**"), and (iii) the Purchaser resigns within thirty (30) days after the end of the Cure Period.

"**Involuntary Termination**" means the termination of the Purchaser's Continuous Service Status (a) by the Company (or any successor or acquirer) without Cause, other than as a result of the Purchaser's death or disability, or (b) by the Purchaser's resignation for Good Reason.

"**Market Stand-Off Period**" has the meaning given in Section 6.4.

"**Purchase Price**" means {{price_per_share}} per share, as adjusted under Section 11.2.

"**Repurchase Option**" has the meaning given in Section 4.1.

"**Securities Act**" means the Securities Act of 1933, as amended.

"**Shares**" means the {{founder_shares}} shares of Common Stock purchased under this Agreement, together with any securities issued in respect of those shares under Section 11.2.

"**Technology Assignment Agreement**" means the Technology Assignment Agreement between the Company and the Purchaser dated as of the Effective Date.

"**Termination Date**" means the date on which the Purchaser's Continuous Service Status terminates for any reason or no reason, including death or disability.

"**Transfer**" means any sale, assignment, gift, pledge, hypothecation, encumbrance, or other disposition of, or creation of a security interest in, any Shares or any right or interest in them, whether voluntary, involuntary, by operation of law, by court order, or otherwise, including any short sale, swap, or other transaction that transfers any economic consequence of ownership.

"**Unvested Shares**" means the Shares that have not vested under Section 3. "**Vested Shares**" means the Shares that have vested under Section 3.

"**Vesting Commencement Date**" means {{vesting_commencement_date}}.

The Shares are issued, and the Purchaser becomes the record owner of them, on the Closing Date, when the Company receives the consideration and the deliveries in Section 2.4(a).

**2.4 Deliveries at Closing.**

(a) The Purchaser shall deliver to the Company: (i) this Agreement, signed by the Purchaser; (ii) the consideration under Section 2.2, including, if applicable, the Technology Assignment Agreement, signed by the Purchaser; (iii) a Stock Assignment Separate from Certificate in the form of **Exhibit A**, signed by the Purchaser with the assignee, number of shares and date left blank (and additional signed copies if the Company requests); (iv) if Alternative A of Section 5 applies, the Joint Escrow Instructions in the form of **Exhibit B**, signed by the Purchaser; (v) if the Purchaser is married or in a registered domestic partnership and resides in a community property or similar marital property jurisdiction, or if the Company otherwise requests, a Spousal Consent in the form of **Exhibit C**, signed by the Purchaser's spouse or domestic partner; and (vi) the Company's standard confidential information and invention assignment agreement, signed by the Purchaser, if not previously delivered.

(b) The Company shall (i) countersign this Agreement and, if applicable, the Technology Assignment Agreement and the Joint Escrow Instructions; (ii) record the issuance of the Shares in its stock ledger; and (iii) within a reasonable time after the Closing, deliver to the Purchaser a notice of issuance of uncertificated shares under Section 151(f) of the Delaware General Corporation Law (the "**DGCL**") (or, if the Board has so determined, a certificate representing the Shares) bearing the legends in Section 10.

**2.5 Fully Paid Shares.** Upon receipt by the Company of the consideration in Section 2.2, the Shares are duly authorized, validly issued, fully paid and nonassessable.

## 3. VESTING

**3.1 Vesting Schedule.**

(a) [[Alternative A: The Shares are initially all Unvested Shares. [[Optional: (b) *Credit for prior service.* The parties acknowledge that the Purchaser provided services in organizing the Company's business for {{prior_service_months}} months before the Closing Date, and that the Vesting Commencement Date reflects that service. Any Shares that, under Section 3.1(a), would have vested on or before the Closing Date are Vested Shares as of the Closing Date.]]

(c) *Rounding.* Vesting is computed in whole shares, rounded down on each vesting date, with any fractional amount carried forward, so that all Shares are vested on the final vesting date.

(d) *No vesting after termination.* Except as provided in Section 3.3, no Shares vest after the Termination Date. Any Shares that are Unvested Shares on the Termination Date are subject to the Repurchase Option.

> **Drafting note:** Use Alternative A (cliff) by default. If the Vesting Commencement Date is earlier than the Closing Date, the Shares that are vested at issuance are not subject to § 83 restrictions; the 83(b) election is still required for the unvested portion and should describe the shares as a whole (see the 83(b) package).

**3.2 Effect of Termination.** On the Termination Date, all Unvested Shares become subject to the Repurchase Option under Section 4, and the Purchaser's right to vest in any further Shares ends, except as provided in Section 3.3.

**3.3 Acceleration.**

[[Alternative A: No acceleration. The Shares vest only under Section 3.1.]]

> **Drafting note:** "Lower of cost or fair market value" is market. At formation both numbers are par, so the distinction matters only if the Company's value falls below the original price.
